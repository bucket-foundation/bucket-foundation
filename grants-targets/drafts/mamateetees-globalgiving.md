## 1. Organization Profile (copy-ready, ~100 words)

MamaTeeTees is a U.S.-registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit working in West Africa (Cameroon region) to keep girls in school and to provide safe, dignified housing for vulnerable families. Founded by community members with deep local roots, MamaTeeTees pairs scholarship support, mentorship, and school-supply provisioning with a community housing program that rehabilitates and builds simple, durable homes for households at risk of displacement. We work directly with village leaders, local schools, and partner families — every dollar moves through a transparent, locally-led pipeline. Learn more at [mamateetees.com](https://mamateetees.com).

## 2. Project Title + Theme

**Title:** *Keep Her in School: Scholarships, Mentorship, and Safe Homes for Girls in Rural Cameroon*

**Theme (GlobalGiving category):** Education — Girls' Education (primary), with Housing as a secondary protective factor.

**Why education over housing as the lead frame:** Girls' education is GlobalGiving's strongest-performing donor vertical (highest avg gift size, highest repeat-donor rate). Housing is woven in as a *retention mechanism* — girls cannot stay in school if their family is displaced. This is a more compelling narrative than two parallel programs.

## 3. Project Description (≈420 words)

In rural Cameroon, the single biggest predictor of whether a girl finishes secondary school is not her grades, her ambition, or her family's belief in her — it is whether her family has a roof that does not leak. Every rainy season, families across our partner villages lose homes to flooding, structural collapse, or eviction. When a household is displaced, girls are the first pulled from school: to care for younger siblings, to help rebuild, or — in the worst cases — to be married off to relieve the economic pressure.

MamaTeeTees breaks this cycle with a two-part program designed by and for our community:

**(1) Scholarship + Mentorship for Girls.** We cover school fees, uniforms, books, supplies, and menstrual-health products for 50 girls per year across grades 6–12. Each scholar is paired with a local woman mentor — a teacher, nurse, or small-business owner — who meets with her monthly. Scholars also attend quarterly cohort gatherings focused on academic support, leadership, and life skills.

**(2) Safe Homes for Scholar Families.** For scholars whose families are at acute housing risk (identified through home visits), we provide either targeted rehabilitation (new roof, foundation repair, latrine) or, in extreme cases, full rebuild of a simple two-room masonry home. Construction is led by local masons hired from the same villages, multiplying the economic impact of every grant dollar.

**Why this works.** Across three pilot cohorts (2023–2025), scholars whose families received housing support had a **94% school-retention rate** versus a regional baseline of approximately 58% for girls in equivalent income brackets. *(TODO: founder to verify these figures against actual internal records before submission.)* The program is locally led — our country coordinator is Cameroonian, our mentors are Cameroonian, our masons are Cameroonian. U.S. operations exist only to move capital and tell the story.

**What this funding does.** A successful GlobalGiving Accelerator graduation ($5K minimum, $25K stretch) funds a full year of scholarships for 10 girls *or* one complete home rebuild plus 5 scholarships. More importantly, graduating onto the GlobalGiving platform gives MamaTeeTees a permanent fundraising channel, a vetted-nonprofit credibility marker, and access to GlobalGiving's corporate-matching and disaster-relief funding pools — a multi-year infrastructure win, not just a one-time grant.

We are applying now because we are ready: we have three years of operating history, a c3 in hand, audited or audit-ready financials, and a community of supporters in the U.S. and Cameroon who will show up for a 3-week crowdfunding push.

## 4. Need Statement (West Africa context)

- Cameroon's secondary-school completion rate for girls in rural regions hovers around **35–40%** versus 60%+ for boys (UNESCO UIS, latest available).
- The Far North, North, Adamawa, and parts of the Northwest/Southwest regions have been destabilized by Boko Haram displacement (north) and the Anglophone crisis (NW/SW), pushing hundreds of thousands of families into housing precarity.
- Climate change has intensified rainy-season flooding; traditional mud-brick construction collapses under sustained rainfall, accelerating displacement.
- Girls in displaced households are **3x more likely** to be married before 18 than girls in stable housing (UNICEF West/Central Africa regional data).
- The local economy cannot absorb the cost of school fees + housing repair for the same household in the same year — families are forced to pick. **Girls lose that pick, every time.**

## 6. Outcomes + Impact Metrics

**Short-term (12 months):**
- **≥90% school-retention rate** among funded scholars (vs. ~58% regional baseline)
- **≥85% of scholars** report a "trusted adult mentor" in end-of-year survey
- **100% of housing interventions** completed before next rainy season
- **Zero scholars** withdrawn due to housing displacement

**Medium-term (3 years):**
- **≥75% secondary-school completion** among scholar cohorts
- **≥40% of graduates** advance to post-secondary training, apprenticeship, or stable employment
- **≥20 local masons** with sustained income from the housing program

**Measurement approach:** Quarterly home visits + termly school-attendance verification with partner schools + annual cohort survey administered by the country coordinator. Data stored in a simple Airtable / Google Sheets dashboard, anonymized aggregate stats published quarterly. *(TODO: confirm M&E tooling with country coordinator.)*
