earth. This colorful style of music and
dance had its origins in China, but it
was frequently transported to Japan by
way of Korea. In the fifth century,
Korean court musicians came here in
Japan to perform at the funeral of an
emperor. It was for the same purpose
that their musical descendants performed

Shawa era. What an incredible
continuity. In the 7th century, another
Korean musician is said to have
introduced to Japan Chinese secular
dance. By the 8th century, Asian
continental music and dance was firmly
entrenched in the Japanese
court. This stately pageant first
evolved during China's colorful Tong
dynasty from a melding pot of popular
and ceremonial dances from all parts of
Asia. During the seventh through the
10th
centuries, foreign goods, traders, and
cultural traditions flowed into China
from the west, overland from Persia and
Syria, and over the sea from India and
the Malay Peninsula, arriving through
Canton and other ports. The cosmopolitan
tastes of Arab, Indian, and Malaysian
merchants created a need for fine
entertainment. In the Chinese capital
city of
Chongan, a tavern owner could hardly
expect to meet the competition without
the musical or dancing talents of some
handsome boy or girl from the west. That
is from what we call the near east. In
the 8th century, the Chinese emperor
established the pear garden, a large
quarter within the imperial precincts of
the city where hundreds of dancers,
musicians were trained for the
entertainment of the court.

nations. For example, Chinese sources of
