## Description

These interviews feature Nobel Laureate Murray Gell-Mann discussing his wide-ranging interests and contributions. Gell-Mann elaborates on the origin of the term "quark", acknowledging its inspiration from James Joyce's Finnegan's Wake and clarifying its "mathematical" nature as being permanently trapped within particles. He then shifts to his work on "plectics", the study of simplicity in complexity, exploring how simple fundamental laws lead to the universe's vast individuality and evolution through "frozen accidents." The physicist also shares his passion for natural history and conservation, particularly his involvement in the Rapid Assessment Program for biodiversity in tropical regions, and recounts his path into physics despite initial disinterest, influenced by his father and older brother. Finally, Gell-Mann reflects on the nature of scientific discovery, the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration at institutions like the Santa Fe Institute, and his views on various societal and environmental issues, including nuclear arms and the teaching of evolution.
