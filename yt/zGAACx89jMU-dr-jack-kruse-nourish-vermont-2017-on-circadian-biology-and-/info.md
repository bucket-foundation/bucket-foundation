## Description

​In his July 2017 presentation at Nourish Vermont, neurosurgeon Dr. Jack Kruse emphasized the critical roles of circadian biology and sun exposure in maintaining optimal health. He highlighted how natural light influences our body's internal clocks and metabolic processes.​

Dr. Kruse explained that exposure to the sun's ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) light is essential for several physiological functions. UV light, for instance, is necessary for the production of melatonin, a hormone that regulates sleep and other vital processes. Additionally, UV-B light enables the synthesis of Vitamin D, crucial for bone health and immune function. Infrared light, particularly near-infrared (IR-A), penetrates deeply into the body, supporting mitochondrial function and facilitating processes like autophagy and apoptosis, which are responsible for cell regeneration and the elimination of damaged cells, respectively. ​

He also discussed the impact of light on insulin regulation. Natural sunlight exposure during the day helps regulate insulin secretion, thereby influencing blood sugar levels and metabolic health. Conversely, exposure to artificial blue light at night can disrupt melatonin production and lead to insulin resistance, contributing to metabolic disorders such as diabetes. ​
Dr. Jack Kruse

Dr. Kruse advised adopting practices that align with our natural circadian rhythms. He recommended morning sun exposure to stimulate the body's natural wakefulness and hormonal cycles, and minimizing exposure to artificial light after sunset to support melatonin production and restorative sleep. He also suggested grounding techniques, such as connecting physically with the earth, to enhance the body's reception of natural light signals. ​
