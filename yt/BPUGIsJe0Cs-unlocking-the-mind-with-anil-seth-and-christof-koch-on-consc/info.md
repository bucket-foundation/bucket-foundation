In this instalment, we explore the insights of two great minds. First, we have Professor Anil Seth from the University of Sussex, a leading neuroscientist co-directing the Sackler Centre for Consciousness Science. Professor Seth's groundbreaking research spans neuroscience, mathematics, and philosophy, with a keen focus on how our brains construct reality—a process he describes as "controlled hallucination." Joining him is Professor Christof Koch, Chief Scientist of the Tiny Blue Dot Foundation in Santa Monica, California. The foundation's mission is to support research into neuroscience-based therapies, aiming to help individuals understand that they live in mental worlds of their own creation, known as their Perception Box. This construct of the mind has limitations and biases that individuals can learn to overcome.

Episode Highlights:
In this episode, we explore the insights of two brilliant minds, Anil Seth and Christof Koch. Christof Koch begins by reading the opening page of his new book, "Then I Am Myself the World," setting the stage for a deep dive into the concept of ego death and its implications. The discussion then moves to fundamental questions of ontology and metaphysics, followed by an exploration of idealism. We delve into the nature of out-of-body experiences and their intersection with quantum physics, before Seth rebukes Koch on illusionism. The conversation continues with the argument that the “Self” is a contingent construct rather than a fundamental feature. Integrated Information Theory (IIT) is introduced and defended, highlighting its relevance to understanding consciousness and complexity. Seth discusses the concept of a consciousness meter, the potential for artificial intelligence to achieve consciousness, and whether single-cell organisms can possess consciousness. The deactivation of the Default Mode Network (DMN) during orgasm is compared to experiences where the distinction between object and subject dissolves. The debate then turns to whether consciousness is an emergent or universal phenomenon. We conclude by discussing IIT as a theory of mereology, the concept of controlled hallucinations, and the idea that consciousness constructs the semantic meaning of the world. Join us as we journey through their profound contributions to understanding the nature of reality and existence.
