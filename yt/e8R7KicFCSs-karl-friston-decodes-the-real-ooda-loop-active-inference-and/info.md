## Description

Karl Friston, FRS, is the most-cited neuroscientist alive and the architect of the Free Energy Principle and Active Inference, the formal mathematical framework that describes how living systems perceive, predict, and engage with their environments under uncertainty. In this conversation, Brian "Ponch" Rivera and Mark McGrath walk Friston through John Boyd’s actual OODA sketch, not the four-box linearized cartoon, and the result is the closest thing yet to a formal validation of what Boyd was reaching for in 1995.

The episode builds the framework piece by piece: the environment and its irreducible features (entropy, ambiguity, novelty), the Markov blanket as the boundary that defines a self, sensory and active states, the generative model Boyd called Orientation, and the two pathways, implicit guidance and control, that Boyd drew but few have ever explained. Friston then formalizes what Wall Street already intuits: risk as a KL divergence between prior preferences and predicted outcomes, ambiguity as volatility, and expected free energy as the formal mathematics of risk management under uncertainty.

This is the episode the show has been building toward for 160+ conversations. It connects Boyd’s framework to active inference, predictive coding, the Bayesian brain, perceptual control theory, ecological psychology, the Constructal Law, the REBUS model and psychedelic-assisted therapy, flow states, McLuhan’s media theory, and the limits of large language models as agentic AI. It also names the gap: most current AI does not encode uncertainty, cannot plan over counterfactual futures, and therefore cannot orient. The path forward is biomimetic, and it looks far more like Boyd’s sketch than like anything currently dominating the market.
