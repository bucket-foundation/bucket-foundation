## Description

It is a homecoming of sorts. Sixteen years after David made Nathan read Jorge Luis Borges, the two return to discuss the great Argentinian writer in an episode that has as many labyrinths (well, not quite) and is as infinite (also probably not true) as every one of Borges’ short stories. And while this episode does have a finite beginning and a finite end and can only focus on three of the hits (Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius, The Library of Babel, and The Garden of Forking Paths), the two still can’t seem to entirely wrap their heads around the author’s rapid world-building, extremely high ratio of ideas vs. word count, and surprisingly playful nature. It’s safe to say that we at Books of Some Substance think Borges is pretty cool.

Take the litmus test: Read some JLB, then be our friend.  We’ll see you at the end (or at the beginning).

Jorge Luis Borges, (24 August 1899 – 14 June 1986) was an Argentine poet, essayist, translator, and short-story writer whose works became classics of 20th-century world literature.His first collection of short stories, El jardín de senderos que se bifurcan (The Garden of Forking Paths), appeared in 1941, composed mostly of works previously published in Sur. These are the stories covered here. Borges' themes include dreams, labyrinths, philosophers, libraries, mirrors, fictional writers, and mythology.
