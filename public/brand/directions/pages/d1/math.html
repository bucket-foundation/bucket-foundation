A Mathematical Theory of Communication

Claude E. Shannon, Bell System Technical Journal 27, 379 to 423.

$$H(p)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i\log_2 p_i$$
Definition. \(H\) is the average number of bits needed to name an outcome drawn from \(p\).

Derivation

  1. 1

    Require continuity, growth in n for equal outcomes, and additivity over successive choices.

  2. 2

    For n equal outcomes these force

    $$H\left(\tfrac1n,\dots,\tfrac1n\right)=K\log n$$
  3. 3

    Split rational probabilities into equal parts and apply additivity:

    $$H(p_1,\dots,p_n)=-K\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i\log p_i$$
  4. 4

    Choose \(K=1\) and base 2 to measure in bits.

    0 ≤ -(p * Real.log p) := by
  have hlog : Real.log p ≤ 0 := Real.log_nonpos h0 h1
  have : p * Real.log p ≤ 0 := mul_nonpos_of_nonneg_of_nonpos h0 hlog
