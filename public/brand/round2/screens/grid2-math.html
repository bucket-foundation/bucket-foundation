Proposition 47

Cite

In right-angled triangles the square on the side subtending the right angle equals the squares on the sides containing it.

$$\textcolor{#1F4E9C}{c^2} = \textcolor{#C8331F}{a^2} + \textcolor{#8A6200}{b^2}$$
  1. Square the hypotenuse, blue, and each leg: red and yellow. I.46.
  2. Drop the perpendicular from the right angle; it splits the blue square in two rectangles.
  3. Each rectangle equals the square on the nearer leg, since \(\triangle ABD \cong \triangle FBC\). I.4, I.41.
  4. Sum both: \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\). Common notion 2.

Figure after Oliver Byrne, The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid, 1847, plate for I.47, public domain. archive.org. Set in KaTeX 0.16.9.
