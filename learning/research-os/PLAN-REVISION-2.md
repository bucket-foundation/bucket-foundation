## 2. Evidence-driven revisions from batch three

### a. Human-AI complementarity on productions

The overlap map's own batch-three note under question 1 already names Buçinca, Malaya, and Gajos (2021, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/hci-human-ai-collaboration/bucinca-malaya-gajos-2021-cognitive-forcing-functions.md`) as the mechanism behind Locate and Quote's evidence-before-Check ordering: a learner gathers and commits to quoted evidence before Check ever runs, the same commit-before-reveal structure the paper's own lab result found reduces over-reliance on a wrong answer past explanation alone, with a retention benefit that outlasts the session. That ordering already ships (`WORKSPACE.md` section 1). What batch three adds past confirming it: Bansal and colleagues (2021, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/hci-human-ai-collaboration/bansal-et-al-2021-ai-explanations-complementary-team-performance.md`) find complementary team performance rare even when a convincing explanation raises trust, and Vaccaro, Almaatouq, and Malone (2024, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/hci-human-ai-collaboration/vaccaro-almaatouq-malone-2024-combinations-humans-ai-useful.md`)'s meta-analysis finds human-AI combination underperforms the better of either alone on content-creation tasks, the task type a Research OS production, a written claim with cited evidence, resembles more than the decision tasks the same review finds combination helps.

**The design response.** Check's own contract gains an explicit commit step ahead of any verdict reveal: the learner submits a self-rating of their own explanation (grounded or not, on the support and contradiction and unknown vocabulary Check itself returns) before Check's real verdict is shown; `sanitizeGradeResult` (`src/lib/research-os/grounding.ts`) gains a field recording the learner's pre-reveal self-rating alongside the model's own verdict. This is a code change, not yet built, scoped as an addition to `ros-04`'s remaining Check-tool work.

**The pilot arm or measure that tests it.** Fold into H1's existing three-arm design (`PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`) as a new measured variable inside the constrained-AI arm alone, since the commit step only ships there: the agreement rate between a learner's pre-reveal self-rating and Check's own verdict, read against H1's near-transfer post-test, following Bansal's own finding that a person's independent accuracy predicts complementary performance better than any explanation-design variable. H1's existing production-diversity secondary outcome (Doshi and Hauser 2024) becomes the direct test of whether a Research OS production behaves like Vaccaro's content-creation category or like a decomposable decision task, per that paper's own open question about separating Locate and Quote's retrieval work from a learner's own claim-writing.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** Buçinca is a real lab study whose mechanism the shipped tool ordering already matches by structure. Bansal and Vaccaro are real studies that complicate rather than confirm a complementary-gain assumption. None of the three has run inside Research OS itself.

**Evidence added in batch four, complicates:** Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/kirschner-sweller-clark-2006-minimal-guidance-does-not-work.md`) argue minimally guided instruction underperforms guided instruction for learners without high prior knowledge, a risk read directly onto the four-tool workspace if its own guidance does not clear that paper's own bar. Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn (2007, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/hmelo-silver-duncan-chinn-2007-scaffolding-response.md`) reply that problem-based and inquiry learning are extensively scaffolded rather than minimally guided, a description structurally closer to the commit-before-reveal design above, conditional on checking their own scaffolding criteria against Check tool by tool. Wineburg and McGrew (2019, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/source-evaluation/wineburg-mcgrew-2019-lateral-reading.md`) add a further complication the commit-step design does not address: the expert strategy for evaluating a source is leaving the page to cross-reference elsewhere, more than reading deeper within one already-located source, so a learner's pre-reveal self-rating could reach high agreement with Check's own verdict while both share the same single-source blind spot.

### b. Progression unidimensionality

The design question `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 5's OPEN-2 already poses: whether the five states stay a ladder per node, the shipped design, one ordinal `stage` column, high-water mark, or become a profile, separately tracked dimensions the way Bloom revised's own two-dimensional grid (OPEN-3) or Linn's continuous knowledge-integration score would require.

Alonzo and Steedle (2009, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/educational-methods/alonzo-steedle-2009-force-motion-learning-progression.md`) is real, field-tested evidence against assuming a single ladder holds by default: three independent placement methods applied to a comparably sized five-level force-and-motion learning progression agreed for only a modest majority of students, with some student response patterns consistent with skipping a hypothesized level. This does not show Bucket's own five states split into a profile. It sets a bar the OPEN-2 Rasch or IRT validation study has to clear before the ladder counts as settled, and it names a concrete second test that study should run alongside a single-dimension fit statistic: repeat Alonzo and Steedle's own three-independent-methods design against Research OS's own states, comparing placement by the Check tool's automated grade, by the diagnostic probe's cold-start branch (`onProbeCheckResult`), and by a teacher's own review decision, as three independent methods over the same nodes, and report their pairwise agreement rate the way Alonzo and Steedle report theirs.

**What the evidence schema needs.** `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4 already lists "no before-and-after stage pair on an event" among its gaps. This revision adds one more: a `placementMethod` field on `EvidenceEvent` (`check` | `probe` | `teacher_review`, alongside the `kind` already carried), so a later Rasch or IRT pass can group evidence by which method set a state and compute Alonzo and Steedle's own agreement statistic without re-deriving method provenance from `kind` and `note` fields after the fact.

The default for Phase 1 stays a per-node ladder. Switching to a profile before the OPEN-2 study runs would build a second data model on an unvalidated premise; Alonzo and Steedle's finding is reason to log the extra field now, ahead of that decision, rather than reason to change the shipped `stage` column today.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** A real, field-tested learning progression of comparable size and structure shows the disagreement risk directly. It has not run against Research OS's own five states or its ICAP, SOLO, and Bloom-revised mapping.

**Evidence added in batch four, extends:** Kuhn (1999, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/epistemic-cognition/kuhn-1999-developmental-model-critical-thinking.md`) and Chinn, Buckland, and Samarapungavan (2011, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/epistemic-cognition/chinn-buckland-samarapungavan-2011-epistemic-cognition.md`) supply two more candidate dimensional structures for the OPEN-2 profile side of this question, alongside Bloom revised and Linn already named above: Kuhn's three forms of meta-knowing (metacognitive, metastrategic, epistemological) and Chinn and colleagues' five-component epistemic-cognition framework (aims and value, knowledge structure, sources and justification, virtues and vices, reliable processes) are both coarser-to-finer alternatives a Rasch or IRT validation pass could test against the shipped ladder, past ICAP, SOLO, and Bloom revised alone. Neither has run against Research OS's own states; both extend rather than resolve OPEN-2's own ladder-versus-profile question.

### c. Prerequisite annotation agreement

**The confidence source policy for inferred edges.** `ROUTING.md` already places `inferred`-sourced edges at 0.3 to 0.65 confidence and routes every proposal to a human review list rather than the graph (`INGESTION.md`; `scripts/research-os/ingest/infer-edges.ts` has no `--apply` mode). Alzetta and colleagues (2018, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/prerequisite-knowledge-graphs/alzetta-et-al-2018-pret-prerequisite-enriched-terminology.md`)'s gold-standard dataset supports keeping that policy: trained human annotators reached only moderate agreement labeling prerequisite pairs in a comparable domain, with disagreement concentrated on same-section pairs where a co-occurrence relation and a true prerequisite relation are easy to conflate, the exposure a lexical-overlap Jaccard method scoped to one branch at a time (`infer-edges.ts`'s own comparison scope) carries by construction.

**The teacher-flag threshold.** `LOW_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD` at 0.6 needs no change on this evidence. What needs a policy statement is how a reviewer's own disagreement with a flagged edge gets read. Alzetta's moderate-agreement baseline sets the expected reading: a nonzero disagreement rate among trained humans, the same reading Jönsson and Svingby (2007) already supply for rubric scoring in `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md`'s OPEN-7, a sign of routine reviewer variation rather than a broken routing calculation. The concrete addition, scoped into `ros-13`'s still-open LLM-assisted edge-inference item: a future `llm_proposed_edge` review item never inherits a confidence above the `inferred` tier's own 0.65 ceiling regardless of the model's own stated confidence, and the review UI's "needs review" badge (`WORKSPACE.md`) names the same-section conflation risk in its title text when `confidence_source` is `inferred` or `llm_infer`, so a reviewer knows the failure pattern to check for.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** Alzetta is a real, gold-standard annotation study measuring the exact operation, prerequisite-pair labeling, Research OS's own review process performs, in a comparable but different domain, an introductory computer science textbook rather than K-12 physics. It has not run against Research OS's own inferred edges, none of which are live in the graph today.

### d. The ros-08 power finding

`PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s Sampling Plan computes: at an assumed effect size d = 0.4 (the lower end of the system review's own heuristic), a naive two-arm comparison needs 119 learners per arm; correcting for class-level clustering at an illustrative ICC of 0.10 (unsourced within this corpus, flagged there for replacement) raises that to 405 learners per arm, 17 classes per arm. `PLAN.md` section 8's own Phase 1 scope, two classrooms, one semester, and `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 3 item 9's unchanged repetition of it, fall far short. At Phase 1's feasible enrollment, four to six classes total, the same draft computes a minimum detectable effect near d = 1.0, an effect size only the largest single studies in this corpus (Bastani and colleagues 2025's own guardrail-versus-unrestricted contrast, read in percentage terms) approach.

This forces every place Phase 1 is described as a study capable of confirming H1's directional claim, `PLAN.md` section 8's "a pre-registered study comparing the constrained workspace, a permissive tutor, and no AI," `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5's ETH fellowship paragraph, and the funding lead paragraphs in `WAVE-1-TARGETS.md`, to carry the same qualifier `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` already applies to itself: Phase 1 is a feasibility and effect-size-estimation pilot for H1, distinct from a fully powered confirmatory test of it. The fully powered target is a named Phase 2 extension of the same registration. H2, H4, H5, and H6, and H1's own process-log and self-report outcomes, stay adequately scoped at Phase 1, since each is individual-level or within-class or runs offline against the engine's held-out corpus rather than needing the cluster-corrected between-class sample the power table above computes.

The power computation is Research OS's own arithmetic, computed from its own stated design constraints, carrying no external-domain caveat the way (a) through (c) above do. Its one real caveat sits inside the arithmetic itself: the ICC assumption driving the cluster-corrected numbers is an illustrative planning figure rather than a published benchmark for this outcome type, flagged in `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` for replacement once a published multi-site ICC benchmark or the pilot's own first-semester variance decomposition exists.

**Status: STABLE.** The power computation is settled arithmetic under stated assumptions; the assumptions themselves carry their own named uncertainty, apart from the arithmetic's own validity given them.

**Evidence added in batch four, supports:** three more published effect-size anchors join Bastani and colleagues (2025) as external checks on the d = 0.4 to 0.5 planning assumption, each closer in kind to Research OS's own pedagogy than a percentage-only contrast: Chen and Yang (2019, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/chen-yang-2019-pbl-meta-analysis.md`), a moderator-tested meta-analysis of project-based learning's achievement effect; Furtak and colleagues (2012, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/furtak-et-al-2012-inquiry-based-science-meta-analysis.md`), whose 37-study meta-analysis of inquiry-based science teaching reports a mean effect size of 0.50; and Lazonder and Harmsen (2016, `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/lazonder-harmsen-2016-inquiry-based-learning-meta-analysis.md`), whose 72-study meta-analysis of guidance in inquiry-based learning reports effect sizes from 0.50 to 0.71 across three outcome types. All three sit inside or above the 0.4 to 0.5 range `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` already assumes, supporting rather than revising the current planning figure, though none corrects the flagged ICC assumption the cluster-corrected table itself depends on.
