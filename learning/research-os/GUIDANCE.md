# Research OS for K-12: Faded Guidance for Low-Prior-Knowledge Learners

**Status:** shipped, bead `ros-14` · **Date:** 2026-09-10 · Reads against `learning/research-os/LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md`, `learning/research-os/ROUTING.md`, `learning/research-os/WORKSPACE.md`, `src/lib/research-os/probe.ts`, `src/lib/research-os/stages.ts`, `src/lib/research-os/EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md`, and `supabase/seed/research-os-sky-blue.json`. Reading list: Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark 2006 (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/project-based-inquiry-learning/kirschner-sweller-clark-2006-minimal-guidance-does-not-work.md`), Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn 2007 (`.../hmelo-silver-duncan-chinn-2007-scaffolding-response.md`), Lazonder and Harmsen 2016 (`.../lazonder-harmsen-2016-inquiry-based-learning-meta-analysis.md`), and Renkl 2002 (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/educational-methods/renkl-2002-worked-out-examples-self-explanations.md`, already cited by `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` OPEN-4).

Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark 2006 argue that minimally guided instruction ignores working-memory limits: a novice with no schema to hang new information on drowns in an unguided search for a solution before they ever reach the concept the search was supposed to teach. Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn's 2007 reply does not dispute the working-memory argument; it argues that well-designed problem-based and inquiry learning is never unguided; scaffolding is what makes it work, and their own account of that scaffolding names the same two moves this bead builds: give a novice a model of the target performance before asking them to produce it, and adjust the amount of scaffolding to how much support that specific learner still needs. Lazonder and Harmsen's 2016 meta-analysis quantifies the second half: across sixty-four studies, guidance that adapted to the learner outperformed guidance that stayed fixed, and process-focused metacognitive scaffolds outperformed data-focused ones for domain-general work. Renkl 2002 gives the two moves this file implements their most direct precedent, worked examples paired with a brief hint at a step's own rationale raise a learner's self-explanation activity; a hint that restates the step's content instead adds nothing. This bead is the Research OS version of that same worked-example-plus-fade pattern, applied to the Understanding stage's own Check tool.

## 1. Guidance level

A learner's guidance level is one of three values, `high`, `medium`, or `low`, computed server-side by `src/lib/research-os/guidance.ts`'s `guidanceLevel(learnerId, chain)`. It composes two rules.

**The base level.** `computeGuidanceLevel` reads the learner's own Stage on the routed chain's first two nodes (`frontier.ts`'s `chain`, farthest-from-target first: the two farthest-back prerequisites, always the nodes closest to the learner's actual working frontier regardless of which node happens to be on screen). Both below Understanding: `high`. Exactly one: `medium`. Neither: `low`. A node with no `learner_node_state` row reads as Stage `access`, the same "no record as access" default every other Research OS rule already uses.

**The fading schedule.** `nextGuidanceLevel` adjusts the base level by one step from the learner's last two in-path Check outcomes, across every node, diagnostic-probe checks excluded (`probe.ts`'s own header already establishes a cold-start probe answer measures something different from an in-path Check, so it is not counted). Two passes in a row drops the level one step, `high` to `medium`, `medium` to `low`, `low` stays `low`. Two abstains or fails in a row raises it one step, the mirror image. A mixed pair, or fewer than two recorded Check outcomes, leaves the base level unchanged. The floor and ceiling never wrap: a learner already at `low` who passes twice more stays at `low`.

Composing the two this way keeps the base level answering "how much does this learner generally know of the nearest foundational material" while the schedule answers "how is this learner doing right now": the schedule can only nudge the base one step in either direction, never skip a level, so a learner's guidance never jumps from `high` straight to `low` on a single strong Check.

`GET /api/research-os/route` computes this level once per request, for the target's own chain, forced to `low` when the learner's class has the arm switch (section 4) off, and returns it as `guidance` (`null` for an anonymous request). `POST /api/research-os/workspace`'s `check` action recomputes it authoritatively server-side, scoped to whichever node is being checked (`computeFrontier` with that node itself as the target, since the workspace route is node-agnostic by design and has no reliable way to know which page-level target a given node belongs to; on Phase 0's single connected seed path this gives the same first-two-ancestor pair as the page target's own chain for every node past the first two hops). Neither route trusts a client-supplied guidance value: both compute it themselves from the learner's own verified state.

## 2. What the workspace shows

Before the learner's own explanation box (the Check tool's textarea), the workspace shows the selected node's own worked example, when one exists (`GraphNode.workedExample`, seeded on the sky-blue path's first six nodes: `light-travels-in-straight-lines`, `sunlight-looks-white`, `air-is-made-of-tiny-particles`, `white-light-splits-into-colors`, `each-color-is-a-wavelength`, `light-can-scatter-off-small-things`):

- **High guidance:** the full worked example.
- **Medium guidance:** the first half only (`src/lib/research-os/worked-examples.ts`'s `firstHalfOfWorkedExample`, whole sentences, rounded up so a 3-sentence example shows 2).
- **Low guidance:** nothing. The learner goes straight to their own explanation box.

A worked example is authored content, a short (2 to 4 sentence), grade-4-level model explanation of the node's own idea, grounded in that node's existing `summary` and its passages.ts source, never a verbatim quotation (contrast `passages.ts`'s `QuotePassage`, which is verbatim by construction). Each carries the source it rests on, formatted the same way `grounding.ts`'s `citationLabel` formats a Check citation, so a worked example's own sourcing is legible next to whatever the Check tool cites.

A worked example is authored content, written in advance by a person: `GraphNode.workedExample` is seed data (`supabase/seed/research-os-sky-blue.json`'s `worked_example` key per node, `{text, source}`), the same authoring discipline `passages.ts`'s header already documents for verbatim quotations. This keeps the S1 to S7 safety floor intact: nothing here is a model call that could hallucinate, and the AI still never writes the learner's own explanation, unchanged from before this bead.

## 3. Check prompts adapt

`src/lib/research-os/grounding.ts`'s `gradeExplanation` takes an optional `guidanceLevel`/`passage` pair. At `high` guidance, when the checked node has a curated verbatim passage (`passages.ts`'s `getPassage`), the grounding block sent to the model gains one `POINTER` line naming the exact passage sentence, and the system prompt gains one matching hard rule: when a verdict is `support`, name that sentence in the feedback, so the learner has something concrete to go check for themselves. At every other level, medium, low, or high with no curated passage yet, the grounding block and prompt are byte-identical to the pre-`ros-14` shape; no pointer is fabricated for a node whose source has not been verified.

This changes only how much the tutor's own feedback text points at. Every code-level contract `WORKSPACE.md` section 1 already enforces, `sanitizeGradeResult`'s closed `result`/`confidence` enums, the single-allowed-citation filter, the complete absence of any field for a rewritten explanation, is unaffected by guidance level: `GradeResult`/`sanitizeGradeResult` take no guidance-related input at all, so nothing about the pointer's presence or absence can widen what a model response is allowed to return. `scripts/test-research-os-workspace-contracts.ts`'s "Guidance level" block asserts both halves of this directly: the pointer only ever appears at `high` guidance with a real passage, and the sanitizer's own contract tests, run once, already cover every level by construction.

## 4. Arm switch and analysis

Every evidence event `EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md` already tracks gains one more optional field, `guidanceLevel` (`stages.ts`'s `EvidenceEvent`/`EvidenceContext`), the level in effect when that event was produced. Every transition function that already accepts an `EvidenceContext` threads the field the same way it threads `sessionId`; in Phase 0, only the workspace route's `check` action computes and passes a value, since that is the one place item 3's prompt adaptation lives and the one place a pilot most needs to compare arms. Open, Transfer, and Production events carry the field on their type but stay unpopulated: none of the three has a guidance-level distinction meaningful enough yet to justify the extra frontier computation their own routes do not otherwise need. A future pass that finds a use for guidance level on one of those events only has to pass it, the schema is already there.

`graph.classes.research_os_guidance_enabled` (migration `20260910060000_research_os_guidance.sql`) is the per-class arm switch, a plain boolean, default `true`. PR #63 (cognitive forcing) had not merged into `main` as of this migration, so no existing forcing-switch column was found on `graph.classes` to mirror the name of; this adds its own column following `graph.classes`' existing convention rather than blocking on that PR. `src/lib/research-os/db.ts`'s `isGuidanceEnabledForLearner` (backed by the pure, unit-tested `decideGuidanceEnabled`) reads it: a learner with no class membership at all reads as enabled, the base product behavior; a learner in one or more classes reads as enabled if ANY of them has the switch on, since a specific class's own control-arm assignment should not be able to silently veto guidance for every other class the same learner happens to also be in. When the switch reads disabled, both `GET /api/research-os/route` and the workspace `check` action force the level to `low`, the same behavior a low-prior-knowledge-but-already-fluent learner would see: no worked example, no pointer. This makes `low` do double duty as both "the learner has shown mastery" and "this class's arm has scaffolding off," a deliberate simplification rather than adding a fourth enum value Phase 0 has no second use for; a later pass that needs to tell the two apart in the evidence log can add a `guidanceDisabledByClass: boolean` field to `EvidenceEvent` without changing `GuidanceLevel` itself.

**Open questions**, left for the PR #63 review pass once it merges: whether the two arm switches should share one naming convention (`research_os_guidance_enabled` versus whatever PR #63 ships), and whether a class ever legitimately wants guidance on but forcing off, or the reverse, which would mean the two switches need to compose rather than duplicate the same on/off shape.

## 5. What is logged and what it rests on

Per evidence event (`EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md`'s own contract, extended): `guidanceLevel`, the level in effect. Combined with the event's own `result`/`confidence`/`abstained` and `fromStage`/`toStage`, a pilot can read, directly off the evidence log with no re-derivation, how a Check outcome correlates with the guidance level the learner was shown at the time, RQ1-style ("does more scaffolding change what a learner does next") without a second logging pass.

`scripts/test-research-os-guidance.ts` covers `computeGuidanceLevel` (every base-level branch, including the zero- and one-entry edge cases), `classifyCheckOutcome`, `nextGuidanceLevel` (both directions, both floor and ceiling, mixed pairs, short history), `decideGuidanceEnabled` (the class-switch OR rule, including a `null` pre-migration value), and `firstHalfOfWorkedExample` (sentence rounding, single-sentence and no-punctuation inputs, empty input). `scripts/test-research-os-workspace-contracts.ts`'s "Guidance level" block covers section 3's own claim directly. No test in either file calls a model or a live database, matching this repo's existing research-os test convention.
