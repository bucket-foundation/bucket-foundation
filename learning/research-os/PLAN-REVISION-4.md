## 2. Evidence-driven revisions from batch five

Fourteen cards named in this pass's own brief carry the load-bearing evidence for this section, all from `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/` on PR #90's branch (`intake/ros-literature-5`, open, not yet merged to `main`; read via `git show origin/intake/ros-literature-5:<path>` per this pass's own instructions rather than assuming the corpus already landed).

### a. Mandatory participation

Revision 3 section 2b named a founder decision, decision 6, on whether any Research OS pilot ever makes Production submission a requirement, and left it OPEN on Grinnell and colleagues (2020)'s misconduct finding against Bangera and Brownell (2014)'s access argument. Batch five adds two cards that state the opposite finding on the same question, and two that reframe it.

Stukas, Snyder, and Clary (1999, `student-research-experiences/stukas-snyder-clary-1999-mandatory-volunteerism.md`) is the founding experimental case for a reactance account: college students required to volunteer showed lower future volunteering intentions than students given a choice, an effect concentrated in students who would not have volunteered freely and largely absent in students already inclined to volunteer. Metz and Youniss (2003, `student-research-experiences/metz-youniss-2003-required-service-heightens-volunteerism.md`) tracks a real school district that instituted required community service and finds the requirement did not deter, and appeared to raise, students' subsequent voluntary service, a direct field-setting contradiction of the reactance account the two papers were not written in conversation with each other to resolve. The Metz and Youniss card's own text names the resulting state as unresolved, and this pass's reading agrees: real, on-the-ground evidence sits on both sides of whether a requirement helps or harms.

Two more cards reframe the question rather than taking a side. Patall, Cooper, and Robinson (2008, `educational-methods/patall-cooper-robinson-2008-choice-intrinsic-motivation-meta-analysis.md`), a 41-study meta-analysis, finds choice raises intrinsic motivation and performance, stronger for children than adults, weaker or absent once a reward attaches to the choice made. Reeve (2006, `educational-methods/reeve-2006-autonomy-supportive-teachers.md`) argues the controlling-versus-autonomy-supportive manner in which a requirement is delivered, offering rationale, acknowledging the learner's perspective, preserving meaningful choice inside the structure, predicts engagement independent of whether a requirement exists at all; students are most engaged, in this account, when a teacher offers both structure and autonomy support together.

**The design response.** Revision 3's own decision 6 default stays the recommended default: Production submission stays opt-in. The provenance guard revision 3 named as the one condition for revisiting that default has now shipped (PR #73), and `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` revision 1 already recorded that the merge alone does not count as the founder's own re-decision. This pass adds one refinement for any class a teacher chooses to make Production mandatory anyway, grounded in Reeve and Patall rather than in Stukas-versus-Metz, which this pass cannot resolve: the teacher-review step around a mandatory Production should be delivered autonomy-supportively (rationale offered, the learner's own perspective acknowledged, a real choice preserved inside the requirement, for instance which of several valid gap nodes to route to next), and no incentive or payment attaches to a required Production, extending `PRODUCTION-GUARD.md` section 4's existing no-payment-before-canon-acceptance rule with Patall's own reward-undermines-choice finding as a second reason rather than only the first.

**The pilot measure.** Add an autonomy-supportive-delivery rating, teacher self-report on a short rubric drawn from Reeve's own named behaviors, as a covariate alongside `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s existing `productionRequired` field, for every class that marks Production required. Read against the same provenance-guard flag-rate secondary outcome the preregistration already registers under H3 (unverified sources, duplicate flags, missing counter-evidence), this tests whether delivery manner predicts the misconduct-adjacent outcomes Grinnell and colleagues' own finding raises the concern about, independent of mandate status alone.

**Status: OPEN.** Stukas, Snyder, and Clary and Metz and Youniss report opposite field-relevant findings on the same underlying question, neither paper resolves the conflict, and this pass does not either; the STRONG LEAN this section can offer is narrower, that Reeve's delivery-manner account and Patall's reward caution give a concrete mitigation for a class that requires Production regardless of which side of the Stukas-versus-Metz conflict turns out to describe Research OS's own setting.

### b. Holdout ledger scoring

`hte.holdout_ledger`'s own hit-rate report and its `MIN_VERIFIED_FOR_LABEL = 20` gate (withholding a validated ranking label until twenty held-out cases accumulate) rest on the Brier score without a documented account of why 20, or of what a low score is diagnosing.

Brier (1950, `scientific-discovery-metascience/brier-1950-verification-of-forecasts-probability.md`) defines the quadratic scoring rule the engine's own campaign runs already report (0.0226 for the `literature` corpus's k-fold holdout); it is a proper scoring rule, rewarding a forecaster whose stated probabilities track observed frequency over one who hedges or overstates confidence, but reports one aggregate number. Murphy (1973, `scientific-discovery-metascience/murphy-1973-vector-partition-probability-score.md`) splits that same score into three terms, uncertainty, reliability (how far stated probabilities track observed frequencies), and resolution (how much probabilities vary across outcome classes independent of calibration), and shows two forecasters can share an aggregate score while differing sharply in which term drives it. PR #48's own real-corpora calibration fix (three bugs found by code inspection, none the originally suspected cap) is exactly the diagnosis this decomposition could have narrowed before the search began.

Two replication-forecasting studies anchor a possible answer to what N the ledger's label should require. Dreber and colleagues (2015, `scientific-discovery-metascience/dreber-et-al-2015-prediction-markets-reproducibility.md`) show market-elicited probabilities predicted which of 44 psychology studies would replicate, better than individual survey forecasts, and that the tested hypotheses carried a low prior probability of holding (median about 9 percent) before replication raised confidence. Camerer and colleagues (2018, `scientific-discovery-metascience/camerer-et-al-2018-evaluating-replicability-nature-science.md`) extend the same market-and-survey design to social-science experiments in Nature and Science, again forecasting replication outcomes at meaningfully better than chance, on a comparable-scale sample this corpus's own card does not restate as a raw count.

**The design response.** Add Murphy's three-term decomposition to `hte.holdout_ledger`'s own report, alongside the aggregate Brier-style score already shown, so a future fix can be checked against whether it moved reliability, resolution, or both, the same check PR #48's own bug hunt had to perform by reading code instead. This is a code change, not yet built, engine-side, independent of every Research OS app item.

**What N the label should require.** Neither Dreber and colleagues nor Camerer and colleagues run a formal power analysis for this ledger's own question; the strongest concrete anchor in this batch is Dreber's 44-study sample, the point at which a forecast-validation design of this shape is demonstrated to work. `MIN_VERIFIED_FOR_LABEL = 20` sits at under half that anchor. This pass recommends raising the floor toward the 40-to-44 range these two studies imply is where the design is demonstrated, sourced to Dreber and colleagues' own N rather than picked arbitrarily, as a recommendation for engine-maintainer confirmation rather than an automatic change: neither anchor is a power calculation built for `hte.holdout_ledger`'s own question, and Dreber's own low-prior finding (about 9 percent) is a separate caution that an unvalidated ranking in a domain with a similarly low true-claim base rate can look more confident than the underlying pool warrants even after the floor is raised.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** The scoring rule and its decomposition are seventy-plus-year-old, standard instruments with no counter-evidence in this batch; the design response is concrete and scoped against code that exists today; the N recommendation is a reasoned anchor rather than a power-analyzed number, and neither card was tested against this engine's own ledger directly.

### c. Explanation quality

PR #60 shipped a disclosed, model-generated understanding artifact per hypothesis with no documented acceptance rubric; `docs/RESEARCH-OS-INTEGRATION.md`'s own overlap-map question 12 (scientific understanding distinct from correctness) stays Partial per revision 2's own table.

Miller (2019, `hci-human-ai-collaboration/miller-2019-explanation-in-ai.md`) argues explainable-AI work should build on established philosophy, cognitive-science, and social-psychology accounts of how people explain to each other, rather than on a researcher's own intuition of what a good explanation looks like. Lombrozo (2006, `hci-human-ai-collaboration/lombrozo-2006-structure-function-explanations.md`) supplies one such account directly: people prefer simpler, broader explanations by default, and the function of a good explanation is generalization, supporting inference to a new, related case beyond the one case explained.

**The design response.** Score the understanding artifact, and separately a learner's own Production explanation, against Lombrozo's own generalization criterion: whether the explanation supports inference to a new, related case, beyond restating the case at hand. This gives Miller's own general call a specific, literature-sourced rubric in place of a fresh researcher intuition, and gives Krenn and colleagues (2022)'s own internalization bar, already cited in `GUIDANCE.md`'s reading list, a concrete mechanism: generalization is what separates an explanation a learner has internalized from one they have only reproduced.

**Whether a non-specialist restatement test enters the pilot.** Recommended as an exploratory addition, scored on a sampled subset and reported descriptively, kept out of the confirmatory analysis: a rater unfamiliar with the specific node reads the learner's or artifact's own explanation and states what they understood from it, using the same sampling convention `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` already applies to its human cross-score. The test is scored for whether the restatement captures the mechanism and would generalize to a new case, per Lombrozo. Restatement alone tests legibility; the generalization check is what reaches the internalization standard Miller and Krenn and colleagues both name as the real target.

**Status: OPEN.** Neither Miller nor Lombrozo tests a model-generated understanding artifact or a K-12 learner's own Production explanation directly; the rubric and the restatement test above are a literature-grounded design response awaiting pilot validation, and revision 2's own Partial status on overlap-map question 12 stands unresolved by this addition.

### d. Ideation versus execution

Si, Hashimoto, and Yang (2025, `ai-and-researchers/si-hashimoto-yang-2025-ideation-execution-gap.md`) had 43 expert researchers each execute one of a set of randomly assigned research ideas, human-written or LLM-generated, over 100-plus hours per idea; blind review of the executed projects found LLM-generated ideas' novelty and effectiveness scores dropped more than human-written ideas' scores after execution, and on several metrics the ranking flips outright, human-written ideas scoring higher once execution outcomes, not only the idea itself, are judged. This is the direct execution-stage follow-up to Si, Yang, and Hashimoto (2024), already in this corpus, which found LLM-generated ideas rated more novel at the ideation stage alone.

**What the engine currently claims.** `novelty.py`'s lexical check against the canon corpus (PR #60) computes a candidate hypothesis's novelty at the point it is generated and ranked, before any holdout evidence has cleared `MIN_VERIFIED_FOR_LABEL`, an ideation-stage signal in this paper's own vocabulary. No current engine document distinguishes that score from a validated novelty claim.

**The design response.** Document, and where the engine surfaces the score, label `novelty.py`'s own output as a candidate-stage signal distinct from a validated claim, until holdout evidence exists for that hypothesis: the same execution-versus-ideation gap this paper measures for expert-executed research ideas is the gap between a lexical novelty score computed at generation time and whatever the engine's own holdout ledger later confirms. This is a documentation change plus, where the score is surfaced outside `novelty.py`'s own module, a labeling change; it does not require gating the score's computation itself behind `MIN_VERIFIED_FOR_LABEL`, only gating the language used to describe it before that floor is met.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** One directly on-point paper, a large, blind, execution-based design with no counter-evidence in this batch; the fix is a naming and documentation change scoped against code that exists today, not yet made, and the paper itself was not run against this engine's own novelty check.
