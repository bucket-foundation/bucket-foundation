## 2. Evidence-driven design revisions

Each revision below cites the paper file in `_intake/research-os-k12-literature/` that motivates it. Status labels: STABLE (the underlying finding is replicated or field-tested and carries a direct, load-bearing constraint), STRONG LEAN (a real study or deployed system supports the revision, not yet tested inside Research OS), OPEN (the supporting evidence is indirect or from a different domain, motivating a design addition rather than confirming one).

### a. Payout design under Gneezy and Rustichini 2000 and Mekler 2017

**Rule.** No cash reward below a threshold large enough to clear the non-monotonic dip Gneezy and Rustichini (2000) document (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/educational-methods/gneezy-rustichini-2000-pay-enough-or-dont-pay.md`); recognition (an Open Badges 3.0 credential, per `CLAUDE.md`'s existing decision to drop Story Protocol in favor of issuer-signed badges) ships first and unconditionally on acceptance; cash only fires at canon acceptance, never at draft or submission.

**Why.** Gneezy and Rustichini's field and lab evidence is that a small payment can produce worse behavior than no payment at all, non-monotonic rather than linearly beneficial, a daycare late-pickup fine that raised late pickups, an incentivized task where a small payment underperformed both zero payment and a larger one. A token citation fee risks landing in exactly that demotivating middle. Mekler and colleagues (2017) complicate the crowd-out worry from the other side: in a controlled image-tagging task, points and levels, a non-payment, recognition-style mechanic, raised output with no measured drop in intrinsic motivation, a real precedent for recognition-first working on its own rather than as a fallback.

**Experiment.** A three-arm comparison inside Phase 1, no payment, recognition-only (badge issued on acceptance), recognition plus a custodial ledger entry with no liquid payout yet, run per `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q13 and Q30 and per `PLAN.md` §7's own requirement that citation payments launch only beside a no-payment control arm. The ledger entry can exist before any jurisdiction opinion clears liquid payout, since `PLAN.md` §7 already sequences "recognition and a custodial ledger ship before liquid payouts."

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** The "no small cash" constraint rests on canon-tier, field-plus-lab evidence (STABLE on its own). The "recognition first" mechanism rests on one controlled study in a non-payment, low-stakes task, real evidence, not yet tested on a citation-payment population of minors. The combined rule is not yet tested as a package.

### b. Frontier routing under Gasparetti 2017

**Rule.** Every `prerequisite` edge in the route API's target-backward walk carries a confidence weight; `computeFrontier`'s target parameter prefers the highest-confidence chain to a target rather than treating every edge in `graph.prereq_ancestor` as equally certain; any edge below a set confidence floor is flagged for a teacher to confirm or reject before it can route a learner, rather than routing silently.

**Why.** Gasparetti and colleagues (2017) (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/prerequisite-knowledge-graphs/gasparetti-et-al-2017-prerequisites-between-learning-objects.md`) build the direct precedent for this operation, a supervised classifier infers prerequisite edges between learning objects, then a personalized path walks the inferred graph backward from a target, the same shape frontier-backward routing already uses. Their finding that instructor disagreement concentrates on weak or optional prerequisite pairs is the direct warning: not every learned or LLM-proposed edge (`Prerequisite edge inference`, system review §4) should enter the graph at `canon` tier by default.

**Experiment.** Extend `ros-03`'s route API tests with a synthetic low-confidence edge and confirm the router prefers the high-confidence alternate chain when one exists, and flags rather than silently routes when it does not. Log contest events the same way `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q3 already asks for on state classification, applied to edges instead of states.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** One published system, deployed on real courseware, is direct precedent for the exact operation Research OS's own router performs, not yet run on K-12 material or against this graph.

### c. The three-arm testbed under Doshi and Hauser 2024

**Addition.** The three-arm testbed (system review §9) adds a class-level diversity outcome alongside its existing individual outcomes (near-transfer, eight-week retention, independent problem-solving): pairwise similarity between the productions a class of learners submits on the same routed target, measured the way Doshi and Hauser measure similarity between AI-assisted stories.

**Why.** Doshi and Hauser (2024) (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/hci-human-ai-collaboration/doshi-hauser-2024-generative-ai-creativity-diversity.md`) find, in a randomized writing experiment, that generative-AI-assisted writers each produced more individually creative output, but the AI-assisted population of stories was less diverse from each other than an unassisted population, a cost that is invisible in any single writer's own score and concentrated in writers who adopted the AI's first suggestion directly rather than diverging from it. A Research OS classroom routes many learners through the same constrained workspace toward the same frontier target; the same mechanism, shared adoption of the same tool's suggestions, could produce a class of citation-worthy but near-identical productions with no per-student metric catching it.

**Experiment.** Add pairwise similarity across a class's accepted productions on one routed target as a secondary outcome in the three-arm design, scored the same way across all three arms so the constrained-AI arm's diversity can be compared against both the full-chatbot arm and the no-AI control, and against whether a learner used the workspace's own suggestion directly versus revised it first, mirroring Doshi and Hauser's own finding that the loss concentrates in direct adoption.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** The underlying mechanism is a randomized, causal finding, not yet tested in this product's own domain (citation production rather than creative writing) or population (K-12 rather than adult writers).

### d. Check-tool robustness under Binz and Schulz 2023

**Addition.** The Check tool's abstain-on-weak-retrieval path (`PLAN.md` §4, DECISIONS.md S1-S7) gets a second failure mode beyond weak retrieval: phrasing fragility. Before Check reports support or contradiction, it runs the same claim-against-span judgment on at least one paraphrase of the claim; a judgment that flips between the original and the paraphrase reports unknown rather than the majority vote, the same abstain path weak retrieval already triggers.

**Why.** Binz and Schulz (2023), cited in the batch-two evidence for overlap-map question 6 (isolated-versus-full-context checking, itself `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q22), find that a model's reasoning accuracy on a fixed task swings with small, meaning-preserving phrasing changes. Nothing in the current Check design tests for this; a single-pass judgment is trusted regardless of how the claim happens to be phrased. This is tangential support, the finding is general-purpose and not run on a citation-checking task, so it motivates a design addition rather than confirming one already planned.

**Experiment.** Fold into the isolated-versus-full-context comparison `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q22 and the overlap map's own question 6 already call for on the engine's held-out corpus, before any student production is involved: run Check on the same claim-span pair under three conditions, the claim as written, one paraphrase, and full-document context, and compare agreement rates.

**Status: OPEN.** The supporting evidence is from a different task domain and does not test citation checking directly; this is a design addition the evidence motivates; no batch-two paper confirms it directly.

## 5. ETH AI Center fellowship fit

The ETH AI Center's application portal opens 2026-09-15, five days from this revision's date.

**Research-question paragraph.** Research OS for K-12 asks whether constraining an AI research assistant to four non-generative tools, find, quote, check, organize, changes what a school-age learner produces and retains compared with an open-ended tutor and no AI at all, and whether routing a learner backward from a chosen frontier target through their own prerequisite gaps beats a fixed forward curriculum on the same measure. The system already runs as working code, a live prototype: a Postgres graph schema, a frontier-backward routing algorithm, and the four-tool workspace run end to end today against a seeded prerequisite path (`learning/research-os/PLAN.md`, PR #6), and a bidirectional bridge already connects this graph to a combinatorial hypothesis-generation engine (`tools/hypothesis-engine`, PR #10, PR #14) that fuses evidence into a belief score and ranks a tournament of machine-generated hypotheses, so a reviewed student production and an AI-generated hypothesis sit in one address space and can be compared on the same terms. The three-arm study design named in `_intake/research-os-k12/RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` §9, constrained AI, full chatbot, no-AI control, scored on near-transfer, eight-week retention, and independent-problem-solving process logs the product already logs natively, is the direct test of the offloading question Bainbridge (1983) and Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner (2011) pose for any automation that removes practice from a skill, sharpened by a batch of 2026 evidence (Doshi and Hauser 2024 on collective diversity loss under shared AI suggestions; Macnamara and colleagues 2024 on learners' inability to self-assess their own AI-assisted skill decay) that complicates the simple offloading story enough to be worth a funded, IRB-governed pilot rather than a demo.

**Two-PI pairing rationale.** A human-AI systems PI and a learning-sciences PI address two separable risks system review §9 already names. The systems PI reviews what a computer scientist should review and a learning scientist may not, the routing algorithm's correctness, the belief-fusion math the engine shares with the K-12 graph, and the AI tool-scoping design that keeps Check and Organize from generating an answer, and serves as the independent technical check system review §9 calls for given the founder is simultaneously the product's builder and the study's designer, a named conflict of interest. The learning-sciences PI reviews what the systems PI is not positioned to, whether the five-state construct (Access, Awareness, Understanding, Internalization, Production) is a valid, distinct measure rather than a relabeling of ICAP or SOLO, whether the three-arm design's outcome measures meet a learning-sciences evidentiary bar, and serves as the IRB-of-record faculty a study involving minors requires, since the founder is not currently affiliated with a research institution and most university IRBs require a faculty PI for human-subjects research with minors. Splitting the two roles, rather than seeking one PI who claims both, is itself the mitigation system review §9 names for the founder's conflict of interest: neither PI alone can wave the design through on the strength of the other's authority, and the pairing matches the ETH AI Center's own AI for Education and Human-AI Collaboration track, which spans exactly this systems-and-learning-sciences boundary.

No claim above goes past what exists on `main` plus the two open PRs named in section 1; the pilot's actual outcomes, sample, and university partner remain the open items section 3's blocking decisions and system review §11's own founder-only questions already name.
