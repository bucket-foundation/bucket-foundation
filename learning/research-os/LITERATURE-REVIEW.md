# Lattices and Hint-Only Tutors Rebuild Research OS

The sources that should change Research OS most are a small set of mathematical books and a larger set of 2020 to 2026 papers. For the knowledge graph, Ganter and Wille's *Formal Concept Analysis* gives a "prime" an exact, computable definition, and Flajolet and Sedgewick's *Analytic Combinatorics* with Rota's Möbius functions turns coverage and novelty into counts. GRADE, W3C PROV-O and nanopublications supply the skeleton for truth tiers and provenance. For AI, Krenn et al. (2023) and Marwitz et al. (2026) are the closest working copies of "AI proposes an edge, a human gates it," and the 2026 Compressive Knowledge Graph Hypothesis paper warns that models reproduce much of a graph from their own priors, so every AI run needs a random-subgraph control. For education, Bastani et al. (2025, PNAS) is the one study showing a design choice that harms learning: unrestricted GPT-4 cut exam scores 17%, while a hint-only tutor removed the harm. For advancing research, Sourati and Evans (2023) and a 2024 Royal Society study, which finds only about 1% of major breakthroughs fit Kuhn's pattern of wholesale theory replacement, should shape the history-of-science hypothesis engine. The searches found no prior work on a Dirichlet-series coverage measure for a concept graph, on Stanley-Reisner minimal nonfaces as a research-frontier map, on a root layer joining science concepts to Hebrew, Arabic, Sanskrit, Chinese and Proto-Indo-European roots, on an evidence-grading scheme for axioms and proofs, or on an evaluation of spaced repetition, graph placement and a hint-only tutor run together. Those are the problems Bucket could own. Each rests on a few searches, so the novelty claim holds as far as the searches reached.

## Lattice theory gives the knowledge graph an exact prime

### Four traditions define a prime four different ways

The literature offers four rigorous meanings of "irreducible concept," and Research OS should keep them apart as separate node properties. **Formal concept analysis** defines a prime as an attribute-irreducible or object-irreducible element of a concept lattice, and the minimal generators of a concept are its prime descriptors; Ganter and Wille's book is the standard reference with 10,000+ citations, and Springer published a revised edition in 2024 ([Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-63422-2)). **Knowledge space theory** defines atoms of a knowledge structure and a surmise relation over them, the math behind ALEKS ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-58625-5)). **Natural Semantic Metalanguage** fixes 65 semantic primes, each with an exponent in every language tested ([NSM summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_semantic_metalanguage)). **Reverse mathematics** asks for the weakest axiom system that proves a theorem, and finds most classical theorems cluster in five subsystems ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reverse-mathematics/)). A node can be lattice-irreducible in one branch and still carry a semantic decomposition; one "prime" flag over all four meanings would make the graph inconsistent.

FCA ranks first because it turns "is concept C reducible to A and B?" into an algorithm over the node-by-prime table Research OS already has. The Guigues-Duquenne basis, built from pseudo-intents, is the smallest implication set that regenerates every valid implication of that table ([Guigues and Duquenne 1986](https://www.numdam.org/item/MSH_1986__95__5_0.pdf)). An implication {i} → {j} with full support becomes a factor proposal for the decompose-further queue. The hard boundary: Babin proved in July 2026 that deciding whether all pseudo-intents have been found is coNP-complete ([arXiv:2607.18534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18534)), so a single global minimal prime basis is intractable at scale. The tractable path computes bases per branch or per subgraph, using the polynomial-time hierarchical "splits" of Nourine and Vilmin ([arXiv:2202.05536](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.05536)) and the granularity condition in He's 2026 PLOS ONE paper, which gives a complete atomic decomposition for graded knowledge spaces and drops key operations from exponential to quadratic cost ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0346061)). A 2025 paper ties knowledge space theory and FCA together through concept lattices, so one lattice engine can serve the graph and the Learn module ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13042-025-02615-y)).

Two techniques run FCA-style analysis on graph data and deserve a prototype against a Research OS export before anyone writes a custom algorithm. Graph-FCA replaces the binary table with a hypergraph and yields n-ary concepts ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Graph-FCA:-An-extension-of-formal-concept-analysis-Ferr%C3%A9-Cellier/9ed15dd41d04cc5f3ef40436bba0a084eff0e75a)); its exact volume and year still need a DOI check. Atomic decomposition of an OWL ontology splits axioms into atoms that cannot be divided without changing logical consequences ([IJCAI 2011 PDF](http://www.cs.man.ac.uk/~delvescc/ijcai-11.pdf)); its author list was reconstructed from the document and needs confirmation. For the math branch, Metamath's set.mm holds 23,000+ machine-checked proofs ([Metamath](https://us.metamath.org/mm_100.html)), and Wernhard and Zombori treat it as a grammar-compressed proof store, a template for measuring redundancy in a prerequisite-laden graph ([arXiv:2505.12305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12305)).

NSM earns its place as a readability check on decompositions. Baartmans et al. (2025) fine-tuned 1B and 8B models on 44,000+ word-usage-explication triplets and beat GPT-4o at writing cross-translatable explications into the 65 primes ([arXiv:2505.11764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11764)). A Research OS node whose definition resists an NSM explication has an unreduced term hiding in it.

### Generating functions turn coverage and novelty into counts

Flajolet and Sedgewick's symbolic method builds a generating function from a combinatorial construction, and the book is free ([PDF](https://algo.inria.fr/flajolet/Publications/book.pdf)). For Research OS it yields a per-prime depth polynomial G_p(x) = Σ a(p,d) x^d, where a(p,d) counts composites at depth d that contain prime p: G_p(1) measures penetration, G_p′(1)/G_p(1) measures mean reach depth, and a second variable marking branch measures cross-branch reach. The baseline for composites built as multisets of k primes is the ordinary generating function (1 − x)^(−k), and size bins that fall below it mark thin regions. Rota's 1964 Möbius-function paper supplies the inversion that stops double counting over a poset ([compiled Rota series](https://www.isibang.ac.in/~soumyashant/misc/foundations_of_combinatorial_theory_Rota_et_al.pdf)): novelty(v) equals an additive score f(v), such as evidence count, minus the inclusion-exclusion sum over its factors. Bergeron, Labelle and Leroux's *Combinatorial Species* matters once Research OS separates a paper's labeled use of a concept from the abstract concept, since species compose labeled and unlabeled structures in one algebra ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/combinatorial-species-and-treelike-structures/D994A1F2877BDE63FF0C9EDE2F9788A8)).

Miller and Sturmfels' *Combinatorial Commutative Algebra* gives the language for the frontier ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b138602)). Realized prime supports and their subsets form a simplicial complex, and a minimal nonface is a prime set never combined whose proper subsets all occur. Ranking each minimal nonface T by its expected count under independence, N · Π(df(i)/N) over i in T, puts the combinations with high expectation and zero observations at the top of the research agenda. Gröbner-basis equation discovery shows this algebra runs at data scale: Gec, Džeroski and Todorovski recovered more than 90% of known recurrences across 27,000+ OEIS sequences, against about 70% for SINDy ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/12/23/3745); [Machine Learning 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-026-07112-z)).

The Leibniz program closes the loop. Leibniz's 1666 *Dissertatio de arte combinatoria* proposed encoding terms as numbers so truths could be computed ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/leibniz/); [OUP edition](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/leibniz-dissertation-on-combinatorial-art-9780198837954)). Database research proved prime-number labeling works for hierarchies: a descendant carries the product of its ancestors' primes, ancestry becomes a divisibility test, and the scheme needs fewer relabelings under updates than interval or prefix schemes ([Wu, Lee, Hsu, ICDE](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~leeml/papers/prime2Jul.pdf)), with an extension to DAGs ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11733836_56)). Research OS can assign the i-th prime number to the i-th concept prime, give each node a squarefree Leibniz number n(v), and read shared prerequisites as gcd and joint scope as lcm.

For graph structure, k-core decomposition is the cheapest backbone finder and ships in Neo4j GDS ([Neo4j docs](https://neo4j.com/docs/graph-data-science/current/algorithms/k-core/)). The centrality survey by Saxena and Iyengar is the reference for the rule that any "importance" badge must name the measure behind it, since measures rank the same graph differently ([arXiv:2011.07190](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.07190)). Spivak and Kent's ologs and Fong and Spivak's *Seven Sketches* are the long-run option for provable composition across branches, with little production deployment found ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0024274); [arXiv:1803.05316](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05316)).

### Evidence tiers and provenance come from medicine and the W3C

GRADE is the most reusable template for truth tiers: four levels, randomized trials start high, observational studies start low, five factors rate a body of evidence down and three rate it up ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21208779/)). The Oxford CEBM levels run from systematic reviews of trials at Level 1 to mechanism-based reasoning at Level 5 ([CEBM](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/levels-of-evidence/ocebm-levels-of-evidence)). Both were built for clinical interventions. Neither has a rule for grading a proof, an axiom, a law or a cosmological derivation, and Bucket's seven branches need that rulebook. The replication literature sets the priors: the Open Science Collaboration found 97% of 100 original psychology studies significant against 36% of replications, with effects about half as large ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aac4716)), and Ioannidis lists the field conditions under which a published finding is more likely false ([PLOS Medicine](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.0020124)). Publication status therefore carries no tier weight by itself.

Automated grading hits a known bottleneck at retrieval. On FEVER, the best full system scored 31.87% with evidence retrieval against 50.91% when retrieval was skipped ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/)), and SciFact supplies 1,409 expert claims against 5,183 annotated abstracts for testing a science verifier ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.609/)). Any Research OS verification score should publish retrieval recall beside it. For propagating confidence through the graph, noisy-OR handles independent sources and Probabilistic Soft Logic enforces consistency ([arXiv:2405.16929](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16929v2)). For display, Padilla, Kay and Hullman find that legible uncertainty displays raise reader trust ([PDF](http://space.ucmerced.edu/Downloads/publications/Uncertainty_Visualization_Padilla_Kay_Hullman_2022.pdf)), which supports showing tier badges to readers.

The claim record should compose four standards. A nanopublication bundles an assertion graph, a provenance graph and a publication-info graph ([Groth, Gibson, Velterop 2010](https://content.iospress.com/articles/information-services-and-use/isu613)). PROV-O, a W3C Recommendation since April 2013, models Entity, Activity and Agent, and citation-payment events can subclass it ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). SWHID became ISO/IEC 18670:2025 and gives a content-derived identifier for the exact code state behind a computational claim, already used by Zenodo, HAL, eLife and OpenAIRE ([Software Heritage](https://www.softwareheritage.org/software-hash-identifier-swhid/)). FAIR supplies the machine-actionable metadata rules ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201618)). No deployed system joins the four into one citeable-forever record.

For medallion layering, cite Armbrust et al.'s CIDR 2021 lakehouse paper for running bronze, silver and gold on one substrate ([PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~matei/papers/2021/cidr_lakehouse.pdf)), and attribute the bronze/silver/gold names to Databricks practice, since no peer-reviewed definition exists ([practitioner retrospective](https://zenodo.org/records/18605847)). Data contracts enforce each layer boundary at the source and can block a downstream run on violation ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-contract)). Delétang et al. show prediction and compression are equivalent, with Chinchilla 70B compressing ImageNet patches to 43.4% against PNG's 58.5% ([arXiv:2309.10668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668)); that gives each layer a metric in bits saved per node kept.

### Roots and languages join through open-licensed sources

CLICS⁴ holds colexifications for 3,447 language varieties under CC BY 4.0 ([clics.clld.org](https://clics.clld.org/)), and Concepticon supplies the shared concept IDs that join CLICS and outside wordlists to Polingual's LaBSE clusters ([concepticon.clld.org](https://concepticon.clld.org/)). The Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible gives lemma and morphology for every word under CC BY 4.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb)). Those three are the cheapest additions. Jackson et al. built emotion colexification networks across 2,474 languages and found valence and arousal organize them everywhere while specific groupings vary by family ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw8160)), a method Bucket can rerun on causation, kinship, color or body parts. A 2025 ACL paper shows partial colexifications improve concept embeddings ([arXiv:2502.09743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09743)), so colexification adds signal to LaBSE, whose 109-language model reached 83.7% bitext retrieval on Tatoeba ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.62/)). EtymDB 2.1 adds 700,000+ etymological relations across 2,536 languages under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([GitHub](https://github.com/clefourrier/EtymDB)), useful as QA on Polingual's own Wiktextract edges ([LREC 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.140/)).

Two sources need a legal read before ingestion. ctext.org's linked data is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, and whether a per-citation fee trips the non-commercial clause is open ([ctext API](https://ctext.org/tools/api)). The Quranic Arabic Corpus is labeled GPL while its data file forbids changes ([download page](https://corpus.quran.com/download/)). Proto-Indo-European roots have no open structured dataset; LIV and Watkins exist in print or scans only ([LIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexikon_der_indogermanischen_Verben)).

## AI proposes edges and the graph must prove it mattered

### Link prediction on concept graphs already works at production scale

Krenn et al. built a temporal network of about 64,700 AI concepts and 18 million edges from 143,000 arXiv papers, predicted which unconnected pairs would connect 1, 3 and 5 years ahead, and reached AUC up to 0.93, with feature-engineered models beating end-to-end embeddings ([arXiv:2210.00881](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.00881v1); [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00735-0)). Their historical time-split, scored against a 0.5 random baseline per horizon, is the evaluation protocol Research OS should copy. Marwitz et al. showed LLM-extracted concepts beat keyword extraction for predicting new materials-science directions and validated suggestions through interviews with domain experts ([arXiv:2506.16824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16824); [Nature Machine Intelligence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01206-y)). Sun et al. raised relation-prediction AUROC from 0.929 to 0.972 on 270,687 concepts by modeling semantic and structural change together ([arXiv:2609.18163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18163)). CHIMERA mines "recombination" cases where a paper joins ideas from separate fields ([arXiv:2505.20779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20779)), the edge type the hypothesis engine most needs as training signal.

The Compressive Knowledge Graph Hypothesis paper is the design warning. Across three language models, from Mistral-7B to Gemini 2.5 Flash, on battery-materials hypotheses, compact top-k subgraphs, and even random or topology-based subsets, matched full-graph performance in many settings, and models recovered much graph content from their priors with no graph at all ([arXiv:2605.27176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27176)). Research OS should feed models compact gold-layer subgraphs and run every hypothesis twice more, once with a scrambled graph and once with none, before crediting the graph.

### Axioms should constrain proposals before generation

AI-Descartes generates candidate formulas and then checks derivability from formal axioms, recovering Kepler's third law and Einstein's time dilation from few data points ([Nature Communications 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37236-y)). AI-Hilbert moves the axioms inside a polynomial optimization so they shrink the search space from the start ([Nature Communications 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-50074-w)). AI-Hilbert is the better template: Research OS's reviewed prime layer plays the role of the axiom set and bounds what an LLM may propose. AI Feynman recovered all 100 Feynman-lecture equations against 71 for prior software, using symmetry and separability priors ([Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aay2631)). DreamCoder's wake-sleep loop folds solved programs back into a growing primitive library ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3453483.3454080)), the right model for how approved decompositions become new reusable primes. ZeroC represents concepts as graphs of constituent concepts and recognizes new ones zero-shot across domains ([arXiv:2206.15049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15049)), outside evidence that Research OS's compositional data model generalizes.

### Sparse features test whether human primes behave as a basis

Anthropic's dictionary-learning line found that a sparse autoencoder on a one-layer transformer produced features with a median interpretability score of 12 against 0 for neurons ([Towards Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features/index.html)), and scaled to 34 million multilingual, multimodal features in Claude 3 Sonnet with large parts still unmapped ([Scaling Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/)). Winnicki, Gnanasekaran and Darve turned SAE features into labeled co-occurrence and mechanism graphs that recovered a biology textbook's chapter structure ([arXiv:2604.23829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23829)), a ready candidate-edge generator for the review gate. Klotz et al. found larger dictionaries can reduce perturbation alignment with human concepts, with moderate sizes best ([arXiv:2606.24716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24716)), a caution against over-splitting primes. Concept Bottleneck Models route every prediction through named concepts that a human can edit at inference time ([ICML 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3524938.3525433)); Research OS can route text queries through the prime layer the same way, scoring candidates by IDF-weighted cosine in the prime basis and masking attention to the seed nodes' factor cone as Graph Attention Networks mask to neighbors ([arXiv:1710.10903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10903)). Cross-model feature universality stays weak, with only 1 to 5% of GPT-2 neurons universal in one study ([arXiv:2401.12181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12181)), so any learned basis needs version pinning and revalidation.

### Evaluation needs a protocol that a constant model cannot game

The standard random-negative knowledge-graph evaluation can give a perfect score to a model that always outputs the same prediction ([WWW 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3442381.3449856)), and random edge splits distort topology ([arXiv:2205.04105](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.04105)). RefD, an unsupervised link-based prerequisite score, beat supervised methods on 1,600 CrowdComp pairs and 1,336 course pairs ([Smart Learning Environments](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-019-0104-3)) and can prescreen prerequisite edges before human review. LLMs4OL found foundation LLMs unfit for ontology construction without fine-tuning ([arXiv:2307.16648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16648)), and causal extraction from abstracts confuses direct and indirect links ([IJCAI 2025 survey](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/1186.pdf)), a false-positive class reviewers should be trained on. Agreement among expert annotators on biomedical relations has measured as low as F 0.415, and CrowdTruth treats disagreement as signal ([CrowdTruth](https://dx.doi.org/10.3233/SW-200415)). SciGraph-LLM ties every extracted claim to a verbatim source span ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3779211.3793169)), which lets a reviewer check a quote in place of a paper. A single LLM-judge novelty score should never auto-accept an edge ([arXiv:2606.12071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12071)).

## Hint-only tutoring is the one design rule with harm data

Bucket Academy already runs FSRS-5, a two-layer prerequisite graph and ALEKS-style placement over 358 atoms, and the literature backs those choices at smaller effect sizes than the product copy might assume. On about 700 million Anki reviews, FSRS-5 reached log-loss 0.291 and 5.3% retention RMSE against SM-2's 0.354 and 16.2% ([comparison](https://www.diane.app/en/guides/fsrs-vs-sm2)), and the Expertium benchmark puts FSRS ahead for 99.5% of users ([benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)). Wozniak disputes the comparison against SuperMemo's newest algorithms, and the dispute is unresolved ([SuperMemo](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/supermemo-is-better-than-fsrs-by-far)). All of this evidence comes from flashcards; none tests a prerequisite-chained science curriculum.

Retrieval practice is the item-design rule. Roediger and Karpicke found 61% retention after one week for free recall against 40% for rereading ([PDF](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf)), and Rowland's meta-analysis puts the testing effect near g = 0.50, emerging without feedback only when practice accuracy exceeds 50% ([ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED599273.pdf)). Every atom quiz should ask for a generated answer and give immediate feedback. Interleaving carries g = 0.42 across all studies through 2018 ([Review of Education](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.3266)), so the session builder should mix atoms across branches; FSRS decides when to review and says nothing about order. Cepeda et al. put the best spacing gap at 20 to 40% of a one-week retention interval, falling to 5 to 10% at 350 days ([Psychological Science](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda%20et%20al%202008_psychsci.pdf)), so retention targets should vary with the learner's horizon.

Placement has the right math and a modest payoff. Doignon and Falmagne's *Learning Spaces* is the reference an engineer should read before touching the placement engine ([ALEKS research](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/knowledge_space_theory)). The 2019 meta-analysis of 15 ALEKS studies found it "as good as, but not better than" classroom teaching ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1232632)), so the claim for placement is speed and targeting. Deep Knowledge Tracing reported a 25% AUC gain over prior tracing ([NeurIPS 2015](https://stanford.edu/~cpiech/bio/papers/deepKnowledgeTracing.pdf)) at a cost in interpretability, which argues for an interpretable per-atom mastery probability as the learner-facing score. IRT-based adaptive testing is the most validated route to efficient ability estimates and loses accuracy in heterogeneous populations ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0150563)).

Tutoring evidence sets the hard product rule. VanLehn's meta-analysis puts human tutoring at d = 0.79, step-based tutors at 0.75 and answer-based tutors at 0.31, and attributes most of Bloom's two sigma to a stricter mastery standard for the tutored group ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/)). Bastani et al. ran about 1,000 Turkish high-school students: unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% and lowered no-AI exam scores 17%, while a hint-only tutor raised practice 127% with no exam harm ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). The Academy tutor must be blocked from emitting final answers or full solutions in code, at the step level. Realistic effects are small: ASSISTments reached g = 0.18 in Maine and 0.29 for low prior achievers ([SRI](https://www.sri.com/publication/education-learning-pubs/digital-learning-pubs/how-big-is-that-reporting-the-effect-size-and-cost-of-assistments-in-the-maine-homework-efficacy-study/)), and Khan Academy reports 0.06 to 0.08 SD a year for Khanmigo, indistinguishable from Khan Academy alone, on its own blog ([Khan Academy](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/)). Mastery-learning effects of about 0.4 SD on local tests fall to about 0.27 and lose significance on standardized ones ([Guskey](https://tguskey.com/wp-content/uploads/Mastery-Learning-3-Lessons-of-Mastery-Learning.pdf)), which is why the public Mastery Profile should stay a signal until validated against real exams.

Meyer and Land's threshold concepts, transformative nodes without which a learner cannot progress, argue for stricter transfer-based mastery checks on a subset of atoms such as entropy or the limit ([Threshold knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_knowledge)). Automated prerequisite mining that combines ten signals can audit the hand-authored graph as it grows past 358 atoms ([arXiv:2509.05393](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05393)).

## The hypothesis engine should model the human before the alien

### History-of-science forecasting has benchmarks and no production system

Swanson's ABC model links A to C through a shared B across disjoint literatures, and his fish-oil and Raynaud's prediction was later supported in clinic ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4031661/)); it belongs in frontier scoring as a pair term. Sourati and Evans showed that models of what human experts can reach predict future discoveries up to 400% better than content-only models, and the same model inverted proposes "alien" hypotheses humans are unlikely to reach soon ([arXiv:2306.01495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01495); [Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01648-z)). That splits Bucket's history hypothesis engine into two models: one backtested against what a period researcher would conclude at a cutoff date, and one tuned away from it. SciPaths gives the backtest a benchmark with 262 expert gold pathways and 2,444 silver ones ([arXiv:2605.14600](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14600)); it is an unreviewed preprint. Google's AI co-scientist recapitulated in two days a gene-transfer mechanism that took a lab over ten years ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/)), while an independent review calls Sakana's fully automated AI Scientist claims wishful ([arXiv:2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14297v3)). Ross King's 2026 history of AI scientists, from Adam to Eve, argues the next systems must fuse learning with logic, probability, causal reasoning and formal record-keeping ([arXiv:2608.14407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14407)).

A 2024 Royal Society study of 750+ major discoveries found the Kuhnian wholesale-replacement pattern in about 1% of cases, with cumulative progress the rule ([Proc. R. Soc. A](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspa.2024.0141)). Canon change in Research OS should be modeled as continuous re-grading and re-linking, which the medallion pipeline already supports. Wang and Barabási's *The Science of Science* is the umbrella book for treating progress as measurable ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/science-of-science/science-of-science/85BBC0DE7E528B55DC6021F9AC5A47FC)). A 2022 paper applies Lakatos's progressive and degenerating programmes to whole journals ([European Journal for Philosophy of Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13194-022-00492-8)); the same appraisal at the level of a canon branch or a claim cluster is unbuilt.

### Paid-to-cite inherits Xanadu's design and Coil's warning

Garfield's 1955 citation index framed citations as an association-of-ideas index ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.122.3159.108)). Ted Nelson's Xanadu paired transclusion with a royalty that paid the rights holder on each reuse ([Gwern](https://gwern.net/xanadu)), the same mechanism x402 now runs: a server answers HTTP 402 with a price, and the client retries with a signed stablecoin payment ([x402 whitepaper](https://www.x402.org/x402-whitepaper.pdf)). The failed precedents are the ones to study. Coil ran Web Monetization on a flat membership and shut its paid service in March 2023 ([Interledger](https://community.interledger.org/radhyr/web-monetization-after-coil-shutdown-4098)); Brave's BAT tied payments to one browser's ad opt-in ([BAT](https://basicattentiontoken.org/)). Bucket's bet is that a citation, a discrete act with per-instance value, can carry a payment on its own. The OA Diamond Journals Study estimated 17,000 to 29,000 no-fee journals, more than half running on under €10,000 a year and a quarter at a loss ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4558704)), which is the funding gap paid-to-cite targets. Paula Stephan's *How Economics Shapes Science* documents how money and career incentives drive researcher behavior ([HUP](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674088160)). No study measures how a one-time per-citation payment changes author or citer behavior. That is the central untested assumption in Bucket's revenue model.

## Conclusion

The literature splits along one line. Where Research OS needs structure, the answers are old, exact and in books: lattices from 1986 to 2024, generating functions from 2009, Möbius inversion from 1964, knowledge spaces from 1999. Where it needs behavior, the answers are recent papers with modest effect sizes and at least one proven way to cause harm. Bucket should build the structural layer from the books with confidence and treat every behavioral claim, whether about tutors, spaced review, AI-proposed edges or paid citations, as an experiment it runs and publishes.

The open problems form one program. The prime layer makes coverage a Dirichlet series, the frontier a set of minimal nonfaces, the evidence tier a branch-specific rulebook, and the language layer a path from a science prime through NSM to a root in five traditions. Bucket owns the data that joins them: a reviewed prime basis, a medallion graph, a live learner population and a citation ledger. Papers on the Dirichlet coverage measure and the integrated tutor evaluation are the two with the shortest path from existing code to a citeable result.
