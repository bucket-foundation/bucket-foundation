Abstract
DRAFT for Gianangelo to rewrite in his own words. Target 100 to 150 words.

Objective: estimate the mean number of years a research problem stays open, from the year it was
posed to the year it was resolved, for the solved problems in the Solvability Atlas. The data are 213
cleaned rows from the atlas problem table built on 2026-10-13, one row per solved problem with
both years recorded. Descriptive work used all 213 rows; inference used a sample of 50 rows drawn
with replacement from the cleaned records under seed 124. The full-data mean is 34.30 years and
the median is 20.00 years. Year posed and years to resolve have r = −0.68 and R2 = 0.46. The
95% t interval from the sample is 18.69 to 34.51 years. The two-sided test of µ = 50 gives t = −5.94
and p < 0.0001, so the sample provides evidence against the benchmark at α = 0.05.

Question. For solved research problems in the cleaned atlas table, is the mean time from posed
to resolved equal to 50 years? Expected relationship: problems posed later have fewer years to
resolve, because a recently posed problem that is already solved cannot have been open for long.
Hypotheses. H0 : µ = 50 years. H1 : µ ̸= 50 years. The benchmark of 50 years is hypothetical.
It was fixed in the code before the sample was drawn, and it stands for two research generations of
25 years each.

2.4   Relationships
Paired count: 213. r = −0.68. Regression equation: ŷ = 848.99 + (−0.42)x, with x the year posed
and ŷ the years to resolve. R2 = 0.46. Sum of squared residuals: 331048.09.
Slope: each later posed year goes with 0.42 fewer years to resolve on average. Intercept: 848.99
years is the fitted value at year 0, far outside the observed years 1506 to 2023, so it has no practical
reading. R2 : year posed accounts for 46.22% of the variation in years to resolve. The relationship
is negative, roughly linear at the old end and weak to moderate in strength. The residuals fan out:
they are wide for problems posed before 1900 and narrow for recent ones, and they are bounded
below because Y cannot be negative.
Prediction at x = 1950, inside the observed range: ŷ = 848.99 + (−0.42)(1950) = 27.96 years. For
fermat (Fermat’s Last Theorem): x = 1637, observed y = 357, fitted 159.75, residual 197.25 years.
Extrapolation: the line gives negative years for posed years after 2016, so it cannot be used beyond
the data. Possible confounder: branch, since older rows come from mathematics and physics.
Causal limit: the data are observational and hold solved problems only. A problem posed in 2010
can appear here with at most 16 years, so part of the negative slope comes from which rows can
be in the table, and no causal claim follows.

3.4   Confidence Interval
                                                                   √
From the saved sample: n = 50, x̄ = 26.60, s = 27.84, SE = 27.84/ 50 = 3.94, df = 49, t∗ = 2.01,
margin = 2.01 × 3.94 = 7.91. Interval: 26.60 ± 7.91, from 18.69 to 34.51 years.
Checks: the draws are independent by design. The sample is right-skewed, with median 13.50 and
maximum 104 years, and n = 50 is large enough for the t procedure to be approximate; outliers
widen the interval. Interpretation: we are 95% confident that the mean time from posed to resolved
for the 213 cleaned atlas problems is between 18.69 and 34.51 years. Across repeated samples of 50
drawn this way, about 95% of the intervals built by this method would contain the reference mean.
This interval contains the full-data mean of 34.30 years. The sample is kept as drawn.

3.5   Hypothesis Test
H0 : µ = 50, H1 : µ ̸= 50, α = 0.05. The benchmark of 50 years is hypothetical. t = (26.60 −
50)/3.94 = −5.94, df = 49, p-value < 0.0001. Decision: reject H0 . Conclusion: the sample provides
evidence that the mean time to resolve differs from 50 years.
The decision agrees with the interval: 50 is outside 18.69 to 34.51. The p-value is the probability,
when the mean is 50 years, of a sample mean at least this far from 50 in either direction. A Type I
error would be concluding the mean differs from 50 years when it equals 50. A Type II error would
be keeping 50 years when the mean differs. The sample mean minus the benchmark is −23.40
years, 46.80% of the benchmark.

Years from posed to resolved are right-skewed. Most solved problems in the atlas closed within
a few decades: the median is 20.00 years and the third quartile is 45.00, while the mean is 34.30
because 7 problems sit beyond the upper fence of 105.00 years. The median is the better centre.
Year posed and years to resolve move in opposite directions, with r = −0.68 and R2 = 0.46; part

                                                                             13
of that slope comes from the table holding solved problems only, since a recent problem that is
already solved has a short time by construction. The saved sample of 50 rows has mean 26.60 years,
and its 95% interval, 18.69 to 34.51, contains the full-data mean of 34.30. The test of the 50-year
benchmark gives p < 0.0001, so the sample leads us to reject H0 . The sample mean differs from
the full-data mean by −7.70 years, or 1.95 standard errors of 3.94.


Conclusion
DRAFT for Gianangelo to rewrite in his own words. Target 100 to 150 words.

The research question asks whether solved atlas problems took 50 years on average. The sample
provides evidence against H0 : t = −5.94, p < 0.0001, and the interval runs from 18.69 to 34.51
years. Two limits apply. The table holds solved problems with both years recorded, so long-open
problems that are still unsolved are missing and the mean understates how long problems stay
open. The rows come from public lists that favour famous problems. One improvement: record
a posed year for every row in the atlas, open or solved, so that open problems can enter as still
running. The claim covers the 213 cleaned rows and no wider population of research problems.
