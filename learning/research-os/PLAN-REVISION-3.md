## 2. Evidence-driven revisions from batch four

Fourteen cards from the batch-four corpus (`_intake/research-os-k12-literature/`, PR #65) carry the load-bearing evidence for this section. `PLAN-REVISION-2.md`'s own batch-four pointers under sections 2a, 2b, and 2d already name three of these cards in brief; each subsection below says explicitly where it extends one of those pointers rather than repeating it.

### a. Guidance for low-prior-knowledge learners

Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006, `project-based-inquiry-learning/kirschner-sweller-clark-2006-minimal-guidance-does-not-work.md`) is this batch's sharpest complication for the Research OS premise as a whole: cognitive-load evidence that minimally guided instruction, discovery, problem-based, experiential, and inquiry-based teaching among them, underperforms guided instruction for learners without high prior knowledge, with the guidance advantage receding only once a learner already holds enough prior knowledge to supply it internally. Read as an AI-shaped veneer over minimally guided instruction, the four-tool workspace risks underperforming for the K-12 learners a research-skill tool should serve best.

Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn (2007, `project-based-inquiry-learning/hmelo-silver-duncan-chinn-2007-scaffolding-response.md`) replies directly: Kirschner and colleagues conflate problem-based and inquiry learning with unguided discovery learning, when both are in practice extensively scaffolded, the same cognitive-load mechanism Kirschner and colleagues invoke against unguided approaches, working in problem-based and inquiry learning's favor rather than against them. The workspace's own explicit constraint, no tool ever writes the learner's answer, evidence gathered and committed to before Check's verdict, teacher review at Production, sits structurally closer to the scaffolding Hmelo-Silver and colleagues defend than to the unguided discovery Kirschner and colleagues critique. The condition on that reading: Hmelo-Silver and colleagues' own scaffolding criteria have to be checked against each of the four tools by name, a check `PLAN-REVISION-2.md`'s own pointer already flags as unresolved and this pass does not perform either.

Lazonder and Harmsen (2016, `project-based-inquiry-learning/lazonder-harmsen-2016-inquiry-based-learning-meta-analysis.md`) quantifies the stakes of that unresolved check: across 72 studies, guidance during inquiry-based learning shows a facilitative effect on learning activities (d = 0.66), performance success (d = 0.71), and learning outcomes (d = 0.50), but guidance type moderates the effect on performance success, so a single "no tool writes the answer" constraint is not, alone, enough specification of which guidance a learner needs. Furtak and colleagues (2012, `project-based-inquiry-learning/furtak-et-al-2012-inquiry-based-science-meta-analysis.md`) sharpens the same point from the teacher side: pooling 37 studies, teacher-led inquiry activities post a mean effect size about 0.40 larger than student-led ones, the gap a scoped AI tool has to close if its own guidance is meant to stand in for a teacher's.

**The design response.** When the diagnostic probe (`onProbeCheckResult`) places a learner below Understanding on the first two nodes of a routed chain, the workspace adds a worked example ahead of Check on the first of those nodes and a partially worked example, one step left for the learner, on the second, with ordinary four-tool use resuming from the third node on. This is a code change, not yet built, scoped as a new addition to `ros-04`'s remaining Check-tool and workspace work, distinct from the commit-step addition `PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2a already scopes.

**The pilot measure.** Add a covariate to H1's existing design (`PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`): whether a learner's diagnostic-probe score triggered the worked-example arm, read against the same near-transfer post-test H1 already measures, split by prior-knowledge tercile rather than pooled. This tests Kirschner and colleagues' own expertise-reversal claim directly, that the guidance benefit shrinks as prior knowledge rises, instead of assuming the constraint helps every learner the same amount.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** Two opposed theoretical positions and two quantified meta-analyses converge on a specifiable design response and a measure that tests it; none of the four has run inside Research OS itself, and Hmelo-Silver and colleagues' own resolution stays conditional on the tool-by-tool scaffolding check neither this pass nor revision 2 performs.

### b. Required participation and production misconduct

Grinnell, Dalley, and Reisch (2020, `student-research-experiences/grinnell-et-al-2020-science-fair-outcomes.md`) supplies this batch's sharpest complicating data point for any mandatory or incentive-attached Production requirement: across a national sample of over 300 students, about two-thirds were required to participate in science fair, and among students who said they had no interest in a science or engineering career and were required to participate anyway, about 10 percent reported research misconduct such as plagiarism. Required participation among the uninterested carries a measured misconduct rate.

Bangera and Brownell (2014, `student-research-experiences/bangera-brownell-2014-cures-more-inclusive.md`) makes the case for the same design choice from the access side: a required, structured research experience embedded in a course reaches every enrolled student; a selection-gated internship reaches only the subset who apply, get chosen, and can afford it, the access argument behind routing every Research OS learner through one graph-based workspace instead of reserving it for volunteers. Sadler and colleagues (2010, `student-research-experiences/sadler-et-al-2010-research-apprenticeships-review.md`) sets the base rate that access argument has to clear: synthesizing 53 studies of research apprenticeships, the literature supports career-aspiration and confidence gains from participation with more consistency than nature-of-science gains, and apprenticeship length and whether an outcome was made an explicit design target, beyond participation alone, shape which outcomes appear. Corwin, Graham, and Dolan (2015, `student-research-experiences/corwin-graham-dolan-2015-modeling-cures.md`) supplies the missing discipline for that last point: pathway models connecting a specific learner action to a specific claimed outcome, open to testing and revision, the same discipline `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md`'s five-state ladder already applies to Research OS's own design.

Read together, these four cards converge on a founder decision this revision did not previously name: whether any Research OS pilot ever makes class participation a requirement instead of leaving it optional, and if it does, whether the requirement extends to submitting a Production or stops at using the routed workspace. Grinnell's own misconduct finding argues against a mandatory, ungrounded Production requirement; Bangera and Brownell's access argument and Sadler's participation-alone caveat argue a purely voluntary design could under-reach the students who would benefit most, and produce inconsistent gains regardless. See section 4's new decision below.

**A design response that holds regardless of that decision.** Check's own citation-validity verification (`WORKSPACE.md` section 1) already strips any citation that does not exactly match the one allowed source label. This revision proposes extending that mechanism into a provenance guard on Production submission: a Production's cited quote text matched against the learner's own logged Quote calls for that session (`sessionId` already groups them, `EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md`), a submission whose claim text scores above a fixed similarity threshold against an existing accepted Production on the same target or against a canon dossier flagged rather than silently accepted, and a flag surfaced to the reviewing teacher rather than acted on automatically. No incentive of any kind attaches before a flagged submission clears teacher review. This is a code change, not yet built, scoped as a new item under `ros-04`/`ros-06` rather than folded into either, since it needs the participation decision below settled first to know whether it guards a mandatory or a voluntary flow.

**Status: OPEN.** The evidence is real and directly conflicting on whether requiring participation helps or harms; no design response converges without the founder decision below, and the provenance guard is scoped but unbuilt regardless of which way that decision goes.

### c. Lateral reading against a single-source Check

`PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2a's own batch-four pointer already names Wineburg and McGrew (2019) as a complication to the commit-step design; this subsection gives it, and its national-scale companion, full treatment.

Wineburg and McGrew (2019, `source-evaluation/wineburg-mcgrew-2019-lateral-reading.md`) studied 45 experienced internet users, ten PhD historians, ten professional fact checkers, and twenty-five Stanford undergraduates, finding the two non-fact-checker groups stayed within one site and were misled by superficial cues such as official-looking logos, while fact checkers left the page to check other sources first, a strategy the paper names lateral reading, before returning to judge the original. Breakstone and colleagues (2021, `source-evaluation/breakstone-et-al-2021-civic-online-reasoning-national-portrait.md`) puts a number on how far that vertical-reading failure reaches without support: across 3,446 US high school students given a live internet connection, 96 percent never learned that a site claiming factual climate reporting had fossil-fuel-industry ties, two-thirds could not tell a news story from an ad on a popular site's home page, and over half judged an anonymously posted, provenance-weak video as strong evidence.

Both cards land on the same Check-tool gap. Check (`gradeExplanation`, `WORKSPACE.md` section 1) verifies a claim against the node's own summary and its prerequisites' summaries, a single, in-workspace source; nothing in the current contract corroborates that source against an independent second one, the exact step both papers find separates expert evaluation from the failure mode Breakstone and colleagues measure at national scale. A learner can pass every Check verdict the current contract offers while never practicing the cross-source step both papers treat as the real skill.

**The design response.** Locate gains a `secondSource` mode: when a learner runs Check on a node, Locate's own substring filter (`locateHits`, never a model call) is queried a second time against the node's title and summary text with the matched node's own slug excluded, surfacing one independent node from elsewhere in the graph that shares vocabulary with the claim under test. Check's own grounding truth does not change; the second source is offered as a companion Locate result a learner can open before submitting an explanation. This is a code change, not yet built, scoped as an addition to `ros-04`'s remaining Locate work.

**The pilot measure.** Add a `usedSecondSource` boolean to the `check` evidence event (`EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md`, an optional field alongside the `retentionInterval`/`schedulingCondition` fields `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` already proposes for H6), recording whether the learner opened the second-source result before submitting, read against the sampled human cross-score H1 already runs on Understanding-state explanations: whether opening a second source predicts a higher grounded-explanation rate is a first-pass test of whether the feature earns its own cost.

**Status: STRONG LEAN.** A controlled expert-novice study and a 3,446-student national assessment converge on the same specific gap in a shipped tool's contract, with a design response scoped against code that exists today; neither paper tested an AI-mediated workspace, and the feature above has not run against a live learner.

### d. Epistemic cognition and argumentation

Osborne (2010, `writing-and-argumentation/osborne-2010-arguing-to-learn.md`) argues collaborative discourse and argumentation, common in how science operates but largely absent from science classrooms, builds conceptual understanding and reasoning skill through the discourse itself, defending a claim against challenge, more than through claim construction alone. A Research OS Production is authored individually against a teacher reviewer; the review step is evaluative and carries none of the peer debate Osborne treats as the mechanism of the benefit.

Sandoval (2005, `epistemic-cognition/sandoval-2005-practical-epistemologies.md`) complicates any claim that closing that gap, or any other workspace change, builds nature-of-science understanding as a side effect of tool use: formal epistemological belief and the practical epistemology that governs how a student investigates are distinct and disconnected in the literature Sandoval reviews, so a learner could pass every Check verdict without their own epistemic stance toward evidence changing at all. `PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2b's own batch-four pointer already reads Kuhn (1999, `epistemic-cognition/kuhn-1999-developmental-model-critical-thinking.md`) as one candidate dimensional structure for the learner-state ladder; this subsection reads the same paper against a different question, overlap-map question 12. Kuhn's three forms of second-order cognition, metacognitive, metastrategic, and epistemological, the last of which, knowing about the nature and sources of knowledge, sits closer to what a citation-and-evidence requirement is meant to build than factual correctness alone, without settling which of the three, if any, Check's own support-or-contradiction verdict develops.

**What a design response could add now.** The Production envelope (`graph.productions.claims`) gains an optional counter-evidence field: a learner names one piece of evidence or one source that complicates or contradicts their own claim, before submission, addressing Osborne's own point about defending a claim against challenge without requiring real-time peer debate the four-tool constraint was not built to support. This is a code change, not yet built. It answers Osborne's own gap only; it does no work against Sandoval's deeper claim that passing behavior and epistemic stance are separable, and Kuhn's three-form vocabulary names no single Check target this pass can point the field at with confidence.

**Status: OPEN.** `PLAN-REVISION-2.md`'s own research-questions table already marks overlap-map question 12, scientific understanding distinct from correctness, as Partial rather than resolved; this batch adds a concrete field a future Production schema could carry and a concrete reason, Sandoval's own practical-versus-formal gap, that field would not close the question on its own.

### e. Effect-size anchoring for the pre-registration

`PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2d's own batch-four pointer already names all three sources below as effect-size anchors supporting the d = 0.4 to 0.5 planning assumption; this subsection states what changes in `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` itself.

`PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s Sampling Plan states its d = 0.4 to 0.5 assumption traces only to an unsourced heuristic in `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 and to Bastani and colleagues (2025)'s percentage-only contrast, with no comparable Cohen's d reported. Chen and Yang (2019, `project-based-inquiry-learning/chen-yang-2019-pbl-meta-analysis.md`) pools project-based-learning-versus-comparison achievement studies directly and finds a positive effect moderated by implementation features, a closer-fit benchmark for the pedagogical family Research OS's Production work sits inside than either current source. Furtak and colleagues (2012, `project-based-inquiry-learning/furtak-et-al-2012-inquiry-based-science-meta-analysis.md`) pools 37 inquiry-based-science studies to a mean effect size of 0.50, with teacher-led activities running about 0.40 higher than student-led ones. Lazonder and Harmsen (2016, `project-based-inquiry-learning/lazonder-harmsen-2016-inquiry-based-learning-meta-analysis.md`) reports d = 0.66 on learning activities, 0.71 on performance success, and 0.50 on learning outcomes across 72 inquiry-based-learning studies.

All three sit at or above the 0.4 to 0.5 range `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md` already assumes. This strengthens the existing assumption: the Sampling Plan's own d = 0.4 case, already labeled the conservative end of its own heuristic, now reads as conservative against three independent, published meta-analyses of a closely related pedagogy, past the one heuristic and one percentage contrast it started with.

**What changes in the pre-registration.** `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s Sampling Plan gains these three sources as named anchors alongside Bastani and colleagues (2025), with the same caveat already applied to that source: each reports on a different task, population, and pedagogy than Research OS's own workspace, so the anchor supports the d = 0.4 planning assumption's direction without replacing the commissioned formal power analysis the Sampling Plan already asks for. Chen and Yang's own moderator analysis, which of the significant moderators map onto features Research OS's design already varies versus features it holds fixed by construction, is logged as a new exploratory-analysis candidate rather than folded into the confirmatory Sampling Plan.

**Status: STABLE.** Three published effect sizes converge on the same direction as the existing planning assumption; the update strengthens a documented number with more evidence rather than resolving an open design question, the same character revision 2's own section 2d power finding carried.
