## 1. The four targets

### Tools Competition

**Deadline.** Phase I abstract due 2026-10-13. The 2027 cycle launches 2026-09-10, today's date, and runs Phase II proposals to 2027-01-21 and Phase III pitches in April 2027, winners announced June 2027.

**Eligibility for a pending 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsor.** Open. Section K's own row already read "welcomes teams at all phases; no 501(c)(3) requirement surfaced," and the 2026-09-10 fetch of tools-competition.org confirms the program "welcome[s] individuals and teams at all phases of development" with no 501(c)(3) status detailed as a gate.

**Fit.** Strong. Three award tiers track Research OS for K-12's own phase structure directly: Catalyst ($50,000, early-stage tools) maps to Phase 0/1, Growth ($150,000, existing users and scale) maps to a funded Phase 1 pilot, Transform ($300,000, 10,000-plus users) maps to Phase 2. Total pool: $4.5 million-plus.

**Ask size.** Catalyst, $50,000, the tier matching where Research OS for K-12 is today (Phase 0 shipped, no pilot yet).

**Lead paragraph.**

> Research OS for K-12 constrains an AI research assistant to four non-generative tools, find, quote, check, organize, so a school-age learner does the cognitive work of research while AI removes the friction of search and citation-checking rather than the friction of thought. The system already runs end to end today: a Postgres graph schema, a frontier-backward routing algorithm, and the four-tool workspace against a seeded grades 3 to 5 physics path, plus a working bridge to a combinatorial hypothesis-generation engine that lets a reviewed student production and an AI-generated hypothesis sit in one address space and be compared on the same terms.

Source: `learning/research-os/PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5 (drawn near-verbatim, sized for a Phase I abstract).

Sources: [tools-competition.org](https://tools-competition.org/), fetched 2026-09-10; `_intake/research-os-k12/03-data-services.md` section K.

### Digital Public Goods Alliance registration

**Deadline.** Rolling; the DPGA's own submission guide states "we try to review all applications within 30 days of submission," so open now.

**Eligibility for a pending 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsor.** Likely open; the legal-entity question stays unresolved. The submission guide requires "an authorised representative of the digital solution" to submit "on behalf of the organisation(s) or individual(s) that own the solution," language that names individuals as eligible submitters and does not state a formal nonprofit-registration requirement. Mark this **UNVERIFIED** on the specific point of whether a legal entity is required; the guide's text did not resolve it either way on this fetch.

**Fit.** Moderate, and non-cash. DPG registration is a credibility and discovery signal without direct funding attached, flagged in `04-funding-and-people.md` section 4 item 8 ("Do the free compliance moves now") as a precondition several larger funders look for before they engage. Research OS's code is MIT-licensed already, which the DPGA's own nine indicators name as satisfying the "open licensing" indicator directly; the other eight (SDG relevance, clear ownership, platform independence, documentation, a non-PII data-export mechanism, privacy-law adherence, standards adherence, and "do no harm by design") are unverified as met and would need a real pass before submitting.

**Ask size.** None. This is a registration step, valued for credibility rather than cash. Included in wave 1 because it unlocks downstream funder credibility at zero cost and a 30-day cycle, inside the 90-day window with room to spare.

**Lead paragraph.**

> Research OS for K-12's code is MIT-licensed and built on an open protocol; the workspace exists to make research skill-building free to any learner, with no gatekeeping between a learner and the graph. Registering as a Digital Public Good makes that openness legible to the funders, governments, and NGOs the DPGA registry already serves as a discovery layer, the same open-source, no-exclusivity posture `MANIFESTO.md` states as a founding commitment for the parent Foundation.

Source: `MANIFESTO.md` section 6 ("What bucket is not," on no exclusivity); `_intake/research-os-k12/04-funding-and-people.md` section 2.3, Digital Public Goods Alliance row.

Sources: [digitalpublicgoods.net/digital-public-goods/submission-guide](https://digitalpublicgoods.net/digital-public-goods/submission-guide), fetched 2026-09-10; `_intake/research-os-k12/04-funding-and-people.md` section 2.1, row 4.

**Lead paragraph.**

> Research OS for K-12 routes a learner backward from a chosen frontier target through their own prerequisite gaps, an approach the AI for Education fund's own framing, breakthrough ideas that drive student success, is built to surface. The workspace's four-tool constraint on AI, find, quote, check, organize, is a direct, testable answer to the offloading question a funder focused on AI-in-education outcomes is already asking of every tool in this category.

### NLnet NGI Zero

**Deadline.** 2026-11-03, 12:00 CET, confirmed directly on nlnet.nl: "Next deadline: November 3rd 2026 12:00 CET (noon)." Inside the 90-day window.

**Eligibility for a pending 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsor.** Open on entity type, individuals are a listed applicant category, but a new eligibility caveat surfaced on this pass that the prior intake docs did not carry: nlnet.nl states "our EU-supported funds require a strong 'European dimension'; usually this means applicants should (mostly) be from Horizon Europe-eligible countries." <!-- voice-ignore-line: verbatim quote from nlnet.nl --> Bucket Foundation and its founder are US-based. Mark this **UNVERIFIED, with a specific new risk flag**: whether a US applicant with a US-based feed402/Research-OS project clears NGI Zero's European-dimension bar was not resolved by this fetch and needs direct confirmation from NLnet before any application effort.

**Fit.** Weak for Research OS for K-12, present for completeness because the deadline is real and imminent. NLnet funds "technical development" of open-licensed software and protocol infrastructure; it fits `feed402`, the citation-payment protocol, far more directly than the K-12 learning product. `_intake/research-os-k12/04-funding-and-people.md` row 7 already scores this "4 for feed402" and does not score it for Research OS at all. This entry stays on the list because the deadline is real, near-term, and verified open, worth acting on alongside the Research-OS-specific applications already in motion; it is the weakest Research OS fit of the four, included for the deadline's own sake.

**Ask size.** EUR 5,000 to 50,000 for a first grant, confirmed on nlnet.nl.

**Lead paragraph, scoped to feed402.**

> feed402 is an open, MIT-licensed protocol and reference implementation proving an AI agent with a Base wallet can discover, pay for, and query a data endpoint over HTTP 402, a live example merchant already running on Base Sepolia. NGI Zero funds exactly this category of open technical infrastructure; the ask is a first grant to harden the reference server, the payment-verification path, and the spec toward a real dataset.
