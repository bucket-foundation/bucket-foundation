## Study Information

### Title

Scoped-tool AI support and frontier-backward routing in a K-12 knowledge-graph workspace: a three-arm pilot.

### Research questions and hypotheses

The study registers six primary, directional hypotheses, each drawn from one of the twelve overlap-map questions in `OVERLAP-RESEARCH-OS-AND-AI-FOR-RESEARCH.md` and selected because `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 4 marks it answerable, in whole or in a first-signal partial form, inside a single Phase 1 pilot rather than requiring district scale. The six questions left out of this registration (overlap-map questions 2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 12) are named at the end of this subsection with the reason each waits.

**H1, extends overlap-map question 1, `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q8 and Q11.** On a delayed (eight-week), unassisted near-transfer post-test, learners assigned to the constrained find-quote-check-organize workspace will score higher than learners assigned to a full open-ended chatbot arm (H1a), and will not score lower than learners assigned to the no-AI control arm (H1b). Grounded in Bastani and colleagues (2025), whose guardrailed hint-only tool preserved most of a practice-performance gain with no significant drop in unassisted exam performance while an unrestricted tool produced a significant drop; in Kosmyna and colleagues (2025), whose EEG measure and later-recall measure both showed the largest cost in the least-constrained condition; and in Risko and Gilbert's (2016) offloading framework, which the three arms are built to separate. A secondary, exploratory outcome under H1, scoped at Phase 1 to a first descriptive signal instead of confirmatory power (`PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 2c), is class-level production diversity: pairwise similarity across a class's accepted productions on the same routed target, scored the way Doshi and Hauser (2024) score similarity between AI-assisted stories, predicted to be higher (less diverse) in the full-chatbot arm than in the constrained-workspace arm, with the loss concentrated in learners who adopt a tool suggestion directly rather than revising it, per Doshi and Hauser's own finding.

**H2, extends overlap-map question 3, `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q5 and Q29.** Within a class, learners randomly assigned a frontier-backward route to a chosen target node will reach Understanding on that target in fewer sessions, and in less elapsed time on task, than learners assigned Academy's forward, reach-weighted default route to the same target. Grounded in Pan and colleagues (2017), Liang and colleagues (2018), Roy and colleagues (2019), and Gasparetti and colleagues (2017), the direct precedent for inferring a prerequisite edge and walking it backward to build a route; and in Barnett and Ceci (2002), whose transfer taxonomy is the framework for stating that this is a near-transfer claim (a template-plus-scenario item varying surface features on the same practiced construct) rather than an undifferentiated one.

**H3, extends overlap-map question 5, `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q13 and Q30.** Embedded within the subset of learners across all three arms who submit an accepted production eligible for a citation event, at least one week's self-explanation quality (scored on the rubric in `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4's Understanding measure) will not differ between a recognition-only arm (an Open Badges 3.0 credential issued on acceptance, no ledger entry) and a recognition-plus-ledger arm (the same badge plus a custodial ledger entry with no liquid payout). Grounded in Deci, Koestner, and Ryan (1999), whose 128-study meta-analysis names tangible, expected, performance-contingent rewards, the category a citation-on-acceptance payment falls into, as the reliable case for undermining intrinsic motivation, and in Gneezy and Rustichini (2000), whose field and lab evidence is that a small payment can perform worse than no payment at all. Neither source predicts harm from a recognition-only or non-liquid-ledger design; Mekler and colleagues (2017) found a non-payment, recognition-style mechanic raised output with no measured motivational cost, the direct precedent for expecting no difference between these two arms. This hypothesis explicitly excludes a live-cash arm, which `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 6 states needs an employment-law opinion and custodial-account infrastructure neither of which exists.

**H4, is overlap-map question 6 and `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q22, run offline against the engine's held-out corpus before any pilot learner is involved.** The Check tool will show lower agreement with human-labeled verdicts when judging a claim against an isolated quoted span than when judging the same claim against full-document context. A second, exploratory sub-hypothesis under H4, motivated by Binz and Schulz (2023)'s finding that small, meaning-preserving phrasing changes swing a model's reasoning accuracy: Check's verdict will show lower within-claim agreement between the claim as originally phrased and one meaning-preserving paraphrase than between two independent runs on the identical, unparaphrased claim.

**H5, extends overlap-map question 8, `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q19 and Q24, run engine-side on productions from Phase 1's own campaign run once `ros-12`'s wiring lands and at least one production reaches `accepted` status.** Holdout calibration of the engine's ranked hypotheses, measured against later-confirmed ground truth, will be closer to observed outcome frequency (lower Brier score, or a calibration curve closer to the identity line) when the corpus includes reviewed, teacher-signed-off Production-tier evidence than when the same corpus runs without those productions. Grounded in Macnamara and colleagues (2024), whose finding that learners cannot self-assess their own AI-assisted skill decay argues sign-off cannot be self-reported, and in Schmidgall and colleagues (2025), whose Agent Laboratory found intermediate-stage human feedback improved output quality more than end-only review, the basis for expecting the reviewed-and-signed-off tier to carry more calibration value than an unreviewed submission would.

**H6, extends overlap-map question 9, `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q2 and Q33.** On the eight-week retention probe (`INSTRUMENTS.md`), learners whose review items are scheduled at FSRS-computed spaced intervals will show a higher pass rate than learners whose review items are scheduled at a fixed, massed interval, replicating the testing-effect direction Roediger and Karpicke (2006) establish. A moderator sub-hypothesis, exploratory rather than confirmatory since no batch-two paper tests it directly (`OVERLAP-RESEARCH-OS-AND-AI-FOR-RESEARCH.md` question 9's own evidence note): the FSRS-versus-fixed advantage will be smaller on the machine-graded (Check-tool) checkpoint than it would be expected to be on a human-graded checkpoint, motivated by Stadler, Bannert, and Sailer (2024)'s finding that LLM assistance during inquiry lowers effort and depth together, a pattern that could mask part of the same depth difference a human grader would catch.

**Questions left out of this registration.** Overlap-map question 2 (two-sigma-class effect at graph-routed scale) is district-scale by construction; a pilot-scale point estimate is recorded as exploratory only. Question 4 (AI-generated and K-12 productions meeting a domain-scientist novelty bar) needs more accepted-Production-tier volume than one pilot semester of the rarest of the five states will produce (`PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 4, row 1 and row 4). Question 7 (literature-based discovery closing gap nodes) is blocked on `ros-12`'s engine-side `GapNode`/`value_of_information` wiring, independent of pilot scale. Question 10 (division of cognitive labor across states, by age) needs a contrasting age band a single grade-banded pilot cannot supply. Question 11 (burden of knowledge predicting gap-node clustering) is explicitly generational and needs longitudinal engine-run data no single semester carries. Question 12 (scientific understanding distinct from correctness) needs a Binz-and-Schulz-style probing battery run on enough Production-tier cases for construct validation, more than one pilot semester's rarest-state volume supplies; a first, exploratory pass on whatever Production-tier cases the pilot does produce is logged as exploratory under H5's own data collection instead of standing as a sixth confirmatory hypothesis.

## Design Plan

### Study type

Experiment, an experimental manipulation is fully controlled by the research team (route and workspace tool access), and no data collection has begun.

### Blinding

Participants (learners) are not blinded to which of the three workspace arms they use, since the tool itself is visibly different across arms. Human raters scoring Understanding and Internalization transfer items (the inter-rater procedure below) are blind to the learner's arm assignment where the rated artifact does not itself reveal it (an explanation or a transfer-item answer carries no arm label); teacher reviewers approving or returning a production are not blinded to arm, since the teacher's own class roster and the workspace the student used are visible in the ordinary course of review, a limitation stated here rather than concealed. The second rater in the inter-rater sampling procedure is blind to the first rater's decision and reason, per `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4.

### Study design

A three-arm, class-cluster-randomized design. Arm assignment happens at the class level (every learner in a given class period uses the same workspace arm), because the four-tool workspace, the full chatbot, and the no-AI condition are each a distinct classroom environment a teacher runs for the whole section, with no per-student toggle between them; this is also the unit `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 4 and `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 both describe when they name "students nested in classrooms" as the clustering structure a proper power analysis has to account for. H2's route-assignment comparison (frontier-backward versus forward) is the one exception: it randomizes at the individual-learner level within a class, per `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q5's own design, since both routes can coexist inside the same constrained-workspace arm without contaminating the arm-level AI-access manipulation.

| Arm | What the learner has access to | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| Constrained AI (the product) | Locate and Quote (retrieval only, no model call), Check (Sonnet-tier, verifies a claim against cited evidence, abstains on weak retrieval), Organize (Haiku-tier, restructures notes into claim-evidence-source shape); no tool ever writes the learner's answer | Tests H1a and H1b directly; the study's own workspace |
| Full chatbot | Open-ended generative AI assistance on the same source material, no tool-boundary restriction | Tests the hypothesized failure mode: short-term ease, worse independent performance once the tool is withdrawn |
| No AI | Same knowledge-graph navigation UI and source material, human-only research tools, no AI assistance of any kind | Baseline |

### Randomization

Class-level random assignment to arm, stratified by grade band and by gifted-versus-Title I outreach channel (`04-compliance-distribution.md` section 12's equity plan, pairing every gifted-program pilot with a Title I pilot in the same outreach cohort), using a random-number-generator seed recorded in the study's own OSF component before assignment, so the sequence is reproducible and auditable. Where a single teacher teaches multiple sections of the same course, sections are treated as separate classes for randomization purposes and the teacher's own identity is entered as a covariate (a teacher effect nested under the class random effect rather than crossed with it) in the Analysis Plan below. Individual-level randomization for H2's route comparison is block-randomized within each class so every class contributes learners to both route conditions.

## Sampling Plan

### Existing data

None. No data collection has begun, and none of the learners, classes, or productions named anywhere in this draft or in any Research OS document exist as enrolled study participants as of this draft's date.

### Data collection procedures

Recruitment follows `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 9's ranked procurement paths; the pilot targets a district-MOU or charter-network path (path 4 or path 6) rather than the fastest teacher-led or homeschool paths, since a research-grade cluster-randomized design needs several classes assigned as intact units under one coordinating agreement rather than several independent, self-selecting individual teachers. Consent and assent follow `IRB-PACKET-OUTLINE.md`. Enrollment closes before the semester's route and arm assignments are drawn; a learner who enrolls after randomization has already run joins the study only as an observed non-randomized case, logged and excluded from the confirmatory analysis, never assigned to an arm retroactively.

### Sample size

**The compiled effect-size and power-analysis sources already in this repo.** `_intake/research-os-k12/RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 and `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 both state, as a "back-of-envelope heuristic," that a three-arm between-subjects design targeting a medium effect (Cohen's d roughly 0.4 to 0.5) at standard power (0.80) and alpha (0.05) needs on the order of 60 to 70 students per arm, and that a smaller expected effect (d roughly 0.3) roughly doubles that; both sources explicitly ask for a commissioned formal power analysis once effect size and clustering are set, rather than treating this heuristic as final. Bastani and colleagues (2025)'s guardrailed-versus-unrestricted contrast (a 127 percent practice-performance gain with no significant exam-performance drop, against a 48 percent practice gain paired with a 17 percent exam drop for the unrestricted condition) is directional support that a scoped-tool effect on unassisted post-test performance could exceed a medium effect size, but the paper reports percentage changes on a different task, population, and country than this pilot, with no standard deviation reported in the intake card this draft can convert into a comparable Cohen's d; it is cited here as reason to treat d = 0.4 as a conservative assumption rather than an inflated one; it stands in for direction, never for a formal power analysis.

**The naive, individually-randomized target this draft computes.** H1's primary contrast (constrained-AI arm versus full-chatbot arm on the near-transfer post-test) is one of two pre-specified primary pairwise comparisons under H1 (the other, constrained-AI versus no-AI control, tests H1b); a Holm-Bonferroni correction across these two comparisons sets the per-comparison alpha at 0.025 for the first-rejected test. Using the standard two-independent-means sample-size formula, n per arm = 2 × (z_alpha/2 + z_beta)^2 / d^2, at alpha = 0.025 two-tailed (z = 2.24), power = 0.80 (z = 0.84):

| Assumed d | Source of the assumption | n per arm, naive (no clustering) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | Upper end of the system-review and compliance-report heuristic | 76 |
| 0.4 | Lower end of the same heuristic; this draft's main assumption | 119 |
| 0.3 | The same sources' own "smaller effect" sensitivity case | 211 |

**The cluster-corrected target.** Because arm assignment is class-level, the naive n above understates the number of learners needed. The design effect is DEFF = 1 + (m_bar minus 1) times ICC, where m_bar is the average class size and ICC is the intraclass correlation of the outcome across classes. No card in this corpus supplies a validated ICC for a K-12 near-transfer or retention outcome measured inside a knowledge-graph workspace. The figures here use an assumed average class size of 25 and an ICC range (0.05, 0.10, 0.20) drawn from the range education cluster-trial design commonly reports for achievement outcomes, an illustrative planning assumption unsourced within this corpus, flagged for replacement by either a published multi-site ICC benchmark or the pilot's own first-semester variance decomposition before treating this number as final.

| ICC | Design effect (m_bar = 25) | n per arm at d = 0.4, cluster-corrected | Classes per arm (m_bar = 25) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 2.2 | 262 | 11 |
| 0.10 | 3.4 | 405 | 17 |
| 0.20 | 5.8 | 691 | 28 |

At every ICC in this range, the fully powered target sits far above what `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 3's own Phase 1 scope, "two classrooms, one semester" (`PLAN.md` section 8), or `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 9's district-MOU path can plausibly deliver in a first pilot. This draft therefore registers Phase 1 as a feasibility and effect-size-estimation pilot for H1's confirmatory claim; the fully powered confirmatory test of that same claim waits for Phase 2, consistent with `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 4's own verdict that question 1's core comparison is answerable at Phase 1 while its diversity addition needs Phase 2 for power. The table above is the pre-specified power target for the Phase 2 extension of this same registration, to be filed as an update once district-scale recruitment is contracted; Phase 1 alone is not expected to reach it.

**What Phase 1's feasible enrollment can detect.** If Phase 1 enrolls four to six classes total (two to three per arm across two arms, or a smaller three-arm slice at a single district-MOU site), at 25 students per class that is 50 to 75 students per arm before attrition. At n = 60 per arm, ICC = 0.10, DEFF = 3.4, the effective n per arm is roughly 18; solving the same formula in reverse for the minimum detectable effect at alpha = 0.025, power = 0.80 gives d approximately 1.0, an effect size only the largest studies in this corpus (Bastani and colleagues 2025's guardrail-versus-unrestricted contrast, in percentage terms) come near. Phase 1 is therefore explicitly underpowered for a definitive test of H1's directional claim on its own; it is adequately scoped, per `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 4, for H2, H4's offline sub-study, H5's engine-side calibration comparison, H6's within-class FSRS comparison, and H1's process-log and self-report outcomes, read at this scale as hypothesis-generating evidence. This draft states that distinction plainly rather than reporting a single pilot's underpowered p-value as if it settled H1.

**Sample size rationale.** Documented in the two tables and the paragraph above.

### Stopping rule

Data collection for a given semester's cohort stops at the semester's own natural end, the pre-scheduled eight-week retention probe date, a fixed point rather than a data-dependent threshold; no interim analysis triggers early stopping, since a three-arm educational pilot with human-subjects protections for minors does not support a sequential-testing design without its own separate IRB provision, which this draft does not request.

**How Understanding is scored.** `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 1 and `EVIDENCE-SCHEMA.md` together define it: the learner submits a free-text explanation; `gradeExplanation` grounds a verdict of support, contradiction, or unknown against the node's own summary and its prerequisites' summaries only; `stage` moves to Understanding only when the result is support, confidence is not low, and the model did not abstain. This study adds a human cross-score on a sampled subset (the same sampling rule as the inter-rater procedure below) using a rubric scored on three dimensions: whether the explanation states the causal or logical mechanism rather than restating the claim, whether it uses at least one prerequisite concept the node's own summary names, and whether a rater with no access to the model's verdict would independently judge the explanation grounded. A human-model disagreement rate above a threshold set once pilot data exists (not fixed in this draft) flags the automated grader for review before it is trusted as this study's sole Understanding judge.

**How Internalization is scored.** A pass requires both an automated pass on a sealed-pool, multi-hop transfer item (`LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4's transfer-task construction rule; see `TRANSFER-TASK-BANK.md` for the sky-blue path's own item bank) and a teacher's `"approved"` decision on that same attempt; a `"returned"` decision requires a one-line reason and leaves the learner at Understanding, per `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 1. This study treats a learner's *pass* status on a delayed transfer item, independent of whether a teacher has yet reviewed it, as the near-transfer and retention outcome variables in the table above, and treats the teacher's separate approve-or-return decision on the same item as the reliability check against the automated grade, following `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4's own framing of that agreement as RQ3's misclassification-rate question rather than as a second, independent outcome.

### Indices

None beyond the fused Understanding-and-Internalization pass rate already defined above; this study does not build a composite index across the six primary hypotheses, since each targets a distinct construct and a combined index would obscure which specific hypothesis a null or positive result belongs to.

## Analysis Plan

### Statistical models

Every confirmatory hypothesis that involves live learners (H1, H2, H3, H6) is tested with a linear or generalized linear mixed-effects model with a random intercept for class, matching the unit of randomization; where a district contributes more than one school, a random intercept for school nests above class. The general form: `outcome ~ arm + covariates + (1 | class)`, or `outcome ~ arm + covariates + (1 | school / class)` when more than one school participates. Binary pass-fail outcomes (Internalization pass, retention-probe pass) use a mixed-effects logistic model; continuous outcomes (near-transfer score, time-to-Understanding, self-explanation rubric score) use a mixed-effects linear model. H4 and H5, which run against the engine's own held-out or fixture corpus rather than live learners, use the engine's existing evaluation methodology (agreement-rate comparison for H4, Brier score and calibration-curve comparison for H5) with no class random effect, since no learner clustering applies to either.

### Covariates

Pre-specified for every live-learner model: the diagnostic-probe baseline score (`src/lib/research-os/probe.ts`'s cold-start probe, where administered), grade band, a binary Title I-versus-gifted-outreach-channel indicator (per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 12's equity plan), and a teacher-identity term nested under the class random effect where one teacher runs more than one section. Prior Academy mastery-profile proficiency (`fusedConceptMastery`, `src/lib/academy/mastery.ts`), where the learner has an existing Academy account, is added as an exploratory covariate rather than a pre-specified one, since `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 3 confirms no code path today joins Academy proficiency to a Research OS row, so this covariate reaches only the subset of learners a manual join can recover.

### Inference criteria

Nominal alpha = 0.05, two-tailed, per confirmatory hypothesis family (H1 through H6), each treated as an independent pre-registered primary question per the Sampling Plan's own reasoning. Within a hypothesis family that carries more than one pairwise contrast, Holm-Bonferroni correction applies across that family's own contrasts before any result within it is called significant; H1's two contrasts (constrained-versus-chatbot, constrained-versus-control) are the concrete case, corrected as shown in the Sampling Plan. Power at 0.80 is the pre-specified target for the Phase 2, fully powered extension of this registration; a Phase 1 result that fails to reach significance at the underpowered enrollment the Sampling Plan describes is reported as inconclusive on H1's own terms alone, never treated as evidence against it, and the hypothesis-generating outcomes (H2, H4, H5, H6, and H1's process-log and diversity measures) are reported descriptively regardless of H1's own confirmatory result.

### Data exclusion

Excluded before the confirmatory analysis runs, following standard cluster-RCT convention: a learner who withdraws consent at any point (data collected before withdrawal is deleted outright from the analysis dataset, per the consent language in `IRB-PACKET-OUTLINE.md`); a learner who enrolls after class-level randomization has already run (logged as an observed non-randomized case, per the Sampling Plan); a class with fewer than five enrolled and consented learners in any arm, an integrity floor below which class-level clustering assumptions do not hold; and a learner whose logged workspace engagement falls below a minimum-use threshold set once pilot baseline engagement data exists, following Oreopoulos and Low (2026)'s finding, cited in `PLAN.md` section 3, that a well-funded tutoring intervention produced near-zero gains among students who did not open the tool, the same engagement-rate risk `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 4 names as the Access-state outcome variable. The primary analysis is intent-to-treat (every consented, randomized learner analyzed in their assigned arm regardless of engagement level); a per-protocol analysis restricted to learners above the engagement threshold is reported as a secondary, pre-specified sensitivity check alongside the intent-to-treat result.

### Missing data

A learner with a missing immediate or delayed post-test, but at least one other recorded outcome, is retained in the mixed-effects model, which handles unbalanced data by design; a learner with no recorded post-test at any interval is excluded from that specific outcome's analysis and reported in an attrition table by arm. No imputation is planned for the primary confirmatory analyses; a missing-completely-at-random assumption is tested by comparing attrition rates and baseline-covariate distributions across arms before results are interpreted, and any arm-differential attrition is reported alongside the primary result rather than adjusted away.

### Exploratory analyses

Named explicitly here so they are not later reported as if pre-specified: the class-level production-diversity secondary outcome under H1; the Production-tier scientific-understanding probing pass named for overlap-map question 12 in the Study Information section above; the prior-Academy-proficiency covariate; the per-protocol sensitivity analysis under Data exclusion; and any subgroup analysis by grade band, gifted-versus-Title I channel, or route condition not already named as a primary contrast.

## Data availability statement

Operational interaction data (which node a learner opened, which explanation they submitted, which teacher decision followed) is generated and stored under the district's data privacy agreement, governed by FERPA's school-official exception per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 1, and is never redisclosed outside that operational relationship without separate consent. Research use of that same interaction data runs under a legally distinct basis: FERPA's studies exception (34 CFR 99.31(a)(6)) is a narrow, school-initiated pathway that `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 both state does not cleanly cover a vendor's own dissertation research, so the research consent track named in `IRB-PACKET-OUTLINE.md`, and never the operational school-official exception, is the actual legal basis for any research use, analysis, or publication of this data. Raw learner-authored text (explanations, transfer-item answers, production drafts) stays access-restricted to the named research team under the data governance model `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 describes, an independent data-access reviewer, ideally the partner faculty PI, holding a technical or procedural check on what the founder can access from live accounts outside the consented research cohort; this text is never posted publicly. A de-identified derived dataset, aggregate outcome variables and model-fit statistics only, with any cell too small to protect a single learner's identity suppressed, may be deposited as an OSF component after publication, gated on the IRB-approved data-use agreement and the partner PI's sign-off, and on a retention and destruction schedule tied to the study's own end date rather than kept indefinitely, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10's own instruction to keep the research data-use agreement legally separate from the operational school DPA.
