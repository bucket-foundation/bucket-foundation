## 2. Personnel and the research question

**Personnel.** The founder, as the product's builder and the study's designer. A faculty co-investigator or principal investigator, not yet named, who serves as IRB of record per `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9's own finding that most university IRBs require a faculty member of record for human-subjects research involving minors, and that the founder, currently unaffiliated with a research institution, cannot serve as PI alone. `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5's two-PI pairing rationale names the shape this role should take once filled: a human-AI systems PI, reviewing the routing algorithm's correctness, the belief-fusion math shared with the hypothesis engine, and the AI tool-scoping design, and a learning-sciences PI, reviewing whether the five-state construct is a valid, distinct measure and whether the three-arm design's outcome measures meet a learning-sciences evidentiary bar, this second role also serving as the IRB-of-record faculty a minors study requires. Splitting the two roles is itself the mitigation `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5 names for the founder's conflict of interest, restated in section 7 below.

**Research question**, drawn verbatim in substance from `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5: does constraining an AI research assistant to four non-generative tools, find, quote, check, organize, change what a school-age learner produces and retains compared with an open-ended tutor and no AI at all, and does routing a learner backward from a chosen frontier target through their own prerequisite gaps beat a fixed forward curriculum on the same measure. `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5 states the system already runs as working code against a seeded prerequisite path, and that a bidirectional bridge already connects this graph to a hypothesis-generation engine, background an IRB reviewer would read as evidence the intervention is not conceptual.

**ETH AI Center fit.** `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5 names the founder's stated target, the ETH AI Center's AI for Education and Human-AI Collaboration track, as aligning directly with the cognitive-offloading research question, and notes that Switzerland's data protection regime (the revised Federal Act on Data Protection, broadly GDPR-aligned per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 3) makes a Swiss pilot geography coherent with both the research affiliation and the compliance posture the product needs regardless. `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 11 lists the IRB-of-record faculty target, ETH-connected or a domestic education-school faculty member, as an open founder question this packet does not resolve; it is named here as the leading candidate rather than a confirmed choice.

## 3. Participant population and eligibility

Learners in participating classrooms, grade band set by `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` blocking decision 1 (the sky-blue grades 3-to-5 path already prototyped, or the quantum-history grades 9-to-12 path `PLAN.md` section 8 names, or both as parallel tracks), teachers of those classrooms, and, for the consent process itself, the parents or legal guardians of any learner under 18. Inclusion requires enrollment in a participating class at a partner school and a completed, active consent (parental permission plus the learner's own assent, per section 5) before any randomized arm assignment reaches that learner. Exclusion criteria mirror `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s own Data exclusion subsection: withdrawn consent at any point, enrollment after class-level randomization has already run, and, for the per-protocol sensitivity analysis only, engagement below a threshold set once pilot baseline data exists. No learner is excluded from the operational product on the basis of declining research participation; `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10's own three-track consent design (below) keeps ordinary product use available to a learner whose family declines the research-data track.

## 4. Minimal-risk justification

This study proposes a minimal-risk determination: the probability and magnitude of harm anticipated is no greater than a learner would ordinarily encounter in daily life or in the performance of routine academic activities. The reasoning:

The intervention itself is educational content delivery through a knowledge-graph workspace with a constrained AI tool surface, carrying no physical or pharmacological component and no deception of any participant; a growing body of state K-12 AI guidance (`04-compliance-distribution.md` section 5) already treats scoped classroom AI tools as ordinary instructional practice subject to teacher-in-the-loop review, the same review structure this product builds in by design. The study's research-specific data collection adds no new category of information beyond what the product's operational use already logs for routing and review purposes; the incremental research burden is the retention probe schedule and the confidence items (`INSTRUMENTS.md`), each adding a few minutes to an ordinary class session, and the teacher time-on-review log, which adds no burden to the learner at all. The primary residual risk this study adds beyond ordinary product use is a privacy and confidentiality risk, the possibility that research-use data handling exposes a learner's information beyond what the operational FERPA basis already permits, addressed directly by the data governance split in section 6 below and by the exclusion of PPRA-protected categories from every instrument by design, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 1's own instruction to keep onboarding and workspace telemetry limited to academic content and skill state. No instrument named in `INSTRUMENTS.md` or `TRANSFER-TASK-BANK.md` touches any of PPRA's eight protected categories.

Two design choices are logged here because a reviewer is likely to ask about them directly. First, the full-chatbot arm is an active comparison condition: `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 5 and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 both treat unrestricted AI access as the condition under live regulatory and research scrutiny. No arm withholds instruction a learner would otherwise receive; the no-AI control arm uses the same source material and graph navigation every other arm uses, with human-only research tools standing in for the AI ones. Second, the citation-payment mechanic named in H3 (`PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`) is explicitly scoped to recognition and a non-liquid custodial ledger entry for this study. No live cash payment to a minor is proposed anywhere in this packet; `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 6 states a written employment-law opinion and custodial-account infrastructure are both prerequisites this venture still needs, and a minimal-risk determination for the payment arm would need re-review once either exists.

## 5. Consent and assent

`04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 both require active, opt-in consent for minors in research, never passive or opt-out consent, plus the child's own assent, waivable by the IRB for minimal-risk research using normal educational practice only up through roughly sixth grade and actively obtained above that, since a novel AI tool being introduced for the study is not an already-established curriculum. Three separate consent tracks run per learner family, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10: using the product at all (the operational track, governed entirely by the district's own FERPA-based data privacy agreement); the research use of interaction data as its own category (the track this section addresses); and, only for families whose learner reaches the Production state and becomes eligible for a citation event, the custodial recognition-and-ledger mechanism named in H3. A family may consent to the first track and decline the second without losing access to the product itself, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10's own framing that the research consent is legally separate from the operational relationship.

Every draft below is a starting point for the partner PI's own institution to adapt, with that institution's own required disclosures added (contact information, IRB protocol number, institutional letterhead); none of it is final consent language yet.

### Parent or legal guardian consent draft

> **Research study: how a limited AI research tool affects learning**
>
> Your child's class is using Research OS, a tool that lets students find, quote, check, and organize information from real sources while studying a topic, with a teacher reviewing what a student submits before it counts. We are studying whether this kind of limited AI tool helps students learn and remember material better than either an open-ended AI chat tool or no AI tool at all.
>
> If you agree, your child's class will be placed into one of three groups: a group using the limited find-quote-check-organize tool, a group using an open-ended AI chat tool on the same material, or a group using no AI tool. Your child will answer a few short questions immediately after finishing a topic, again about a week later, and again about two months later, to see what they remember. These take a few extra minutes beyond the normal class activity. We will also look at records the tool already keeps of how your child used it, such as which questions they asked and how long tasks took.
>
> This study is being conducted by [founder's name], the tool's builder, together with [partner PI's name and institution], who oversees the research independent of the tool's own development. Your child's regular schoolwork and grades are not affected by whether you agree to this research study; declining does not remove your child from using the classroom tool itself if the school has adopted it for instruction, only from the additional research questions, record review, and any later published results.
>
> Your child's answers and activity records are kept separate from their regular school record, are not shared outside the research team without your separate written permission, and are stored and later reported only in a form that does not identify your child by name. You may withdraw your child from this research study at any time, for any reason, with no effect on their schoolwork; any information already collected will be deleted from the research records rather than kept.
>
> A separate form, described below, covers whether your child's accepted work may earn a citation credential and a non-cash credit toward a custodial account; you do not need to agree to this research study to decline that separate mechanism, or the reverse.

### Student assent draft, under 13

> **Would you like to help us learn about a research tool?**
>
> Your class is trying out a tool that helps you find real information, quote it, check whether your explanation matches what the source says, and organize your notes. A grown-up on our team wants to know whether this tool helps students learn better than other ways of using computer help, or no computer help at all.
>
> If you say yes, your class will be split into groups that use different tools, and you will answer a few short questions right after you finish a topic, again about a week later, and again a couple of months later, about what you remember. We will also look at how you used the tool, like what you asked it and how long things took you.
>
> You do not have to say yes. If you say yes now and change your mind later, that is okay too, just tell your teacher or a grown-up on our team, and we will stop and remove your answers. Saying no will not get you in trouble or change your grade.

### Student assent draft for ages 13 to 17

> **Research study: does a limited AI tool change how you learn**
>
> Your class is using Research OS, a workspace that limits what an AI assistant can do while you research a topic: it can help you find sources, quote them, check your explanation against them, and organize your notes, but it never writes your answer for you. We want to find out whether working this way changes what you learn and remember compared with using an open-ended AI chatbot, or no AI at all.
>
> If you agree to participate, your class will be assigned to one of three groups (the limited tool, an open chatbot, or no AI tool), and you will complete a few short follow-up questions right after a topic, again about a week later, and again about two months later, to measure what you retained. We will also review records the system keeps of how you used it.
>
> Participating or not participating in this research does not change your grade or your ability to use the classroom tool if your school has adopted it for instruction; it only affects whether your specific answers, timing, and usage data are included in the research analysis. Your answers are stored separately from your school record, reported only in a form that does not identify you by name, and you can stop participating at any point by telling your teacher or a member of the research team, with no penalty and no effect on your coursework; anything you already submitted to the research record will be deleted.
>
> A separate, optional part of this study involves whether accepted work you submit can earn a citation credential and a non-cash credit toward a custodial account controlled by a parent or guardian; you and your parent or guardian decide on that separately from this consent.

### Teacher consent draft

> **Research study participation, teacher role**
>
> You are being asked to use Research OS with one or more of your class sections as part of a study on limited-AI research tools in K-12 instruction. Your participation involves teaching with the assigned tool condition for your section (the limited find-quote-check-organize workspace, an open chatbot, or no AI tool, depending on random assignment), reviewing student productions through the review queue as you ordinarily would, and completing two brief surveys, one at the start of the semester estimating your typical grading time on a comparable assignment, and one at the end reporting on the review queue's usefulness and time cost.
>
> The system logs how long you spend reviewing each submission, your position in the review queue when you open each item, and whether you use batch-approval, records used only to evaluate whether the tool saves or costs review time, never to evaluate your own teaching performance. These logs are stored separately from any personnel or evaluation record your school or district keeps and are not shared with your school administration in individually identifiable form.
>
> Your participation is voluntary. Declining to participate, or withdrawing at any point, does not affect your employment or your standing with your school or district; the research team has no role in either. You may decline any specific instrument (the start-of-semester or end-of-semester survey) while still teaching with the assigned classroom tool, if your school has independently adopted it for instruction outside the research study.

## 6. Data governance and the operational FERPA basis

`04-compliance-distribution.md` section 1 and section 10, and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9, all state the same underlying rule: the product's ordinary operation runs under FERPA's school-official exception, which requires the school to retain direct control over the data's use, a defined purpose limited to delivering the product, and no redisclosure without consent. That exception does not extend to the founder's own research use of the same data, since FERPA's studies exception (34 CFR 99.31(a)(6)) is a narrow, school-initiated pathway built for a district commissioning its own program evaluation. `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 states plainly that this exception "should not be the sole legal basis here" for a vendor's own dissertation research. The research consent track in section 5 above is therefore the actual legal basis for any research use, analysis, or publication of interaction data, kept legally separate from the operational school data privacy agreement per that same section's own instruction.

**Data-access boundary.** The founder, as the product's builder, has operational access to live student accounts for ordinary product maintenance; that access is broader than what the research protocol should permit for research purposes. An independent data-access reviewer, the partner faculty PI per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9, holds a technical or procedural check on what the founder can access from live accounts outside the consented research cohort, and on what leaves the operational system for research analysis at all. The exact technical mechanism (a scoped service-role credential, an audit log the partner PI reviews on a fixed schedule, or a data-export step the partner PI must co-sign) is not yet built and is named here as a requirement this packet's eventual full submission must specify concretely, with the partner PI holding final say over the choice.

**De-identification and retention.** Any dataset that leaves the product's operational systems for research analysis is de-identified before that transfer, per a standard this packet's full submission will specify (direct identifiers removed, and any quasi-identifying combination, such as a rare grade-band-and-district pairing, suppressed or aggregated). Retention and destruction follow a fixed schedule tied to the study's own end date, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10's instruction; `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s own data availability statement names the same rule for what, if anything, is deposited publicly after publication.

## 7. Conflict of interest disclosure

The founder is simultaneously the product's builder, the study's designer, and the person whose PhD application benefits from the study's result, a conflict `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10 and `RESEARCH-OS-K12-SYSTEM-REVIEW.md` section 9 both name directly as a known category of research conflict, the founder-as-researcher problem. This packet states it plainly rather than mitigating it through disclosure alone: the mitigation is structural, the two-PI pairing `PLAN-REVISION-1.md` section 5 describes, in which a systems PI and a learning-sciences PI each review a different half of the design neither the founder nor the other PI alone is positioned to review, and neither PI alone can approve the design on the strength of the other's authority. The learning-sciences PI additionally serves as the IRB-of-record faculty this packet's minors-research submission requires, meaning the same person holding independent authority over the study's methodological soundness also holds the formal compliance authority over whether it runs at all. Section 6's data-access boundary is the third leg of this same mitigation: the partner PI holds the procedural check on what the founder can see from live research-cohort data.

This disclosure is stated in every consent and assent draft in section 5 in age-appropriate terms (naming the founder as the tool's builder, working "together with" an independent overseeing researcher), and will be stated again, in full, in the study's own IRB application narrative and in any resulting publication or PhD application material, per `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 10's instruction to state the conflict plainly rather than let it go unremarked.
