## What we use instead (the legal corpus — already wired in Bucket)

- **arXiv** (`cond-mat`, `q-bio`, `physics.bio-ph`, `physics.chem-ph`) — full text, open.
- **bioRxiv / medRxiv** — preprints, open.
- **PMC Open Access Subset** — millions of full-text articles, open.
- **LibreTexts** (Physics, P-Chem, Biology, Biophysics) — open textbooks.
- **OpenStax** (University Physics, Chemistry, Biology) — open textbooks.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** — lecture notes, problem sets, exams (open license).
- **NCBI Bookshelf** — open sections of major references (MBoC, etc.).
- **Project Gutenberg / Internet Archive (public domain)** — classics.
- **Wikisource** (e.g. 1911 Britannica) — public domain reference.
- **x402-research-gateway** (Gian's) + **OpenAlex/PubMed** — metadata + OA full text.

These cover the entire **prerequisite + nucleus** span legally. The paid textbooks in §A
are *nice-to-have polish and problem sets*, not gatekeepers to the foundations.

## Suggested first purchases (if buying anything)

Ranked by leverage-per-dollar for the **biophysics general exam**:
1. **Nelson, Biological Physics** — closest single book to the whole nucleus.
2. **Dill & Bromberg, Molecular Driving Forces** — the stat-mech/thermo spine.
3. **Phillips et al., Physical Biology of the Cell** — quantitative modeling mindset.

Everything else: borrow first (university library / interlibrary loan), buy only if you
keep returning to it.
