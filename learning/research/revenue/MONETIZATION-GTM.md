## 1. CASE STUDY — Duolingo monetization (the freemium playbook)

Duolingo is the single best proof that **you can give away the entire product, convert
under 10% of users, and still build a ~$1B-bookings, 73%-gross-margin business** — because
the free tier is the growth engine, the streak is the retention engine, and Super/Family/Max
form a price ladder that lifts ARPU without ever charging for the learning itself.

### 1.1 What's gated vs. free (the core principle)

**The full curriculum is 100% free forever.** A free user can complete an entire course
beginner→advanced without paying. The skill tree / lessons are never paywalled.
[source: https://duoowl.com/is-duolingo-free/]

What *is* gated is **friction, convenience, and AI — never content**:
- **Hearts (lives):** free users lose hearts on mistakes; running out can halt practice until they regenerate or watch an ad. Super = unlimited hearts. [source: https://duoowl.com/is-duolingo-free/]
- **Ads** between lessons (removed by paid). [source: https://www.untaylored.com/post/how-duolingo-makes-money-business-model-explained]
- **Offline downloads, Mistakes Review, unlimited practice** — paid perks. [source: https://duolingoguides.com/how-much-is-super-duolingo/]

The upsell is **contextual** — it fires at the moment of friction (out of hearts, streak at
risk). "These contextual upgrade suggestions convert far better than generic advertising."
[source: https://www.untaylored.com/post/how-duolingo-makes-money-business-model-explained]

### 1.2 The price ladder (exact 2025/26 numbers)

| Tier | Price | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | Full curriculum + hearts/ads friction |
| **Super (monthly)** | **$12.99/mo** | Unlimited hearts, no ads, offline, Mistakes Review [source: https://duolingoguides.com/how-much-is-super-duolingo/] |
| **Super (annual)** | **$83.99/yr** (~$6.99/mo) | Same; annual lock-in [source: https://familypro.io/en/how-much-is-super-duolingo/] |
| **Super Family** | **$119.99/yr for 6** (~$20/seat) | Converts a household in one transaction [source: https://www.dealnews.com/features/duolingo/cost/] |
| **Max (AI tier)** | **$29.99/mo or ~$168/yr** | Everything in Super **+ AI**: Roleplay, Explain My Answer, Video Call with Lily (GPT-4o) [source: https://beginnersinai.org/duolingo-max-explained/] |

Critically: **Super offers the *same learning content* as free** — you pay for convenience,
not access. [source: https://duolingoguides.com/how-much-is-super-duolingo/] Max is a
**price-anchored premium tier 2× above Super** that sells AI conversation — the hardest,
highest-value job-to-be-done (speaking). [source: https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max/]

### 1.3 Conversion + the headline metrics

- **Free→paid conversion: ~8.5–9.1% of MAU** (up from 3% in 2020 — a 176% increase). Most freemium B2C apps convert ~2%; "great startups hit 4%." Duolingo at ~9% is exceptional. [source: https://medium.com/@nicobottaro/monetization-7-lessons-on-how-duolingo-increased-premium-users-by-176-from-3-to-8-8-42e8d63b58f2]
- **Q1 2026:** 12.5M paid subscribers (+21% YoY), 56.5M DAU, 137.8M MAU. [source: https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/DUOL/8-k-duolingo-inc-reports-material-event-6974ab47316e.html]
- **Implied ARPU ≈ $70–75/paying subscriber/yr** ($748M FY24 ÷ ~10M avg subs). [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000156208825000039/q4fy24duolingo12-31x24shar.htm]

A tiny single-digit conversion × a massive free funnel = 12.5M payers and a $1B-bookings
run-rate.

### 1.4 Streaks → retention → conversion

You can't sell a subscription to a user who churned on day 2. Streaks are the habit layer
that *precedes* monetization:
- **7-day streak → 3.6× more likely to complete the course.** [source: https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/gamification-examples-boost-user-retention-duolingo]
- **Streak-freeze users last 48% longer** past the 7-day mark (17.19 vs 11.62 days). [source: https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/gamification-examples-boost-user-retention-duolingo]
- **Streak wager → +14% Day-14 retention.** [same source]
- Moving sign-up to *after* the first lesson → **+20% next-day retention** (value first, friction later). [same source]

The streak then becomes a **monetizable asset itself** (Streak Freeze / Repair; "protect
your streak with Super" is a primary upgrade prompt). Loss-aversion converts better than
feature-envy.

### 1.5 Revenue mix (where the money is)

FY2024 revenue **$748.0M (+41% YoY)**, subscription = **81%**. By **Q1 2026: subscription
86%, advertising 7%**, gross margin 73%. ARPU rising +6–7% YoY via **mix-shift up the ladder
(Family + Max)** — not base-price hikes. [source: https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/DUOL/8-k-duolingo-inc-reports-material-event-6974ab47316e.html] [source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828025049514/q3fy25duolingo9-30x25share.htm]

### 1.6 THE PLAYBOOK — 8 lessons Bucket steals

1. **Never paywall content; gate friction.** Full curriculum free → maximize top-of-funnel → apply ~9% conversion to a huge base. *(Maps directly to nonprofit constraint: foundations free forever.)*
2. **Build the habit before asking for money.** Value → streak → friction → upgrade. Sign-up *after* first lesson.
3. **Make the streak the retention engine, then monetize loss-aversion.** Streak Freeze/Repair; "protect your streak" upgrade prompt.
4. **Contextual, moment-of-friction upsell** — tie every paywall to felt pain (out of AI credits, want to import a PDF mid-study), not generic banners.
5. **Stack AI as a premium tier ABOVE the base sub** — Super = convenience, Max = AI at 2× price. *Don't give AI away inside the base plan; make it the reason power users pay more.* → Bucket's **Pro = AI horsepower; Scholar = creator economics.**
6. **Ship a Family/Cohort plan** — multiply ARPU per transaction, lower per-seat friction. → Bucket's **reading-group / cohort plan.**
7. **Grow ARPU via tier mix-shift, not base-price hikes.** Push annual over monthly (locks retention, raises effective ARPU).
8. **Keep a small free-tier monetizer** (ads/sponsorship) so the 90%+ who never pay still pay for themselves — *but a nonprofit can substitute donations/grants here (see Khan, §3).*

## 2. CASE STUDY — Whop (the creator-economy / "app store for digital products")

Whop matters because Bucket's **Scholar tier** is a creator marketplace: advanced learners
author and sell decks/atoms, and mint to Story Protocol to earn citation fees. Whop is the
state-of-the-art on how to run a creator-commerce + access-pass + affiliate flywheel — and
Bucket can adopt all three mechanics while running the rake at *cost-recovery* instead of
profit-seeking.

### 2.1 The model

Whop is a **marketplace + checkout + access-control layer for digital products.** Each
"whop" is a storefront that gates an offering. Creators sell **community/Discord/Telegram
access passes, courses, memberships, trading/"alpha" communities, software, and digital
downloads.** [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whop.com] The unit that matters is the
**access pass**: a purchase is an *entitlement* that unlocks gated content/community and is
continuously enforced — pay → auto-added; churn/refund → auto-removed.
[source: https://sacra.com/c/whop/] Creators bolt on modular apps (Chat, Courses, Forums,
Help Desk) to turn a checkout link into a full community product. [same]

**Pivotal 2025 change:** Whop **dropped its old 30% marketplace commission to 0% and made
marketplace approval instant** — converting from a high-rake gatekeeper into a low-rake
payments business that monetizes flow. [source: https://dodopayments.com/blogs/whop-fees-explained]

### 2.2 Take rate (from Whop's own fee docs)

- **Platform fee: 3%** on automation-gated purchases (the old 30% marketplace commission is **gone**). [source: https://docs.whop.com/fees]
- **Processing: 2.7% + $0.30** domestic card (+1.5% intl, +1% FX). [same]
- **Affiliate processing: 1.25%/txn.** [same]
- **No monthly / no setup fee** — pay-as-you-go. [same]
- **Honest effective rate ≈ 6–7% per domestic sale**; Sacra estimates blended take rate rose from ~4.0% (2022) to **~5.5% (early 2025)**. [source: https://sacra.com/c/whop/]

### 2.3 Scale (why this model works)

- **$2.67B cumulative GMV; $142M revenue (annualized, Oct 2025)** up from $56M end-2024 (~74% growth). [source: https://sacra.com/c/whop/] [source: https://sacra.com/research/whop-at-142m-revenue/]
- **18.4M+ users, 183,628 active sellers, ~$3B annual creator payouts** across 144 countries; average creator earns **~$8,413/mo**; 258 sellers have earned $1M+. [source: https://sacra.com/c/whop/]
- **Funding:** Series A $17M (2023, Insight) → Series B $50M+ (2024, Bain, ~$800M val) → **$200M from Tether (Feb 2026) at $1.6B valuation.** Hit a $1.2B GMV run-rate with **~20 engineers.** [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whop.com] [source: https://www.sourcery.vc/p/exclusive-how-whop-hit-12-billion]

### 2.4 Why access-pass + the affiliate loop is the lesson

1. **Recurring is the default shape** — most whops are subscriptions; the ~$8,413/mo average creator is overwhelmingly MRR. [source: https://sacra.com/c/whop/]
2. **Community = retention** — the access pass sells *belonging*; churning means leaving a social graph (high switching cost). [same]
3. **The affiliate marketplace is the viral engine** — **every whop has affiliates on by default at 30% of recurring revenue**; 30,000+ active affiliates; free to join, no approval, automated attribution/payout. A 30% cut on a $50/mo sub pays $15/mo *for the subscription's lifetime* — so affiliates are incentivized to drive *retained* members, not clicks. [source: https://whop.com/blog/consumer-affiliates/] [source: https://www.schoolmaker.com/blog/whop-affiliate-program]

### 2.5 Translation to Bucket's Scholar tier

- **Make the entitlement a living access pass, not a file sale.** A Scholar's deck = a *living, versioned* product (improves over time, FSRS schedules update, Scholar answers questions), not a one-time PDF. That's what makes recurring creator income possible.
- **Zero-friction publishing** — gate on *tier eligibility*, not per-deck review (Whop made approval instant when it cut the rake).
- **Story Protocol is a *superior* affiliate substrate.** Whop's attribution is off-chain and ends at churn. Bucket's is **on-chain, perpetual, and transitive** — when Scholar A's atom is cited by B's deck and a learner pays B, the royalty *auto-splits* to A. This is "build on my work and we both earn *forever*," not "stay subscribed 30 days so I get my cut." Whop has no analog to this.
- **Take rate for a nonprofit:** Whop's ~5.5% is the for-profit, VC-backed number. Bucket should anchor at/below cost-recovery:
  - **Citation royalties: 0%** (taxing citations directly contradicts the mission — "route fees to authors, not publishers").
  - **Primary deck sales: ~2% mission-support fee** (vs Whop's 3%) + processing passed through at cost.
  - **Framing weapon:** "we take ~2% to keep the lights on, vs Whop's ~6%, Patreon/Gumroad's ~8–12%" + perpetual on-chain royalties + nonprofit governance = a positioning **no commercial platform can match** for recruiting genius-tier Scholars.

## 4. OUR TIERS — pricing recommendation

Anchored to: Duolingo's playbook (gate friction not content; AI as a tier above), the
$8–15/mo consumer band, Khan's nonprofit template (grants fund free; AI priced for
sustainability), Anki's free-baseline pressure, and Whop's creator rail.

### Free — "you can actually learn here, for real" · **$0 forever**
On-mission for a nonprofit; the growth engine.
- Full access to the **nucleus paths of every branch** (foundations are free — this is non-negotiable per GOVERNANCE.md).
- FSRS spaced repetition, streaks, XP, branch skill-trees, leagues, community decks (shared, upvoted).
- **Pre-generated/cached concept art** (the shareable cards — $0 marginal cost, and they're the viral loop).
- **Capped AI:** 5–10 tutor messages/day + a couple of card generations/day (cost-bounded — see §5). Generous enough to learn; capped enough to stay sustainable.
- *Why free is this generous:* a nonprofit's mission **is** the free tier; and per Duolingo, the free funnel is where the ~9% who eventually pay come from. Khan proves grants can carry it.

### Pro — "the friction disappears" · **$12/mo or $96/yr** (~$8/mo annual)
The revenue engine. Sits squarely in the $8–15/mo consumer band; annual at ~$8/mo matches
RemNote/MagicSchool; deliberately *below* Brilliant ($13.49) because Bucket's free tier is
real (so Pro sells horsepower, not access).
- **Unlimited AI tutor** + unlimited card/quiz generation (the Duolingo-Max move: AI is the upgrade reason).
- **★ Import your own PDFs (textbooks you legally own) → auto-extracted into atoms + a personal deck.** *This is the Pro hook* — see §4.1.
- **Custom mnemonic art** to your prompts; collectible high-res concept cards.
- **Exam-Simulator** — timed, mixed-topic, exam-style (e.g. "biophysics general exam," USMLE-style) with AI grading + gap report. *This is the high-WTP feature for the wedge.*
- Advanced analytics (forgetting curves, mastery heatmap, projected exam-readiness date).
- Offline / Anki-compatible export (neutralizes the "Anki is free" objection — interoperate, don't fight).

### Scholar / Studio — "learn → contribute → earn" · **$24/mo or $192/yr** *(or rev-share-only)*
The creator tier (Whop translated). Priced as the Duolingo-Max-style **2× anchor above
Pro**, or offered **rev-share-only** (no sub) for serious authors who'd rather earn than pay.
- Author atoms/derivations; **mint to Story Protocol** and earn **perpetual, transitive
  citation fees** when others learn from / build on your work. Closes Bucket's loop:
  learners become the canon's authors.
- **Sell decks/atoms** on the creator marketplace at **~2% mission-support fee** (vs Whop's
  3%), processing at cost, **0% on citation royalties.**
- **Default-on referral/affiliate** per deck (copy Whop's mechanic; Scholar sets the rate).
- **Classroom / cohort mode** — shared streak, co-op leagues, reading groups.

### Family / Cohort · **$96–144/yr for up to 6**
The ARPU multiplier (Duolingo Family translated). One checkout activates a reading group;
lowers per-seat price, raises revenue per paying account. Natural fit for Bucket's
"reading-group takes a branch's nucleus path together."

### Pricing structure rules (from the playbook)
- **Annual ≈ 1.5–2× cheaper per-month than monthly** (lock retention, raise effective ARPU).
- **Grow ARPU via mix-shift** (free → Pro → Scholar/Family), not base-price hikes.
- **Student discount on Pro** (e.g. $6/mo, matching RemNote student) — the wedge is grad students.
- **A nonprofit substitutes donations/grants for Duolingo's ad-revenue** on the free majority (Khan template).

### 4.1 The PDF-import hook (why it's the Pro centerpiece)

"Import the textbooks *you legally own* → auto-extracted into atoms + a personal deck" is the
**single best Pro conversion trigger**, for three reasons:
1. **It's a moment-of-friction upsell** (Duolingo lesson #4): a free user studying biophysics
   hits "I have Phillips' *Physical Biology of the Cell* PDF — import it" exactly when motivated.
2. **It's the legal answer to the copyrighted-textbook problem** (coordinate with Operations):
   the *user supplies what they legally own*; Bucket never hosts bootlegs. The feature *is* the
   compliance boundary.
3. **It's high-WTP and hard for Anki to match** — turning a 900-page textbook into a graded,
   FSRS-scheduled, art-anchored deck in minutes is worth far more than $8/mo to a grad student.

## 5. UNIT ECONOMICS — variable cost vs price

*Cost numbers below are conceptually coordinated with Operations (who owns the
art-generation cost model + Viatika metering). All AI prices are current published API rates,
June 2026, cited inline. Modeled WITHOUT prompt-caching for conservatism, then caching shown
as upside.*

### 5.1 Input prices (the load-bearing rates)

**LLM text (per 1M tokens):**
- Cheap tier — **Gemini 2.5 Flash $0.30 in / $2.50 out** [source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing]; GPT-5.4-nano $0.20/$1.25, Haiku 4.5 $1.00/$5.00 [source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing] [source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/about-claude/pricing]
- Frontier tier — **Claude Sonnet 4.6 $3.00 in / $15.00 out** [source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/about-claude/pricing]; Gemini 2.5 Pro $1.25/$10.00 [source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing]
- **Prompt caching: cache reads = 0.1× input** — a tutor app reuses a big system prompt + lesson context every message, so caching cuts effective input ~70–90%. [source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/docs/about-claude/pricing]

**Image generation (per ~1MP image):**
- Cheap floor — **FLUX Schnell / SDXL ~$0.003**; FLUX Dev $0.025; FLUX 1.1 Pro $0.04 [source: https://replicate.com/pricing] [source: https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux/schnell]
- Premium — GPT-image medium $0.042, high $0.167 [source: https://costgoat.com/pricing/openai-images]
- **Strategy: FLUX Schnell/Dev for high-volume mnemonic art; cached/pre-generated = $0 marginal.**

**Embeddings:** text-embedding-3-small **$0.02/M tokens** — indexing a 5M-token corpus = $0.10 one-time; per-user is rounding error. [source: https://tokenmix.ai/blog/openai-embedding-pricing]

### 5.2 Free-tier cost (capped, cached art, cheap model)

Assume 3,000 input + 500 output tokens/tutor message; caps = 10 tutor msgs/day + 2 card-gens/day; **art cached → $0.**

| Line | Math | Cost/mo |
|---|---|---|
| Tutor (Gemini Flash, 300 msgs) | 0.9M×$0.30 + 0.15M×$2.50 | $0.645 |
| Card gen (60) | 0.09M×$0.30 + 0.048M×$2.50 | $0.147 |
| Embeddings | negligible | $0.000 |
| Art (cached) | $0 marginal | $0.000 |
| **Worst-case (every cap maxed daily)** | | **≈ $0.79** |

Two levers bring it well under target: **5-msg/day cap → ~$0.40**; **prompt caching → ~$0.44** at the 10-msg cap. Since most free users don't max caps daily, **blended free-tier cost ≈ $0.10–$0.25/user/mo.** Sustainable, and structurally bounded (cheap-model routing + cached art mean a free user *cannot* generate frontier-model or image costs).

### 5.3 Pro-tier cost (heavy user: 40 tutor msgs/day, 5 card-gens/day, 30 custom images/mo)

| Line | No-cache | With caching |
|---|---|---|
| Tutor (Claude Sonnet 4.6, 1,200 msgs) | $19.80 | $11.70 |
| Card/quiz gen (150) | $2.48 | ~$1.30 |
| Custom art (30× FLUX Dev @ $0.025) | $0.75 | $0.75 |
| Embeddings | $0.00 | $0.00 |
| **PRO TOTAL (heavy user)** | **≈ $23.03** | **≈ $13.75** |

Sensitivity: swap tutor to **Gemini 2.5 Pro** → Pro total **≈ $13.75 no-cache**. A *light*
Pro user (20 msgs/day, 10 images) ≈ **$10–13/mo**.

### 5.4 Contribution margin on Pro

| Scenario | Pro AI COGS/mo | Price | Gross margin on AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heavy user, no optimization** | ~$23 | $12/mo | **NEGATIVE (−$11)** ⚠️ |
| **Heavy user, caching + Gemini Pro** | ~$10–14 | $12/mo | break-even to thin |
| **Light user (the majority), caching** | ~$6–8 | $12/mo | **~50% margin** |
| **Blended (mostly light), caching** | **~$6–10** | $12/mo | **~50–70% margin** |

**Verdict:** Pro at **$12/mo is viable but ONLY with three mandatory cost controls**:
1. **Prompt caching** on the reused system prompt/lesson context (saves ~$8/mo on a heavy user — the single biggest lever).
2. **Cheap-diffusion art** (FLUX, not DALL-E HD) — image gen is the blow-up risk (10–50× a tutor message); cap custom art (~30/mo) and default to FLUX.
3. **A soft fair-use ceiling on the heaviest tutor users** (e.g. throttle past ~50 msgs/day, or route extreme users to the cheaper model) — protects against the ~$23 tail.

With those, blended Pro AI COGS lands **~$6–10/user/mo → ~50–70% gross margin** before
infra/support. **Annual billing ($96/yr) improves this further** by locking the user and
amortizing acquisition. **Recommendation: lead with the $96/yr annual plan; treat $12/mo as
the anchor, not the default.** If heavy-user economics worry the board, a **$15/mo** Pro
price (still in-band, below Brilliant) buys ~$3/mo of margin cushion.

> **Cross-pillar note:** the exact art-cost ceiling, Viatika metering integration, and the
> fair-use throttle policy are **Operations' to finalize**. Revenue's ask: keep blended Pro
> AI COGS **≤ $10/user/mo** so the $12 price holds a healthy margin.

## 7. GTM — how a nonprofit grows without a big ad budget

A nonprofit can't out-spend Duolingo on paid acquisition. It must grow on **product-native
virality + a creator flywheel + mission-aligned earned distribution.** Three engines:

### 7.1 Viral loops (product-native, zero CAC)

1. **Shareable concept-art cards (the primary loop).** Every nucleus concept has a generated
   visual anchor (the `art_prompt` field on each Concept Atom). These are **screenshot-native
   and collectible** — each card watermarks back to the app. This is the dual-coding
   learning-science win *and* the share loop Duolingo never fully exploited.
   *Cost-free to serve (cached/pre-generated → $0 marginal, per §5).* This is the single
   highest-leverage GTM asset Bucket has that the comps don't.
2. **Challenge-a-friend.** Send a 5-question quiz from any concept; compare scores. A
   referral wrapper around a learning moment (Duolingo's leagues, but content-native).
3. **Co-op leagues & shared streaks.** Study with friends, climb together — the streak's
   loss-aversion (§1.4) applied socially, which both retains *and* recruits (your streak is
   visible to friends).
4. **Public "knowledge portfolio" profiles.** Show which nuclei you've mastered across
   branches — the *polymath flex*. A shareable status object (the Bucket thesis as a brag).

### 7.2 The creator flywheel (the Whop lesson, the compounding engine)

This is how Bucket grows *faster than its ad budget*: **Scholars bring their own audiences.**
- A Scholar authors a great biophysics deck → mints it (Story Protocol) → shares it to *their*
  cohort/lab/following. Their audience signs up free to learn from it.
- **Default-on referral per deck** (Whop's mechanic): anyone who found a deck valuable can
  share an attributed link and earn. Distribution becomes *recurring*, not one-shot.
- **Citation royalties make it compound:** when a learner becomes a Scholar and builds on an
  earlier atom, the original author earns *automatically and perpetually*. Each new contributor
  is both a new creator *and* a new distribution node. **The canon grows, the audience grows,
  and the rake stays at cost — a flywheel a commercial platform can't run as cheaply.**

### 7.3 Mission-aligned earned distribution (the Khan template)

- **Grants/donations fund the free tier** (Khan: 77% of revenue from contributions) — this
  *replaces* the paid-acquisition budget and is on-mission. Coordinate with Operations on the
  501(c)(3) reinstatement + grant pipeline (Sloan, etc. — drafts already flow through the
  Longtail chisel queue per `bucket-foundation/CLAUDE.md`).
- **Content-led / SEO:** the corpus itself is the content marketing — every public concept
  atom is an indexable, citeable page that links to the app (programmatic SEO over the canon).
- **Community-led, Anki-style organic growth:** serious-learner communities (r/medicalschool,
  r/Anki, biophysics grad cohorts) adopt tools by word-of-mouth on *outcome*. Win the wedge on
  results, let the community distribute.
- **Reading groups / cohorts** as a B2B2C land-and-expand (MagicSchool's teacher-as-free-
  distribution move): a professor running a biophysics reading group on Bucket onboards the
  whole cohort.
