# Polyglot and Polymath Evidence

Works are cited by id from `works.jsonl` in this folder. Education effects read against Kraft's benchmark: the median education RCT moves outcomes 0.10 SD, and 0.20 SD counts as large [kraft-2020-interpreting-effect-sizes]. Language-specific findings on input, output and instruction sit in `../language/sla/SYNTHESIS.md`; this file cites them only where breadth depends on them.

## How Much Does Practice Explain

Ericsson's violin study put the best students at about 10,000 solitary practice hours by age 20, double the weakest group [ericsson-1993-deliberate-practice]. The theory holds that goal-directed practice with feedback at the edge of ability builds expert performance [ericsson-2008-deliberate-practice-expert].

| Synthesis | Share of performance variance from practice |
|---|---|
| All domains [macnamara-2014-deliberate-practice-meta] | 12% overall; games 26%, music 21%, sports 18%, education 4%, professions under 1% |
| Sports [macnamara-2016-sports-practice-meta] | 18% overall, 1% among elite athletes |
| Chess and music reanalysis [hambrick-2014-deliberate-practice-enough] | about 34% and 30% |
| Violin replication [macnamara-2019-music-practice-replication] | about 26%; best violinists practiced no more than good ones |

Ericsson replied that the meta-analyses pooled activities that fail his definition [ericsson-2016-summing-up-hours]. The dispute is open: the definition and the measured hours rarely match in the primary studies. Hours to chess master range from about 3,000 to 23,000 [gobet-2007-chess-practice-starting-age], and practice is necessary and insufficient [campitelli-2008-chess-practice-necessary-not-sufficient, howard-2012-chess-starting-age]. Ability and practice interact: higher-ability chess players gain more per game [vaci-2019-chess-practice-ability]. A 10,500-twin study found no within-pair effect of music practice on music ability [mosing-2014-practice-does-not-make-perfect]. Multifactorial models now dominate [ullen-2016-gene-environment-expertise, hambrick-2016-multifactorial-expertise].

Learning curves are steep then flat. Group data fit a power law [newell-1981-power-law-practice]; individual curves fit an exponential [heathcote-2000-power-law-repealed]. Either way the first hundreds of hours in a domain buy more per hour than the last thousands, which is the economic basis of breadth.

## What Expertise Is Made Of

Chess masters recall real positions far better than novices and lose the edge on random boards [chase-1973-perception-in-chess]. Experts hold large chunks and templates in long-term memory [gobet-1996-templates-chess-memory, gobet-1998-expert-memory-chunks-templates, gobet-2001-chunking-mechanisms] and use retrieval structures that act as extended working memory [ericsson-1995-long-term-working-memory]. Physics experts sort problems by principle and novices by surface [chi-1981-physics-expert-novice]. Declarative knowledge compiles into procedures with practice [anderson-1982-acquisition-cognitive-skill], and the same account fits L2 skill [dekeyser-2007-skill-acquisition-theory-sla].

Consequence for a polymath: chunks are domain bound. Expertise in one field leaves another field at near-novice level except for shared elements [feltovich-2006-generic-expertise-studies, gobet-2016-understanding-expertise]. Guidance helps novices and hinders experts [kirschner-2006-minimal-guidance, kalyuga-2003-expertise-reversal], so a learner in field five needs novice instruction there even when expert in field one.

## Transfer

Near transfer is reliable. Far transfer from training a general faculty is near zero.

| Training | Far transfer |
|---|---|
| Chess in children [sala-2016-chess-instruction-transfer-meta] | d 0.34 overall, near 0 with active controls |
| Working memory in children [sala-2019-working-memory-training-second-order] | about 0 after bias correction |
| Cognitive training, second order [sala-2019-cognitive-training-second-order-meta] | about 0 |
| Music in children [sala-2020-music-training-meta] | about 0 with active controls |
| Bilingual executive control in adults [lehtonen-2018-bilingualism-executive-meta] | about 0 after bias correction |
| Language learning in older adults, RCT [kliesch-2022-language-learning-older-adults-rct] | no gain beyond active control |

Thorndike found the same in 1901: transfer tracks identical elements [thorndike-1901-influence-improvement-mental-function], later formalized as shared production rules [singley-1989-transfer-cognitive-skill]. Barnett and Ceci give nine dimensions of transfer distance [barnett-2002-taxonomy-far-transfer]; Perkins splits low-road from high-road transfer [perkins-1992-transfer-of-learning].

High-road transfer can be taught. Comparing two analogues induces a schema that transfers [gick-1983-schema-induction-analogical-transfer]; analogical encoding of cases tripled transfer in management students [gentner-2003-analogical-encoding]; case comparison averages d 0.50 [alfieri-2013-case-comparison-meta]. Experts retrieve analogies by structure [novick-1988-analogical-transfer-expertise], and method experts transfer scientific reasoning to new content where content experts without the method fail [schunn-1997-cross-domain-transfer-expertise]. The transferable asset is a method or a structural schema, learned with explicit comparison.

Languages are the clearest case of near transfer. Bilinguals tend to learn a third language faster [cenoz-2003-third-language-acquisition]. L3 transfer draws from the language perceived as closest [rothman-2011-typological-primacy-model] or property by property from both [westergaard-2017-linguistic-proximity-model]. Perceived similarity drives early positive transfer [ringbom-2007-cross-linguistic-similarity]. Distance from L1 predicts difficulty and outcome [chiswick-2005-linguistic-distance, schepens-2013-cross-linguistic-similarity]; FSI rates English speakers at 600 to 750 class hours for Category I languages and about 2,200 for Category IV [fsi-2018-language-difficulty].

## Generalists and Specialists

Epstein argues that breadth and late specialization win in wicked domains [epstein-2019-range]; critics say the book selects cases. The primary sources are stronger in sport than elsewhere.

- World-class adult athletes started their main sport later, did less early main-sport practice and more multi-sport practice than national-class peers [gullich-2022-multidisciplinary-practice-meta, gullich-2017-sampling-elite-sports].
- Predictors of junior and senior success run in opposite directions [barth-2020-predictors-junior-senior-success, gullich-2017-youth-soccer-pathways].
- Güllich extends the pattern to Nobel laureates, chess and music [gullich-2023-nobel-laureates-multidisciplinary]. The science data are retrospective and thin.

In science, generalist mathematicians beat specialists in fast-changing fields and lose in stable ones [teodoridis-2019-generalists-specialists-innovation]. Interdisciplinary scientists are more visible and less productive [leahey-2017-interdisciplinarity-productivity]. Distant-discipline citation predicts long-run impact [lariviere-2015-long-distance-interdisciplinarity], novel papers win late [wang-2017-bias-against-novelty], and risky innovation earns more citations and is rarer [foster-2015-tradition-innovation]. Topic switching is rising and carries an impact penalty [zeng-2019-topic-switching]. Hot streaks follow a period of exploration and then exploitation [liu-2021-exploration-exploitation-hot-streaks, liu-2018-hot-streaks]; the top paper lands at a random point in a career [sinatra-2016-random-impact-rule]. <!-- voice-ignore-line -->

Reading: breadth pays in uncertain domains and at the level of combination. Depth is still the entry fee for each combined field.

## Combination and the Burden of Knowledge

High-impact papers pair a conventional base with a small injection of atypical combinations [uzzi-2013-atypical-combinations]. Age at first invention rises and specialization narrows as knowledge grows [jones-2009-burden-of-knowledge, jones-2010-age-and-scientific-genius]. Teams now produce most high-impact work [wuchty-2007-teams-dominate]; small teams disrupt and large teams develop [wu-2019-large-teams-develop-small-disrupt]; fresh teams are more original [zeng-2021-fresh-teams-originality]. Disruptiveness has fallen since 1945 [park-2023-papers-patents-less-disruptive], a contested measure. Science of science reviews collect these regularities [fortunato-2018-science-of-science].

The individual polymath and the diverse team are two routes to the same combination. Jones predicts the team route wins as frontiers recede; AI tutors that compress the time to working depth in a second field are the lever that could reopen the individual route.

## Polymaths

Nobel laureates are about 2.85 times as likely as typical scientists to keep an arts or crafts avocation [root-bernstein-2008-arts-crafts-nobel]; avocations predicted impact in a 40-scientist longitudinal sample [root-bernstein-1995-correlations-avocations-scientific-style]. Root-Bernstein argues polymaths share thinking tools across fields [root-bernstein-2009-multiple-giftedness]. Araki defines polymathy as breadth, depth and integration [araki-2018-polymathy-framework, araki-2023-polymathic-leaders]. Burke's history of 500 polymaths finds them rarer as knowledge grew [burke-2020-the-polymath]. Creativity has a general layer and strong domain-specific layers [baer-2009-amusement-park-creativity, baer-1998-domain-specificity-creativity].

All polymath evidence is correlational. Avocations may mark curiosity or ability that also drives the main career.

## Ability

SMPY youths in the top 1 in 10,000 at age 13 reached doctorates, patents and senior posts at many times base rates [lubinski-2006-tracking-exceptional-talent, lubinski-2014-life-paths-smpy-top-graduate]. Differences within the top 1% still predict output [wai-2005-smpy-creative-accomplishment]. Math-verbal tilt predicts the domain of achievement [park-2007-ability-tilt-smpy], spatial ability adds to patents [kell-2013-spatial-ability-creativity], and age-13 profiles predict the kind of eminence [bernstein-2019-smpy-top-graduate]. Simonton models talent as multiplicative components [simonton-1999-talent-emergenic-epigenetic], finds hits scale with output [simonton-1997-creative-productivity-age], and ties creator traits to the hierarchy of sciences [simonton-2009-hierarchy-scientific-creativity]. General ability predicts work performance [schmidt-2004-general-mental-ability-work], with corrected validity revised down to about r .31 [sackett-2022-revisiting-validity]. Cognitive heritability rises with age [plomin-2016-top-ten-replicated-behavioral-genetics].

Language aptitude correlates r .49 with L2 proficiency [li-2016-aptitude-meta]. Late learners who pass as native have high aptitude [abrahamsson-2008-age-onset-aptitude-nativelike], and close scrutiny finds almost none fully nativelike [abrahamsson-2009-age-onset-nativelikeness].

## Hyperpolyglots

Erard's survey finds self-described hyperpolyglots clustering around 11 languages in active use, with heavy daily input and practice [erard-2012-babel-no-more]. Hudson proposes six or more as a threshold [hudson-2008-hyperpolyglot-threshold]. Exceptional learners combine aptitude, motivation and many hours [hyltenstam-2016-advanced-proficiency-multilingualism]. Polyglots show smaller language-network responses to their native language [jouravlev-2023-polyglot-language-network]; one post-mortem case is suggestive at best [amunts-2004-hyperpolyglot-brain]. Evidence is self-report and case study. No study measures hyperpolyglot proficiency across all claimed languages with standard tests.

## Sequencing and Interference

Interleaving beats blocking for similar categories and hurts for word lists [brunmair-2019-interleaving-meta: g 0.42 overall, 0.67 for paintings, negative for words]. Classroom math interleaving gave d 0.83 a month later [rohrer-2020-interleaved-math-classroom-rct, rohrer-2007-shuffling-math-problems]; learners misjudge it as worse [kornell-2008-interleaving-induction]. Random motor practice slows acquisition and helps retention [shea-1979-contextual-interference].

No study tests interleaving whole domains or whole languages. Interference is the counterforce: prior lists drive most forgetting [underwood-1957-interference-forgetting], retrieval suppresses related items [anderson-1994-retrieval-induced-forgetting], and speaking a new language suppresses native words [levy-2007-inhibiting-native-language]. Languages in one mind compete for resources [de-bot-2007-dynamic-systems-sla]. Two close languages started together carry the most interference risk; the L3 models predict confusion where perceived similarity is high.

## Retention and Maintenance

Spanish learned in school drops for 3 to 6 years and then holds for 25 years or more in permastore [bahrick-1984-permastore-spanish]. Algebra lasts 50 years for those who took courses beyond it [bahrick-1991-lifetime-maintenance-math]. Wider gaps between relearning sessions gave better 5-year retention [bahrick-1993-maintenance-foreign-vocabulary]. The optimal gap is 10 to 20% of the retention interval [cepeda-2008-spacing-temporal-ridgeline, cepeda-2006-distributed-practice-meta]. Retrieval beats restudy: g 0.50 [rowland-2014-testing-effect-meta], g 0.61 [adesope-2017-practice-testing-meta], rated high utility with spacing [dunlosky-2013-learning-techniques]. Forgetting makes relearning more productive [bjork-1992-new-theory-disuse, murre-2015-ebbinghaus-replication].

Attrition depends on proficiency and age at loss; high proficiency resists [schmid-2011-language-attrition, de-bot-1991-attrition-dutch-migrants]. Receptive skills last and productive skills fall first [weltens-1987-attrition-school-french]. Maintenance load per language therefore falls with proficiency, and the cheapest maintenance is spaced, retrieval-based and receptive.

## Aging

Processing speed and episodic memory decline from the late 20s [salthouse-2009-cognitive-decline-onset, hedden-2004-cognitive-aging]; vocabulary peaks after 60 [hartshorne-2015-when-does-cognitive-functioning-peak]. Adult knowledge across domains grows into middle age [ackerman-2000-domain-knowledge-adulthood] through investment of fluid ability steered by interests [ackerman-1996-ppik-theory]. Grammar learning ability holds until about 17 [hartshorne-2018-critical-period], a contested model. Learning demanding new skills improved older adults' episodic memory [park-2014-synapse-project]. Bilingual aging benefits are contested [bak-2014-bilingualism-cognitive-aging, bialystok-2012-bilingualism-mind-brain, paap-2015-bilingual-advantage-absent].

## Input and Vocabulary

Reading unassisted needs about 98% known-word coverage [hu-2000-unknown-vocabulary-density], which takes 8,000 to 9,000 word families in English [nation-2006-vocabulary-size-reading]. Extensive reading gives d 0.71 [jeon-2016-extensive-reading-meta]. Explicit instruction beats implicit [norris-2000-explicit-instruction-meta, spada-2010-instruction-meta]. Input is necessary [krashen-1982-input-hypothesis, ellis-2002-frequency-effects]; output fixes accuracy that input alone leaves [swain-1985-output-hypothesis].

## AI-Assisted Learning

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Turkish high school, GPT-4 [bastani-2024-generative-ai-harm-learning] | RCT, ~1,000 students | Base GPT: practice +48%, exam -17%. Guarded tutor: harm removed |
| Harvard physics [kestin-2024-ai-tutoring-outperforms-active-learning] | crossover RCT, 194 students | about 2x learning gains vs active learning |
| Tutor CoPilot [wang-2024-tutor-copilot] | RCT, 900 tutors | +4 pp mastery; +9 pp for weakest tutors |
| Nigeria after-school GPT-4 [de-simone-2025-nigeria-ai-tutoring] | RCT | 0.31 SD in English |
| Coding students [lehmann-2024-ai-meets-classroom] | RCT | helps as complement, harms as substitute |
| Essay writing [fan-2024-metacognitive-laziness-chatgpt] | RCT, 117 | higher essays, no knowledge gain |
| EEG essay study [kosmyna-2025-your-brain-on-chatgpt] | experiment, 54 | lower engagement and recall; preprint |

Step-based tutoring systems nearly match human tutors, d 0.76 vs 0.79 [vanlehn-2011-tutoring-effectiveness]; ITS median 0.66 [kulik-2016-its-meta]. Bloom's 2 sigma [bloom-1984-two-sigma] has not replicated at that size. AI that answers for the learner harms; AI that forces retrieval and gives hints helps.

## Knowledge Graphs and Prerequisites

Learning hierarchies [gagne-1962-acquisition-knowledge-hierarchy] and knowledge space theory [doignon-1985-knowledge-spaces] model prerequisite structure; ALEKS runs it at scale [falmagne-2006-aleks-knowledge-space]. Knowledge tracing estimates per-skill mastery [corbett-1994-knowledge-tracing, piech-2015-deep-knowledge-tracing]. Prerequisites can be inferred from links and course text [liang-2015-concept-prerequisite-wikipedia, pan-2017-prerequisite-moocs]. Concept maps improve retention and transfer [nesbit-2006-concept-maps-meta, novak-2008-concept-maps]. Spaced repetition can be fit per learner [settles-2016-trainable-spaced-repetition, tabibian-2019-optimal-spaced-repetition]. Cognitive load theory explains why prerequisites matter [sweller-1988-cognitive-load].

## Open Disputes

1. Deliberate practice share of variance: Ericsson vs Macnamara and Hambrick. Unresolved on definitions.
2. Early sampling in science: Güllich's sport data are strong; the science extension rests on retrospective small samples.
3. Far transfer: Sala and Gobet find near zero; proponents point to subgroups. The second-order meta-analyses favor null.
4. Bilingual cognitive advantage: Bialystok vs Paap and Lehtonen. Corrected estimates favor null.
5. Critical period: Hartshorne's model assumptions are disputed.
6. Disruption decline: Park 2023 metric contested.
7. AI tutoring: effects range from -17% to +2x depending on guardrails; no long-term retention data beyond one term.
8. Hyperpolyglot claims lack standardized multi-language testing.
