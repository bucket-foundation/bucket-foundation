# Multilingual Learning Synthesis

Bead bkt-yx1wr. Evidence base for Polingual, a language app for people who learn many languages. Work ids point to `works.jsonl` in this folder. Verification status sits on each record; a claim that rests on an `unconfirmed` record is marked with a dagger (†) and needs a source check before it ships in product copy.

## What the Evidence Says

### Intercomprehension

1. Readers of one Romance language can read a second one from the first day. EuroCom names seven sieves that extract what transfers: international words, pan-Romance words, sound correspondences, spelling, syntax, function words and affixes (`klein-2000-eurocomrom-sieben-siebe-romanische`†, `mccann-2003-eurocomrom-seven-sieves-read`). EuRom5 trains reading in five Romance languages at once (`bonvino-2011-eurom5-leggere-e-capire`). Germanic and Slavic adaptations exist (`hufeisen-2007-eurocomgerm-sieben-siebe-germanische`†, `zybatow-2003-eurocomslav-eurocomdidact`†).
2. Mutual intelligibility is measurable and tracks lexical and phonetic distance. In Scandinavian, phonetic distance predicts spoken intelligibility better than lexical distance (`gooskens-2007-contribution-linguistic-factors-intelligibility`). A web test across 16 Germanic, Romance and Slavic languages found intelligibility follows lexical distance and exposure, with asymmetric pairs (`gooskens-2018-mutual-intelligibility-between-closely`, `golubovic-2015-mutual-intelligibility-between-west`). Distance tables exist for the three families (`heeringa-2013-lexical-orthographic-distances-between`).
3. A known L2 helps decode a related unknown language. Dutch speakers who know German recognize more Danish cognates (`swarte-2015-does-german-help-speakers`). Cognate guessing in an unknown language rises into adulthood and holds into old age, driven by L2 knowledge and fluid intelligence (`vanhove-2015-lifespan-development-cognate-guessing`). Readers learn a single correspondence rule from brief exposure and apply it to new words (`vanhove-2016-early-learning-interlingual-correspondence`†). Guessing works by abduction over correspondences (`berthele-2011-abduction-receptive-multilingualism-evidence`†).
4. Receptive multilingualism, or lingua receptiva, works in live talk: each person speaks their own language and understands the other's (`ten-thije-2007-receptive-multilingualism-linguistic-analyses`, `rehbein-2012-lingua-receptiva-lara-remarks`). Online platforms such as Galanet build this through collaborative tasks (`degache-2003-presentation-du-projet-galanet`†, `melo-pfeifer-2018-multilingual-interaction-chat-rooms`†). Learners slip into English as a bridge when Romance resources run out (`melo-pfeifer-2014-intercomprehension-between-romance-languages`†).

### Third and Fourth Language Acquisition

5. Bilinguals tend to learn a third language better than monolinguals learn a second. Cenoz's reviews find most studies positive, with the benefit strongest when the learner reads and writes both prior languages (`cenoz-2003-additive-effect-bilingualism-third`, `cenoz-2013-influence-bilingualism-third-language`). Immigrant and heritage bilinguals in German schools outperform monolingual peers in English after SES control, and minority-language literacy predicts the gain (`maluch-2015-effect-speaking-minority-language`†, `hopp-2019-bilingual-advantages-early-foreign`†, `lechner-2014-double-threshold-bi-multilingual`†).
6. Multilinguals learn new vocabulary faster. An integrative review finds direct linguistic routes (more cognates, more phonological categories) and indirect cognitive routes (phonological memory, control) (`hirosh-2018-direct-indirect-effects-multilingualism`†). Bilinguals learn novel words better even with unfamiliar phonology (`kaushanskaya-2009-bilingual-advantage-novel-word`†). Polyglots show higher phonological short-term memory (`papagno-1995-verbal-short-term-memory`†). The benefit scales with how similar the new words are to known ones (`bartolotti-2017-bilinguals-existing-languages-benefit`).
7. Cross-linguistic influence in L3 comes from both prior languages. Four models compete:
   - L2 status factor: the L2 dominates early L3 syntax because both are learned late and stored alike (`bardel-2007-role-second-language-third`†, `falk-2011-object-pronouns-german-l3`, `bardel-2017-l2-status-factor-hypothesis`†).
   - Typological primacy: the parser picks the closer language wholesale, with lexical similarity as the first cue (`rothman-2011-l3-syntactic-transfer-selectivity`†, `rothman-2015-linguistic-cognitive-motivations-typological`†).
   - Linguistic proximity and scalpel models: transfer runs property by property from either language and can harm as well as help (`westergaard-2017-crosslinguistic-influence-acquisition-third`†, `slabakova-2017-scalpel-model-third-language`).
   - Cumulative enhancement: prior languages only help or stay neutral (`flynn-2004-cumulative-enhancement-model-language`†). Later work rejects the "only help" claim.
   A systematic review of 71 studies found typological proximity predicts the transfer source in most, and both L1 and L2 effects appear (`puig-mayenco-2020-systematic-review-transfer-studies`†). Lexical transfer favors the closer language and the more recent one (`cenoz-2001-effect-linguistic-distance-l2`, `ringbom-2007-cross-linguistic-similarity-foreign`†). Early L3 speech borrows function words from the L2 (`williams-1998-language-switches-l3-production`†, `hammarberg-2001-roles-l1-l2-l3`†).
8. Jessner and Herdina model a multilingual as one dynamic system. Each language needs maintenance effort, and a multilingualism factor (metalinguistic awareness, learning skill) grows with each language (`herdina-2002-dynamic-model-multilingualism-perspectives`†, `jessner-2008-teaching-third-languages-findings`†).

### Heritage Speakers

9. Heritage speakers have strong oral comprehension and pronunciation and weaker literacy and inflectional morphology. Their grammars are systematic (`polinsky-2018-heritage-languages-their-speakers`†, `montrul-2016-acquisition-heritage-languages`†, `polinsky-2007-heritage-languages-wild-classroom`†). A national survey of 1,700 heritage learners found strong listening, weak reading and writing, and cultural motives (`carreira-2011-results-national-heritage-language`†). Heritage learners beat L2 learners on oral and implicit tasks and trail them on written, explicit ones (`montrul-2008-gender-agreement-adult-second`†, `bowles-2011-measuring-implicit-explicit-linguistic`†). Explicit instruction with feedback helps them (`montrul-2010-grammar-instruction-beneficial-heritage`†). Whether to call their grammar "incomplete" is disputed (`kupisch-2018-terminology-matters-why-difference`†).
10. Early exposure leaves traces after loss. Adoptees who lost Korean relearn its consonants faster than controls (`choi-2017-early-phonology-revealed-international`†), and lost Chinese tone leaves neural traces (`pierce-2014-mapping-unconscious-maintenance-lost`†).

### Cognates and the Bilingual Lexicon

11. The bilingual lexicon is integrated and access is language-nonselective (BIA+, `dijkstra-2002-architecture-bilingual-word-recognition`†; Multilink, `dijkstra-2019-multilink-computational-model-bilingual`†). Cognate facilitation grows with form overlap, and identical cognates get an extra gain (`dijkstra-2010-cross-language-similarity-task`†). Cognates shared by three languages are recognized faster than those shared by two (`lemhofer-2004-three-languages-one-echo`†). L3 cognate effects need L3 proficiency (`van-hell-2002-foreign-language-knowledge-can`†).
12. Cognates are learned faster and forgotten less than noncognates; concreteness and frequency add to that (`de-groot-2000-hard-learn-easy-forget`†, `lotto-1998-effects-learning-method-word`†). The same holds for loanword cognates in distant pairs (`rogers-2015-do-cognacy-characteristics-loanwords`†) and for incidental learning from TV (`peters-2018-incidental-vocabulary-acquisition-through`†). Learners vary in whether they notice cognates, and that awareness is teachable (`otwinowska-2015-cognate-vocabulary-language-acquisition`†). New words attach to similar known words in any language, the parasitic model (`ecke-2015-parasitic-vocabulary-acquisition-cross`†).

### Attrition and Maintenance

13. School Spanish vocabulary drops for 3 to 6 years, then the remainder holds for 50 years (`bahrick-1984-semantic-memory-content-permastore`†). Relearning spaced 56 days apart gave the best five-year retention (`bahrick-1993-maintenance-foreign-language-vocabulary`†). Proficiency at the end of study predicts retention more than any other variable (`bardovi-harlig-2010-variables-second-language-attrition`†). Adult L1 attrition is limited (`schmid-2011-language-attrition`†); children who lose input early can lose a language almost fully (`hyltenstam-2009-dominant-language-replacement-case`†, `pallier-2003-brain-imaging-language-plasticity`†).
14. Learning a new language moves the old ones. L1 speech drifts toward L2 phonetics within weeks of class (`chang-2012-rapid-multifaceted-effects-second`†), and an L3 shifts a late L2's phonology more than the L1 (`cabrelli-amaro-2017-testing-phonological-permeability-hypothesis`†).

### Interference and Mixing

15. Related languages compete during production. Trilinguals show inhibition costs when switching (`philipp-2007-inhibitory-processes-language-switching`†), and those with weaker executive control produce more cross-language intrusions (`festman-2010-individual-differences-control-language`†). Bilinguals suppress L1 interference better when learning new words (`kaushanskaya-2009-bilingualism-reduces-native-language`†). Learning semantically related words together slows learning (`tinkham-1993-effect-semantic-clustering-learning`†, `erten-2008-effects-vocabulary-acquisition-presenting`†), though spacing reduces the cost (`nakata-2019-effects-massing-spacing-learning`†).

### Simultaneous Versus Sequential

16. No controlled adult study compares learning two new languages at once against one after the other. The evidence is indirect: interference from similar sets (finding 15), EuRom5 teaching four or five languages together for reading only, and child trilingual case studies (`wang-2008-growing-up-three-languages`, `barnes-2006-early-trilingualism-focus-questions`†). Learners transfer more from the language they learned most recently (`cenoz-2001-effect-linguistic-distance-l2`).

### Hyperpolyglots

17. Erard surveyed hyperpolyglots and placed the threshold near 11 languages (`erard-2012-babel-no-more-search`†). Hudson coined the term. Accomplished polyglots show higher phonological working memory (`biedron-2012-working-memory-short-term`†) and a smaller, more efficient response of the language network (`jouravlev-2021-small-efficient-language-network`†). Lomb Kató credited reading novels in the target language with a dictionary, plus motivation (`lomb-2008-polyglot-i-learn-languages`†). Arguelles promotes shadowing and copying by hand (`arguelles-2016-foreign-language-polyglot-shadowing`†). All of this is case evidence.

## Intercomprehension With Comprehensible Input

Comprehensible input needs about 95 to 98% known-word coverage (`hu-2000-unknown-vocabulary-density-reading`†). For a learner who knows Spanish, an Italian or Portuguese text starts with a large share of words already readable through cognates and correspondence rules. Intercomprehension raises effective coverage on day one, and input then does the slow work.

- Count a target word as "known" for coverage when it is a high-confidence cognate of a word the learner knows in any language, after applying the correspondence rules in `SIEVES.md`. Use that adjusted coverage to pick graded texts.
- Teach correspondence rules as explicit cards, one at a time, attached to the first text where the rule fires (`vanhove-2016-early-learning-interlingual-correspondence`†).
- Mark false friends inside texts at first sight. Cognate awareness varies by learner and is teachable (`otwinowska-2015-cognate-vocabulary-language-acquisition`†).
- Start receptive. EuroCom and EuRom5 build reading first; speaking in a close language adds production interference (finding 15). Let reading run ahead of speaking by design.
- Use listening only after reading coverage is high: phonetic distance hurts spoken intelligibility more than written (`gooskens-2007-contribution-linguistic-factors-intelligibility`).

## Design Implications for a Polyglot App

### Language Sequencing

- Recommend the next language by its distance to the learner's whole repertoire, using the closest known language as the bridge. Typological proximity predicts which language transfers (finding 7).
- Warn on near pairs. Spanish then Portuguese gives fast reading and heavy production intrusions. Offer a "reading first" track for near pairs and a full track for distant ones.
- Offer a parallel reading track across a family, in the EuRom5 pattern, and keep production study to one new language at a time until there is direct evidence on simultaneous production learning (finding 16).
- Prefer a language the learner will use. Motivation for languages beyond English drops (`dornyei-2017-motivational-foundation-learning-languages`†); perceived positive interaction between languages raises it (`thompson-2015-multilingualism-perceived-positive-language`†). Show the learner what they already understand.

### Cognate-Based Coverage

- Store each word with its cognate links to all of the learner's languages and a form-overlap score. Order new vocabulary so cognates come first for fast coverage gains, and schedule noncognates with more reviews since they are forgotten faster (`de-groot-2000-hard-learn-easy-forget`†).
- Credit triple cognates more than double ones (`lemhofer-2004-three-languages-one-echo`†).
- Polingual's photon index already has etymology and phonetic axes. Use them to compute cognate confidence and to render the sieve rule that links two forms.

### Managing Interference

- Space related languages apart in the review queue, and avoid same-session drills of near-identical items across languages, by analogy to semantic-set interference (finding 15).
- Tag each review with the language that intruded when the learner errs; feed intrusions back as contrast cards.
- Keep old languages alive with spaced maintenance. A sparse schedule at long intervals holds vocabulary (`bahrick-1993-maintenance-foreign-language-vocabulary`†). Track a maintenance budget per language, in the spirit of the Dynamic Model (`herdina-2002-dynamic-model-multilingualism-perspectives`†).

### Heritage Learners

- Ask on onboarding whether the learner grew up hearing a language. Place heritage learners by separate listening, speaking, reading and writing scores; a single level misplaces them (finding 9).
- Give heritage learners literacy and explicit morphology with feedback, and skip the basic oral work (`montrul-2010-grammar-instruction-beneficial-heritage`†).
- Treat a heritage language as a strong bridge for its relatives, and build literacy in it first: literacy in the minority language predicts the L3 gain (`lechner-2014-double-threshold-bi-multilingual`†, `hopp-2019-bilingual-advantages-early-foreign`†).
- Learners who lost a childhood language may relearn its sounds faster (`choi-2017-early-phonology-revealed-international`†). Offer a "reactivation" path.

## Open Disputes

- Which L3 transfer model is right: L2 status, typological primacy, property-by-property, or cumulative enhancement. The systematic review favors proximity and leaves room for L2 effects (finding 7).
- The bilingual executive advantage: Bialystok defends it (`bialystok-2017-bilingual-adaptation-minds-accommodate`†); meta-analyses with bias correction find none (`lehtonen-2018-bilingualism-associated-enhanced-executive`†, `paap-2013-there-no-coherent-evidence`†). The L3 learning advantage stands apart from this dispute and rests on linguistic transfer and literacy.
- Whether heritage grammars are incomplete or different (`kupisch-2018-terminology-matters-why-difference`†).
- Simultaneous versus sequential learning of related languages: no direct adult trial.
- Hyperpolyglot data comes from self-report and anecdotes (`erard-2012-babel-no-more-search`†, `hyltenstam-2016-polyglot-initial-characterization-basis`†).
- Intercomprehension evidence is strong for reading and weak for long-term productive outcomes.
