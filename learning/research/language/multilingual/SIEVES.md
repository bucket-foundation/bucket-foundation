# EuroCom Sieves and Correspondence Tables

Source method: Klein and Stegmann 2000 (`klein-2000-eurocomrom-die-sieben-siebe`), McCann, Klein and Stegmann 2003, Hufeisen and Marx 2007 for Germanic, Zybatow 2003 for Slavic. Tables below are reusable data for cognate mapping. Each row is a regular tendency with exceptions; treat them as candidate rules for a matcher and score matches, never as hard rewrites.

## The Seven Sieves

| # | Sieve | What the reader extracts | App use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | International vocabulary | Words shared across Europe from Latin, Greek, French and English: *nation, telefon, problem* | Seed set of free words for every new language |
| 2 | Pan-Romance vocabulary | Core words inherited from Latin across the family, about 400 items in the EuroCom base list | Coverage gain before any study |
| 3 | Sound correspondences | Regular sound changes from Latin into each language | Rule tables below |
| 4 | Spelling and pronunciation | How each orthography maps to sound: Portuguese *nh*, Spanish *ñ*, Italian *gn*, French *gn* all write /ɲ/ | Grapheme maps below |
| 5 | Pan-Romance syntactic structures | Nine core sentence types shared across the family | Parse templates |
| 6 | Morphosyntactic elements | Function words and inflection endings: articles, plural markers, verb endings | Paradigm cards |
| 7 | Prefixes and suffixes | Shared Latin affixes: *-tion/-ción/-zione/-ção*, *-mente/-ment* | Derivation rules |

EuroCom adds the "profiling" step, a list of words that look alike and differ in meaning (false friends), and the "optimized deduction" step, where the reader builds hypotheses and checks them in context (Meissner 2004 calls this the didactic monitor).

## Data Shape for the App

Each rule can be stored as `{family, source_pattern, target_lang, target_pattern, position, confidence, examples[]}`. A cognate detector applies rules to a known word, generates candidate forms in the target, and scores against the target lexicon with edit distance. Vanhove 2016 (`vanhove-2016-the-early-learning-of-interlingual-correspondence`) shows readers learn a single correspondence rule after short exposure and apply it to new words, so rules can be taught one at a time as explicit cards.
