# Second Language Acquisition Evidence for Polingual

Works are cited by id from `works.jsonl` in this folder. Effect sizes are Cohen's d or Hedges' g unless marked r. For L2 research, Plonsky and Oswald set between-group benchmarks at d 0.40 small, 0.70 medium and 1.00 large [plonsky-2014-effect-sizes]; read every figure below against those.

## Input

Krashen holds that comprehensible input at a level just above current competence drives acquisition and that taught rules only monitor output [krashen-1982-principles, krashen-1985-input-hypothesis]. Critics found the constructs untestable as stated [gregg-1984-krashen-monitor, mclaughlin-1987-theories] and argued that some grammar needs evidence that input alone does not supply [white-1987-against-comprehensible-input, trahey-1993-input-flood].

The strongest support for input comes from comprehension-only programs. Canadian children who only listened and read for three years matched children in regular ESL classes [lightbown-1992-comprehension-only]; after six years they matched on comprehension and lagged on written accuracy [lightbown-2002-comprehension-6yr]. Delaying speaking for four weeks helped adult Russian learners later [postovsky-1974-listening]. Comprehension-based and production-based grammar instruction tie on production tests, and comprehension-based instruction wins on receptive tests [shintani-2013-input-output-meta]. Processing instruction, which forces learners to map form to meaning in input, improves both comprehension and production [vanpatten-1993-processing-instruction, vanpatten-2004-processing].

Input is necessary. Sufficiency is unproven for productive accuracy.

## Output and Interaction

Immersion students with years of input understood French well and produced it with persistent errors, which led Swain to propose that output forces learners to notice gaps [swain-1985-communicative-competence, swain-1995-output-noticing, swain-2000-collaborative-dialogue]. Long's interaction hypothesis places negotiation for meaning at the joint of input, attention and output [long-1983-native-speaker-nonnative, long-1996-linguistic-environment].

| Synthesis | Effect |
|---|---|
| Interaction studies [mackey-2007-interaction-meta] | d 0.75 immediate, 1.02 delayed |
| Task-based interaction [keck-2006-interaction-meta] | d 0.92 |
| Task-based programs [bryfonski-2019-tblt-meta] | d 0.93 |
| Oral corrective feedback [lyster-2010-oral-feedback-meta] | d 0.74, prompts beat recasts |
| Corrective feedback [li-2010-corrective-feedback-meta] | d 0.61, durable |
| Feedback on grammar [russell-2006-cf-meta] | d 1.16 |

Output tasks also beat input tasks for incidental vocabulary [huang-2012-output-vocab-meta, hulstijn-2001-involvement-test, laufer-2001-involvement]. Repeated speaking with constant content builds fluency that lasts [dejong-2011-fluency].

## Noticing and Attention

Schmidt argued that input turns into intake only when noticed, drawing on his own Portuguese diary [schmidt-1990-consciousness, schmidt-1986-portuguese]. Tomlin and Villa split attention into parts and placed detection below awareness [tomlin-1994-attention]; Truscott found little support for noticing in grammar [truscott-1998-noticing]. Learners who reported awareness of a form outperformed those who did not [leow-2000-awareness]. Usage-based accounts explain why adults miss frequent, low-salience morphemes: cue competition and blocking from L1 [ellis-2006-selective-attention].

Making forms salient in text has a small effect. Typographic enhancement gave d 0.22 on grammar and cost d 0.26 on comprehension [lee-2008-enhancement-meta]. Eye tracking showed enhanced forms drew fixations without measurable learning [winke-2013-enhancement-eye]. Effects depend on form and learner readiness [han-2008-enhancement-review, sharwood-1993-input-enhancement]. Input flood taught a new adverb position and failed to remove an L1-transferred error [trahey-1993-input-flood].

## Explicit and Implicit Instruction

| Meta-analysis | Explicit | Implicit |
|---|---|---|
| Norris and Ortega [norris-2000-meta] | d 1.13 | d 0.54 |
| Spada and Tomita [spada-2010-meta] | d 0.88 simple, 1.12 complex | d 0.68, 0.62 |
| Goo and colleagues [goo-2015-explicit-implicit-meta] | g 1.29 | g 0.77 |
| Kang, Sok and Han [kang-2019-instruction-meta] | large immediate | implicit held better at delay |

Explicit instruction wins on the tests these studies use, and those tests favour explicit knowledge [doughty-2003-instructed-sla]. Timed and untimed tests load on separate factors [ellis-2005-measuring-implicit, ellis-2009-implicit-explicit-book]. The interface question stays open: DeKeyser and Suzuki find a path from explicit to automatized knowledge [dekeyser-1997-automatizing, dekeyser-2007-practice, suzuki-2017-practice]; Paradis holds that no conversion occurs [paradis-2009-declarative]; Ellis proposes that explicit knowledge primes implicit learning by directing attention [ellis-2005-explicit-implicit-interface]. Ullman's model has late L2 grammar start in declarative memory and shift to procedural memory with exposure [ullman-2001-dp-model].

## Usage-Based Learning

Learners tune to input frequency at every level, from sounds to constructions [ellis-2002-frequency]. Naturalistic learners first acquire the most frequent and prototypical verb in each construction [ellis-2009-construction]. Grammar emerges from item-based chunks [bybee-2008-usage-based, tomasello-2003-constructing]. Speakers store multiword strings whole [wray-2002-formulaic], and teaching learners to notice chunks raised rated oral proficiency [boers-2006-chunks].

## Lexical Coverage and Frequency

Coverage is the share of running words a reader knows.

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 98% coverage gives adequate unassisted reading | hu-2000-unknown-vocab |
| 95% gives minimal and 98% optimal comprehension | laufer-2010-coverage, laufer-1989-coverage |
| Comprehension rises linearly with coverage; 98% gives about 70% comprehension | schmitt-2011-coverage |
| Reading needs 8,000 to 9,000 families; listening 6,000 to 7,000 | nation-2006-vocab-size |
| Listening holds up at 90 to 95% coverage | vanzeeland-2013-listening |
| Movies: 3,000 families give 95%, 6,000 give 98% | webb-2009-tv-coverage |
| TV: 3,000 families give 95%, 7,000 give 98% | webb-2009-tv |
| The top 2,000 families cover about 80% of text | nation-1997-vocab-size |

Frequency lists put those families in order. Subtitle frequencies predict word processing better than written corpora [brysbaert-2009-subtlex, vanheuven-2014-subtlex-uk]. Core lists are stable across corpora [brezina-2015-ngsl, west-1953-gsl], and per-language lists exist [davies-2006-spanish-frequency]. Academic text has its own layer [coxhead-2000-awl]. Vocabulary size tracks CEFR level, about 3,250 lemmas for B2 [milton-2009-vocab].

## Vocabulary: Incidental and Intentional

Incidental learning from reading is real and slow. Readers of one novel learned 76% of invented words [saragi-1978-clockwork-orange], while a later replication found about 5 words [horst-1998-clockwork-orange]. From graded readers, few words met fewer than 8 times survived three months [waring-2003-graded-readers]. Encounter count correlates r .34 with learning [uchihara-2019-repetition-meta]; ten encounters give large gains [webb-2007-repetitions], and six beat two or four [rott-1999-exposures]. A month of reading touched 65% of targets in some aspect [pigada-2006-er-vocab].

Intentional activities give larger and faster gains [webb-2020-incidental-meta, schmitt-2008-review]. Nation's four strands give equal time to meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused study and fluency [nation-2007-four-strands, nation-2001-learning-vocab].

Reading while listening beats listening alone for word learning [brown-2008-reading-while-listening, webb-2015-er-listening] and raises reading rate [chang-2015-rwl]. Reading, listening and viewing gave similar gains in one direct comparison [feng-2020-er-listening]. Captions help listening (g 0.99) and vocabulary (g 0.87) [montero-2013-captions-meta, peters-2019-captions-tv]. One TV documentary yields incidental gains tied to frequency [peters-2018-tv-vocab].

## Extensive Reading and Listening

| Meta-analysis | Group contrast | Pre-post |
|---|---|---|
| Jeon and Day [jeon-2016-er-meta] | d 0.57 | d 0.79 |
| Nakanishi [nakanishi-2015-er-meta] | d 0.46 | d 0.71 |

Adults and longer programs gain more in both. Extensive reading also builds vocabulary [liu-2018-er-vocab-meta]. Field studies support it: Fijian book floods [elley-1983-book-flood], Japanese university classes [mason-1997-er-japan, robb-1989-er], and reading rate gains tied to words read [beglar-2012-pleasure-reading]. Day and Bamford set the method: easy, plentiful, self-selected text [day-1998-extensive-reading, nation-2020-er-book, krashen-2004-power-of-reading, krashen-1989-reading-vocab].

Extensive listening has fewer studies and points the same way [chang-2014-er-audio, renandya-2011-extensive-listening, vandergrift-2007-listening-review, rodgers-2013-tv-comprehension].

## Narrow Input

Texts on one topic or by one author recycle mid-frequency words [gardner-2008-narrow-reading, krashen-2004-narrow-reading]. Related TV episodes need fewer new words [rodgers-2011-narrow-viewing]. Narrow reading gave more vocabulary gain than unrelated texts [kang-2015-narrow-reading].

## TPRS and TPR

Acting out commands aids listening and recall [asher-1969-tpr]. TPRS turns stories into circled questions [ray-1997-tprs]. Comparisons favour or tie TPRS with weak designs and small samples [watson-2009-tprs, lichtman-2015-tprs-review]. Evidence is thin.

## Spacing

Spaced practice beats massed practice, and the best gap grows with the retention interval [cepeda-2006-spacing-meta]. For L2, spacing gives d 0.58 [kim-2022-spacing-meta]. A 56-day gap gave the best five-year retention of foreign words [bahrick-1993-spacing-foreign]. Expanding and equal schedules perform about the same [nakata-2015-spacing-flashcards]. Grammar spacing is less consistent [rogers-2017-spacing-grammar]; for morphology a 3.3-day gap beat 7 days [suzuki-2017-spacing-grammar]. Half-life regression at Duolingo raised daily engagement 12% [settles-2016-hlr]. Most flashcard software lacks these features [nakata-2011-flashcard-software, pimsleur-1967-memory-schedule].

## Shadowing and Pronunciation

Shadowing improved listening, most for low-proficiency learners [hamada-2016-shadowing, kadota-2019-shadowing], and mobile shadowing improved comprehensibility in eight weeks [foote-2017-shadowing].

High-variability phonetic training, many talkers and many contexts, generalizes to new voices where single-talker training does not [logan-1991-hvpt, lively-1993-hvpt-generalization]. Gains transfer to production [bradlow-1997-hvpt-production, thomson-2011-hvpt]. HVPT raises identification about 17 points [thomson-2018-hvpt-review]; perception training transfers to production at g 0.54 [sakai-2018-perception-meta]. Pronunciation instruction gives d 0.89, smaller in spontaneous speech [lee-2015-pronunciation-meta, saito-2019-comprehensibility]. Intelligibility is the target [derwing-2005-pronunciation]. Category formation depends on how L2 sounds map to L1 sounds [flege-1995-slm, best-2007-palm].

## Age

Grammar scores fall with age of arrival [johnson-1989-critical-period, dekeyser-2000-critical-period, dekeyser-2010-age-cross]. A sample of 669,498 places the drop in grammar learning ability near 17 [hartshorne-2018-critical-period]. Others find a linear decline with no cutoff [birdsong-2001-age, flege-1999-age-accent]. Under close scrutiny almost no late learner is nativelike [abrahamsson-2009-nativelikeness]. Adults learn faster at first [snow-1978-critical-period, munoz-2006-age-instruction], and input quantity predicts outcomes better than starting age in classroom settings [munoz-2011-input-hours, munoz-2011-age-review]. Verbal aptitude moderates late attainment [dekeyser-2000-critical-period, li-2016-aptitude-meta].

## Hours to Proficiency

FSI estimates about 600 to 750 class hours for languages close to English and about 2,200 for Arabic, Chinese, Japanese and Korean [fsi-language-difficulty, jackson-2001-fsi]. Linguistic distance and morphological complexity predict difficulty [chiswick-2005-distance, schepens-2013-distance]. Dreaming Spanish learners log about 1,500 hours of input for its level 7 [dreaming-spanish-roadmap]; those logs are self-reported with no control group.

## Motivation

The ideal L2 self predicts intended effort (r .61) and achievement weakly (r .20) [alhoorie-2018-l2mss-meta, dornyei-2005-psychology, dornyei-2009-l2-self, papi-2010-l2mss]. Motivation correlates r .37 with grades [masgoret-2003-meta, gardner-1972-attitudes]. Anxiety correlates r -.36 with achievement [teimouri-2019-anxiety-meta, horwitz-1986-anxiety]. Teacher behaviour and climate rank first among motivational strategies [dornyei-1998-commandments].

## Design Implications for an Input-First Polyglot App

1. Gate content by coverage. Estimate the learner's known families and serve text at 95 to 98% coverage, audio at 90 to 95% [hu-2000-unknown-vocab, schmitt-2011-coverage, vanzeeland-2013-listening].
2. Order new vocabulary by subtitle frequency per language, then by the user's chosen topics [brysbaert-2009-subtlex, nation-2006-vocab-size].
3. Default to narrow input: series, single authors, one topic per week, so mid-frequency words recur six to ten times [rodgers-2011-narrow-viewing, webb-2007-repetitions, uchihara-2019-repetition-meta].
4. Pair text with audio, and offer captions in the target language [brown-2008-reading-while-listening, montero-2013-captions-meta].
5. Add a short intentional layer: spaced review of words met in input, scheduled by a trained recall model [webb-2020-incidental-meta, kim-2022-spacing-meta, settles-2016-hlr].
6. Add brief explicit notes on forms that input misses, triggered by the content the learner is reading [spada-2010-meta, ellis-2006-selective-attention].
7. Run HVPT drills for hard contrasts in each L1 and L2 pair before heavy listening [thomson-2018-hvpt-review, sakai-2018-perception-meta].
8. Add an output track once input reaches a threshold: shadowing, retelling with feedback, and tutor or AI conversation with prompts [foote-2017-shadowing, lyster-2010-oral-feedback-meta, mackey-2007-interaction-meta].
9. Log hours per language and show them against FSI and community benchmarks, with distance from the learner's known languages factored in [jackson-2001-fsi, schepens-2013-distance].
10. Frame progress around the learner's ideal self per language and keep anxiety low [alhoorie-2018-l2mss-meta, teimouri-2019-anxiety-meta].
11. Measure outcomes with timed tests and speaking samples, since app studies to date rely on untimed receptive tests [ellis-2005-measuring-implicit, loewen-2020-babbel].

## Open Disputes

- Whether input alone reaches productive accuracy [krashen-1985-input-hypothesis, swain-1985-communicative-competence, lightbown-2002-comprehension-6yr].
- Whether explicit knowledge becomes implicit [dekeyser-2007-practice, paradis-2009-declarative, ellis-2005-explicit-implicit-interface].
- Whether noticing requires awareness [schmidt-1990-consciousness, tomlin-1994-attention, truscott-1998-noticing].
- Whether the explicit advantage in meta-analyses reflects test bias [doughty-2003-instructed-sla, kang-2019-instruction-meta].
- The coverage threshold for listening, and whether coverage has a threshold or a linear slope [schmitt-2011-coverage, vanzeeland-2013-listening].
- The shape of the age curve: a critical period near 17 or a linear decline [hartshorne-2018-critical-period, birdsong-2001-age].
- Whether early speaking harms later accuracy, as silent-period proponents claim [postovsky-1974-listening, dreaming-spanish-roadmap].
- App efficacy, since most studies are commissioned, uncontrolled or both [vesselinov-2012-duolingo, jiang-2021-duolingo-reading, burston-2015-mall-review].
- TPRS evidence quality [lichtman-2015-tprs-review].
- Optimal spacing schedules for grammar [rogers-2017-spacing-grammar, suzuki-2017-spacing-grammar].
