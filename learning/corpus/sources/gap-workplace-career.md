# Workplace and Career Learning Gap

98 works in `gap-workplace-career.jsonl`: 96 DOI-confirmed against Crossref or OpenAlex, 2 books confirmed by catalogue URL. 35 carry an effect copied from a fetched abstract. The other 63 hold `effect: null` with the reason in `notes`.

## Strong Support

- Debriefs raise performance: d = .67, 46 samples, N = 2,136 (Tannenbaum 2012).
- Error management training helps transfer: d = 0.44 overall, 0.80 for adaptive transfer, 24 studies (Keith 2008).
- Feedback helps on average, d = .41, and over one third of interventions lower performance (Kluger 1996). Audit and feedback lifts clinician compliance by a median 4.3% (Ivers 2012).
- Formal training: d near 0.62 on learning, behaviour and results (Arthur 2003).
- Job search interventions: odds of employment 2.67 times higher, 47 evaluations (Liu 2014).
- Advisor quality causes PhD outcomes: one SD of faculty quality adds 13 points to top-journal publication (Waldinger 2010). Losing a star coauthor cuts output 5% to 8% (Azoulay 2010).
- Years of experience do not maintain skill: 52% of 62 evaluations show physician performance falling with years in practice (Choudhry 2005).
- AI raises output for novices: 15% more issues resolved per hour across 5,172 agents (Brynjolfsson 2025); 26% more tasks across 4,867 developers, SE 10.3% (Cui 2026).
- Wrong AI advice drags experts down: experienced radiologists fell from 82.3% to 45.5% correct (Dratsch 2023).

## Popular and Weak

- **70-20-10.** Clardy 2018 finds the evidence for the 70% informal learning rule weak.
- **Deliberate practice explains expertise.** 18% of variance in sport and 1% among elites (Macnamara 2016). The 1993 violinist study failed replication (Macnamara 2019). Ericsson 2019 counters with 29% under a narrower definition.
- **Tacit knowledge converts to explicit knowledge.** Gourlay 2006 finds no evidence for the Nonaka conversion modes. Tacit transfer rests on case studies (Collins 2001).
- **Interest fit predicts job satisfaction.** rho = 0.19 (Hoff 2020).
- **Mentoring drives careers.** Meta-analyses call effects on objective outcomes small (Allen 2004, Eby 2008).
- **Research experiences build scientists.** Linn 2015 calls the evidence weak; over half of 60 studies rest on self-report.
- **People learn from failure.** Five experiments, N = 1,674, show less learning from failure feedback (Eskreis-Winkler 2019).
- **AI speeds up experts.** Experienced developers took 19% longer and believed they were 20% faster (Becker 2025).

## Unknown

- Whether planned happenstance or career adaptability training changes outcomes.
- How far AI use erodes skill. Evidence is one before-and-after study in colonoscopy, ADR 28.4% to 22.4% (Budzyń 2025), one preprint RCT (Shen 2026) and a 16-pilot study.
- Whether cognitive apprenticeship improves outcomes. Reviews are qualitative.
- Effect sizes for Blume, Rudolph and Whiston: abstracts gave none.
- Toner-Rodgers 2024 is withdrawn; exclude it.

## Design Implications by Evidence Strength

1. Add a structured debrief after each project, built on objective records.
2. Keep feedback on the task and next step.
3. Let learners make and correct errors in practice when the goal is new problems.
4. Reassess experienced users on a schedule.
5. Measure transfer with a source other than the learner, at follow-up as well as completion.
6. Have users commit a judgement before seeing AI output on tasks they must check.
7. Keep unaided practice for skills users must retain, and log time and quality.
8. Help applicants weigh advisor quality; connect juniors with near peers (Feldon 2019).
9. Make research experiences last two terms at ten hours a week (Hernandez 2018).
10. Present interest fit and mentoring as small effects, and drop 70-20-10.
