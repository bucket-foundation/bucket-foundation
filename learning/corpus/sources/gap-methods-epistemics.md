# Methods and Epistemics Gap Fill

178 works sit in `gap-methods-epistemics.jsonl`: 161 with a DOI matched to its Crossref or OpenAlex record, 13 confirmed by URL, 4 unconfirmed. `methods-table.jsonl` holds 32 methods. Effects come from abstracts or fetched pages; a missing number is null.

## Methods Ranked by Evidence

1. Retrieval practice: g = 0.499 in classrooms, 222 studies, 48,478 students. Transfer d = 0.40.
2. Spacing: spaced against massed retrieval g = 0.74. The best gap is 5 to 40% of the test delay.
3. Interleaving: g = 0.42 overall, d = 0.83 in a preregistered trial of 54 mathematics classes. It reverses for word lists, g = -0.39.
4. Elaborated feedback: 0.49 for an explanation, 0.32 for the correct answer, 0.05 for right or wrong alone. Over a third of feedback interventions lowered performance.
5. Self-explanation: g = 0.55. Delayed evidence in mathematics is thin.
6. Worked examples: g = 0.48 in mathematics, reversing for advanced learners.
7. Intelligent tutoring systems: g = 0.42 against class teaching, and no different from human tutors.
8. Human tutoring: 0.288 SD pooled, 0.16 to 0.22 SD at scale on independent tests.

Next tier: case comparison d = 0.50, teaching others g = 0.56, generation d = 0.40, productive failure g = 0.36, metacognitive strategy instruction g = 0.50.

## Popular Methods With Weak Evidence

- Two sigma. Bloom's figure came from two dissertations on narrow tests. Later syntheses give 0.37, 0.29 and 0.2 SD.
- Growth mindset: d = 0.05 overall and 0.02 in the best studies. A close replication and a 101-school trial found nothing.
- Learning styles: almost no study shows the required crossover.
- Formative assessment at 0.4 to 0.7: the usable studies give 0.20.
- Mastery learning: 0.52 on experimenter tests, nothing on standardized tests.
- Gamification: g = 0.49 on test scores from 19 small samples. A broken streak lowers later engagement.
- Expanding intervals: g = 0.034 against uniform spacing.
- Far transfer from brain training: small or null with active controls.
- Duolingo and Anki: no randomized trial in this set. Anki's best result is one Step 1 point per 1,700 cards, observational.

## What Counts as Learned

Gettier shows a correct answer with a reason can be luck. Reliabilism counts a belief by the reliability of the process behind it. Bucket should count an atom as known after correct recall on varied items across spaced sessions.

Ryle separates knowing how from knowing that. Kvanvig places understanding above knowledge. Recall, performance and explanation need separate evidence.

Most knowledge arrives by testimony. Each atom should carry its source, and source-trust items belong in the canon.

Belief comes in degrees. A confidence rating per answer gives a calibration curve.

The definitions disagree on purpose. Dewey defines education as "that reconstruction or reorganization of experience which adds to the meaning of experience, and which increases ability to direct the course of subsequent experience." Biesta names qualification, socialisation and subjectification. Sen and Nussbaum count what a person becomes able to do. Bucket measures qualification alone, and should say so or add a measure of use in the learner's own work.

One estimate puts signalling at 30% of the return to schooling. A Bucket credential has to show retained skill.

## Measuring Impact

- Retest at 7 days, 30 days and 6 to 12 months. Unassisted retention is two thirds to three quarters at one year.
- Use held-out items and claim near transfer only.
- Expect small effects: large trials average 0.06 SD, and 36% of trial effects fall below 0.05 SD. Small studies with their own tests report double.
- Use an active control and a preregistered primary endpoint.
- Report standardized gains with the pretest as covariate. Normalized gain favours high-pretest groups.
- Calibrate items with IRT and write a validity argument for the 0.7 mastery cut.
- Compare predicted recall with observed recall.
