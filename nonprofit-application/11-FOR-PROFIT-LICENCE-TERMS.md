# For-Profit Licence Terms

Term sheet for counsel. It covers the agreement between Bucket Foundation and the for-profit company that will sell Polingual. The founder will sit on both sides, so every term is a legal judgment: confirm with counsel.

## The Four Rules

| Rule | Meaning here | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No inurement | No Foundation earnings or assets reach the founder except fair pay for services | [IRS inurement and private benefit](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/inurement-private-benefit-charitable-organizations) |
| Private benefit stays incidental | The company gains only what it pays fair value for | Same |
| No excess benefit | A deal that favours the founder draws excise taxes on the founder and on directors who approved it | [IRS intermediate sanctions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/intermediate-sanctions) |
| Unrelated business income is taxed | Royalties are excluded. Service fees and income from a controlled company are counted. | [Publication 598](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p598) |

## First Question: Who Owns Polingual Today

No entity exists, so the founder holds the work in a personal capacity. The repository code is public under the MIT licence, which already lets anyone use it. The assets with value are the name and marks, the app store listings, the curated language decks and any non-public data.

| Route | What moves | Royalty flows | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Foundation owns, company licenses | Founder assigns Polingual rights to the Foundation at formation. Foundation licenses them to the company. | Company pays Foundation | Strongest for the Foundation. The assignment is a gift from the founder. |
| B. Company owns, Foundation is licensee | Founder assigns Polingual rights to the company. Company grants the Foundation a free licence for its free programs. | None | Simplest. The Foundation never owned the asset, so nothing leaves charity. |
| C. Spinout after formation | Foundation owns first, then sells the rights to the company | One sale price at fair market value | Needs an independent valuation and the heaviest review |

Counsel picks the route with the founder before the articles are filed. Form 1023 Part IV line 7 asks what intellectual property the Foundation owns and how it is used.

## Terms The Agreement Needs

| # | Term | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties | The Foundation and the company, each with its own signer. The founder signs for one side only. |
| 2 | Approval | Independent directors vote with the founder out of the room. Minutes record the data relied on. |
| 3 | Price | Fair market royalty or price, backed by an independent valuation or comparable licences |
| 4 | Presumption of reasonableness | Independent approval, comparability data and same-day minutes together shift the burden to the IRS ([rebuttable presumption](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/rebuttable-presumption-intermediate-sanctions)) |
| 5 | Scope | Field limited to the paid language app. Non-exclusive where possible. Exclusivity only for added payment. |
| 6 | Reserved rights | The Foundation keeps free use of the same material in its free programs, forever |
| 7 | Royalty form | A share of gross revenue or a fixed fee. No share of profit and loss, which would make a partnership and flip worksheet question 22. |
| 8 | Services | The Foundation performs no services for the company under the licence. Service income loses the royalty exclusion. |
| 9 | Shared costs | Hosting, tools or staff time used by both sides is billed at cost under a written agreement with time records |
| 10 | Separation | Separate bank accounts, books, domains, app store accounts and insurance |
| 11 | Names | The company does not present itself as the charity. Every Foundation gift page states that the company is separate and for profit. |
| 12 | Donations | No gift to the Foundation pays for the company's product, marketing or staff |
| 13 | Equity | The Foundation holds no equity. A stake above 50% makes royalties taxable under the controlled organisation rule in Publication 598. |
| 14 | Audit | The Foundation may audit royalty reports each year |
| 15 | Term and exit | Fixed term, renewal by independent vote, termination for breach or non-payment, rights revert to the Foundation |
| 16 | Reporting | Disclosed in Form 1023 Part V and each year on Form 990 Schedule L |

Arm's length standard and the 35% ownership question: [Instructions for Form 1023](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023), Part V.

## Shared Founder Conflicts

| Conflict | Control |
|---|---|
| Founder sets the price on both sides | Independent directors and an outside valuation set it |
| Founder time drifts to the paid product | Yearly disclosure of hours, per the conflict policy |
| Foundation users are steered to the paid app | Free programs stay complete on their own. Any referral to the paid app is a board-approved term with fair payment. Confirm with counsel. |
| Company uses the Foundation's name for trust | Trademark licence terms and a public separation statement |
| Marketplace funds reach the company | Barred. The company and its staff are ineligible recipients. |
