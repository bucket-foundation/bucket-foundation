## Abstract

Broadband near-infrared spectroscopy (bNIRS) has emerged as a promising technique for non-invasive monitoring of the redox state of cytochrome-c-oxidase (CCO), a key enzyme in cellular energy production. While early work focused on linear approaches based on the modified Beer-Lambert law (MBLL), recent decades have seen substantial diversification in both instrumentation and computational strategies.
