## 1. Inventory of candidate canon entries

Each entry: edition-of-record, mechanism justification, strong/borderline
flag. ~38 entries. Pass-2 will adjudicate borderline calls and prune.

### 1.1 Perception

- **Helmholtz 1867.** *Handbuch der physiologischen Optik*, vol. 3 (Voss,
  Leipzig). The unconscious-inference doctrine: perception as the result
  of unconscious probabilistic inference from sense data plus prior
  knowledge. Edition-of-record: Southall trans. *Treatise on Physiological
  Optics* (Optical Society of America, 1924–1925; reprinted Dover 1962).
  **Strong c1, anchor entry for `perception/`.** Dual-primary with
  `09-art/perception/`; cross-link both ways.
- **Hering 1878.** *Zur Lehre vom Lichtsinne* (Gerold, Vienna). The
  opponent-process theory of color, originally posed in opposition to
  Helmholtz's trichromatic line. **Borderline c1.** Pass-2: confirm
  whether the opponent-process foundational claim is best held in
  `09-art/color-form/` (color theory primary) with a cross-link, or
  promoted here as a perception primary. Lean: keep in `09-art/`, cross-
  link from here.
- **Gibson 1950.** *The Perception of the Visual World* (Houghton
  Mifflin). The optic-array and ground-theory predecessor to ecological
  psychology. **Borderline c1** for an alternate-paradigm primary; the
  more decisive text is Gibson 1979.
- **Gibson 1979.** *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*
  (Houghton Mifflin). Affordances, direct perception, the ecological
  approach. **Strong c1.** Stands as the canonical alternative to the
  Helmholtz-to-Marr inferentialist line and must appear in canon for the
  branch's representational debate to be balanced.
- **Marr 1982.** *Vision: A Computational Investigation into the Human
  Representation and Processing of Visual Information* (W.H. Freeman).
  The three-levels-of-analysis (computational / algorithmic / implementational),
  the 2½-D sketch, the founding monograph of computational vision.
  Posthumous publication; manuscript completed before Marr's death in
  1980. **Strong c3** by the discipline-standard pedagogical-primary
  rule; arguably c1 since Marr is sole originator of the framework.
  Pass-2: settle the c1-vs-c3 designation. See contestable-calls §4.

### 1.2 Computational theory of mind

- **McCulloch and Pitts 1943.** "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent
  in Nervous Activity," *Bull. Math. Biophys.* 5, 115–133.
  doi:10.1007/BF02478259. The neuron-as-logic-element primary; the first
  formal model of a neural network. **Strong c1, anchor entry for
  `computational-theory/`.** Dual-primary with
  `04-information/computation/` (the logical-calculus side); cross-link.
- **Turing 1950.** "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," *Mind* 59
  (236), 433–460. doi:10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433. The imitation game; the
  founding text of the computational approach to the mind question.
  Edition-of-record: original *Mind* paper; reprinted in Copeland ed.
  *The Essential Turing* (Oxford 2004). **Strong c1.** Cross-link from
  `04-information/computation/`.
- **von Neumann 1958.** *The Computer and the Brain* (Yale UP, posthumous,
  ed. Klara Dan von Neumann). The unfinished Silliman lectures
  comparison of digital computation and neural information processing.
  **Borderline c1.** Argument for inclusion: it is the originator's
  primary statement of the comparative analysis at a time when no
  successor existed. Argument against: it is unfinished and superseded by
  later neuroscience. Pass-2 lean: include with explicit "unfinished
  primary" annotation.
- **Newell and Simon 1976.** "Computer Science as Empirical Inquiry:
  Symbols and Search," *Comm. ACM* 19(3), 113–126.
  doi:10.1145/360018.360022. The physical-symbol-system hypothesis. ACM
  Turing Award lecture. **Strong c1.**
- **Fodor 1975.** *The Language of Thought* (Crowell). The
  language-of-thought hypothesis (mentalese). **Strong c1.**

### 1.3 Predictive brain / Bayesian brain

- **Mumford 1992.** "On the Computational Architecture of the Neocortex.
  II. The Role of Cortico-Cortical Loops," *Biol. Cybernet.* 66, 241–251.
  doi:10.1007/BF00198477. The pre-Rao-Ballard articulation of cortical
  feedback as carrying predictions. **Strong c1** as the
  predictive-processing predecessor.
- **Rao and Ballard 1999.** "Predictive Coding in the Visual Cortex: a
  Functional Interpretation of Some Extra-Classical Receptive-Field
  Effects," *Nat. Neurosci.* 2(1), 79–87. doi:10.1038/4580. The
  predictive-coding originator paper for vision. **Strong c1.**
- **Knill and Pouget 2004.** "The Bayesian Brain: the Role of Uncertainty
  in Neural Coding and Computation," *Trends Neurosci.* 27(12), 712–719.
  doi:10.1016/j.tins.2004.10.007. The naming text for the Bayesian-brain
  program. **Strong c1** in the framework-naming sense.
- **Friston 2010.** "The Free-Energy Principle: a Unified Brain Theory?"
  *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 11(2), 127–138. doi:10.1038/nrn2787. The
  unification statement. **Strong c1** as the canonical unified
  presentation. Pass-2: settle whether to also include Friston 2005
  ("A Theory of Cortical Responses," *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 360,
  815–836, doi:10.1098/rstb.2005.1622) as the prior originator paper.
  Lean: include both, with 2010 as edition-of-record for citation.
- **Pearl 1988.** *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems*
  (Morgan Kaufmann). Bayesian networks as a representation and inference
  apparatus. **Borderline.** The methodology is canon at the
  AI/statistics border; for cognition specifically the canon entry is
  the application-to-cognition derivative. Pass-2 lean: keep Pearl 1988
  as `04-information/learning-theory/` candidate (or `01-mathematics/
  probability/`), with cross-link from here. Do not double-mirror.

### 1.4 Decision theory and cognition

- **Savage 1954.** *The Foundations of Statistics* (Wiley). Subjective
  expected utility; the Savage axioms. **Strong c1** for the foundations
  of subjective-probability decision theory.
- **Tversky and Kahneman 1974.** "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics
  and Biases," *Science* 185 (4157), 1124–1131.
  doi:10.1126/science.185.4157.1124. The heuristics-and-biases program
  founding paper. **Strong c1.**
- **Kahneman and Tversky 1979.** "Prospect Theory: an Analysis of
  Decision under Risk," *Econometrica* 47(2), 263–291.
  doi:10.2307/1914185. **Strong c1.** Cross-link to economics if a
  branch later opens.
- **Kahneman 2011.** *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Farrar, Straus and
  Giroux). **Landscape, not canon.** Synthesis-and-popularization by an
  originator of the underlying program; the foundational papers carry
  the canon weight, this monograph carries the public name. Cite freely,
  do not mirror.

### 1.5 Philosophy of mind

- **Searle 1980.** "Minds, Brains, and Programs," *Behav. Brain Sci.*

- **Block 1995.** "On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness,"

- **Chalmers 1995.** "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness," *J.
  Conscious. Stud.* 2(3), 200–219. The hard-problem formulation. **Strong
  c1.**
- **Putnam 1967.** "Psychological Predicates," in W. H. Capitan and D. D.
  Merrill eds. *Art, Mind, and Religion* (University of Pittsburgh Press,
  1967), 37–48. The functionalism founding paper (Putnam later renamed
  the position to "the nature of mental states"). **Strong c1.**
- **Dennett 1991.** *Consciousness Explained* (Little, Brown). **Borderline.**

### 1.6 Neuroscience of cognition

- **Hebb 1949.** *The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological

- **Edelman 1987.** *Neural Darwinism: The Theory of Neuronal Group

## 3. Boundary calls

### 3.1 vs `04-information/`

The cleanest rule: **explanandum, not formalism.** A result about what
any computation can in principle achieve is `04-information/`; a result
about how the brain or mind in fact computes is `07-mind/`. Identical
math on either side of the line is fine — VC-dimension theorems live in
information; the empirical claim that human category learning has
VC-like sample-complexity behavior would live here.

This rule lets predictive-coding (Rao–Ballard, Friston) sit cleanly here:
the explanandum is the cortex. It lets Pearl 1988 sit cleanly in
information: the explanandum is inference under uncertainty as such.

### 3.2 vs `05-biophysics/`

The boundary is the level of organization. Membrane biophysics, ion
channels, single-action-potential dynamics, synaptic transmission at the
molecular level — biophysics. Hebb-rule learning, cell-assembly dynamics,
systems-level cortical computation, dopaminergic prediction-error signals,
hippocampal memory consolidation, attention, perception, decision,
consciousness — mind. Hodgkin–Huxley 1952 lives in biophysics with a
cross-link from here; Hebb 1949 lives here with a cross-link from
biophysics.

### 3.3 vs `09-art/perception/`

Physiological optics and acoustics — the substrate of perception of art
objects — live in `09-art/perception/`. Cognitive theories of perception
— what the perceptual system *does* with that substrate — live here. The
Helmholtz Handbuch is dual-primary by founder-decision precedent: the
substrate volumes (vol. 1 dioptrics, vol. 2 retina-and-light-sensitivity)
weight to art; the unconscious-inference doctrine in vol. 3 weights here.
Single edition-of-record (Southall trans., OSA 1924–1925, Dover 1962
reprint), mirrored from both sides, both `CANON_INDEX.md` files list it.

### 3.4 vs `01-mathematics/foundations/`

Frege 1892 is the hardest call. Three ways to carve it:

1. Both Frege papers (1892 sense/reference, *Begriffsschrift* 1879) in
   math; cross-link from here.
2. Both in mind; cross-link from math.
3. Split: 1879 and *Grundgesetze* in math (formal-logic primary); 1892
   in mind (philosophy-of-language primary).

Pass-1 picks (3). The argument: 1892 is read overwhelmingly today as the
founding text of analytic philosophy of language and of theories of
mental content (sense as mode of presentation, the Hesperus/Phosphorus
case driving everything from Kripke through twin-earth). Putting 1892
in math erases the philosophy-of-mind cone of influence; putting *all*
of Frege in mind misrepresents the *Begriffsschrift*'s status as a
foundational logic primary. Split it. Russell 1905 follows Frege 1892
here for the same reason.

## 4. Contestable calls for pass-2

### 4.1 Marr 1982 — c1 or c3?

The book is sole-author originator monograph by a researcher who died
before publication. The three-levels-of-analysis framework, the 2½-D
sketch, and the computational-theory-of-vision program are all Marr
originals, not syntheses. Lean: c1. The c3 designation in the README
hedge exists only because the canonical citation is a posthumous book
rather than a journal paper. Pass-2 should resolve to c1.

### 4.2 Friston free-energy — one paper or bundle, and which is ed-of-record?

Two candidate originator papers: Friston 2005 *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*
360, 815–836 (the cortical-responses paper) and Friston 2010 *Nat. Rev.
Neurosci.* 11, 127–138 (the unification statement). Lean: include both;
2010 carries the discipline citation weight, 2005 carries the priority
claim. Annotate accordingly. Do not collapse the program into a single
paper — that misrepresents how it was built.

### 4.3 Tversky–Kahneman — psychology canon or behavioral-economics
landscape?

The 1974 *Science* paper is unambiguously canon for cognitive psychology
of judgment; the 1979 *Econometrica* paper is unambiguously canon for
the formalization of choice under risk. Both stand. The objection that
"behavioral economics is downstream-application, not canon" is rejected:
prospect theory is a primary derivation of a model of decision, not a
catalogue of empirical findings. Hold both as strong c1.

### 4.4 Searle 1980 Chinese Room — originator or commentary?

Objection: a thought experiment is not a derivation; the Chinese Room is
a piece of philosophical commentary on a position (strong AI / functional
equivalence) that other people held. Counter: the argument is original to
Searle, the *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* publication includes 27
peer commentaries plus Searle's reply (the BBS open-peer-commentary
format), and the argument is the c1 statement of the
syntax-vs-semantics objection in the field. Lean: keep as strong c1.

### 4.5 Chomsky generative grammar's continuing-validity question

Generative-grammar's empirical fortunes have shifted; the Minimalist
Program (1995) and later work has moved the theoretical apparatus
substantially, and usage-based and construction-grammar critics deny
the central premises. Question: does this affect the canon status of
1957 and 1965? Answer: no. Canon status is about *originating a
foundational framework that the field had to contend with*, not about
the framework's current empirical standing. Newtonian mechanics is
canon; relativity superseded much of its scope; both are canon. Same
rule here. 1957 and 1965 stand.

### 4.6 Edelman *Neural Darwinism* — originator framework or sole-author
speculation?

The neuronal-group-selection hypothesis is an Edelman original; it has
not propagated as a working research program in the way that
Hebb-rule learning, predictive coding, or the global-workspace theory
have. Lean: include as borderline c1 with explicit annotation that the
theory is canon-as-originator-statement, not canon-as-mature-paradigm.
The branch should hold the originator statement and let pass-3 or later
sweeps add or remove based on whether successor literature
materializes.

### 4.7 Wittgenstein 1953 — canon or landscape?

This is the philosophical question of the branch. The case for canon:
*Philosophical Investigations* is the originator text of the
private-language argument, the rule-following considerations, the
language-game framing, and the meaning-as-use doctrine — every one of
which has been a load-bearing input to philosophy of mind from the
1960s onward (rule-following → Kripke 1982, language-games → Putnam's
later work, private language → Dennett-style functionalism, meaning-as-
use → Brandom). The case against: the *Investigations* is aphoristic,
posthumous, edited by Anscombe and Rhees from notes Wittgenstein did
not himself prepare for publication, and contains no derivations or
mechanism statements in the sense the canon rule requires. Other sweeps
(chemistry pass-3 §3) wrote the rule-architecture as "axioms, real
math, rules, laws, principles, primary derivations" — *Investigations*
is none of these in genre.

Honest sweep take: include Wittgenstein 1953 as a borderline c1 with
a flag in `CANON_INDEX.md` reading "primary text of the
private-language and rule-following arguments; aphoristic genre; canon
inclusion is by *originator-of-foundational-arguments* rule, not by
mechanism rule." The alternative — relegating the *Investigations* to
landscape — would say the canon has no room for the single most
influential 20th-century philosophy-of-mind text outside the
analytic-empiricist line, and that asymmetry is harder to defend than
the genre-flexibility cost of letting it in. Pass-2 owner: please
push back if you disagree.
