## 1. Inventory of candidate canon entries

Each entry: author, title, year, edition-of-record where it differs from the
original, mechanism justification, sub-domain, strength (*strong* /
*borderline*).

### Pre-relativistic foundations

**Isaac Newton — *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, Book III
("De Mundi Systemate"), Streater, London, 1687.** Already canon in
`02-physics/classical-mechanics/`. Cross-link only. Universal gravitation
*as cosmology* — Book III applies the inverse-square law to the solar
system and the tides. The argument for a separate cosmology entry: the
*system of the world* framing is cosmological, not mechanical. The argument
against: Newton's mechanism is the inverse-square law, which is in physics;
no second primary statement here. **Cross-link only**, not a separate
canon entry. `pre-relativistic/`.

**Heinrich Wilhelm Olbers — "Über die Durchsichtigkeit des Weltraums",
*Astronomisches Jahrbuch für das Jahr 1826* (Bode, ed.), Späthen, Berlin,
1823, pp. 110–121.** The dark-night-sky paradox in its modern form. Olbers
restated the puzzle (it goes back to Kepler and Halley) and made it
canonical. **Borderline.** The paradox is genuinely foundational —
resolving it requires either a finite age or a non-Euclidean geometry —
but the *resolution* is in 20th-century cosmology, and the paper is short
on mechanism. Pass-2 ratifies or moves to landscape. `pre-relativistic/`.

**Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel — "Bestimmung der Entfernung des
61sten Sterns des Schwans", *Astronomische Nachrichten* 16, 65–96 (1838).**
First successful measurement of stellar parallax (61 Cygni). The
observational anchor that fixed the distance to a star outside the solar
system and made the cosmic distance ladder a finite, measurable thing.
**Strong.** `pre-relativistic/`.

**Henrietta Swan Leavitt — "1777 variables in the Magellanic Clouds",
*Annals of the Astronomical Observatory of Harvard College* 60, no. 4,
87–108 (1908); "Periods of 25 Variable Stars in the Small Magellanic
Cloud", *Harvard College Observatory Circular* 173, 1–3 (1912).** Primary
statement of the Cepheid period-luminosity relation. The single rung that
made the cosmic distance ladder reach beyond parallax — Hubble 1925 (M31
distance) and Hubble 1929 (the distance–velocity law) both run on
Leavitt's calibration. **Strong.** `pre-relativistic/`.

### Relativistic foundations

**Albert Einstein — "Kosmologische Betrachtungen zur allgemeinen
Relativitätstheorie", *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preussischen Akademie
der Wissenschaften zu Berlin* 1917, 142–152 (1917).** Primary statement of
relativistic cosmology and introduction of the cosmological constant Λ.
The first paper to apply the GR field equations to the universe as a whole.
**Strong.** Cross-link from `02-physics/relativity/general/` (where the
field equations live). `relativistic-foundations/`.

**Willem de Sitter — "On Einstein's theory of gravitation, and its
astronomical consequences. Third paper", *Monthly Notices of the Royal
Astronomical Society* 78(1), 3–28 (1917).** The de Sitter universe — an
empty, exponentially expanding solution. The first non-static cosmological
solution and the model that resurfaces in inflation and dark-energy
asymptotics. **Strong.** `relativistic-foundations/`.

**Alexander Friedmann — "Über die Krümmung des Raumes", *Zeitschrift für
Physik* 10(1), 377–386 (1922); "Über die Möglichkeit einer Welt mit
konstanter negativer Krümmung des Raumes", *Zeitschrift für Physik* 21(1),
326–332 (1924).** Primary statements of the Friedmann equations governing
the time evolution of a homogeneous, isotropic universe under GR. The first
paper covers positive curvature; the second covers negative. **Strong**
(both). `relativistic-foundations/`.

**Georges Lemaître — "Un Univers homogène de masse constante et de rayon
croissant rendant compte de la vitesse radiale des nébuleuses
extra-galactiques", *Annales de la Société Scientifique de Bruxelles*
A47, 49–59 (1927).** Edition-of-record for the English: G. Lemaître
(translation expanded by the author), "A Homogeneous Universe of Constant
Mass and Increasing Radius Accounting for the Radial Velocity of
Extra-Galactic Nebulae", *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical
Society* 91, 483–490 (1931). Primary statement of the expanding-universe
solution and an independent derivation of the linear distance–velocity
relation two years before Hubble 1929. The IAU's 2018 General Assembly
Resolution B4 renamed the law "Hubble–Lemaître"; this canon honors that.
**Strong.** `relativistic-foundations/` plus `expansion-and-hubble/`
(cross-listing — see §3). `relativistic-foundations/`.

**Howard Percy Robertson — "Kinematics and World-Structure", *Astrophysical
Journal* 82, 284–301 (1935); 83, 187–201 (1936); 83, 257–271 (1936).**
**Arthur Geoffrey Walker — "On Milne's Theory of World-Structure",
*Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* (2) 42, 90–127 (1936).**
Joint primary statement of the FLRW metric in its modern form — the
maximally symmetric homogeneous-isotropic spatial metric that the Friedmann
equations evolve. **Strong** (joint). `relativistic-foundations/`.

### Expansion and the distance–velocity law

**Vesto Melvin Slipher — "Nebulae", *Proceedings of the American
Philosophical Society* 56, 403–409 (1917).** Primary catalog of radial
velocities of spiral nebulae — the observational input that made the
expansion claim possible. Slipher's redshifts were the data Hubble plotted
against his distances in 1929. **Strong.** `expansion-and-hubble/`.

**Edwin Hubble — "Cepheids in Spiral Nebulae", *Popular Astronomy* 33,
252–255 (1925) [the M31 distance announcement; the underlying paper is
Hubble's 1925 *AAS* abstract; the full extragalactic argument is in the
1926 *Astrophysical Journal* 64, 321–369 paper "Extragalactic Nebulae"].**
The "spiral nebulae are external galaxies" finding that ended the Shapley–
Curtis debate. **Strong.** `expansion-and-hubble/`.

**Edwin Hubble — "A Relation between Distance and Radial Velocity among
Extra-Galactic Nebulae", *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*
15(3), 168–173 (1929).** Primary statement of the linear distance–velocity
relation. The observational anchor of cosmic expansion. The priority is
shared with Lemaître 1927 and is now formally so per IAU Resolution B4
(2018). **Strong.** `expansion-and-hubble/`.

### Big-Bang nucleosynthesis

**George Gamow — "Expanding Universe and the Origin of Elements",
*Physical Review* 70(7–8), 572–573 (1946).** Primary statement of the
program: the light elements were synthesized in a hot dense early
universe. **Strong.** `nucleosynthesis/`.

**Ralph A. Alpher, Hans Bethe and George Gamow — "The Origin of Chemical
Elements", *Physical Review* 73(7), 803–804 (1948).** The αβγ paper.
Primary statement of nucleosynthesis from a primordial neutron gas.
Bethe's authorship is honorary; the work is Alpher and Gamow. **Strong.**
`nucleosynthesis/`.

**Chushiro Hayashi — "Proton-Neutron Concentration Ratio in the Expanding
Universe at the Stages preceding the Formation of the Elements", *Progress
of Theoretical Physics* 5(2), 224–235 (1950).** Primary correction to αβγ
— recognizes that weak interactions equilibrate the neutron-proton ratio
before nucleosynthesis. Foundational for the modern light-element
predictions. **Strong.** `nucleosynthesis/`.

**Robert V. Wagoner, William A. Fowler and Fred Hoyle — "On the Synthesis
of Elements at Very High Temperatures", *Astrophysical Journal* 148, 3–49
(1967).** First modern computation of BBN abundances against a coupled
nuclear network. The reference computation that all subsequent BBN papers
benchmark against. **Strong.** `nucleosynthesis/`.

### Cosmic microwave background

**Arno A. Penzias and Robert W. Wilson — "A Measurement of Excess Antenna
Temperature at 4080 Mc/s", *Astrophysical Journal* 142, 419–421 (1965).**
Primary observational discovery of the CMB. **Strong.** `cmb/`.

**Robert H. Dicke, P. James E. Peebles, Peter G. Roll and David T.
Wilkinson — "Cosmic Black-Body Radiation", *Astrophysical Journal* 142,
414–419 (1965).** The companion theoretical interpretation. The two
papers are bound together by physical convention; canon honors that and
lists them paired. **Strong.** `cmb/`.

**John C. Mather, E. S. Cheng, R. E. Eplee Jr., et al. — "A Preliminary
Measurement of the Cosmic Microwave Background Spectrum by the Cosmic
Background Explorer (COBE) Satellite", *Astrophysical Journal Letters*
354, L37–L40 (1990); follow-up Mather et al., "Measurement of the Cosmic
Microwave Background Spectrum by the COBE FIRAS Instrument", *ApJ* 420,
439–444 (1994).** Primary observation that the CMB is a black-body to
extraordinary precision — the most precise black-body spectrum ever
measured of any natural source. **Strong.** `cmb/`.

**George F. Smoot, C. L. Bennett, A. Kogut, et al. — "Structure in the
COBE Differential Microwave Radiometer First-Year Maps", *Astrophysical
Journal Letters* 396, L1–L5 (1992).** Primary detection of CMB anisotropy
— the seeds of structure. **Strong.** `cmb/`.

**David N. Spergel, L. Verde, H. V. Peiris, et al. — "First-Year Wilkinson
Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) Observations: Determination of
Cosmological Parameters", *Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series* 148,
175–194 (2003).** Primary cosmological-parameter determination from the
CMB power spectrum at the precision-cosmology threshold. Pair with
Hinshaw et al., "Nine-Year WMAP Observations: Cosmological Parameter
Results", *ApJS* 208, 19 (2013) as the WMAP final. **Strong** (paired).
`cmb/`.

**Planck Collaboration (N. Aghanim, Y. Akrami, M. Ashdown, et al.) —
"Planck 2018 Results. VI. Cosmological Parameters", *Astronomy &
Astrophysics* 641, A6 (2020).** Live edition-of-record for the
cosmological parameter set. Note the explicit *living* status — this is
expected to be superseded by the next mission's flagship paper, and the
canon convention is to mirror the superseded edition into `_archive/` and
update the binding row. The 2014, 2015, and 2016 Planck releases are
intermediate; the 2018 release is the final mission paper. **Strong.**
`cmb/`. (Contestable: see §4.2 on living parameter sets.)

### Inflation

**Alan H. Guth — "Inflationary Universe: A Possible Solution to the Horizon
and Flatness Problems", *Physical Review D* 23(2), 347–356 (1981).** Primary
statement of inflation. **Strong.** `inflation/`.

**Andrei D. Linde — "A New Inflationary Universe Scenario: A Possible
Solution of the Horizon, Flatness, Homogeneity, Isotropy and Primordial
Monopole Problems", *Physics Letters B* 108(6), 389–393 (1982).** Primary
statement of "new inflation" — the slow-roll scalar-field version that
fixed the graceful-exit problem of Guth's original. **Strong.**
`inflation/`.

**Andreas Albrecht and Paul J. Steinhardt — "Cosmology for Grand Unified
Theories with Radiatively Induced Symmetry Breaking", *Physical Review
Letters* 48(17), 1220–1223 (1982).** Independent and contemporaneous slow-
roll formulation. The Guth–Linde–Albrecht–Steinhardt priority bundle is
the originator set; canon lists them together. **Strong.** `inflation/`.

### Dark matter

**Fritz Zwicky — "Die Rotverschiebung von extragalaktischen Nebeln",
*Helvetica Physica Acta* 6, 110–127 (1933).** Primary observation of the
mass discrepancy in the Coma cluster — the first published case for
"dunkle Materie". **Strong.** `dark-matter/`.

**Vera C. Rubin and W. Kent Ford Jr. — "Rotation of the Andromeda Nebula
from a Spectroscopic Survey of Emission Regions", *Astrophysical Journal*
159, 379–403 (1970).** First detailed extended rotation curve showing the
flat-velocity profile in M31. **Strong.** `dark-matter/`.

**Vera C. Rubin, W. Kent Ford Jr. and Norbert Thonnard — "Extended Rotation
Curves of High-Luminosity Spiral Galaxies. IV. Systematic Dynamical
Properties, Sa → Sc", *Astrophysical Journal Letters* 225, L107–L111
(1978); follow-up *ApJ* 238, 471–487 (1980).** The systematic survey that
made flat rotation curves a generic property of spirals, not an Andromeda
peculiarity. **Strong.** `dark-matter/`.

**R. Brent Tully and J. Richard Fisher — "A New Method of Determining
Distances to Galaxies", *Astronomy and Astrophysics* 54, 661–673 (1977).**
The Tully–Fisher relation between H I line width and absolute luminosity.
A second-rung distance indicator and an independent dark-matter
fingerprint. **Strong.** `dark-matter/`.

### Dark energy

**Adam G. Riess, Alexei V. Filippenko, Peter Challis, et al. (High-z
Supernova Search Team) — "Observational Evidence from Supernovae for an
Accelerating Universe and a Cosmological Constant", *Astronomical Journal*
116(3), 1009–1038 (1998).** **Saul Perlmutter, G. Aldering, G. Goldhaber,
et al. (Supernova Cosmology Project) — "Measurements of Ω and Λ from 42
High-Redshift Supernovae", *Astrophysical Journal* 517(2), 565–586
(1999).** The joint Nobel-2011 papers — primary observational discovery of
cosmic acceleration via Type Ia supernovae. **Strong** (paired).
`dark-energy/`.

### Structure formation

**William H. Press and Paul Schechter — "Formation of Galaxies and
Clusters of Galaxies by Self-Similar Gravitational Condensation",
*Astrophysical Journal* 187, 425–438 (1974).** Primary statement of the
Press–Schechter mass function — the foundational analytic prediction for
the abundance of collapsed structures. The framework that all modern halo
mass-function work (Sheth–Tormen, etc.) extends. **Strong.**
`structure-formation/`.

**P. James E. Peebles — *The Large-Scale Structure of the Universe*,
Princeton University Press, Princeton, 1980 (ISBN 0-691-08240-5).**
Originator monograph for the modern statistical theory of large-scale
structure: correlation functions, the BBGKY hierarchy in cosmology, the
N-point statistics machinery. Peebles is the originator of much of the
content. **Borderline** (c1 originator monograph vs c2 textbook). The
chemistry-branch precedent for Pauling 1960 *Nature of the Chemical Bond*
favors promotion under condition 1. Pass-1 lean: **canon, with the same
condition-1 justification used for Pauling and for Dirac 1958.** Pass-2
ratifies. `structure-formation/`. (Contestable: see §4.1.)

### Black holes (gravity-cosmology-information)

**Karl Schwarzschild — "Über das Gravitationsfeld eines Massenpunktes nach
der Einsteinschen Theorie", *Sitzungsberichte der Königlich Preussischen
Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Berlin* 1916, 189–196 (1916).** Already
canon in `02-physics/relativity/general/`. Cross-link only. The exterior
Schwarzschild solution is GR machinery; the *thermodynamic* re-reading is
cosmology. `black-holes/` (cross-link).

**Roy P. Kerr — "Gravitational Field of a Spinning Mass as an Example of
Algebraically Special Metrics", *Physical Review Letters* 11(5), 237–238
(1963).** Primary statement of the rotating black-hole solution. The
metric every astrophysical black-hole observation since (X-ray binaries,
M87*, Sgr A*, LIGO mergers) is interpreted against. **Strong.**
`black-holes/`.

**Jacob D. Bekenstein — "Black Holes and Entropy", *Physical Review D*
7(8), 2333–2346 (1973).** Primary statement that black-hole horizons carry
an entropy proportional to area. The opening move in black-hole
thermodynamics and, downstream, the holographic principle. **Strong.**
`black-holes/`.

**Stephen W. Hawking — "Black Hole Explosions?", *Nature* 248, 30–31
(1974); "Particle Creation by Black Holes", *Communications in
Mathematical Physics* 43(3), 199–220 (1975).** Primary statement of
Hawking radiation. The pair: a *Nature* announcement and the full *CMP*
derivation. **Strong** (paired). `black-holes/`.

### Reference works

**NASA LAMBDA — Legacy Archive for Microwave Background Data Analysis,
hosted at the NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov.**
Discipline-standard normative archive for CMB data products (COBE, WMAP,
Planck, SPT, ACT). **Strong** under condition 3. `reference/`.

**ESA Planck Legacy Archive, pla.esac.esa.int.** Discipline-standard
normative archive for the Planck mission. **Strong** under condition 3.
`reference/`.

**International Astronomical Union — Resolution B4, "On a suggested
renaming of the Hubble Law" (XXX General Assembly, Vienna, 2018).**
Discipline-standard normative resolution. The IAU instrument by which the
expansion law is now formally "Hubble–Lemaître". **Strong** under
condition 3. `reference/`.

### Borderline / under contest (see §4)

- **Bondi–Gold–Hoyle 1948 steady-state cosmology.** Hermann Bondi and
  Thomas Gold, "The Steady-State Theory of the Expanding Universe",
  *MNRAS* 108, 252–270 (1948); Fred Hoyle, "A New Model for the Expanding
  Universe", *MNRAS* 108, 372–382 (1948). Historically a co-equal rival to
  Big Bang cosmology, killed by the CMB. Canon entry with a falsification
  flag, or landscape only? Pass-1 §3 argues.
- **Brandon Carter — "Large Number Coincidences and the Anthropic
  Principle in Cosmology", in M. S. Longair (ed.), *Confrontation of
  Cosmological Theories with Observational Data* (IAU Symp. 63), Reidel,
  Dordrecht, 1974, pp. 291–298.** Canonical statement of the anthropic
  principle. Borderline; pass-1 §4 argues.
- **Andrei D. Linde — "Eternally Existing Self-Reproducing Chaotic
  Inflationary Universe", *Physics Letters B* 175(4), 395–400 (1986).**
  Eternal inflation / multiverse. Borderline.
- **B. P. Abbott et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo
  Collaboration) — "Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary
  Black Hole Merger", *Physical Review Letters* 116(6), 061102 (2016).**
  Founding observational paper of gravitational-wave astronomy.
  Borderline as cosmology canon (it is gravity-confirmation rather than
  cosmology-of-the-universe), strong as physics or as a cross-branch
  observational milestone.
- **Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration — "First M87 Event Horizon
  Telescope Results. I. The Shadow of the Supermassive Black Hole",
  *Astrophysical Journal Letters* 875, L1 (2019).** Borderline.
- **JWST early-universe results.** A class of borderline cases — pass-1
  defers to pass-2 once the dust settles on the JWST high-z papers.
- **Steven Weinberg — *Gravitation and Cosmology: Principles and
  Applications of the General Theory of Relativity*, Wiley, 1972; and
  *Cosmology*, Oxford University Press, 2008.** Pedagogical synthesis by
  an originator. Pass-1 lean: **landscape**, on the same precedent as
  *Course of Theoretical Physics* in physics.
- **Edward W. Kolb and Michael S. Turner — *The Early Universe*, Addison-
  Wesley, 1990.** Pass-1 lean: **landscape.**
- **P. James E. Peebles — *Principles of Physical Cosmology*, Princeton
  University Press, 1993.** Pass-1 lean: **landscape**, distinct from the

## 3. Boundary calls — explicit

The README gives the principle. Pass-1 fixes five specific test cases.

| Text | Side | Reason |
|------|------|--------|
| Newton, *Principia* Book III | physics primary, no cosmology entry | Universal gravitation is a physics mechanism. Book III applies it to the solar system; the *system of the world* framing is rhetorical, not a second mechanism. Cosmology gets a contributor-index entry in `canon-figures/` and nothing in `bucket-canon/`. |
| Schwarzschild 1916 | physics primary, cosmology cross-link | The exterior solution is GR-content. Black-hole *thermodynamics* (Bekenstein, Hawking) is the cosmological reading. |
| Kerr 1963, Bekenstein 1973, Hawking 1974/1975 | cosmology primary | The conversation here is global (entropy bounds, information loss, holography). Physics pass-2 caught these as missing on the gravity-information boundary; cosmology absorbs them. |
| Leavitt 1908/1912 (Cepheid period-luminosity) | cosmology | Astronomy in service of the distance ladder; it is the foundation of the ladder Hubble 1929 stands on. |
| Bondi-Gold-Hoyle 1948 (steady-state) | cosmology, **with falsification flag** | Historically necessary as the rival the CMB killed. Canon entry under a `_falsified/` convention (which the branch creates here for the first time). The argument for inclusion: a foundation that turns out to be wrong is still a *foundation researchers had to engage with*. The argument against: the canon is supposed to be small and load-bearing for current work. Pass-1 lean: **include with falsification flag.** Pass-2 ratifies. |

Operational rules ratified by pass-1:

- **GR field equations and exact solutions of GR → physics primary.**
  Cosmology cross-links.
- **Cosmological specializations of GR (FLRW metric, cosmological constant
  in its 1917 cosmology framing, de Sitter solution) → cosmology primary.**
  Physics cross-links.
- **Inflation, BBN, dark matter, dark energy, structure formation → cosmology
  primary.** Particle-physics dark-matter candidate models stay in physics
  landscape, not cosmology canon.
- **Black-hole metrics → physics; black-hole thermodynamics → cosmology.**
  This is the single hardest call (see §5).
- **Pre-modern world models → `08-deep-history/` and `canon-figures/`, not
  `bucket-canon/06-cosmology/`.** The canon begins with 1917.

## 4. Contestable calls — for pass-2

Five questions pass-1 deliberately does not resolve. Each gets a structured
argument both ways. Pass-2 picks a side; pass-3 makes it binding.

**4.1 Peebles 1980, *The Large-Scale Structure of the Universe*.** Argument
for canon: Peebles is the originator of the modern statistical theory of
large-scale structure, and the 1980 monograph is where the originator
material is consolidated (correlation-function machinery, cosmological
BBGKY, the analytic predictions that N-body simulations later confirmed).
The chemistry branch promoted Pauling 1960 *Nature of the Chemical Bond*
under exactly this rule (originator monograph, condition 1). Argument
against: the originator content is in Peebles's 1965–1980 *ApJ* papers; the
1980 book systematizes. Pass-1 lean: **canon**, listed strong above,
flagged here only because the rule is the same one the chemistry branch
spent a section settling. The 1993 *Principles of Physical Cosmology* is a
different beast — it is a teaching book, and it goes in landscape. Pass-2
ratifies.

**4.2 Living parameter sets — what canon does with the next Planck-class
release.** The Planck 2018 paper is the live edition-of-record for the
cosmological parameter set. A Simons Observatory / CMB-S4 / LiteBIRD
flagship paper in the late 2020s will supersede it. Two options. (a)
*Versioned binding row*: the `CANON_INDEX.md` row points to the current
edition; the previous edition moves to `_archive/`. The chemistry-branch
precedent is CODATA — the rule there is "current adjustment is canon, prior
is archived." (b) *Static citation, dynamic mirror*: canon binds to the
2018 paper forever (it is the paper that exists), and a separate
`reference/cosmological-parameters-current.md` tracks the live state. Pass-1
lean: **(a)**, on the CODATA precedent, with the explicit convention that
parameter releases are the only canon class with a versioning rule (BBN
papers, inflation papers, etc. are not versioned this way because they are
discoveries, not measurements). Pass-2 ratifies.

**4.3 Hubble vs Lemaître priority on the expansion law.** The IAU's 2018
Resolution B4 settled the *naming*: it is the Hubble–Lemaître law. The
canon question is *what to bind*. Three options. (a) Bind both, Lemaître
1927 (in *Annales* with the 1931 *MNRAS* English edition-of-record) and
Hubble 1929 (*PNAS*), as a paired primary statement. (b) Bind only Lemaître
1927 (he has priority and provides both the GR derivation and the
distance–velocity prediction). (c) Bind only Hubble 1929 (he provides the
empirical fit). Pass-1 lean: **(a)** — the Higgs / Englert–Brout precedent
in `02-physics/quantum-field-theory/` shows the canon convention for
discoveries with multiple originators. Pass-2 ratifies.

**4.4 LIGO, EHT, JWST as canon vs landscape.** The argument for canon: each
opens a new observational channel; LIGO 2016 in particular is the first
direct detection of a phenomenon (gravitational waves) predicted by Einstein
1916 and is a foundation-level event in the same sense as Penzias–Wilson
1965 was. The argument against: most of these are *single observation
release papers*, not foundations of new model structure. Pass-1 lean:
**LIGO 2016 yes** (it is the first-detection paper of a phenomenon-class,
not just a measurement); **EHT 2019 borderline** (it is more confirmation
than discovery; the mechanism it tests is in Kerr 1963); **JWST early-
universe results no** (they are measurements that update parameter
contours, not foundations). Pass-2 settles.

**4.5 The multiverse / anthropic bundle (Carter 1974, Linde 1986).**
Argument for inclusion: these are foundations of *the structure of
explanation* in cosmology — anthropic reasoning shapes which initial
conditions are taken to require explanation, and eternal inflation is the
context in which the multiverse becomes a serious physics object. Argument
against: neither is a primary derivation of a physical law in the canon
sense; both are framing moves whose status as physics (rather than as
philosophy of physics) is contested inside the field. Pass-1 lean:
**Carter 1974 borderline → landscape**; **Linde 1986 borderline → canon
under inflation**, on the grounds that eternal self-reproduction is a
mechanism (the slow-roll scalar field plus quantum fluctuations
*generates* it), not a framing. Pass-2 settles.

## 5. Honest take

**Top-5 must-have entries.** Friedmann 1922 (the equations every modern
cosmology runs); Lemaître 1927 with the 1931 *MNRAS* English edition-of-
record (the GR-derivation-plus-prediction that nailed the expansion two
years before Hubble); Hubble 1929 (the observation that fixed it to the
sky); Penzias–Wilson 1965 paired with Dicke–Peebles–Roll–Wilkinson 1965
(the CMB discovery, single foundation, two papers); Guth 1981 with the
Linde and Albrecht–Steinhardt 1982 priority bundle (inflation, the model
that everything in modern early-universe cosmology is built around). If
forced to a single sixth pick, Riess 1998 / Perlmutter 1999 — dark energy
is the most recent foundation of comparable weight.

**Single hardest boundary call vs `02-physics/`.** Black-hole
thermodynamics. The Schwarzschild metric is unambiguously physics; the
Kerr metric is unambiguously physics in its construction; but Bekenstein
1973 and Hawking 1974/1975 are the moment the conversation goes global — it
becomes about gravity, information, and entropy at the universe scale, and
the downstream traffic (holographic principle, AdS/CFT, the firewall
debate, the ER=EPR conjecture, the black-hole-information problem) is
cosmology-and-information rather than gravitational physics in the narrow
sense. Pass-1 places Schwarzschild in physics with a cosmology cross-link,
and places Kerr 1963, Bekenstein 1973, and Hawking 1974/1975 in cosmology
with a physics cross-link. The argument for the reverse — Kerr in physics
because its construction is a metric-engineering feat — is real, and a
defensible pass-2 reversal of Kerr alone is possible. Bekenstein and
Hawking have to land here either way; their content is global.

**Peebles 1980: canon or landscape?** Honest take: **canon**, under the
same condition-1 rule that promoted Pauling 1960 in chemistry and Dirac
1958 in physics. Peebles is the originator of the modern statistical
theory of large-scale structure; the 1980 monograph is where the
originator content is consolidated; everything in modern N-body cosmology
is a numerical instantiation of its analytic predictions. The 1993
*Principles of Physical Cosmology*, by contrast, is a teaching book — it
goes in landscape, alongside Weinberg 1972/2008, Dodelson, Mukhanov, and
Kolb–Turner. The chemistry branch's pass-3 §3.1 argued exactly this rule
(originator monograph promotes; teaching synthesis does not), and the
cosmology branch inherits it. The single risk is that the originator-
monograph rule, applied generously, creeps until every senior figure's
"my book" promotes; the discipline against that is the boring one — read
the table of contents and check whether the load-bearing material is
present in primary papers or only in the book. For Peebles 1980 the
answer is "primarily in the book"; for *Principles of Physical Cosmology*
1993 the answer is "synthesized from a literature that includes Peebles
1980." Pass-1 promotes 1980, lands 1993 in landscape.
