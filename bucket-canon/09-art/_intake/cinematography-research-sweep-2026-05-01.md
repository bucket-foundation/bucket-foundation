# Cinematography Research Sweep — 2026-05-01

Intake document. Not promoted. Proposes how (and whether) a cinema sub-branch should enter `09-art/` under the standing promotion rule (primary statement of a law/principle/mechanism by the originator, or discipline-standard monograph advancing a named foundational framework). All edition-of-record claims are checked against current scholarly consensus as of this date.

Author: data pillar (research sweep).
Scope target: lighting, framing geometry, angle, color, sound-image, and the theoretical architecture of the moving image.

## 1. Canon candidates — primary theoretical texts in cinema

Each entry: author, title, year, edition-of-record, justification, proposed sub-folder, strength.

### Strong canon

**Hugo Münsterberg — *The Photoplay: A Psychological Study* (1916).**
Edition of record: D. Appleton, New York, 1916 (PD). Modern critical reissue: Allan Langdale (ed.), *Hugo Münsterberg on Film*, Routledge, 2002.
Justification: First systematic theoretical account of cinema as a mental act. Münsterberg derives the basic film operations (close-up, cut, flashback) from psychological functions (attention, memory, imagination, emotion). Mechanism-level claim about how the apparatus works on a viewer; predates and grounds nearly every later cognitive-film argument. Originator text.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/theory-of-the-medium/`.

**Sergei Eisenstein — *Film Form: Essays in Film Theory* (1949) and *The Film Sense* (1942).**
Editions of record: Jay Leyda (ed./tr.), Harcourt, Brace, New York. *The Film Sense* 1942; *Film Form* 1949 (1977 reissue, copyright renewed Leyda 1977). The Leyda volumes remain the English editions of record despite later partial re-translations.
Justification: Primary statement of the montage theory — the dialectical collision of shots as the generator of meaning. Includes the typology (metric, rhythmic, tonal, overtonal, intellectual montage). Originator, mechanism-level, with method.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/montage/`.

**André Bazin — *Qu'est-ce que le cinéma?* (4 vols., 1958–1962).**
Edition of record (French): Éditions du Cerf, Paris, 1958–62. English: Timothy Barnard (tr.), *What is Cinema?*, Caboose, Montréal, 2009 (single-volume selection); Hugh Gray (tr.), 2 vols., University of California Press, 1967/1971 — historically influential but flagged by the field as paraphrastic. Barnard is the current scholarly preference.
Justification: Primary statement of the ontological-realist position — the photographic image as mechanically-derived trace, mise-en-scène in depth and the long take as the formal correlate of that ontology. Originator. Counter-pole to Eisenstein.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/realism-and-ontology/`.

**Rudolf Arnheim — *Film as Art* (1932; revised 1957).**
Edition of record: University of California Press, 1957 (revised English edition; the 1957 selection is the canonical text and supersedes the 1933 *Film*). Original German: *Film als Kunst*, Rowohlt, Berlin, 1932.
Justification: Argues that cinema is art precisely because of, not despite, its limitations relative to perception (flat frame, monocular view, absence of space-time continuum, framed image, absence of color in the silent era). Mechanism-level: each constraint generates a formal resource. Cross-link to `perception/` (Arnheim is already canon there).
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/theory-of-the-medium/`.

**Jean Mitry — *Esthétique et psychologie du cinéma* (2 vols., 1963, 1965).**
Edition of record (French): Éditions Universitaires, Paris, 1963/1965. English: Christopher King (tr.), *The Aesthetics and Psychology of the Cinema*, Indiana University Press, 1997 (single combined volume).
Justification: The most exhaustive systematic synthesis of classical film theory, treating image, montage, rhythm, sound, color, and space at mechanism level. Discipline-standard monograph; the bridging text between the Eisenstein/Bazin generation and the semiotic turn.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/theory-of-the-medium/`.

**Gilles Deleuze — *Cinéma 1: L'image-mouvement* (1983); *Cinéma 2: L'image-temps* (1985).**
Edition of record (French): Éditions de Minuit, Paris, 1983/1985. English: Hugh Tomlinson and Barbara Habberjam (1) and Hugh Tomlinson and Robert Galeta (2), University of Minnesota Press, 1986/1989.
Justification: Primary statement of the image-classification framework derived from Bergson and Peirce — perception-image, affection-image, action-image (vol. 1), then time-image, crystal-image, opsign/sonsign (vol. 2). Originator of a named foundational framework now standard in moving-image scholarship.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/theory-of-the-medium/`.

**Christian Metz — *Essais sur la signification au cinéma* (1968).**
Edition of record (French): Klincksieck, Paris, 1968. English: Michael Taylor (tr.), *Film Language: A Semiotics of the Cinema*, Oxford University Press, 1974; reprint University of Chicago Press, 1991 (ISBN 0226521303). Companion text: *Langage et cinéma*, Larousse, 1971; English tr. Donna Jean Umiker-Sebeok, Mouton, 1974.
Justification: Founding text of film semiotics. Introduces the *grande syntagmatique* as a typology of how shots combine into narrative segments. Originator of the framework.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/semiotics/`.

**Vsevolod Pudovkin — *Film Technique* (1926) and *Film Acting* (1934).**
Edition of record: Ivor Montagu (tr.), single combined English volume *Film Technique and Film Acting*, Vision Press, London, 1954; reprint Grove Press, 1970. Original Russian essays 1926, 1934 (PD).
Justification: Primary statement of the constructive-editing position (alongside Eisenstein but distinct — building, not colliding). The five editing relations (contrast, parallelism, symbolism, simultaneity, leit-motif) are the standard reference.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/montage/`.

**Dziga Vertov — collected writings.**
Edition of record: Annette Michelson (ed.), Kevin O'Brien (tr.), *Kino-Eye: The Writings of Dziga Vertov*, University of California Press, 1984.
Justification: Primary statement of the kino-eye / kino-pravda position — the camera as a perceptual apparatus superior to the human eye, montage as the construction of an "interval". Originator. Sits in productive disagreement with Eisenstein and Pudovkin and is required reading to understand the Soviet dispute as a three-way one.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/montage/`.

**Lev Kuleshov — *Art of the Cinema* and related writings.**
Edition of record: Ronald Levaco (ed./tr.), *Kuleshov on Film: Writings of Lev Kuleshov*, University of California Press, 1974.
Justification: Primary source for the Kuleshov effect — the empirical demonstration that meaning is generated by shot juxtaposition rather than shot content. The foundational experimental result of montage theory.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/montage/`.

**David Bordwell and Kristin Thompson — poetics-of-cinema corpus.**
Anchor texts: Bordwell, *Narration in the Fiction Film*, University of Wisconsin Press, 1985; Bordwell, *On the History of Film Style*, Harvard, 1997; Bordwell, *Poetics of Cinema*, Routledge, 2008; Thompson, *Breaking the Glass Armor: Neoformalist Film Analysis*, Princeton, 1988; Bordwell, Staiger, Thompson, *The Classical Hollywood Cinema: Film Style and Mode of Production to 1960*, Columbia, 1985.
Justification: Discipline-standard monographs advancing the neo-formalist / historical-poetics framework. Mechanism-level (norms, schemata, modes of narration). The reference text for formal analysis of the moving image since the mid-1980s. *Film Art: An Introduction* is pedagogy and does NOT promote — it sits in landscape.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/poetics-and-formal-analysis/`.

### Borderline canon

**Noël Burch — *Praxis du cinéma* (1969); English: *Theory of Film Practice*, Helen R. Lane (tr.), Praeger, 1973; reprint Princeton University Press, 1981.**
Justification: Originator framework for "spatial articulations" (the fifteen possible relations between two consecutive shots in terms of off-screen and on-screen space). Strong on a narrow mechanism. Borderline because Burch later partially repudiated the structuralist claims; the framework itself is canon-eligible, the larger book is mixed.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/poetics-and-formal-analysis/`.

**Étienne Souriau (ed.) — *L'Univers filmique* (1953).**
Edition of record: Flammarion, Paris, 1953. No complete English translation. Foundational filmology vocabulary (afilmique, profilmique, filmographique, diégétique, écranique, spectatoriel) is the durable contribution.
Justification: Originator of the diegetic/non-diegetic distinction now standard everywhere downstream (especially in film-music theory). Borderline because the volume is a multi-author research-program report rather than a single primary text; promotion would be on the strength of the terminological apparatus.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/semiotics/` with cross-ref to music-theory.

### Flagged — keep out of canon, log in landscape

Siegfried Kracauer, *Theory of Film* (1960) — historicized realism thesis is largely subsumed by Bazin and Mitry; landscape.
Béla Balázs, *Theory of the Film* (1949) — early but largely descriptive; landscape unless a specific mechanism-claim chapter is excerpted.
Laura Mulvey, "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975) — single essay, programmatic; cite as reference, not canon.

## 2. Lighting / geometry / angle / color / music — sub-domain map

### Lighting

**John Alton — *Painting With Light*, Macmillan, 1949; reprint University of California Press, 1995, with introduction by Todd McCarthy.**
The primary practitioner statement of cinematographic lighting as a formal system: keys, fills, backlights, the geometry of the three-point setup, low-key noir derivations. Originator-tier within the craft tradition. Strong canon candidate for `09-art/cinema/cinematography/` (or for a `craft-foundations/` cross-link).

**Joseph V. Mascelli — *The Five C's of Cinematography*, Cine/Grafic Publications, Hollywood, 1965; current: Silman-James reprint.**
Codifies the five operating constraints (camera angles, continuity, cutting, close-ups, composition). Discipline-standard practitioner text. Borderline canon — pedagogical in form but mechanism-level in content.

Cross-link to physics: photometry and the inverse-square law live in `02-physics/optics`. The cinematographic disposition of light is a separate, art-side proposition.

### Color

**Patti Bellantoni — *If It's Purple, Someone's Gonna Die: The Power of Color in Visual Storytelling*, Focal Press, 2005.**
Mechanism claim about color affect on screen. Discipline-standard within cinematography pedagogy but borderline at canon level — empirical base is anecdotal corpus rather than controlled study. Flag rather than promote; cross-link to `color-form/`.

**Stan Brakhage — *Metaphors on Vision* (1963; critical edition: Anthology Film Archives / Light Industry, ed. P. Adams Sitney, expanded 2017).**
Originator statement on the perceptual basis of non-objective filmic color and rhythm. Borderline — closer to artist's manifesto than to mechanism — but the perceptual claims (hypnagogic vision, "an untutored eye") are cited as foundational by the avant-garde lineage.

For the underlying color science, Goethe, Chevreul, Itten, and Albers are already canon in `09-art/color-form/`. There is no need to duplicate them in a cinema sub-branch; cinema texts on color sit downstream of those.

David Batchelor's *Chromophobia* (Reaktion, 2000) is landscape, not canon.

### Framing geometry, screen space, angle

The two anchors are already canon in `perception/`: Arnheim (Gestalt of the picture plane) and Wölfflin (the five polar pairs, applicable to any framed image). Cinema-specific extensions:

**Burch — spatial articulations** (above): the mechanism for off-screen / on-screen space.
**Bordwell — "Camera Movement and Cinematic Space"** in *Poetics of Cinema* (2008) and the staging chapters of *Figures Traced in Light* (University of California Press, 2005): the mechanism for staging in depth.
**Thompson — *Breaking the Glass Armor*** (1988) on neoformalist analysis of frame and staging.

A dedicated "framing" sub-folder is not required if these texts are filed under `cinema/poetics-and-formal-analysis/`.

### Sound and image

**Michel Chion — *L'Audio-Vision: Son et image au cinéma*, Nathan, 1990.**
Edition of record: English: Claudia Gorbman (tr./ed.), *Audio-Vision: Sound on Screen*, Columbia University Press, 1994; revised 2nd ed. Columbia, 2019.
Strong canon. Originator of the synchresis mechanism (the forced perceptual welding of a coincident sound and image), the typology of listening modes (causal, semantic, reduced — the last from Schaeffer), and the added-value account of how sound shapes the image. Cross-links into `music-theory/` and into `perception/`.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/sound-image/`.

**Claudia Gorbman — *Unheard Melodies: Narrative Film Music*, Indiana University Press / BFI, 1987.**
Discipline-standard monograph advancing a named framework (the seven principles of classical narrative film scoring; the diegetic/non-diegetic distinction inherited from Souriau and made standard for music). Strong canon.
Proposed: `09-art/cinema/sound-image/`.

**Pierre Schaeffer — *Traité des objets musicaux*, Seuil, 1966.**
Already a candidate for `music-theory/` (acousmatic / reduced listening, the *objet sonore*). Cross-link to cinema sound-image. Edition of record French: Seuil, 1966, reissued 2002. English: Christine North and John Dack (tr.), *Treatise on Musical Objects*, University of California Press, 2017. Strong canon, primary author for the music-theory side; cinema cites him via Chion.

### Music and emotion (cross-link to existing music-theory canon)

Juslin & Sloboda (eds.), *Handbook of Music and Emotion: Theory, Research, Applications*, Oxford University Press, 2010, and Annabel Cohen's congruence-association model (in journal articles, not yet a single monograph) are the empirical anchors. These belong to `music-theory/` or to `perception/`, not to a cinema sub-branch. A film-music sub-folder under `cinema/sound-image/` cross-references them.

## 3. Gap analysis vs existing 09-art canon

### Where cinema texts extend an existing branch

- Chion *Audio-Vision* extends `music-theory/` into the synchronized image-sound regime. Synchresis is a perceptual mechanism that has no analog in pure music theory or pure picture theory.
- Arnheim *Film as Art* extends Arnheim's own *Art and Visual Perception* (already in `perception/`) to the moving frame. Filing both Arnheims under the same author cross-link is the cleanest carving.
- Bordwell and Thompson extend Wölfflin's formal-analysis program (already in `perception/`) into the time-based image. The methodological lineage is direct and worth annotating.
- Alton *Painting With Light* extends `craft-foundations/` (Cennini, Alberti, Leonardo on light and pigment) into the light-only craft of cinematography.

### Where cinema texts open a new sub-branch

The bulk of cinema theory (Münsterberg, Eisenstein, Bazin, Mitry, Deleuze, Metz, Pudovkin, Vertov, Kuleshov) is not absorbed by existing branches. It addresses a distinct object — the moving photographic image plus its temporal articulation — for which no current `09-art/` sub-folder is the right home. A new sub-branch is justified.

Recommended folder name: `09-art/cinema/` (not `moving-image/`). Rationale: "moving image" is broader (includes video art, animation, computer-generated imagery, GIFs, scroll-based UI, generative video). Bucket can open a separate `moving-image/` later if and when canon-tier texts on those distinct objects are identified. Starting narrow with `cinema/` keeps the promotion bar honest.

Recommended internal structure:

```
09-art/cinema/
  README.md
  CANON_INDEX.md
  theory-of-the-medium/    Münsterberg, Arnheim Film as Art, Mitry, Deleuze 1+2

  semiotics/               Metz, Souriau (flagged)
  poetics-and-formal-analysis/  Bordwell, Thompson, Burch (flagged)
  cinematography/          Alton, Mascelli (flagged)
  sound-image/             Chion, Gorbman; cross-ref Schaeffer
```

### Holes in the current 09-art canon that this sweep exposes

Three real gaps, ranked.

1. **No canon on perception of motion or temporal aesthetics.** `perception/` covers the static picture (Arnheim, Gombrich, Wölfflin, Panofsky, Baxandall). It has nothing on apparent motion, the phi phenomenon (Wertheimer 1912), persistence of vision (long since corrected by short-range and long-range apparent-motion theory), or the temporal grouping rules that any moving-image theory presupposes. Wertheimer's *Experimentelle Studien über das Sehen von Bewegung* (Zeitschrift für Psychologie 61, 1912) is a direct candidate for `perception/`. This is the biggest gap.
2. **No canon on the apparatus / phenomenology of viewing.** The space between the eye and the picture (theatre, frame, projection, screen, viewer attention) is theorized by Münsterberg, Bazin, the apparatus-theory tradition (Baudry, Comolli — landscape), and Sobchack (*The Address of the Eye*, Princeton, 1992 — borderline canon). `perception/` could absorb a phenomenology-of-pictures sub-folder, or the cinema sub-branch could carry it.
3. **No canon on photography as a distinct medium.** Roland Barthes *La Chambre claire* (1980), Susan Sontag *On Photography* (1977), and most importantly Joel Snyder & Neil Walsh Allen "Photography, Vision, and Representation" (*Critical Inquiry*, 1975), plus the Vilém Flusser *Towards a Philosophy of Photography* (1983), would constitute a `09-art/photography/` sub-branch. Flagged because this sweep was scoped to cinema, but the gap is real and adjacent: cinema theory cites photography theory upstream constantly (Bazin's ontology argument *is* a photography argument).

## 4. Landscape memo — film and global culture (deep-history tier, NOT canon)

**Target folder if accepted:** `08-deep-history/` or a sibling research-landscape area on cultural history. This is not a candidate for `09-art/` canon. Approximately 600 words.

The Lumière brothers projected paying audiences in Paris in December 1895, and Méliès, a stage magician, recognized within months that the same apparatus could fabricate the impossible rather than record the actual. That split — actuality versus construction — has structured every subsequent argument in the medium. Edison's Kinetoscope had been in arcades since 1893 in the United States, but the projected image to a seated public is the cultural fact. Within a decade, fixed-camera tableaux gave way to continuity editing in Brighton, Britain (Smith, Williamson) and at Biograph (Griffith), and the medium acquired the syntactic basics that classical Hollywood would later ossify.

The First World War broke European production and handed market dominance to the United States. The 1920s in turn produced the medium's first three deeply theorized national schools: German Expressionism (1919–27, *Caligari* through *Metropolis*), French Impressionism and the avant-garde (Epstein, Dulac, Buñuel), and Soviet montage (Kuleshov, Eisenstein, Pudovkin, Vertov, Dovzhenko). The Soviets weaponized cinema for political education explicitly, and the *Battleship Potemkin* Odessa-steps sequence is the first piece of moving-image rhetoric whose effect on a public was demonstrably engineered from the editing table outward.

Sound arrived in 1927 and the studio system locked into place: vertically integrated production-distribution-exhibition, the star system, the Hays Code from 1934. American cinema became a soft-power instrument before anyone in Washington had a name for it. Newsreels (Pathé, Movietone, Universal) carried the war into theatres on both sides; Riefenstahl's *Triumph des Willens* (1935) and *Olympia* (1938) demonstrated that the documentary apparatus inherited from Vertov could be turned to fascist ends without changing a single technical principle. Allied propaganda (Capra's *Why We Fight*, Jennings in Britain) answered in kind. By 1945 the medium had moved entire publics through grief, hatred, mobilization, and reconciliation.

The postwar new waves — Italian neorealism first (Rossellini, De Sica, Visconti), then the Nouvelle Vague in France, Free Cinema in Britain, the Czechoslovak New Wave, New German Cinema, Cinema Novo in Brazil, the Iranian New Wave from the late 1960s through Kiarostami, the Japanese postwar masters (Ozu, Mizoguchi, Kurosawa, then Imamura and Oshima), Satyajit Ray and the parallel cinema in India, and from the 1990s onward Nollywood as a high-volume direct-to-video industry serving a continent — each rebuilt the moral imagination of a public after a rupture. Italian neorealism is the clearest case: the bombed-out streets and amateur cast of *Roma città aperta* (1945) and *Ladri di biciclette* (1948) gave Western Europe a self-image at scale that no other medium produced as fast.

American cinema had its own rupture in the late 1960s and 1970s (the so-called New Hollywood: *Bonnie and Clyde*, *The Graduate*, the auteurs through to *Apocalypse Now*), then re-corporatized through the blockbuster (*Jaws* 1975, *Star Wars* 1977) into the franchise era. Bollywood scaled across the Indian diaspora; Hong Kong action cinema reshaped global choreography; in the 21st century Korean cinema and television, deliberately funded as cultural export from the late 1990s, became the most studied state-backed soft-power success since Hollywood itself.

Digital capture, non-linear editing, and streaming distribution erased the marginal cost of distribution. The platform era (Netflix, YouTube, then TikTok) shifted the unit of attention from the two-hour film to the sub-minute clip and put algorithmic recommendation between maker and audience. The result by the mid-2020s is that the dominant moving-image culture is no longer cinema-as-cinema — it is short-form, vertical, sound-on-by-default, and trained on engagement metrics rather than authored intent.

**What Bucket should do.** Open `09-art/cinema/`. The minimum viable seed is five texts: Münsterberg 1916 (the medium as a mental act), Eisenstein *Film Form* (the construction position), Bazin Barnard 2009 (the ontological-realist position), Deleuze *Cinema 1+2* (the standing classification framework), and Chion *Audio-Vision* 1994/2019 (sound-image, with a mechanism nobody else owns). Those five cover the medium's two-pole dispute, its standing taxonomy, and the only foundational text on the audiovisual synthesis. Everything else in section 1 can sit in `_intake/` until promoted by the same rule that admitted Wölfflin, Helmholtz, and Alberti.
