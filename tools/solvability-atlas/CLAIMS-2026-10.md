A confirmation that names an expert or a refereed venue is the single path to `solved`. Schema: `records/SCHEMA.md`, Claims.

## Mapping

The mapping was derived from the repository's own catalogue at commit `fd4aeeb2e`, in 71 requests: `CONTENTS.md` for the 372 family statements and 64 Lean scope notes under `lean/docs/`. Each family statement was embedded with the shipped model and compared with all 5,209 rows by similarity and by shared proper names, and each candidate pair was then read by hand against the row's stored statement. The catalogue carries no Erdős problem numbers, so Erdős rows were matched by statement. `claims/openai-math-2026-10.rows.md` lists every entry with its family, match, Lean scope and reason; the ledger holds the statement claimed and the scope note text.

The first-pass list in the news report of 2026-10-12 named 43 scored rows. This mapping keeps 41 of them and adds 25 scored rows: 13 Erdős rows the report held in a separate count and 12 it missed, among them the Gaussian moat problem, Siegel zeros, the Borel conjecture, the strong sensitivity conjecture, Guy's crossing number conjecture and the finitely presented Burnside question. Two report rows became notes: the Eisenbud-Green-Harris row, where the claim covers characteristic zero and the row names no restriction, and the De Giorgi row for dimensions up to eight, where the claim is dimension eight and the variant for that dimension is the claimed row.

Ten claimed rows have a Lean file that covers less than the row:

| Row | What the Lean file covers |
|---|---|
| Flint Hills series | the irrationality exponent of pi; the convergence consequence is outside |
| Erdős 142, the o(N / log N) bound | the reciprocal-sum statement; the quantitative bound is outside |
| Sidorenko for graphons | the finite graph counterexample |
| Barker sequences | even lengths |

Rows listed and left unmarked: the bounded Brennan spectrum at minus two, where the catalogue states a neighbouring value, and De Giorgi in dimensions four to seven, which the catalogue does not name. Erdős problems 293, 604 and 802 sit on a third-party list of 23 and have no atlas row. Four Erdős rows on that list stay open with a note: 120 as a special case, and 148, 508 and 970 as weaker bounds, the last with the bound claimed and the order of magnitude open.

## Reading

The two baselines agree, and the reading rests on the one that predates the release. The frontier does not separate what this batch claimed from what it left. Before the release, inside rows are claimed at 5.2% and outside rows at 4.2%; the interval for the difference runs from -1.6 to 3.4 points and holds zero. Reach ranks a claimed row above an unclaimed one 49.5% of the time, which is chance. At the parent of this work the rates are 4.9% and 5.0% and the AUC is 0.496. The 2021 backtest of the frontier paper measures a different outcome on a different cohort, dated rows that the source lists record as solved over five years, and reports 13% inside against 1% outside. On this batch the two rates are level.

The class the atlas ranks first carries no advantage. Before the release the three classes sit at 4.8%, 5.3% and 4.2% over every full claim. Under the Lean restriction the top class is the lowest in both baselines, 2.4% against 3.6% and 3.6% before the release and 2.6% against 3.3% and 4.1% at the parent. The intervals overlap in every case, so the order itself is not established.

The Erdős rows give a reach AUC of 0.655 in both baselines, with an interval that reaches 0.497. The top class is claimed at 6.1% against 2.4% for borderline before the release. The outside class holds 19 rows with one claimed there and 13 rows with none at the parent, so the difference from outside is unreadable in both.

Fourteen outside rows are claimed in the pre-release state, twelve of them with a Lean statement of the full problem, among them the Unique Games Conjecture and L = RL. The atlas labelled each as needing a new idea.

The table over every branch shows +3.6 points before the release and +3.5 at the parent, with a permutation p of 0.0001. That is branch composition: most outside rows sit in branches the batch never attempted, and they enter the denominator with no chance of a claim. It is the reason the test is restricted to mathematics and information.

## Caveats

- **Unconfirmed.** Every row here is a claim. Three manuscripts were withdrawn and 14 revised within a day of release. The three withdrawn manuscripts had left the catalogue by the pinned commit, so no ledger entry cites one. The revisions named in `history.md` fall in families 002, 034 and 376, which the ledger holds as notes on rows that stay open, and in families with no atlas row; no claimed row sits in a family with a named revision. A withdrawn entry returns its row to open and leaves these tables.
- **Selection.** One system attempted about 4,000 problems that its lab chose, and the list is unpublished. The rows it claimed are a selected sample of what is solvable, and the atlas rows it never attempted cannot be told from the ones it attempted and failed. A null here says reach did not predict this lab's successes among atlas rows; it leaves open whether reach predicts solvability for other solvers.
- **Scope.** Ten claimed rows have a Lean file that covers less than the row and 20 have none. The restricted table keeps the 45 scored rows whose scope note states the full problem, and scope notes are the claimant's own description of its files.
- **Matching.** The mapping was made by hand from catalogue text and stored statements. It misses problems the atlas lists under another name, and variants were marked only where the catalogue statement covers them, so 79 is a lower bound.
- **One problem, several rows.** Some problems appear under two source lists. The bootstrap resamples them as one group; the counts in the tables are rows.
- **Text.** Reach is similarity of statement text under one small encoder. A claim on a row far from every solved row shows that the row's wording was far from solved wording. Whether the proof used a new method is a separate question the test cannot answer.

## What Would Change The Reading

Each condition is checked by rerunning `claims_frontier.py` after the ledger changes.

1. **Confirmation.** Once at least 30 claimed rows carry a confirmation, the table is rerun on confirmed rows alone. An inside minus outside interval that excludes zero on the positive side, with a reach AUC interval above 0.5, supports the frontier out of sample. The same intervals holding zero and 0.5 on confirmed rows bound the effect on confirmed outcomes within those intervals.
2. **Withdrawals.** If withdrawals fall on outside rows at more than twice the inside rate, the claims the atlas called far were the weak ones, and the present null overstates how well the model did outside.
3. **The denominator.** If the list of about 4,000 attempted problems is published, the test moves to attempted rows, where a failure is observed. A higher success rate inside than outside there would support the frontier even with equal rates over the whole atlas.
4. **A second solver.** A batch from another lab or from human work over the same months, with a different order by class, would show this result to be one system's choice of problems.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 claims_build.py            # ledger from the hand map and the catalogue
python3 claims.py --apply          # status into the site data and the records
python3 claims_frontier.py         # the tables above
python3 -m pytest tests/test_claims.py tests/test_claims_frontier.pypython3 embed_ablation.py --bench embeddinggemma-2   # speed and peak memory, under memrun
python3 embed_ablation.py && python3 embed_compare.py   # needs the model in the local Hugging Face cache
```

`claims_frontier.py --freeze` rewrites both baseline tables from their commits, and `ATLAS_FULL_REPEAT=1` makes the test rerun all 2,000 draws and compare the whole result file.
