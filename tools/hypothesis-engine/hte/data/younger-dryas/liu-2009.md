abstract: >
  Model Behavior The initial pulse of warming during the last deglaciation, which defined the start of
  an interval called the Bølling-Allerød, occurred abruptly about 14,500 years ago. To date, the most
  detailed simulations used models of intermediate complexity, not with more sophisticated Coupled
  Global Climate Models (CGCMs) that can synchronously couple both oceanic and the atmospheric
  components. Overcoming practical and technical challenges, Liu et al. (p. 310 ; see the Perspective
  by Timmermann and Menviel ) performed such a simulation using CCSM3, a state-of-the-art ocean-
  atmosphere CGCM. In contrast to previous studies, which indicated that the Bølling-Allerød was
  triggered by a nonlinear bifurcation between modes of deep ocean circulation in the Atlantic, the
  results suggest that the event was a transient response caused by the cessation of meltwater input
  into the surface ocean in the North Atlantic region.
