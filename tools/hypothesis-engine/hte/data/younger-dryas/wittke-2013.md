abstract: >
  Significance We present detailed geochemical and morphological analyses of nearly 700 spherules from
  18 sites in support of a major cosmic impact at the onset of the Younger Dryas episode (12.8 ka).
  The impact distributed ∼10 million tonnes of melted spherules over 50 million square kilometers on
  four continents. Origins of the spherules by volcanism, anthropogenesis, authigenesis, lightning,
  and meteoritic ablation are rejected on geochemical and morphological grounds. The spherules closely
  resemble known impact materials derived from surficial sediments melted at temperatures >2,200 °C.
  The spherules correlate with abundances of associated melt-glass, nanodiamonds, carbon spherules,
  aciniform carbon, charcoal, and iridium.
