abstract: >
  Abstract Previously, a large platinum (Pt) anomaly was reported in the Greenland ice sheet at the
  Younger Dryas boundary (YDB) (12,800 Cal B.P.). In order to evaluate its geographic extent, fire-
  assay and inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (FA and ICP-MS) elemental analyses were
  performed on 11 widely separated archaeological bulk sedimentary sequences. We document discovery of
  a distinct Pt anomaly spread widely across North America and dating to the Younger Dryas (YD) onset.
  The apparent synchroneity of this widespread YDB Pt anomaly is consistent with Greenland Ice Sheet
  Project 2 (GISP2) data that indicated atmospheric input of platinum-rich dust. We expect the Pt
  anomaly to serve as a widely-distributed time marker horizon (datum) for identification and
  correlation of the onset of the YD climatic episode at 12,800 Cal B.P. This Pt datum will facilitate
  the dating and correlating of archaeological, paleontological, and paleoenvironmental data between
  sequences, especially those with limited age control.
