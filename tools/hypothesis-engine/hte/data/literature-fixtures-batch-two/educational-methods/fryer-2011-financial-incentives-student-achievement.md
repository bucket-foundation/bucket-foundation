# Financial Incentives and Student Achievement

Randomized field experiments across several US cities: paying students for academic
inputs raised achievement in some cities; paying for outputs, test scores, raised
achievement in none.

## Key Claims

- Output-based pay (test scores) produced no significant achievement gain in either
  city where it was tested.
- Input-based pay (books read, attendance, assignments) produced significant gains in
  at least one of two cities tested.
- No single incentive design worked at every site; effects varied by city.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Why input-based pay worked in some cities but not others.
- Whether input incentives generalize from reading and attendance to a knowledge-graph
  system's own logged input behaviors.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Points the payout design toward paying for the process the four-tool workspace already
logs (a located quote, a run check) rather than only the final accepted grade, since
paying on tier alone is the output-based design that failed in Fryer's own trials.
Extends `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q13 and Q14.
