# Nobel Turing Challenge

Proposes a staged research program toward an AI system capable of Nobel-worthy
scientific discovery, and defines discovery along a spectrum from confirming a known
result to formulating an entirely new theoretical framework.

## Key Claims

- The paper proposes a grand challenge culminating in an AI system that autonomously
  hypothesizes, experiments, and produces a discovery judged Nobel-worthy.
- Discovery is defined along a spectrum: confirming a known hypothesis, finding an
  unexpected result, formulating a new framework.
- Autonomous experimentation, not only hypothesis proposal, is named as the binding
  constraint separating current systems from the spectrum's higher end.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What intermediate milestones on the spectrum are reachable short of the full
  grand-challenge target.
- Whether the confirmation-to-new-framework spectrum maps onto a K-12 learner's own
  progression.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Offers a graded notion of discovery closer to Research OS's own five-state ladder than
a binary correctness grade, bearing on the overlap map's question 12. Its spectrum
also gives `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q6 a way to score whether a routed gap node
produces a confirming production or a genuinely gap-closing one.
