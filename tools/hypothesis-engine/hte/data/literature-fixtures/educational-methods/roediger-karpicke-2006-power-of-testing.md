# The Power of Testing Memory: Basic Research and Implications for Educational Practice

The empirical basis for spaced retrieval and the testing effect, the mechanism the FSRS spaced-review scheduler already shipping in Academy's mastery.ts is built to exploit.

## Key Claims

- Retrieval practice (taking a test) produces more durable long-term retention than an equivalent amount of restudying, even when restudying produces better performance on an immediate test.
- The benefit of testing over restudying grows with the retention interval; testing effects are often invisible on an immediate post-test and only appear after days or weeks.
- Repeated testing outperforms repeated study for long-term retention, contrary to most students' own study strategies.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which retrieval formats (recall versus recognition, cued versus free) produce the largest effect, and how that generalizes across subjects.
- Why students underuse testing as a study strategy despite its benefit, a question with direct implications for how a workspace should present spaced review.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Justifies FSRS's placement inside the Understanding-to-Internalization pipeline: a node re-surfaced for retrieval at a computed interval outperforms a re-read prompt, so the spaced-review scheduler's item bank should default to recall-style items over recognition-style ones wherever the domain allows a deterministic check.
