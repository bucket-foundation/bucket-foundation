\title{Human-AI-Computer Interaction: Measuring Whether AI Strengthens Unaided Judgment}
\author{Gianangelo Dichio\\Bucket Foundation}
\date{2026-10-07}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A tool that helps a person answer today and leaves them no better at answering alone next week has made them dependent on it. We state a measurement protocol that separates the two outcomes. On one task set, three scores are taken: the person's \term{hp}{unaided score} $H_p$, the model's \term{a}{AI score} $A$, and the person's \term{jp}{joint score} $J_p$ with the model's answer in view. The \term{mp}{multiplier} is $m_p = J_p / \max(H_p, A)$, the gain is $D = J_p - \max(H_p, A)$, and the unaided test is repeated after seven days to give \term{rp}{retention} $R_p = H_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d}) - H_p(t)$ and learning $L = J_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d}) - H_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d})$. A tool that raises $J_p$ while $L$ stays at or below zero across its interval marks \term{dependence}{dependence}. The instrument is the \texttt{hai} probe in Bucket's \texttt{bkt} package: a frozen bank of 998 four-choice items across the seven canon branches, of which 68 are held out by an automated distractor review \bank; sessions of 40 items in 20 pairs matched on tier and on whether the model got the pair's items right; a 20 second think window before the model's answer appears; no feedback until the retest; guess-corrected scores and a bootstrap over pairs for every interval \hai. The learning side of the measurement is the minimum prerequisite learning problem: the catalog is a directed acyclic graph of concepts, a learner's state is a closed set of mastered concepts, and the least a learner must add to reach a target is the \term{required}{required set} $R(t, M)$, the target's \term{closure}{prerequisite closure} minus their mastery, a count proved minimal in Lean 4 under stated assumptions. Per-concept \term{mastery}{mastery} is $M = P^{\alpha} R^{\beta}$ with FSRS-5 \term{retrievability}{retrievability} as $R$ and $\alpha = \beta = 1$ as implemented. The protocol draws probe items at the learner's \term{frontier}{frontier}, the ready concepts of that graph, and reads $R_p$ against the FSRS prediction. The instrument is built and no probe has been retested, so this report carries no result on a person; the learning system's own evaluation, 57,060 enumerated planning cases with zero errors and a synthetic prediction experiment, is reported with its source. The report states the planned analysis, paired comparisons stratified by education level, a divergence hypothesis on how reliance on one model narrows the range of answers across a group, and the rule by which the scores decide which tasks Research OS hands to the model and which it keeps with the person.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Research OS puts a model next to a person on every task in the product: recall of a result, application of a rule, derivation, teaching. Each of those tasks can be left with the person, augmented with the model, or handed to the model. The product has no basis for that choice until it can measure, per person and per task class, what the model adds today and what the person keeps a week later. Field studies report the first number, the lift with the model in hand \autocite{dellacqua2023jagged,brynjolfsson2023generative}, and a meta-analysis across 106 experiments finds that human and AI together score below the better of the two on average \autocite{vaccaro2024combinations}. The second number, what the person retains, is the one a learning product cannot do without: in a randomized study of high school mathematics, access to a model raised practice scores and lowered unaided exam scores \autocite{bastani2025guardrails}. This report defines both numbers on the same items, describes the instrument that takes them, and says what will be done with them.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Complementary team performance, the case where the pair beats both the person and the model, was named and tested in a user study of AI explanations, which found explanations raised acceptance of the model's answer whether it was right or wrong \autocite{bansal2021whole}. The jagged frontier study randomized consultants to model access and found gains inside the model's competence and losses outside it \autocite{dellacqua2023jagged}; the customer-support study found the largest gains for the least experienced workers \autocite{brynjolfsson2023generative}. The retention arm of this protocol follows the testing-effect literature, in which an unaided retrieval test is itself the strongest predictor of later recall \autocite{roediger2006testing}. The divergence hypothesis of \Cref{sec:divergence} follows a study in which model assistance raised the rated quality of each writer's story and lowered the diversity across writers \autocite{doshi2024diversity}. Intervals use the pair bootstrap \autocite{efron1979bootstrap}.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\begin{definition}[Item and bank]
\label{def:bank}
An item is a prompt with four choices, one correct, drawn from the Academy pack and frozen with three distractors fixed under a seed, so the same text is shown to every person and to the model. The bank is versioned by a hash of its items \hai. An automated review flags an item whose distractors duplicate each other, contain or overlap the answer by token Jaccard at or above 0.5, answer a second question on the same atom, or whose answer runs to at least twice the length of its longest distractor; a flagged item stays out of probes and scoring until a person clears it \hai.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scores]
\label{def:scores}
For $c$ correct answers out of $n$ four-choice items, the guess-corrected score is $s = (c/n - 1/4)/(3/4)$, so chance scores 0 and a perfect run scores 1 \hai. The \term{hp}{unaided score} $H_p$ is $s$ over a person's solo items; the \term{jp}{joint score} $J_p$ is $s$ over their pair items; the \term{a}{AI score} $A$ is $s$ for the model over those same pair items.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Probe]
\label{def:probe}
A \term{probe}{probe} is 40 unseen bank items in 20 pairs. Items are grouped by tier and by whether the model got them right, pairs are drawn inside a group, and a seeded coin sends one item of each pair to the solo condition and the other to the pair condition \hai. In the pair condition the model's choice and a rationale of at most 25 words appear after 20 seconds; the person may accept or override, and the acceptance is recorded \hai. No correctness is shown on any probe item, and probe items never enter spaced review, until the retest ends \hai.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Retest]
\label{def:retest}
Seven days after a probe, the person answers all 40 of its items again, unaided, in a reshuffled order \hai. Scores and answers appear only after the retest.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Multiplier and gain]
\label{def:multiplier}
The \term{mp}{multiplier} is $m_p = J_p / \max(H_p, A)$, reported as null when $\max(H_p, A) < 0.1$; the gain is $D = J_p - \max(H_p, A)$ \hai. $D$ is the primary statistic and $m_p$ is reported beside it.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Retention and learning]
\label{def:retention}
\term{rp}{Retention} is $R_p = H_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d}) - H_p(t)$ over the solo items. Learning is $L = J_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d}) - H_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d})$: at the retest, the score on items first seen with the model minus the score on items first seen alone, both unaided \hai.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dependence]
\label{def:dependence}
\term{dependence}{Dependence} is flagged when the 95\% interval of $J_p - H_p$ lies above zero and the 95\% interval of $L$ includes or lies below zero \hai. The gain was there with the model in the room and gone a week later.
\end{definition}

\section{The learning system}
\label{sec:learning}

The protocol of \Cref{sec:prelim} measures a person on items. The learning system says which items, and what a person's state is before and after. Its contract is the minimum prerequisite learning problem stated in the learning-system brief and checked in Lean 4 \brief, and its per-concept state is the mastery formula of the Academy engine \mast.

\subsection{The minimum prerequisite learning problem}
\label{sec:minprereq}

\begin{definition}[Catalog, closed state, frontier]
\label{def:catalog}
The catalog is a finite set of concepts $V$ with a directed prerequisite relation $E \subseteq V \times V$, where $p \to v$ means $p$ is mandatory before $v$, and the implementation rejects any cycle, so $(V, E)$ is a directed acyclic graph. A knowledge state is a set $K \subseteq V$. $K$ is closed when every immediate prerequisite of every member is a member: $p \to v$ and $v \in K$ imply $p \in K$. A concept $v \notin K$ is ready, and lies on the \term{frontier}{frontier} of $K$, when every $p$ with $p \to v$ lies in $K$ \bm{LearningSystem.Closed}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure and required set]
\label{def:required}
For a target $t$, the \term{closure}{prerequisite closure} is the target with every concept on a prerequisite path into it, and the \term{required}{required set} removes accepted mastery $M$:
\begin{equation}
  C(t) = \{t\} \cup \{p \in V : p \leadsto t\}, \qquad R(t, M) = C(t) \setminus M .
  \label{eq:required}
\end{equation}
$C(t)$ is the inductive predicate \texttt{Required} and $R$ is its conjunction with $\neg\,\mathrm{known}$ \bm{LearningSystem.Required}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Transition and feasible sequence]
\label{def:transition}
A step adds one fresh ready concept and keeps everything already known:
\begin{equation}
  K_{i+1} = K_i \cup \{v_i\}, \qquad v_i \notin K_i, \qquad (p \to v_i \Rightarrow p \in K_i).
  \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
A finite list $\pi = (v_1, \ldots, v_n)$ is feasible from $K_0$ when every step satisfies \Cref{eq:transition}; it reaches $t$ when $t \in K_0$ or $t$ occurs in $\pi$ \bm{LearningSystem.Path, LearningSystem.After}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cost model]
\label{def:cost}
Each concept carries a fixed cost $w(v) \in \mathbb{N}$, zero allowed. The effort of a sequence and the minimum effort to a target are
\begin{equation}
  W(\pi) = \sum_{v \in \pi} w(v), \qquad c^*(M, t) = \sum_{v \in R(t, M)} w(v),
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
and the unit-cost distance is $d^*(M, t) = \min\{|\pi| : \pi \text{ feasible from } M_C,\ \pi \text{ reaches } t\}$, with $M_C = M \cap C(t)$ the mastery restricted to the closure \bm{LearningSystem.Effort}.
\end{definition}

The brief states five assumptions the results rest on \brief: every encoded edge is mandatory and the snapshot holds every prerequisite the target needs (A1); $C(t)$ is finite and the planner constructs a prerequisite-first order (A2); for each mastered $v \in C(t)$ every immediate prerequisite of $v$ is mastered, so $M_C$ is closed (A3); a step adds one concept and nothing is forgotten during the episode (A4); costs are fixed natural numbers shared across the sequences compared (A5).

\begin{lemma}[Closure is preserved]
\label{lem:closed}
If $K$ is closed and $v$ is ready in $K$, then $K \cup \{v\}$ is closed; by induction every state reached by a feasible sequence from a closed state is closed \bm{LearningSystem.after\_closed}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Target mastery entails ancestor mastery]
\label{lem:ancestors}
If a closed state $K$ contains $t$, then $C(t) \subseteq K$ \bm{LearningSystem.required\_known}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Necessary additions]
\label{thm:necessary}
From a closed $M_C$, every feasible sequence $\pi$ that reaches $t$ contains every member of $R(t, M)$, so $|\pi| \geq |R(t, M)|$ \bm{LearningSystem.remaining\_necessary}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:closed} the final state is closed; by \Cref{lem:ancestors} it contains $C(t)$. The final state is $M_C$ together with the additions of $\pi$, and a member of $R$ is absent from $M_C$, so it is one of the additions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Attained minimum]
\label{thm:minimum}
Let $\pi$ list each element of $R(t, M)$ once in an order where every prerequisite of $v_i$ lies in $M_C$ or earlier in $\pi$. Then $\pi$ is feasible, reaches $t$, and $d^*(M, t) = |R(t, M)|$ \bm{LearningSystem.minimum\_unit\_distance}. With fixed costs the same $\pi$ attains $c^*(M, t)$ \bm{LearningSystem.minimum\_weighted\_effort}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Mastery shrinks the required set]
\label{cor:antitone}
If $M_1 \subseteq M_2$ then $R(t, M_2) \subseteq R(t, M_1)$ and $|R(t, M_2)| \leq |R(t, M_1)|$ \bm{LearningSystem.distance\_antitone}.
\end{corollary}

\Cref{fig:prereq} shows the diamond that separates the theorem from chain planning: $F \to A$, $F \to B$, $A \to T$, $B \to T$ with empty mastery. The chain $F, A, T$ has three nodes and never reaches $T$, since after $\{F, A\}$ the target is still blocked by $B$; the feasible sequence $F, A, B, T$ has four transitions, equal to $|R(T, \emptyset)|$, and $F, B, A, T$ is a second optimum \bm{LearningSystem.diamond\_minimum, diamond\_chain\_insufficient}. The planner's running time with adjacency lists is $O(|C(t)| + |E_C|)$ for $E_C$ the edges inside the closure \brief; this bound is a human argument outside Lean.

\label{sec:leancontract}

The learning-system source is \texttt{lean/\allowbreak Bucket/\allowbreak Learning.\allowbreak lean}, a copy of \texttt{learning/\allowbreak research-os/\allowbreak learning-system/\allowbreak lean/\allowbreak LearningSystem.\allowbreak lean} built by \texttt{make lean} under Lean 4.33.1 with bundled \texttt{Std} and no other dependency \bm{papers/human-ai-multiplier/lean}. \begin{lstlisting}[style=bucketlean]
def Closed (edge : a -> a -> Prop) (known : a -> Prop) : Prop :=
  forall p v, edge p v -> known v -> known p

inductive Required (edge : a -> a -> Prop) (target : a) : a -> Prop where
  | target : Required edge target target
  | prerequisite {p v} : edge p v -> Required edge target v -> Required edge target p

theorem remaining_necessary {edge : a -> a -> Prop} {known : a -> Prop}
    {target v : a} {steps : List a} (hc : Closed edge known)
    (hp : Path edge known steps) (ht : After known steps target)
    (hr : Required edge target v) (hu : ~ known v) : v in steps

theorem minimum_unit_distance {edge : a -> a -> Prop} {known : a -> Prop}
    {target : a} {order : List a} (hc : Closed edge known)
    (en : Enumerates edge known target order) (earlier : Earlier edge known order) :
    Path edge known order /\ After known order target /\
      forall steps, Path edge known steps -> After known steps target -> order.length <= steps.length

theorem minimum_weighted_effort {edge : a -> a -> Prop} {known : a -> Prop}
    {target : a} {order : List a} (weight : a -> Nat) (hc : Closed edge known)

        Effort weight order <= Effort weight steps

\subsection{The knowledge region}
\label{sec:coverage}

\begin{definition}[Coverage]
\label{def:coverage}
Fix a catalog $V$ of factored concepts and a set $J$ of primitive axes; each concept $v$ has a nonempty set of distinct supports $S(v) \subseteq J$. Its allocation and each axis denominator are
\begin{equation}
  A_{vj} = \frac{\mathbf{1}[j \in S(v)]}{|S(v)|}, \qquad d_j = \sum_{v \in V} A_{vj} > 0 .
  \label{eq:alloc}
\end{equation}
With $m(v) \in \{0, 1\}$ the confirmed flag, the confirmed coordinate on axis $j$, the catalog \term{coverage}{coverage} and the radial extent are
\begin{equation}
  g_j = \frac{\sum_v A_{vj}\, m(v)}{d_j}, \qquad G = \frac{\sum_j d_j g_j}{\sum_j d_j}, \qquad r = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_j d_j g_j^2}{\sum_j d_j}} .
  \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Bounds and identity]
\label{prop:bounds}
Every $g_j \in [0, 1]$, $G = \sum_v m(v) / |V|$, and $G \leq r \leq \sqrt{G}$ \brief.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each row of $A$ sums to one, so interchanging sums gives $\sum_j d_j = |V|$ and $\sum_j d_j g_j = \sum_v m(v)$. The weighted variance $\sum_j d_j (g_j - G)^2 / \sum_j d_j = r^2 - G^2 \geq 0$ gives $G \leq r$; $g_j^2 \leq g_j$ on $[0, 1]$ gives $r^2 \leq G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity]
\label{prop:mono}
With $V$, $S$ and $d$ fixed, if no flag $m(v)$ decreases then no $g_j$, $G$ or $r$ decreases \brief. The natural-weight coverage sum and the integer squared extent are the cases proved in Lean \bm{LearningSystem.coverage\_monotone, squared\_extent\_monotone}.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{fig:region} draws the worked fixture: $S(a) = \{a\}$, $S(b) = \{b\}$, $S(t) = \{a, b\}$, so $d_a = d_b = 3/2$; mastering $a$ gives $g = (2/3, 0)$, $G = 1/3$ and $r^2 = 2/9$; adding $b$ gives $(2/3, 2/3)$, $G = 2/3$ and $r^2 = 4/9$; adding $t$ gives $G = r = 1$ \ls. $r$ and $G$ measure different things, since profiles with the same $G$ can differ in $r$, and neither carries a meaning of intelligence or general competence.

\subsection{Mastery and retention as implemented}
\label{sec:mastery}

\begin{definition}[FSRS-5 retrievability]
\label{def:fsrs}
After a review that left a concept at stability $S > 0$ days, the predicted recall $t$ days later is
\begin{equation}
  R(t, S) = \left(1 + F\,\frac{t}{S}\right)^{-1/2}, \qquad F = 0.9^{-2} - 1 = \frac{19}{81},
  \label{eq:fsrs}
\end{equation}
so $R(S, S) = 0.9$ by construction and the next review is scheduled at the interval where $R$ falls to the requested retention $0.9$ \mast. Stability and difficulty update after each rating by the FSRS-5 recurrences with the 19 default weights $w_0 = 0.40255$ through $w_{18} = 0.6621$ \mast.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Mastery]
\label{def:mastery}
Proficiency is a logistic function of the concept's ability estimate $\theta$ against the apply-depth offset $b_{\mathrm{apply}} = -0.2$, retention is retrievability at the 90-day horizon, and \term{mastery}{mastery} is their product:
\begin{equation}
  P = \sigma(\theta - b_{\mathrm{apply}}), \qquad R = R(90, S), \qquad M = P^{\alpha} R^{\beta}, \qquad \alpha = \beta = 1,
  \label{eq:mastery}
\end{equation}
clipped to $[0, 1]$, with $M = 0$ when either factor is zero and $M = 1 - e^{-S/21}$ when no proficiency record exists; a concept counts as mastered at $M \geq 0.7$ \mast.
\end{definition}

The exponents are the \texttt{MASTERY\_ALPHA} and \texttt{MASTERY\_BETA} constants of \texttt{src/\allowbreak lib/\allowbreak academy/\allowbreak mastery.ts}, both $1.0$, so the formula as shipped is the plain product and the exponents are a hook for a later fit rather than a fitted parameter \mast. \Cref{fig:mastery} shows the two factors. The set $M$ in \Cref{sec:minprereq} is the accepted mastery that the planner reads; the mastery formula here is the engine's per-concept signal, and the brief's A3 is the rule that a concept enters $M$ only when its evidence and its prerequisites' evidence satisfy the contract. The mapping from $M \geq 0.7$ to membership in $M$ is a product decision that no theorem covers.

\section{Connecting the learning system to the protocol}
\label{sec:connect}

The probe of \Cref{def:probe} draws 40 unseen bank items grouped by tier and by the model's correctness \hai. The learning system adds a third rule for which atoms those items come from, and a prediction for what the day 7 retest should find. \Cref{fig:protocol} draws both over a learner's knowledge region.

\paragraph{Items at the frontier.}
The unaided test $H_p$ is scored on items drawn from atoms at the learner's \term{frontier}{frontier}: concepts not in their accepted mastery $M$ whose immediate prerequisites are all in $M$, the outlined nodes of \Cref{fig:prereq}. An item on a known atom measures recall of something already mastered and an item on a blocked atom measures a prerequisite gap, and neither is what the multiplier is for; an item at the frontier is one the person could answer from what they know, which is where a model's help can either teach or substitute. The draw keeps the pairing of \Cref{sec:method}, so within the frontier set, pairs are still matched on tier and on the model's correctness. This rule is specified here and is not yet in \texttt{packages/bkt/src/hai}, whose grouping today reads tier and model correctness only \hai.

\paragraph{The same items in every condition.}
The AI condition $A$ is scored once per frozen item, before any draw, so $A$ on a frontier item is the same number whether that item lands in a solo or a pair slot. The joint condition $J_p$ uses the pair half of the same frontier draw, and the retest reuses all 40. Nothing about the frontier rule changes the scores' definitions, only the population of items they are taken on.

\paragraph{Retention against the FSRS prediction.}
$R_p = H_p(t + 7\,\mathrm{d}) - H_p(t)$ is an observed change in recall over seven days. For each solo item the engine also carries a prediction, $R(7, S)$ from \Cref{eq:fsrs} with $S$ the atom's stability at the probe, and the protocol reports the predicted mean recall beside the observed one. A retest that lands under the prediction on the pair half and at it on the solo half is the dependence pattern of \Cref{def:dependence} seen from the engine's side; a retest above the prediction on both halves is the testing effect \autocite{roediger2006testing}. Probe items stay out of spaced review until the retest ends, so $S$ is frozen across the seven days and the prediction is fixed before the observation.

\paragraph{What the decision rule reads.}
The cells of \Cref{tab:decision} are read per task class, and the frontier rule makes a task class a set of atoms at a depth: recall, apply, derive or teach, on concepts at the frontier. A class that falls in the augment cell has its atoms' $M$ updated from the pair half as well as the solo half; a class in the dependence cell updates $M$ from the solo half only, so a model's answer can never raise a concept into accepted mastery on its own.

\paragraph{The instrument.}
The probe lives in \texttt{packages/bkt/src/hai/} and runs inside the \texttt{bkt} terminal app, offline, with answers sealed in the same encrypted store as the person's other attempts; nothing leaves the device, and the person can export or wipe their data \hai. \texttt{bkt hai freeze} writes the bank, \texttt{bkt hai review} writes the flags, \texttt{bkt hai score} sends the bank to the model through a batch job and stores each answer with its rationale, model id and date, \texttt{bkt hai} runs a probe or its retest, and \texttt{bkt hai report} prints the scores as sentences \hai.

\paragraph{The AI condition.}
$A$ is one run of \texttt{claude-opus-5-5} at low effort over each frozen item, the reply constrained to a JSON object holding a letter and a rationale, with 400 output tokens at most \hai. A reply that fails to parse, names no valid letter or hits the token cap is counted as malformed and left out of $A$ and of pair matching \hai. The model sees the same four choices the person sees, so $A$ is a property of the item and the bank version.

\paragraph{Matching.}
Pairs are matched on tier and on the model's correctness so that $H_p$ and $J_p$ are taken on items of the same difficulty and the same model behaviour. A matched pair in which the model is wrong measures whether the person overrides it; a pair in which it is right measures whether they accept it.

\paragraph{Intervals.}
Every interval is a 95\% percentile bootstrap over matched pairs, 2,000 draws under a fixed seed, resampling pairs and keeping both items of each \hai. An interval is withheld when fewer than half the draws yield a finite statistic \hai. For a half-width of 0.10 on $J_p - H_p$ at a true score near 0.5, the guess-corrected scale needs about 342 items per condition; a half-width of 0.15 needs about 152 \calc. The 998-item bank caps one person at 499 items per condition \bank, so the design target is a half-width of 0.10 after 17 probes, and no trend is shown before 5 retested probes \hai.

\subsection{Planned analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

Per person, the report is $D$ with its interval, $m_p$ beside it, $R_p$, $L$ and the dependence flag, each with the number of pairs behind it, and the number of malformed model replies left out. Within a person, $D$ and $L$ are also broken out by tier and by whether the model was right on the pair, so a positive $D$ driven by accepting correct answers is told apart from one driven by overriding wrong ones.

Across people, every comparison is paired: each person contributes their own $H_p$, $J_p$ and retest scores on their own matched items, and the group statistic is the mean of the per-person differences with a bootstrap over people. Participants are stratified by highest education level reported at consent, in four bands: secondary, bachelor's, master's, doctoral. The question for the strata is whether $D$ and $L$ move together or apart with prior training, since the field studies cited above found the largest lift for the least experienced \autocite{brynjolfsson2023generative} and the learning study found that unaided exam scores fell where the model gave answers and held where it gave hints \autocite{bastani2025guardrails}. The education band is stored at consent and nowhere else, and a band with fewer than 20 people reports counts only.

\subsection{The divergence hypothesis}
\label{sec:divergence}

The hypothesis is that a group of people answering with the same model converges on the model's answers and the model's phrasings, so the range of ideas the group produces narrows even where each person's score rises. Four-choice items cannot show this, since the answer space is fixed, so the test needs open items: a second bank of short free-response prompts in the derive and teach tiers, each with a rubric. Each person answers a matched half alone and a half with the model's draft in view. The responses are embedded with the same sentence encoder Bucket uses for the solvability atlas, and divergence is measured as the mean pairwise cosine distance among the group's answers to one prompt, solo against pair, with a bootstrap over people. A second measure is the number of distinct answer clusters at a fixed distance threshold. The prediction, following \textcite{doshi2024diversity}, is a higher rubric score and a lower pairwise distance in the pair condition. A product that raises every person's score while collapsing the group's spread has optimized the wrong thing for a research community, whose value is in the answers nobody else gave.

\subsection{Decision rule for Research OS}
\label{sec:decision}

The scores decide, per task class, what Research OS does with the model. \Cref{tab:decision} gives the rule. A class where $D$ is above zero and $L$ is at or above zero is augmented: the model's answer stays in view and the person's own attempt stays first. A class where $D$ is above zero and $L$ is below zero, the dependence pattern, is augmented only when the task is a means and never when it is a learning target; in the Learn module it falls back to unaided practice with the model held until after the attempt. A class where $D$ is at or below zero is left with the person. A class where $A$ exceeds $H_p$ and $J_p$ sits at $A$ is a candidate for replacement, meaning the model answers and the person reviews, and only outside the Learn module. The rule is applied per person, since the same class can fall in different cells for different people, and the cells are re-read after each retested probe.

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

The central claims are the definitions, and their calibration is the instrument's test suite and the Lean listing. The tests check that the freeze is deterministic across builds, that pairs match on tier and model correctness, the guess correction at chance and at a perfect run, the four statistics on fixed data, the pair bootstrap on fixed data under a fixed seed, that no feedback is shown before the retest, and that the retest is gated by an injected clock \hai. \Cref{app:lean} checks the learning-system contract of \Cref{sec:leancontract}, that the retest time is after the probe time, that the guess-corrected numerator lies between the negative item count and the denominator for any count of correct answers at most the item count, that it is zero at chance, and that the dependence flag requires a positive gain and cannot fire when learning is positive. The error budget on a reported statistic is its bootstrap interval, and \Cref{sec:method} gives the item counts that interval needs.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{No data.}
No probe has been retested, so nothing here has been measured on a person. The bank, the matching and the statistics have been exercised by tests on fixed data only.

\paragraph{One model, one run.}
$A$ is one run of one model at one effort setting. A different model, a higher effort or a second sample would give a different $A$ and a different pairing. The bank version and the model id are stored with every score so that a re-run is a new instrument version and never a silent change.

\paragraph{Four-choice items.}
The bank measures recognition under four fixed choices. It cannot show divergence, and it rewards a model that is good at eliminating distractors. The open-item bank of \Cref{sec:divergence} is unbuilt.

\paragraph{Ceilings, drift, regression and practice.}
Four artefacts of a repeated test can mimic or hide an effect. A ceiling: when $J_p$ and $A$ both sit near the task maximum, $D$ cannot show a gain and $m_p$ collapses toward 1; the bank's tiers run from recall to teach so that each probe carries items the model and the person miss, and a pair group in which the model is wrong is matched on purpose. Task drift: a probe draws items the person has never seen, so two probes a week apart test different items, and a change in $H_p$ between probes mixes the person's change with the items' difficulty; the design answers this with matching on tier and model correctness inside each probe, so every within-probe statistic compares items of the same difficulty, and item-level difficulty matching across probes, from the pooled item accuracy, is planned before any trend is read. Regression to the mean: a person who scored low on day 0 by chance will tend to score higher on day 7, which inflates $R_p$; the retest reuses the same 40 items, so $R_p$ is a within-item difference and the chance component is bounded by the bootstrap interval, and the planned control arm, a probe with no AI on either half, gives the expected $R_p$ under retest alone. Practice: the weekly unaided retest is itself a retrieval event that raises later recall \autocite{roediger2006testing}; the design reads $L$ as the difference between the pair and solo halves at the same retest, so the practice effect falls on both halves, and parallel item forms, a second frozen bank with the same atoms and new distractors, are planned so that a later probe can test an atom without repeating its item.

\paragraph{The frontier rule.}
The probe draws by tier and model correctness today; the frontier rule of \Cref{sec:connect} is specified and unimplemented, and until the Academy's prerequisite graph is bound to the bank's atoms by version, a probe cannot tell a frontier item from a known one. The learning system's theorems hold under A1 through A5, and A1, that every encoded edge is mandatory and none is missing, is a property of the curriculum that no proof can supply.

\paragraph{Synthetic evaluation only.}
The 57,060 graph cases are every DAG on at most five nodes; the planner is untested above that size except by its fixtures. The statistical experiment detects what its own generator planted and says nothing about a real learner or a real item bank.

\paragraph{Seven days.}
The retest interval is fixed at seven days. Retention at thirty days or ninety is a different quantity, and a single interval cannot separate forgetting from a spacing effect.

\paragraph{Self-selection and the flagged items.}
Participants are users of a terminal app who opt in; the education strata will reflect that. The 68 flagged items are out until a person clears them, and the automated review is a set of surface rules, so an item with a subtler cue can pass it.

\paragraph{The decision rule.}
\Cref{tab:decision} is a rule with no result behind it yet. Its cells are set by the signs of two statistics and say nothing about effect size; a positive $D$ of 0.02 and one of 0.30 fall in the same cell. Thresholds on size wait for the first intervals.

\paragraph{Licences.}
The item bank is derived from the Academy pack, whose lesson text is open and whose sources are listed per atom in the pack; the probe code is MIT with the repository. Per-person data never leaves the device, and the planned cross-person analysis uses only exported, consented records.
