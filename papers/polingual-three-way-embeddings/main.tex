\title{Three-Way Word Embeddings for Polingual: Script, Sound and Meaning, and a Re-Ranked Lexicon}
\author{Gianangelo Dichio\\Bucket Foundation}
\date{2026-11-06}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Polingual translates single words offline from a Wiktionary \term{lexicon}{lexicon} for 28 languages. The lexicon lists English meanings in English frequency order, so a rare sense can come first: Hebrew \emph{akhil} (edible) was the first translation of English \emph{good}. We embed 529,131 words of 29 languages four ways: rendered script pixels with a small CNN, character n-grams, phonetic features from Wiktionary IPA with a grapheme model for words without IPA, and LaBSE meaning vectors, plus a joint projection of all four \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 manifest.json, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/vocab.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/meaning.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/store.py]}. Meaning vectors then re-rank every translation pair, with gloss senses added as candidates. On the held-out half of an independent Tatoeba-aligned word set, the first English sense shown is right for 68.3\% of words, up from 58.0\% \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank\_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: CUDA\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}. For English to target words, a model audit of 100 changed first answers finds 91 right after re-ranking and 60 before \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/audit\_report.json sha256 c63af75e, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/audit\_sample.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/audit\_judge.py]}. A secondary any-token score in the app evaluation, which includes the tuning rows and rewards the longer answers, rises from 67.5\% to 77.2\% \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt and data/swift\_after.txt, 2026-11-06, Mac: TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --package-path BucketKit --filter translateEvalReport]}. The shipped change is the re-ranked lexicon, 0.24 MB larger \tg{[empirical: du -cb apple/Shared/Corpus/lexicon/*.tsv.z before and after, 2026-11-06]}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A word lookup in Polingual returns up to three English senses from the bundled lexicon. For the lexicon of pull request 1198, any-token precision was 98.7\% on the Wiktionary reference row and 67.5\% on a word set aligned from Tatoeba sentence pairs \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt, 2026-11-06, Mac: TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --package-path BucketKit --filter translateEvalReport]}. The gap comes from sense order: Wiktionary translation tables list a foreign word under every English headword that uses it, and the builder kept the four most frequent English headwords. A foreign word that translates a rare sense of a frequent English word inherits that word.

The founder asked for three separate embeddings per word, of script, sound and meaning, and a joint one, and for higher accuracy. This paper builds the four, measures each against a task it should solve, and uses them to score every translation pair in the lexicon.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

LaBSE trains a dual encoder on translation pairs and places translations near each other \autocite{feng2020labse}. Bilingual lexicon induction retrieves translations by nearest neighbour across aligned spaces \autocite{conneau2017muse}. Subword n-grams give vectors to unseen spellings \autocite{bojanowski2016fasttext}. Rendered text as model input handles noise and unseen scripts \autocite{salesky2021visrep,rust2022pixel}. Contrastive objectives with in-batch negatives train both our script encoder and our joint projection \autocite{chen2020simclr,oord2018cpc}. Tatoeba sentence pairs are a standard multilingual test source \autocite{tiedemann2020tatoeba}. Phonetic features come from the PanPhon feature table and IPA from WikiPron, a scrape of Wiktionary pronunciations; neither has a DOI or arXiv identifier, so we name them with their repository paths in \Cref{sec:method}.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\begin{definition}[Lexicon]
\label{def:lexicon}
For a language $L$, the \term{lexicon}{lexicon} maps a headword $w$ to an ordered list $E(w)$ of English words and a gloss $g(w)$, a list of short English sense strings from the word's own Wiktionary entry. The app shows the first three items of $E(w)$, or $g(w)$ when $E(w)$ is empty. For English word $e$ the lexicon also holds an ordered list $F(e)$ of target words; English-to-target lookups show the first.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Modality]
\label{def:modality}
A \term{modality}{modality} $m$ is a map from words to unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{d_m}$. The similarity of words $a,b$ under $m$ is the dot product $\cos_m(a,b)$ of their unit vectors.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Joint score]
\label{def:joint}
Given weights $w_m$, the \term{joint}{joint score} of a pair is $s(a,b)=\sum_m w_m \cos_m(a,b)$. With weight one on meaning and zero elsewhere it equals the meaning cosine, proved as \texttt{jointScore\_meaning\_only} in \Cref{app:lean}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Precision]
\label{def:precision}
For an evaluation row with $J$ judged answers of which $R$ are right, \term{precision}{precision} is $R/J$ in percent; \texttt{precisionPct\_le} in \Cref{app:lean} proves it stays within the percent scale when $R\le J$. First-sense precision judges only the text before the first semicolon of the answer; any-token precision judges the whole answer.
\end{definition}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

All embedding and scoring ran on host charizard, AMD Radeon RX 7600 with ROCm 6.4 and torch 2.9.1; the re-ranker ran on its CPU with the GPU hidden. Each tag below names the report file in dataset v1, the first eight hex digits of its sha256 from \texttt{manifest.json}, and the command.

\paragraph{Vocabulary.} The lexicon builder of pull request 1198 runs in a dump mode, \texttt{python3 apple/scripts/translate/build\_lexicon.py dl out/base 900000 repo/apple/Shared/Corpus - out/candidates.json}, that writes, for every shipped headword, all English candidates in frequency order and the gloss, and for every English headword all target candidates. Gloss senses that equal an English headword add target candidates to its list. The vocabulary is every headword, every candidate and every evaluation word: 26,887 English words and 502,244 words across the 28 target languages \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 manifest.json, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/vocab.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/meaning.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/store.py]}.

\paragraph{Script.} Each word is rendered in Noto Sans for Latin, Greek and Cyrillic, Noto Sans Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Tamil and Thai, and Noto Sans CJK for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, with HarfBuzz shaping through Pillow's Raqm layout, into a $32\times128$ grey raster. A four-block CNN maps the raster to 64 dimensions. It trains for 2,000 steps of batch 512 on 116,000 words, 4,000 per language, rendered at font weights 300, 400 and 700; two views of a word with random scale, shear, shift and noise form a positive pair under a symmetric InfoNCE loss with temperature 0.07 \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/script\_report.json sha256 3931c793, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/script.py]}. The character view hashes the word's 1 to 3-grams with boundary marks into $2^{20}$ buckets and sums a fixed Gaussian vector per bucket, 64 dimensions \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/script\_report.json sha256 3931c793, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/script.py]}.

\paragraph{Phonetic.} WikiPron files \texttt{CUNY-CL/wikipron/data/scrape/tsv} give space-separated IPA; we use up to 150,000 entries per language, 1,589,580 in all \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic\_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. Each segment maps to the 24 PanPhon features (\texttt{dmort27/panphon}) in $\{-1,0,1\}$; the word vector averages the segment features inside four equal position bins, giving 96 dimensions that keep coarse order \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic\_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. For words without IPA a grapheme model predicts the vector: the word splits into units, extended grapheme clusters by default, Hangul jamo for Korean, pinyin initials and finals for Chinese, kana with small-kana merging for Japanese; each unit, each unit at its position bin, each unit bigram and the first and last unit index a learned 96-d vector in an EmbeddingBag, and the mean of those vectors regresses the IPA vector under squared error for 8 epochs \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic\_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. The unit vectors are the letter and grapheme-cluster level of the phonetic embedding. 313,678 of the 529,131 vocabulary rows carry IPA; the grapheme model fills the rest \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic\_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}.

\paragraph{Meaning.} LaBSE, \texttt{sentence-transformers/LaBSE} at revision \texttt{836121a0}, encodes each bare word to 768 dimensions \autocite{feng2020labse}. Gloss strings are encoded the same way, with parenthesised notes removed \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 manifest.json, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/vocab.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/meaning.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/store.py]}.

\paragraph{Joint.} Two joint forms are fitted on translation pairs from 80\% of each language's headwords, split by hash \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint\_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}, and the other 20\% are test queries \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint\_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}. The weighted form searches $w_m\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ for script, character and phonetic with meaning fixed at one, maximising P@1 on 300 training queries per language; it selects 0.05 for script and zero for the other two \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint\_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}. A logistic regression on the four cosines gave a phonetic weight of $-2.05$ and was dropped \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint\_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}: sound similarity between a foreign word and its English translation carries signal only for cognates. The projection form scales each modality, concatenates the 992 dimensions and maps them linearly to 256, trained for 4,000 steps of batch 1,024 under symmetric InfoNCE at temperature 0.05 with duplicate English targets masked \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint\_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}.

\paragraph{Re-ranking.} For headword $w$, candidates are $E(w)$ in frequency order $j$ plus up to three gloss senses at gloss position $j$. Candidate $x$ scores $\cos(w,x) - \beta \log(1+j)$, plus a bonus $b$ for the first gloss sense, and the top four ship. For English $e$, target candidate $t$ scores $\cos(e,t) - \beta_r \log(1+j) + \gamma\,\pi(t)$ with $\pi(t)=1-\log(1+r_t)/\log(60001)$ from the wordfreq rank $r_t$ of $t$ in its language, zero when unranked; a candidate spelled the same as $e$ drops to the end. The scorer, $\beta$, a drop threshold, a relative cut, gloss use and $b$ were chosen on the even rows of the Tatoeba-aligned word set by first-sense precision with coverage held at the baseline \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank\_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: CUDA\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}, and $\beta_r\in\{0,0.05,0.1\}$ and $\gamma\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3\}$ on the even half of 378 common English words with Tatoeba-derived targets \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank\_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: CUDA\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}. Selected: meaning cosine, $\beta=0.05$, no drop threshold, gloss on, $b=0.1$, $\beta_r=0.1$, $\gamma=0.75$, an interior optimum of the $\gamma$ grid \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank\_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: CUDA\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}. The odd rows and odd words are held out. The builder applies the English-to-target order only when \texttt{REV\_RERANK=1}, the default, set by the audit in \Cref{sec:audit}: \texttt{REV\_RERANK=1 python3 apple/scripts/translate/build\_lexicon.py dl out/rerank 900000 repo/apple/Shared/Corpus out/order.json}.

\paragraph{English-to-target references.} For each English word and language, target tokens are scored by Dice co-occurrence with the English token over Tatoeba pairs, character 1 to 3-grams for Chinese, Japanese and Thai, and the top three with count at least three are the references \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/regression\_report.json sha256 64a4ec7a, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: python3 apple/scripts/embed/regression.py]}. A first choice is right when it equals a reference, shares its first four letters with one, or for the unspaced languages contains or is contained in one.

\subsection{Polysemous case study}

The ten words good, right, light, fair, well, kind, mean, bank, play and run were added after the founder saw \emph{good} become \emph{akhil} in Hebrew. They were not held out from the design of the English-to-target prior, so they are a case study: the first choice matches a Tatoeba-derived reference in 109 of 158 language cases after re-ranking and 81 before \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/regression\_report.json sha256 64a4ec7a, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: python3 apple/scripts/embed/regression.py]}, and \emph{good} to Hebrew now gives \emph{tov}.

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Every hyperparameter was chosen on one half of an evaluation set and is reported on the other half, \Cref{tab:heldout} and \Cref{sec:audit}. The Python replica of the app lookup gave 66.8\% any-token precision on the full Tatoeba row for the lexicon of pull request 1198, where the Swift evaluation gave 67.5\% \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 lexicon of PR 1198, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: python3 apple/scripts/embed/pyeval.py lexicon, and data/swift\_before.txt]}; the replica skips the deck corpus layer, clitic stripping and NLTagger lemmas, which accounts for the 0.7 point gap. A rebuild of pull request 1198's lexicon with this pipeline in dump mode reproduces every line of every file. The Tatoeba references are noisy: they are the three English words that co-occur most with the target word, so a right sense that is less frequent in Tatoeba counts as wrong. The error budget for the headline is the sampling error of a proportion near 0.68 over 2,325 held-out words, a standard error of 0.97 points, small next to the 10.3 point change \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank\_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, charizard CPU: CUDA\_VISIBLE\_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}. The audit covers 100 items, so its 31 point gap carries a standard error near 5.7 points \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/audit\_report.json sha256 c63af75e, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/audit\_sample.py, then python3 apple/scripts/embed/audit\_judge.py]}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The English-to-target audit is a model audit; a human audit of the same 100 items would settle it. LaBSE has no Latin, Ancient Greek, Sanskrit or Old English training data, so meaning scores there lean on shared subwords. The phonetic view ignores tone, so Mandarin, Thai and Vietnamese homophones that differ by tone collapse. Swedish and Hebrew have 5,054 and 2,872 WikiPron entries, too few for a good grapheme model: G2P R@10 is 5.5\% and 13.5\% \tg{[empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic\_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, charizard RX 7600 ROCm 6.4 torch 2.9.1: python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. The script CNN trains on weight changes of one font family per script, so it learns little about handwriting or other typefaces. Tamil has no usable Tatoeba-aligned references, since the alignment tokenizer splits Tamil vowel signs. The joint projection improves P@10 but not P@1 over meaning, and the re-ranker selected the meaning cosine; the joint vectors ship in the dataset and stay out of the app.

def jointScore (w c : List Int) : Int := dot w c
theorem jointScore_meaning_only (s p c m : Int) :
    jointScore [0, 0, 0, 1] [s, p, c, m] = m
def precisionPct (right judged : Nat) : Nat := right * 100 / judged
theorem precisionPct_le (right judged : Nat) (h : right <= judged) :
    precisionPct right judged <= 100
theorem keepSenses_length (keep : a -> Bool) (senses : List a) :
    (keepSenses keep senses).length <= senses.length
