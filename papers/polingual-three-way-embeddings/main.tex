\title{Three-Way Word Embeddings for Polingual: Script, Sound and Meaning, and a Re-Ranked Lexicon}
\author{Gianangelo Dichio\\Bucket Foundation}
\date{2026-11-06}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Polingual translates single words offline from a Wiktionary \term{lexicon}{lexicon} for 28 languages. The lexicon lists English meanings in English frequency order, so a rare sense can come first: Hebrew \emph{akhil} (edible) was the first translation of English \emph{good}. We embed 529,131 words of the 29 languages four ways: rendered script pixels with a small CNN, character n-grams, phonetic features from Wiktionary IPA with a grapheme model for words without IPA, and LaBSE meaning vectors, plus a joint projection of all four \tg{[empirical: \dset/manifest.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/store.py]}. Each view is scored on its own task. The vectors, models and pair scores are stored as dataset v1 at \texttt{\textasciitilde/agfarms/data/polingual-embeddings/v1} on the build machine. Meaning vectors then re-rank every translation pair, with gloss senses added as candidates. On an independent Tatoeba word set the app's word precision rises from 67.5\% to 77.2\% \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt and data/swift\_after.txt, 2026-11-06, TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --filter translateEvalReport]}, and the first English choice for ten polysemous words is right in 115 of 158 language cases, up from 81 \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/regression\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/regression.py]}. The shipped change is the re-ranked lexicon, 0.24 MB larger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A word lookup in Polingual returns up to three English senses from the bundled lexicon. Precision on the Wiktionary reference row was 98.7\% for the lexicon of pull request 1198, while precision on a word set aligned from Tatoeba sentence pairs was 67.5\% \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt and data/swift\_after.txt, 2026-11-06, TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --filter translateEvalReport]}. The gap comes from sense order: Wiktionary translation tables list a foreign word under every English headword that uses it, and the builder kept the four most frequent English headwords. A foreign word that translates a rare sense of a frequent English word inherits that word.

The founder asked for three separate embeddings per word, of script, sound and meaning, and a joint one, and for higher accuracy. This paper builds the four, measures each against a task it should solve, and uses them to score every translation pair in the lexicon.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

LaBSE trains a dual encoder on translation pairs for 109 languages and places translations near each other \autocite{feng2020labse}. Bilingual lexicon induction retrieves translations by nearest neighbour across aligned spaces \autocite{conneau2017muse}. Subword n-grams give vectors to unseen spellings \autocite{bojanowski2016fasttext}. Rendered text as model input handles noise and unseen scripts \autocite{salesky2021visrep,rust2022pixel}. Contrastive objectives with in-batch negatives train both our script encoder and our joint projection \autocite{chen2020simclr,oord2018cpc}. Tatoeba sentence pairs are a standard multilingual test source \autocite{tiedemann2020tatoeba}. Phonetic features come from the PanPhon feature table and IPA from WikiPron, a scrape of Wiktionary pronunciations; neither has a DOI or arXiv identifier, so we name them with their repository paths in \Cref{sec:method}.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\begin{definition}[Lexicon]
\label{def:lexicon}
For a language $L$, the \term{lexicon}{lexicon} maps a headword $w$ to an ordered list $E(w)$ of English words, at most four of which ship, and a gloss $g(w)$, a list of short English sense strings from the word's own Wiktionary entry. The app shows the first three items of $E(w)$, or $g(w)$ when $E(w)$ is empty. For English word $e$ the lexicon also holds an ordered list $F(e)$ of target words; English-to-target lookups show the first.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Modality]
\label{def:modality}
A \term{modality}{modality} $m$ is a map from words to unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{d_m}$: script $d=64$, character $d=64$, phonetic $d=96$, meaning $d=768$, joint $d=256$. The similarity of words $a,b$ under $m$ is the dot product $\cos_m(a,b)$ of their unit vectors.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Joint score]
\label{def:joint}
Given weights $w_m$, the \term{joint}{joint score} of a pair is $s(a,b)=\sum_m w_m \cos_m(a,b)$. With weight one on meaning and zero elsewhere it equals the meaning cosine (Lean: \texttt{jointScore\_meaning\_only}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Precision]
\label{def:precision}
For an evaluation row with $J$ judged answers of which $R$ are right, \term{precision}{precision} is $100R/J$ percent; it never exceeds 100 when $R\le J$ (Lean: \texttt{precisionPct\_le}).
\end{definition}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Vocabulary.} The lexicon builder of pull request 1198 runs in a dump mode that writes, for every shipped headword, all English candidates in frequency order and the gloss, and for every English headword all target candidates. Gloss senses that equal an English headword add target candidates to its list. The vocabulary is every headword, every candidate and every evaluation word: 26,887 English words and 502,244 words across the 28 target languages \tg{[empirical: \dset/manifest.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/vocab.py]}.

\paragraph{Script.} Each word is rendered in Noto Sans for Latin, Greek and Cyrillic, Noto Sans Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Tamil and Thai, and Noto Sans CJK for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, with HarfBuzz shaping through Pillow's Raqm layout, into a $32\times128$ grey raster. A four-block CNN maps the raster to 64 dimensions. It trains for 2,000 steps on 116,000 words rendered at font weights 300, 400 and 700; two views of a word, each with random scale, shear, shift and noise, form a positive pair under a symmetric InfoNCE loss with temperature 0.07. The character view hashes the word's 1 to 3-grams with boundary marks into $2^{20}$ buckets and sums a fixed Gaussian vector per bucket.

\paragraph{Phonetic.} WikiPron files \texttt{CUNY-CL/wikipron/data/scrape/tsv} give space-separated IPA; we use up to 150,000 entries per language, 1,589,580 in all \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/phonetic\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. Each segment maps to the 24 PanPhon features (\texttt{dmort27/panphon}) in $\{-1,0,1\}$; the word vector averages the segment features inside four equal position bins, giving 96 dimensions that keep coarse order. For words without IPA a grapheme model predicts the vector: the word splits into units, extended grapheme clusters by default, Hangul jamo for Korean, pinyin initials and finals for Chinese, kana with small-kana merging for Japanese; each unit, each unit at its position bin, each unit bigram and the first and last unit index a learned 96-d vector in an EmbeddingBag, and the mean of those vectors regresses the IPA vector under squared error. The unit vectors are the letter and grapheme-cluster level of the phonetic embedding. 313,678 of the 529,131 vocabulary rows carry IPA; the grapheme model fills the rest \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/phonetic\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}.

\paragraph{Meaning.} LaBSE encodes each bare word \autocite{feng2020labse}. Gloss strings are encoded the same way, with parenthesised notes removed.

\paragraph{Joint.} Two joint forms are fitted on translation pairs from 80\% of each language's headwords, split by hash \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/joint\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}; the other 20\% are test queries \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/joint\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}. The weighted form searches $w_m\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ for script, character and phonetic with meaning fixed at one, maximising P@1 on 300 training queries per language; it selects 0.05 for script and zero for the other two \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/joint\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]}. A logistic regression on the four cosines gave a phonetic weight of $-2.05$, which reflects that sound similarity between a foreign word and its English translation is noise outside cognates, and was dropped. The projection form scales each modality, concatenates the 992 dimensions and maps them linearly to 256, trained for 4,000 steps of symmetric InfoNCE at temperature 0.05 with duplicate English targets masked.

\paragraph{Re-ranking.} For headword $w$, candidates are $E(w)$ in frequency order $j$ plus up to three gloss senses at gloss position $j$. Candidate $x$ scores $\cos(w,x) - \beta \log(1+j)$, plus a bonus $b$ for the first gloss sense, and the top four ship. For English $e$, target candidate $t$ scores $\cos(e,t) - \beta_r \log(1+j) + \gamma\,\pi(t)$ with $\pi(t)=1-\log(1+r_t)/\log(60001)$ from the wordfreq rank $r_t$ of $t$ in its language, zero when unranked, and a candidate spelled the same as $e$ drops to the end. The scorer, $\beta$, a drop threshold, a relative cut, gloss use and $b$ were chosen on the even rows of the Tatoeba-aligned word set by first-sense precision with coverage held at the baseline; $\beta_r$ and $\gamma$ on the even half of 378 common English words with Tatoeba-derived targets. Selected: meaning cosine, $\beta=0.05$, no drop threshold, gloss on, $b=0.1$, $\beta_r=0.05$, $\gamma=0.5$ \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/rerank\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]}. The odd rows and odd words are held out.

\paragraph{English-to-target references.} For each English word and language, target tokens are scored by Dice co-occurrence with the English token over Tatoeba pairs, character 1 to 3-grams for Chinese, Japanese and Thai, and the top three with count at least three are the references. A first choice is right when it equals a reference, shares its first four letters with one, or for the unspaced languages contains or is contained in one.

\section{Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Every hyperparameter was chosen on one half of an evaluation set and is reported on the other half (\Cref{tab:heldout}, \Cref{tab:regression}). The Python replica of the app lookup gave 66.8\% on the full Tatoeba row for the lexicon of pull request 1198, where the Swift evaluation gave 67.5\% \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt and data/swift\_after.txt, 2026-11-06, TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --filter translateEvalReport]}; the replica skips the deck corpus layer, clitic stripping and NLTagger lemmas, which accounts for the 0.7 point gap. The re-ranked lexicon builds from the same inputs as the shipped one: a rebuild of pull request 1198's lexicon with this pipeline in dump mode reproduces every line of every file. The Tatoeba references are noisy: they are the three English words that co-occur most with the target word, so a right sense that is less frequent in Tatoeba counts as wrong. The error budget is the sampling error of a proportion near 0.7 over 2,325 held-out words, a standard error of 0.95 points, small next to the 11.2 point change.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Longer answers help the any-sense metric: the mean answer grew from 5.35 to 8.58 words, because gloss senses are phrases, which is why the first-sense metric is reported beside it. The English-to-target Wiktionary row falls from 94.7\% to 89.1\% \tg{[empirical: data/swift\_before.txt and data/swift\_after.txt, 2026-11-06, TRANSLATE\_EVAL=current swift test --filter translateEvalReport]}: its references are the translation-table lists themselves, and target words added from gloss senses, such as Japanese \emph{ii} for \emph{good}, count as wrong there while the Tatoeba-derived references in \Cref{tab:regression} count them right. LaBSE has no Latin, Ancient Greek, Sanskrit or Old English training data, so meaning scores there lean on shared subwords. The phonetic view ignores tone, so Mandarin, Thai and Vietnamese homophones that differ by tone collapse. Swedish and Hebrew have about 5,000 and 2,900 WikiPron entries, too few for a good grapheme model: G2P R@10 is 5.5\% and 13.5\% \tg{[empirical: \dset/reports/phonetic\_report.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]}. The script CNN trains on weight changes of one font family per script, so it learns little about handwriting or other typefaces. Tamil has no usable Tatoeba-aligned references, since the alignment tokenizer splits Tamil vowel signs. The joint projection improves P@10 but not P@1 over meaning, and the re-ranker selected the meaning cosine; the joint vectors ship in the dataset and not in the app.

\section{Lean listings}
\label{app:lean}

\texttt{lean/Bucket/Embed.lean} states the dot product, the joint score, precision in whole percent and sense filtering, and proves that the meaning-only joint score is the meaning cosine, that precision is at most 100 when right answers do not exceed judged ones, and that filtering senses never lengthens a list.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=bucketlean]
def jointScore (w c : List Int) : Int := dot w c
theorem jointScore_meaning_only (s p c m : Int) :
    jointScore [0, 0, 0, 1] [s, p, c, m] = m
def precisionPct (right judged : Nat) : Nat := right * 100 / judged
theorem precisionPct_le (right judged : Nat) (h : right <= judged) :
    precisionPct right judged <= 100
theorem keepSenses_length (keep : a -> Bool) (senses : List a) :
    (keepSenses keep senses).length <= senses.length
\end{lstlisting}
