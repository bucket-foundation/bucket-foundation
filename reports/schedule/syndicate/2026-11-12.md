# A Dictionary Said "Good" Means "Edible"

*Re-ranking a Wiktionary lexicon by meaning raised first-sense precision from 58.0% to 68.3% across 28 languages*

A user of Polingual, a word-translation app, looked up the English word "good" in Hebrew and got *akhil*, which means edible. The word is in the dictionary. It is a real sense of the Hebrew entry. It was also the first answer shown, and that was the bug.

## The finding

Polingual translates single words offline from a lexicon built out of Wiktionary for 28 languages. For each foreign headword the lexicon lists English meanings in English frequency order. When a headword has a rare sense that happens to match a common English word, that sense can land first, and the app shows the first three.

We scored every candidate translation by how close its meaning vector sits to the headword's, restricted to candidates the dictionary already lists. On the held-out half of a Tatoeba-aligned word set, the first English sense shown is right for 68.3% of words, up from 58.0% [empirical: papers/polingual-three-way-embeddings/data/rerank_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES= python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]. The set has 2,325 held-out words, so the sampling error on 68.3% is 0.97 points, small next to the 10.3 point change [empirical: same file, same command].

The unweighted mean over the 25 languages with held-out rows rises from 55.3% to 66.3% for the first sense, and no language falls. Hindi is the one language with no change, 47.1% before and after, on 34 held-out words [empirical: same file, same command]. Hebrew *good* now gives *tov*.

## The method

We embedded 529,131 words of 29 languages four ways: a small CNN over rendered script pixels, character n-grams, phonetic features from Wiktionary IPA (with a grapheme model for words without IPA), and LaBSE meaning vectors. A fifth, joint projection combines all four [empirical: dataset v1 manifest.json, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/vocab.py then meaning.py then store.py].

Each view was tested on a task it should solve. For translation retrieval, meaning reaches 57.1% P@10 on average, against 10.8% for script, 11.3% for character n-grams and 5.6% for phonetic [empirical: dataset v1 reports/joint_report.json sha256 11b0b40e, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/joint.py]. The phonetic view earns its place elsewhere: it finds homophones better than character n-grams in 23 of 27 languages, with mean R@10 of 80.2% against 62.3%, and the gap is widest where spelling hides sound. Chinese scores 81.3% against 6.5% [empirical: dataset v1 reports/phonetic_report.json sha256 5273260e, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/phonetic.py]. The joint projection reaches 58.9% P@10 but does not beat meaning alone at rank one, so the re-ranker uses the meaning cosine.

The re-ranker scores a candidate as its meaning cosine minus a small penalty on its frequency position, plus a bonus for the first gloss sense. Every parameter was chosen on the even rows of the evaluation set and reported on the odd rows. For English-to-target lookups the score also adds a word-frequency prior for the target word, tuned on the even half of 378 common English words.

## The numbers on the other direction

English to target is harder to judge, because no gold dictionary exists for it. Re-ranking changes the first answer for 1,488 of 4,848 common or polysemous English words. We sampled 100 of the changed answers and had a model, Claude Opus 5.5, judge each against the common Wiktionary sense and Tatoeba usage. The frequency order was right 60 times and the re-ranked order 91 times. The split was 34 wins, 3 losses, 57 right under both and 6 wrong under both. At 100 items the standard error on the 31 point gap is near 5.7 points [empirical: dataset v1 reports/audit_report.json sha256 c63af75e, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/audit_sample.py then audit_judge.py].

The 3 losses are new errors the re-rank introduced: *why* to Spanish moved from *por qué* to *por*, *inside* to Japanese moved from *naka ni* to the loanword *in*, and *hot* to Korean moved from *deopda* to the loanword *hat* [empirical: same file, same command].

A second check uses references derived from Tatoeba sentence pairs. On the held-out half of the common English words, first-choice accuracy rises from 60.6% to 68.0% [empirical: dataset v1 reports/rerank_report.json sha256 968b6767, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/rerank.py]. For ten polysemous words (good, right, light, fair, well, kind, mean, bank, play, run), the first choice matches a reference in 109 of 158 language cases after re-ranking and 81 before. Those ten words were picked after the *akhil* bug, so they are a case study with no held-out status [empirical: dataset v1 reports/regression_report.json sha256 64a4ec7a, 2026-11-06, python3 apple/scripts/embed/regression.py].

## What it costs

The shipped lexicon is 0.24 MB larger [empirical: du -cb apple/Shared/Corpus/lexicon/*.tsv.z before and after, 2026-11-06]. Answers get longer, because gloss senses are phrases: the mean answer grew from 5.35 to 8.58 words. In the app's own evaluation, which includes the tuning rows and rewards longer answers, the any-token score on the Tatoeba word row rises from 67.5% to 77.2%. The English-to-target Wiktionary row falls from 94.7% to 89.9% [empirical: data/swift_before.txt and data/swift_after.txt, 2026-11-06, TRANSLATE_EVAL=current swift test --package-path BucketKit --filter translateEvalReport]. We report that fall in the paper.

## What to doubt

The audit is a model's judgment of 100 items. A 30-item human spot check is written out and waiting for verdicts. The Tatoeba references are the three English words that co-occur most with the target word, so a right sense that is rare in Tatoeba counts as wrong. The lexicon-induction scores use the Wiktionary candidate set as gold and share the known faults of that setup. Tamil has no usable Tatoeba references, and the phonetic view ignores tone.
