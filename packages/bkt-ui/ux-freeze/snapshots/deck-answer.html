The nuclear atom

Intuition

Reach out and touch anything — a table, your own hand, a glass of water — and you are touching a structure built from atoms. The deep surprise of the atom is how empty it is. Nearly all the mass hides in a vanishingly small central knot called the nucleus, while the volume is filled by a thin, fast-moving haze of electrons. If the nucleus were a marble on the centre line of a football stadium, the electrons would be a faint mist out near the seats.

Precisely

An atom is the smallest unit of a chemical element (a pure substance such as carbon or oxygen). It contains three particles:

  • protons — charge +1+1+1, found in the nucleus;
  • neutrons — charge 000, found in the nucleus;
  • electrons — charge −1-1−1, occupying the surrounding volume.

The atomic number ZZZ is the number of protons, and it defines the element. The mass number A=Z+NA = Z + NA=Z+N counts protons plus neutrons. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. Charge is measured in units of the elementary charge e≈1.602×10−19 Ce \approx 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}e≈1.602×10−19 C; mass in atomic mass units, with the proton and neutron each near 1 u1\ \text{u}1 u and the electron about 1/18361/18361/1836 of that.

Atoms of the same element that differ only in neutron count are called isotopes; they are chemically near-identical because chemistry depends on the electrons, which ZZZ fixes. The radius of a typical atom is around 10−10 m10^{-10}\ \text{m}10−10 m (one ångström), while the nucleus is near 10−15 m10^{-15}\ \text{m}10−15 m — a factor of 100,000100{,}000100,000 smaller in radius, and so vanishingly tiny in volume that the atom is, by volume, almost entirely empty space populated only by the electron cloud.

Worked example

Consider an atom with 6 protons, 8 neutrons, 6 electrons. The proton count Z=6Z=6Z=6 means it is carbon. The mass number is A=6+8=14A = 6 + 8 = 14A=6+8=14, so it is carbon-14. Protons (666) equal electrons (666), so the net charge is 6(+1)+6(−1)=06(+1) + 6(-1) = 06(+1)+6(−1)=0 — a neutral atom. (Change the neutron count and you change the isotope, not the element.)

Why it's true / how it works

In 1911 Ernest Rutherford fired alpha particles at a thin gold foil. Most passed straight through, but a tiny fraction bounced sharply backward. A diffuse atom could never do that; only a small, dense, positively charged core could deflect a fast particle so hard. This pinned the positive charge and almost all the mass into a nucleus roughly 10,00010{,}00010,000 times smaller than the atom itself.

Before Rutherford, the prevailing "plum pudding" picture spread the positive charge smoothly through the whole atom. That model predicted alpha particles should sail through with only gentle deflections, so the rare hard backscatters were decisive: they could only come from a near head-on encounter with a concentrated charge. The fraction that bounced back, combined with the geometry of the scattering, even let physicists estimate the nucleus's size.

Where it connects

Because the chemistry of an atom is governed by its electrons in the field of the nucleus, ZZZ is the single most important number in the whole subject — it sets the electron configuration, drives the periodic table, and underlies effective nuclear charge. In ordinary chemistry the nucleus never changes; only electrons rearrange.

Watch out

Do not picture electrons as tidy planets on circular orbits. That Bohr image is a useful cartoon but physically wrong — electrons occupy fuzzy probability clouds (orbitals). Also, it is the proton count, not the mass or the electron count, that fixes the element's identity.

Finally, do not confuse mass number AAA (a whole-number count of nucleons for one isotope) with the atomic weight on the periodic table (a decimal average over an element's natural isotope mix) — carbon's mass numbers are 121212, 131313, 141414, yet its tabulated atomic weight is about 12.01112.01112.011.
