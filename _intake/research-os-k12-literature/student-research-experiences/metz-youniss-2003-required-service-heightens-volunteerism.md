# A Demonstration That School-Based Required Service Does Not Deter-But Heightens-Volunteerism

A school-based mandate to perform community service did not reduce, and appeared to
raise, students' subsequent volunteering, a direct complication to a reactance-based
account of required participation.

## Key Claims

- Following students through a school district that instituted a required
  community-service program, the authors compare students' volunteering behavior before
  and after the requirement took effect against students who were not subject to the
  requirement.
- School-based required service is found not to deter subsequent voluntary community
  service; the paper's own title states the requirement heightened, rather than
  suppressed, students' later volunteering.
- The finding runs counter to a reactance-based prediction, that an external mandate
  would undermine intrinsic motivation to volunteer once the mandate is lifted, the same
  prediction Stukas, Snyder, and Clary (1999) confirm in a different, laboratory-adjacent
  setting.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What design feature of the school-based program this paper studies, structured
  integration into the school setting, sustained duration, or a different population than
  Stukas, Snyder, and Clary (1999)'s college sample, accounts for the opposite result, a
  reconciliation this paper does not offer since it was not written to respond to that
  study directly.
- Whether a Research OS pilot's own Production requirement, delivered through a
  sustained, school-integrated workspace rather than a one-time mandate, is closer in kind
  to this paper's own heightening result or to Stukas, Snyder, and Clary (1999)'s
  reactance result.
