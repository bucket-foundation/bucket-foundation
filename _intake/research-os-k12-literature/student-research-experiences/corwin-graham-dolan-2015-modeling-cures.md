# Modeling Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences: An Agenda for Future Research and Evaluation

Builds pathway models of how a course-based research experience is hypothesized to produce
its claimed outcomes, reviewing the literature to score which outcomes have real empirical
support, and naming instruments already available for each.

## Key Claims

- A systems approach generated pathway models hypothesizing how specific student actions
  during a course-based research experience connect to specific claimed outcomes.
- Reviewing CURE and research-internship studies together found CURE students achieve many
  of the same outcomes documented for independent-internship students.
- Which CURE design features are necessary and sufficient for the claimed outcomes has not
  been identified; the pathway models are offered as testable hypotheses, not settled
  findings.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which specific design features are load-bearing for a claimed outcome versus incidental.
- Whether the named CURE instruments transfer to a K-12 research workspace without their
  own validation work.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the pathway-model discipline overlap-map question 4 needs: a testable, revisable
connection between a specific learner action and a specific claimed outcome, the same
discipline `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md`'s five-state ladder and `PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2b's
ladder-versus-profile question already apply to Research OS's own design.
