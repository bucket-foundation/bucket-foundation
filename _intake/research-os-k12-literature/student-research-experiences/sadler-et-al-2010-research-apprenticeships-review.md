# Learning science through research apprenticeships: A critical review of the literature

Synthesizes 53 empirical studies of research apprenticeships for secondary students,
undergraduates, and teachers, scoring which claimed learning outcomes the literature
supports.

## Key Claims

- Across 53 studies, the literature supported many presumed positive associations between
  apprenticeship participation and outcomes such as career aspirations, content
  understanding, and confidence for doing science.
- Findings on nature-of-science understanding were mixed, some studies finding gains and
  others none, an unsettled outcome category rather than a supported one.
- Apprenticeship length and whether an outcome was made an explicit design focus, rather
  than participation alone, shaped which outcomes materialized.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a single-semester Research OS pilot supplies enough duration for the
  duration-dependent outcomes this review names.
- Whether making an outcome an explicit design target is an actionable lever, and which of
  Research OS's five learner states each targeted outcome should attach to.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Sets the base-rate expectation overlap-map questions 4 and 12 need: participation in
research-apprenticeship-style activity produces some outcomes with more consistency than
others without design attention, and duration matters. Complicates any assumption that
workspace use alone produces Understanding- or Internalization-state gains, supporting
`PLAN-REVISION-2.md` section 2d's caution that Phase 1 is hypothesis-generating rather than
confirmatory.
