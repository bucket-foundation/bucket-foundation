# Examining Summer Laboratory Research Apprenticeships for High School Students as a Factor in Entry to MD/PhD Programs at Matriculation

A large-n (236,432) quantitative study tracing high-school-level summer laboratory research
apprenticeships across roughly a decade to MD/PhD program matriculation, with a focus on
underrepresented-group outcomes.

## Key Claims

- High-school-level summer laboratory research apprenticeships show a measurable association
  with MD/PhD matriculation nearly a decade later, tracked across 236,432 individuals.
- The study examines whether this association holds for Black, Hispanic/Latino,
  and American Indian/Alaska Native students.
- The decade-long analysis window is framed as evidence that precollege research experiences
  bear on trajectories only visible over a horizon far longer than the experience itself.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the association is causal or reflects a selection effect, since the apprenticeship
  is a competitively awarded opportunity rather than a randomly assigned one.
- What the equivalent long-horizon outcome for a Research OS pilot would be, given this
  study's decade-long window exceeds any funded Phase 1 or Phase 2 timeline.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the long-horizon precedent the funding narratives assert but cannot yet demonstrate:
a precollege research experience with a trackable downstream effect years later, at a scale no
pilot approaches. Bears on `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 12's equity plan directly,
since this study's underrepresented-group focus matches the Title I outreach channel; supports
tracking diversity of who gets a research experience, alongside whether one is offered at
all, as a future-phase outcome.
