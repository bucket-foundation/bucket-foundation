# Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences Can Make Scientific Research More Inclusive

Argues a course-based undergraduate research experience (CURE) reaches a wider and more
representative slice of students than an independent research internship does, because a CURE
is embedded in a required course rather than gated by faculty selection, application writing,
or prior connections.

## Key Claims

- A course-embedded research experience reaches every enrolled student rather than only the
  subset who apply for, are selected into, and can afford an independent research internship.
- Independent research internships select for students who already have the confidence,
  information, and social connections to seek one out.
- Moving course-based research earlier, into introductory-level courses, is framed as a lever
  for reaching students before the internship-selection filter sorts them out.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether inclusion by access also produces inclusion by outcome, a connection this piece
  hopes for rather than measures.
- What an introductory-level, course-embedded research experience should look like below the
  undergraduate level this piece addresses.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Names the access argument for embedding research inside a required, structured pathway rather
than an optional, selection-gated one, the same design choice Research OS for K-12 makes by
routing every learner through one graph-based workspace. Bears on `04-compliance-
distribution.md` section 12's equity plan and on overlap-map question 4: broadening who gets
to attempt research says nothing about whether what they produce meets a domain-scientist
novelty bar, the same gap Auchincloss and colleagues (2014) leave open for lack of a validated
instrument.
