# High school science fair: Positive and negative outcomes

Extends the same research group's prior survey to a national sample, adding career-interest
and research-misconduct measures to the question of what follows from requiring science fair
participation.

## Key Claims

- About 60 percent of surveyed students in the 2017-2018 national cohorts said they were
  interested in a science or engineering career, and about 60 percent said science fair
  participation increased that interest.
- About two-thirds of students were required to participate, and that requirement reduced
  the frequency of students reporting increased interest, relative to voluntary
  participants.
- Among students uninterested in a science or engineering career who were required to
  participate, about 10 percent reported research misconduct such as plagiarism.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the misconduct figure generalizes to a Production workflow where an AI-checked
  citation trail changes what misconduct looks like and how detectable it is.
- What measurable disinterest threshold should route a learner away from a mandatory
  Production requirement rather than toward a citation-payment incentive.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the sharpest complicating data point for overlap-map question 5: among disinterested
students under a participation requirement, a measurable share turned to research misconduct
rather than genuine engagement, a failure mode this batch's Deci-Koestner-Ryan and
Gneezy-Rustichini entries frame in motivational terms without naming the misconduct behavior
itself. Argues for Check's citation-validity verification as a misconduct-detection function
wherever Production carries any mandatory or incentive-attached component.
