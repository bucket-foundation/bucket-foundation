# The Project Ownership Survey: Measuring Differences in Scientific Inquiry Experiences

Builds and validates the Project Ownership Survey (POS), a scaled instrument for measuring
whether a student experiences a research project as their own in a working sense, and shows it
distinguishes students in traditional versus research-based lab courses.

## Key Claims

- Project ownership is one of the psychosocial factors linked to student retention in the
  sciences in undergraduate lab-learning research.
- The 18-item POS, combined with 30 emotion-measurement items, reached a coefficient alpha
  of 0.92.
- The survey distinguished, across repeated comparisons, traditional from research-based lab-course students,
  paralleling prior findings on what characterizes project ownership.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the POS holds its psychometric properties on a K-12 rather than undergraduate
  population.
- Whether project ownership is itself an outcome to track or a mediator toward a different
  outcome such as retention.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies a validated instrument the preregistration draft's Measured Variables table does not
carry: nothing in the current design reads whether a learner experiences a Production
submission as their own, distinct from whether Check or a teacher approves it. A candidate
covariate for H1, testing whether the constrained-AI workspace preserves authorship the way
the no-AI control is expected to, and the full-chatbot arm is hypothesized to erode.
