# Getting Everyone to the Fair: Supporting Teachers in Broadening Participation in Science and Engineering Fairs

Reports a teacher-facing intervention aimed at broadening equitable student participation in
science and engineering fairs, treating teacher capacity as a lever for broadening access,
alongside student interest.

## Key Claims

- Broadening fair participation is framed as a problem teachers can act on directly through
  teacher-facing support rather than only student-facing recruitment.
- The intervention targets teacher capacity to run an inclusive fair process, implying
  participation gaps are partly a function of teacher preparation and support.
- Getting a broader student population to the fair is treated as a precondition for any later
  claim about what fair participation produces, since an outcome measured only among an
  already self-selected pool cannot speak to excluded students.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What the Research OS analog of teacher-facing broadening support looks like, since the
  workspace routes every enrolled learner through the same tools by default rather than
  relying on an opt-in entry point.
- Whether a teacher-support intervention modeled on this approach should target
  `TEACHER-LAYER.md`'s onboarding design, given Research OS's participation model already
  differs structurally from a fair's opt-in structure.

## How It Bears on Research OS

A direct precedent for the teacher-facing, equity-focused support `04-compliance-
distribution.md` section 12's outreach-cohort pairing and `TEACHER-LAYER.md`'s onboarding
design should draw on, read alongside Steegh and colleagues (2019)'s diagnostic finding that
competition-shaped participation is not gender-neutral by default: this paper supplies the
intervention half of the same problem.
