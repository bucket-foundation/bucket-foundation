# Gender differences in mathematics and science competitions: A systematic review

A systematic review of gender differences in participation and achievement across mathematics
and science competitions and Olympiads, documenting that competition-shaped STEM activity is
not gender-neutral by default.

## Key Claims

- Across Olympiad-format competitions internationally, large gender differences in
  participation appeared in every subject reviewed except the biology Olympiad.
- In fairs and national Olympiads, overall participation was not strongly gendered, but
  female students preferred biology topics and male students preferred physics topics, with
  males outperforming females at Olympiads, smallest gap in biology.
- Gender stereotypes, through their influence on self-concept and interest, are proposed as
  a mechanism shaping both participation and achievement.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the biology-versus-physics topic-preference split would reappear in Research OS's
  own routed targets if route choice correlates with gender, undetected by the current
  design.
- Whether a non-competitive, individually-routed Production workflow avoids this gap by
  construction, or whether the same stereotype mechanism could still shape engagement by
  subject branch.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Direct equity evidence for `04-compliance-distribution.md` section 12's stratification plan
and `PREREGISTRATION-DRAFT.md`'s Analysis Plan covariates: competition-format participation
and achievement carry a measurable gender pattern, an argument for tracking route and subject
branch by gender as an exploratory covariate, and a named risk for any future feature
introducing a leaderboard or ranked visibility across learners' Production records.
