# Rethinking Transfer: A Simple Proposal with Multiple Implications

Proposes preparation for future learning (PFL) as a transfer measure: score how well
prior learning lets a learner use new resources on a novel problem, rather than
scoring an unassisted final answer alone.

## Key Claims

- Standard unassisted transfer tests undercount learning that shows up only when a
  learner is given new resources to work with.
- A PFL test gives learners a resource after the original learning phase and measures
  how well they use it on a new problem.
- Learners who built generalizable structure outperformed rote learners on the PFL
  measure, showing no such gap on the standard unassisted test.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which resource-provision design counts as a fair PFL test when the workspace tools
  are themselves the resource under test.
- Whether PFL and standard transfer measures diverge enough in a K-12 population to
  justify running both.
