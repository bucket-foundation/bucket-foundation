# The 2 Sigma Problem: The Search for Methods of Group Instruction as Effective as One-to-One Tutoring

Bloom's 1984 paper reports the result that gives Research OS for K-12 its founding benchmark: students
tutored one-to-one, with short formative tests and correctives between units, ended up about two standard
deviations ahead of students taught the same material in a conventional classroom. A group-instruction
variant that added the same feedback-and-correctives cycle without individual tutors reached roughly half
that gain. Bloom's question, stated directly in the paper's title, is what a classroom can borrow from
tutoring's feedback loop without the cost of one tutor per student. That is the same question a
knowledge-graph platform with per-node mastery tracking and AI-mediated routing is built to answer at a
different scale.

Key claims:
- One-to-one tutoring with mastery testing produced a two-sigma achievement gain over conventional instruction.
- Group mastery learning with feedback and correctives, but no individual tutor, reproduced about one sigma of that gain.
- The feedback-corrective cycle and time-on-task, not individual attention alone, are named as the mechanisms worth reproducing at scale.

Open questions:
- Whether the effect holds over a multi-year curriculum rather than a short instructional unit.
- What computational process should replace a tutor's judgment in deciding when a learner is stuck and what correction to serve.
