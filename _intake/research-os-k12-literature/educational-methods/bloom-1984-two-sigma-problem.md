# The 2 Sigma Problem: The Search for Methods of Group Instruction as Effective as One-to-One Tutoring

One-to-one human tutoring moved the average student to roughly the 98th percentile of a conventionally taught class, a two-standard-deviation gain Bloom called an open problem for any system that cannot staff one tutor per learner. Research OS for K-12 proposes a constrained AI tutor and a frontier-backward routing engine as a scalable substitute for that one-to-one relationship.

## Key Claims

- Students who received one-to-one tutoring under mastery-learning conditions scored about two standard deviations above students in a conventional 30-student classroom.
- The gap between tutored and conventional outcomes holds across multiple replications at different grade levels and subjects.
- Bloom frames closing this gap without one tutor per student as the central open problem for educational research.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which specific components of one-to-one tutoring (pacing, feedback latency, error correction, affect) carry the effect, and which are replicable by a non-human system.
- Whether the effect replicates at the scale and cost a school system can fund.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Bloom's 2-sigma finding is the founding problem statement for the whole product: Research OS names frontier-backward routing, the FSRS plus IRT/Elo mastery fusion in mastery.ts, and the constrained Locate/Quote/Check/Organize tutor as its attempt at a scalable stand-in for the one-to-one tutor Bloom studied. The system review's own three-arm research design (constrained AI vs. full chatbot vs. no AI) is built to measure how much of the 2-sigma gap a scoped AI tutor can close, and how much a full chatbot instead erodes through cognitive offloading.

Bloom's own explanation, and the mastery-learning condition he paired with tutoring, both bear directly on the five-state model (Access through Production) that Research OS adopts from Academy's shipped four-rung ladder.
