# The Wick in the Candle of Learning

Grounds curiosity-driven learning as a measurable state, high-curiosity trivia questions predict better incidental memory, rather than a design metaphor, relevant to how the frontier-backward router's interest-vector reranking should be built.

## Key Claims

- Participants' self-rated curiosity about a trivia question's answer predicted their memory for that answer a week later, independent of how surprising the answer turned out to be.
- High-curiosity states were associated with anticipatory activity in reward-related brain regions before the answer was revealed, framing curiosity as reward-anticipation rather than a purely cognitive gap-filling state.
- Curiosity about a target question also improved incidental memory for an unrelated face shown during the waiting period, evidence that a curious state broadly enhances nearby encoding.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether curiosity-driven interest tracking generalizes from trivia facts to a structured, prerequisite-gated curriculum, where a learner's next step is constrained by what they already know rather than free choice.
- How to measure curiosity passively, from navigation behavior, rather than by self-report, a requirement for any interest-vector computation running at product scale.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supports treating the frontier-backward router's interest-vector rerank as more than a tie-breaking convenience: if curiosity functions as reward-anticipation that also lifts encoding of nearby material, routing a learner toward a self-selected frontier node over an arbitrarily chosen one of equal prerequisite validity may carry a measurable memory benefit worth testing in the planned pilot, beyond a preference-satisfaction one.
