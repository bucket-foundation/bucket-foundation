# Origin and Description of the SOLO Taxonomy

Names the second framework `learning/research-os/LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 2 maps the five Research OS states against, a structural-complexity scale for a single learner response that carries its own claim to unidimensionality, the property `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` item 28 asks whether the five states share.

## Key Claims

- A learner's response to a task can be coded by structural complexity alone, independent of whether it is correct, into five levels: prestructural (the task is missed entirely), unistructural (one relevant element used), multistructural (several relevant elements used, unconnected), relational (the elements integrated into a coherent whole), extended abstract (the relational structure generalized to a new domain or reconceptualized at a higher level).
- The five levels are argued to form one ordered trait, later work runs a formal item-response or Rasch analysis to test that claim on a level-by-level basis rather than asserting it.
- SOLO codes a single response to a single task, not a learner's overall standing in a subject; a learner scoring relational on one task can score unistructural on another, so a SOLO level is a property of a response, not a fixed property of the learner.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an aggregate SOLO-style measure taken across many tasks and nodes, rather than one task at a time, still forms a single ordered trait at the level of a whole subject or a whole graph.
- How to code a task whose material was mostly located and quoted, with limited original synthesis, on the extended-abstract end of the scale, a boundary case Bucket's own Production state runs into directly.

## How It Bears on Research OS

`LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` section 2 maps Awareness to prestructural or unistructural, Understanding to multistructural or relational, Internalization to relational or extended abstract, and notes that Production, a reviewed and cited addition to a shared graph other learners' work will reference, sits past SOLO's own ceiling, since SOLO scores the complexity of one response to a given task rather than the act of adding a new node other tasks will later draw on. `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` item 28's Rasch or IRT fit question borrows SOLO's own unidimensionality claim as the bar the five states have to clear.
