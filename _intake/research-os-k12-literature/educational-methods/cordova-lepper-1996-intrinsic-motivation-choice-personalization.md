# Intrinsic Motivation and the Process of Learning: Beneficial Effects of Contextualization, Personalization, and Choice

An experimental study showing even instructionally incidental choice raised measured
learning, not just stated interest, in elementary-school children.

## Key Claims

- Elementary school children worked with computer-based activities teaching
  arithmetical order-of-operations rules under one control condition (abstract
  presentation) and four experimental conditions crossing contextualization or
  personalization of the material with the presence or absence of incidental choice.
- Contextualization, personalization, and choice each produced large increases in
  students' motivation, depth of engagement, amount learned in a fixed time period, and
  perceived competence and aspiration, relative to the abstract control condition.
- The choices offered were instructionally incidental, for example which of several
  equally valid problem-story settings to use, not choices over the arithmetic content
  itself, yet still produced a motivational and learning benefit.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an incidental-choice design, offering a K-12 learner a choice among equally
  valid gap nodes or equally valid evidence sources rather than a choice over whether to
  do research at all, could recover part of this study's own learning benefit inside a
  mandatory Production requirement.
- Whether the contextualization and personalization manipulations, distinct from choice
  itself, have an analog in how the workspace presents a routed node's own prompt text, a
  design lever this study's own three-factor design suggests is separable from the
  participation-requirement question.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies experimental, not only correlational or meta-analytic, evidence that an
incidental choice, one that does not touch whether the core task is required, still
raises measured learning outcomes, a design option `PLAN-REVISION-3.md` section 2b's own
founder decision does not yet consider: a mandatory Production requirement need not
choose between full learner autonomy and no choice at all, since this study's own
incidental-choice conditions recovered most of the motivational and learning benefit
choice offers without touching whether the underlying task was required.
