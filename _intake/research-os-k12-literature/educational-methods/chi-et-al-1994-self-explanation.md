# Eliciting Self‐Explanations Improves Understanding

The empirical basis for treating self-explanation as a load-bearing activity in the Understanding stage rather than a decorative add-on the AI could shortcut by generating the explanation itself.

## Key Claims

- Students prompted to self-explain while studying worked examples learned more than students who received the same materials without prompting, controlling for time on task.
- High self-explainers generated more inferences that filled gaps in an example's own stated steps, catching comprehension failures low self-explainers missed.
- Self-explanation training, teaching students how to self-explain, improved outcomes further, evidence the skill itself, beyond disposition alone, drives the effect.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which prompts reliably induce productive self-explanation versus shallow paraphrase, a design question for any AI tool meant to elicit it.
- Whether the effect holds when self-explanation is checked or scored, versus purely self-directed as in the original studies.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Directly supports the Research OS design rule that Check "returns a verdict and a pointer to what is missing, never a corrected version of the student's own sentence": the inference-generation act itself produces the learning gain, distinct from exposure to a correct explanation, so an AI that writes the explanation for the student would remove the mechanism this paper identifies.
