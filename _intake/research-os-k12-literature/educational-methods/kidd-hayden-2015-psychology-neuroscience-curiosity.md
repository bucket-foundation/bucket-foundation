# The Psychology and Neuroscience of Curiosity

Reviews evidence that curiosity and voluntary exploration follow an inverted-U across
novelty and complexity, function like a homeostatic drive, and show trait-level
individual differences independent of task reward structure.

## Key Claims

- Curiosity and exploration follow an inverted-U of novelty and complexity, peaking at
  intermediate levels.
- Curiosity functions like a homeostatic drive, building during deprivation and
  resolving on information acquisition.
- Baseline trait curiosity predicts exploration independent of a task's specific reward
  structure.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the inverted-U pattern from adult lab studies holds for K-12 learners in a
  structured workspace.
- How a routing algorithm should combine trait-level curiosity with the state-dependent
  inverted-U pattern.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Bears on `ROUTING.md`'s low-confidence threshold: a target near that threshold may
also sit near the complexity range associated with sustained engagement, a second,
engagement-based rationale for the same threshold.
