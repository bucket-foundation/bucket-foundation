# When and where do we apply what we learn?: A taxonomy for far transfer

Barnett and Ceci's taxonomy names exactly what varies between a practiced task and a transfer task, content, context, and modality among them, giving Research OS's Internalization-stage transfer item a vocabulary for how far a held-out generated item should sit from the material a learner practiced.

## Key Claims

- The transfer literature's own inconsistent findings partly trace to different studies testing near transfer (small variation from the practiced task) while calling it far transfer.
- A taxonomy of transfer distance separates what changes between learning and test along several independent dimensions: content, physical context, temporal context, functional context, social context, and modality.
- Far transfer, generalizing a learned principle across several of these dimensions at once, is rare in the empirical literature and needs a specific kind of instructional design to produce.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which combinations of instructional conditions produce far transfer, as opposed to the more common near transfer, is only partly resolved.
- How to measure transfer distance objectively enough to compare results across different studies and domains.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Internalization is defined as the ability to use an idea 'on a problem never seen before,' generated from a sealed held-out pool with distractors drawn from an error model; Barnett and Ceci's taxonomy is the missing specification for how far from the practiced material that generated item should sit, and their finding that near transfer is far more common than far transfer is a caution against overclaiming Internalization from an item that varies only cosmetically from practice.
