# The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation

Reinterprets curiosity as arising from a perceived information gap between current and
desired knowledge, predicts curiosity peaks at intermediate rather than extreme
uncertainty, and shows naming a specific gap raises measured curiosity over gesturing
at a broad topic.

## Key Claims

- Curiosity arises from a perceived gap between current and desired knowledge, a felt
  deprivation rather than pure reward-seeking.
- The account predicts curiosity peaks at intermediate uncertainty rather than maximal
  or minimal uncertainty.
- Making a gap feel salient and specific raises measured curiosity and
  information-seeking.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a routing target framed as a named gap changes engagement independent of the
  target's graph position.
- How intermediate-uncertainty prediction should set a router's own confidence
  threshold.
