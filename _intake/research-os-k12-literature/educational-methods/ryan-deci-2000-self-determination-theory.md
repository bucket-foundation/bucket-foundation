# Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being

Self-determination theory identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness as the conditions under which intrinsic motivation flourishes, and names a specific mechanism, controlling extrinsic reward, by which gamification can undermine the engagement it targets, directly informing the system review's rule against leaderboards, streaks, and variable-ratio rewards.

## Key Claims

- Intrinsic motivation is supported by conditions that satisfy three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
- Controlling extrinsic rewards (rewards contingent on performance in a way that feels coercive) can undermine intrinsic motivation for an activity that was already interesting on its own.
- Informational feedback that affirms competence without controlling behavior supports intrinsic motivation, while evaluative pressure and surveillance undermine it.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- How the theory's three needs translate into concrete interface decisions at scale, as opposed to the lab and classroom studies the theory was built from.
- Whether a payment-eligible production pipeline introduces the same controlling-reward dynamic the theory warns against, given that money is among the strongest controlling rewards studied.

## How It Bears on Research OS

The gamification section's explicit rejection of leaderboards, streak-loss shaming, and variable-ratio reward mechanics is a direct application of self-determination theory's warning that controlling extrinsic rewards undermine intrinsic motivation for an already-interesting activity; the review's choice to make every stage transition and citation payout deterministic and explainable, rather than an unpredictable bonus, follows the same logic. The theory's autonomy need also bears on the production pipeline's payment design, an open question this paper itself raises: whether payment for a citable production is informational (affirming competence) or controlling, given that money is among the strongest controlling rewards the theory's own research base studied.
