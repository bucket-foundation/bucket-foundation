# Resisting Overzealous Transfer: Coordinating Previously Successful Routines With Needs for New Learning

Names the failure mode where a previously successful routine is overgeneralized to a
case its assumptions no longer fit, and argues coordinating reuse with revision is a
distinct, trainable skill.

## Key Claims

- A routine that worked in earlier training can be applied confidently to a case where
  its assumptions no longer hold.
- Coordinating an old routine with new learning is distinct from either efficient
  execution or open-ended exploration alone.
- Alternating exploration and routine-practice phases produces learners better able to
  detect when a routine needs revision.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What signal in a production would distinguish correct reuse from overzealous
  misapplication.
- Whether alternating exploration and practice maps onto a sequencing choice in the
  router.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Complicates `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q4: a production that reuses a prior claim
structure is not itself evidence of correct transfer, since overzealous reuse produces
the same surface pattern. A Check that verifies only internal claim-evidence
consistency would reward this failure mode rather than catch it.
