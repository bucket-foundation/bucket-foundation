# Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems

Introduces the coding scheme classifying learners as good or poor self-explainers by
counting inference-generating statements in a study transcript, and shows
self-explanation quality predicts later problem-solving accuracy better than time on
task does.

## Key Claims

- Unprompted self-explanation during worked-example study produced more correct later
  problem solving.
- Good self-explainers generated inferences filling implicit steps and caught their own
  errors; poor self-explainers restated steps without inference.
- Self-explanation quality drove the effect more than study time or example count did.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the hand-built coding scheme can be automated at classroom volume.
- How self-explanation quality relates to the Understanding-to-Internalization
  transition, as distinct from problem-solving accuracy at large.
