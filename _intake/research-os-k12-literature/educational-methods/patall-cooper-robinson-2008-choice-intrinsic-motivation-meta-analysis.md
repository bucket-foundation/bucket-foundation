# The Effects of Choice on Intrinsic Motivation and Related Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis of Research Findings

A 41-study meta-analysis finding choice raises intrinsic motivation, effort, and task
performance, with a reward attached to the choice weakening the effect.

## Key Claims

- Pooling 41 studies across child and adult samples, providing choice enhanced intrinsic
  motivation, effort, task performance, and perceived competence relative to no choice,
  across a variety of experimental and applied settings.
- The choice effect on intrinsic motivation was stronger for children than for adults,
  stronger when two to four successive choices were offered rather than one or many, and
  weaker or absent when a reward was given after the choice was made.
- Instructionally irrelevant choices (for example, choice of pencil color) produced a
  larger motivational effect than choices between the instructional activities
  themselves, a counterintuitive finding for any design that assumes only substantive
  choice matters.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a Research OS pilot that makes Production mandatory could still capture part
  of the choice effect by offering choice on an instructionally irrelevant dimension, for
  example which of several equally valid gap nodes to route to, without touching whether
  Production itself is required.
- Whether the reward-weakens-choice moderator this meta-analysis finds interacts with
  the citation-payment incentive overlap-map question 5 already raises, a combination
  (required participation plus payment) this meta-analysis does not test directly since
  its own studies vary choice and reward separately.
