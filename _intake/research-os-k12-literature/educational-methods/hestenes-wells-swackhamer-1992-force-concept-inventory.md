# Force Concept Inventory

Introduces a multiple-choice instrument whose wrong answers are drawn from documented
student misconceptions about force and motion, and reports that instructors
systematically overestimate how well their students understand the concepts it tests.

## Key Claims

- The FCI's distractors are drawn from documented misconceptions, making a wrong
  answer diagnostic of which misconception a learner holds.
- Instructor judgments of student understanding routinely overestimated measured FCI
  performance.
- FCI gains under interactive-engagement instruction exceeded lecture-based gains by
  roughly two standard deviations across later replications, making it one of the
  most-replicated instruments in physics education research.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a misconception-distractor design generalizes past physics, where
  misconception catalogs are thinner.
- How a fixed inventory's diagnostic power compares with an open-ended Check verdict.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Bears on `LEARNER-STATE-MODEL.md` OPEN-5's Rasch or IRT validation question: the FCI
is the field's canonical example of a concept instrument built through that process.
Its teacher-overestimate finding sharpens OPEN-7's question about the rate at which
a teacher's contest tracks the graph's classification versus the teacher's own
confidence.
