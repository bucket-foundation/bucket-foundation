# Assessing the effects of gamification in the classroom: A longitudinal study on intrinsic motivation, social comparison, satisfaction, effort, and academic performance

This is a rare longitudinal classroom study measuring gamification's effect on real outcomes rather than short-term engagement, and it found a badge-and-leaderboard design lowered intrinsic motivation, satisfaction, and final exam scores relative to a non-gamified control, a direct empirical warning against the leaderboard mechanic Research OS's design explicitly excludes.

## Key Claims

- Across a 16-week university course, students in a gamified section (badges, a leaderboard, and levels) reported lower motivation, satisfaction, and perceived autonomy over time than students in a non-gamified section covering the same material.
- Final exam scores were lower in the gamified section than in the control, the opposite of what a naive engagement-first framing of gamification would predict.
- Social comparison introduced by the leaderboard is the specific mechanism the authors point to for the drop in intrinsic motivation, distinct from gamification as a broader category.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether gamification elements that avoid social comparison (progress bars, mastery badges without ranking) produce different results than the leaderboard-based design tested here.
- How gamification effects differ by age band, since this study used university students rather than K-12 learners.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Hanus and Fox's finding, that the leaderboard (via social comparison) drove the drop in motivation and performance, is direct empirical support for the system review's rule against leaderboards ranking children against each other and against public comparison for young learners; the review's choice to make the visible frontier on the canvas itself (mastered territory lit, the target ahead) the primary game loop, with no separate points system, is the kind of gamification design this study's own null-to-negative leaderboard result argues for instead.
