# What We Know, What We Still Need to Know: Teaching Adolescents to Write

Synthesizes three lines of evidence on effective adolescent writing instruction: a
meta-analysis of experimental and quasi-experimental studies, a meta-analysis of
single-subject design studies, and a qualitative analysis of effective schools and teachers.

## Key Claims

- The synthesis draws on a comprehensive meta-analysis of experimental and
  quasi-experimental studies, an original single-subject-design meta-analysis, and a
  qualitative analysis of effective-schools themes.
- Specific effective adolescent writing-instruction elements are named with measured support
  across these three evidence sources, rather than treating writing instruction as one
  undifferentiated practice.
- Recommendations for further research are linked to gaps in these three analyses, extended
  toward the need to contextualize research-based writing interventions in real instructional
  settings.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which named effective writing-instruction elements, drawn from adolescent classroom
  writing broadly, transfer to the shorter, evidence-anchored claim format a Production
  requires rather than an extended essay.
- Whether contextualizing a research-based writing intervention for a real setting, this
  article's own closing concern, is already addressed by Research OS's teacher-review step.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies a named, evidence-ranked menu of writing-instruction elements for whatever Check and
Organize eventually scaffold in a Production's claim-writing step, extending Bangert-Drowns,
Hurley, and Wilkinson (2004)'s metacognitive-prompting finding with a broader adolescent-
writer-specific evidence base. Bears on overlap-map question 5 by naming what process signals
a payout tied to writing quality should reward.
