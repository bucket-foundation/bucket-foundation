# The Effects of School-Based Writing-to-Learn Interventions on Academic Achievement

A meta-analysis of 48 school-based writing-to-learn programs, finding a small positive
achievement effect overall, with metacognitive prompting and treatment length predicting
stronger effects.

## Key Claims

- This meta-analysis of 48 programs found writing can have a small, positive impact on
  academic achievement, an effect the prior literature had characterized as ambiguous.
- Metacognitive prompting within the writing task and increased treatment length predicted
  enhanced effects.
- Implementation in Grades 6 through 8 and longer individual writing assignments predicted
  reduced effects.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether Check's self-explanation and Organize's restructuring prompts already function as
  the metacognitive prompts this meta-analysis finds predict enhanced effects, or need
  explicit redesign to carry that function.
- Whether the weaker Grades 6-8 effect this meta-analysis finds bears on Research OS's choice
  of a grades 3-5 front door over the grades 9-12 quantum-history path.
