# Oversold and Underused: Computers in the Classroom

A historical study of computer adoption in US schools through the 1980s and 1990s,
finding usage settled into existing instructional routines rather than the promised
reorganized, student-centered teaching, shaped by school structural constraints rather
than the technology itself.

## Key Claims

- Heavy computer investment settled mostly into existing routines rather than the
  reorganized teaching the investment case promised.
- Teachers who used computers most did so for tasks fitting their existing practice
  with least disruption.
- School structural constraints, fixed class periods, teacher isolation, testing
  pressure, shaped adoption regardless of which technology was involved.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether these structural constraints still bind a Research OS pilot, or the
  workspace's own scoping sidesteps them.
- What evidence would distinguish a pilot's adoption pattern from the
  routine-absorption pattern this study documents.
