# Learning Analytics Dashboard Applications

Surveys learning-analytics dashboards across higher education and finds most ship
without a published evaluation of their effect on teacher decisions, favoring raw
activity counts over inferred constructs despite the latter's stronger predictive
value.

## Key Claims

- Most surveyed dashboards had no published study of their effect on teaching
  decisions, outcomes, or teacher time.
- Dashboards visualizing raw activity counts were far more common than ones
  visualizing inferred constructs, despite weaker predictive value for raw counts.
- Uptake depended on interpretive effort, a design variable separate from data volume.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an inferred-state visualization avoids the raw-count pattern this survey
  finds dominant, or reduces to the same interpretive load in different form.
- What published evaluation standard a class view would need to clear.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Fills the citation gap `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q49 already names without a corpus card
until now. Its finding that most dashboards ship unevaluated cautions against treating
class-view design as validated by precedent rather than by Q49's own pilot measurement.
