# The Effect of Teacher Coaching on Instruction and Achievement: A Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence

Reviews causal studies of teacher coaching and finds individualized, content-specific
feedback on a teacher's own instruction produces larger effects on practice and
achievement than group professional development, with effects shrinking as coaches
serve more teachers.

## Key Claims

- Individualized, content-specific coaching feedback outperformed group professional
  development on both instructional practice and student achievement.
- Effect sizes on practice were larger and more consistent than effect sizes on
  achievement.
- Program effects shrank as the number of teachers per coach grew.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a class view's per-student data functions as feedback on a teacher's own
  instruction, or only on individual students.
- What scale limit a class view would hit if its signal quality depends on
  individualized attention.

## How It Bears on Research OS
