# Addressing First- and Second-Order Barriers to Change: Strategies for Technology Integration

Splits classroom technology adoption barriers into first-order, extrinsic and
resourcing-solvable, and second-order, intrinsic beliefs that resist removal by
resourcing alone, and argues removing only the first kind leaves practice unchanged.

## Key Claims

- First-order barriers, time, access, training, are extrinsic and removable by
  resourcing decisions above the classroom.
- Second-order barriers, a teacher's beliefs about learning and good teaching, are
  intrinsic and resist removal by resourcing alone.
- Removing first-order barriers alone leaves teachers using a new resource inside their
  existing pedagogical routine rather than a changed one.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What second-order barrier a class view would need to address before a teacher trusts
  it enough to change a grouping decision.
- Whether district AI training targets first-order or second-order barriers, or
  neither.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Bears on `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q15 and Q16: a class view removes a first-order
barrier, access to information, but this framework argues that alone will not move a
teacher's decisions without engaging existing beliefs about evidence of understanding.
