# A Large-Scale Implementation of Predictive Learning Analytics in Higher Education: The Teachers' Role and Perspective

Studies a predictive learning-analytics dashboard deployed at institutional scale and
finds teacher trust rose with mechanism training rather than output alone, varied by
role, and concentrated distrust on individual mismatches rather than blanket
skepticism.

## Key Claims

- Teachers trained on how the model generated risk flags reported higher trust and
  more frequent use than teachers who saw flags without that explanation.
- Trust varied by role: teachers directly responsible for an outcome trusted and acted
  on flags more than teachers in a supporting role.
- Distrust of a specific flag most traced to a mismatch with a teacher's own
  independent knowledge, rather than general skepticism of predictive analytics.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a contest path resolves the mismatch pattern this study finds, or teachers
  keep distrusting the underlying model after one successful override.
- How training on the state model's mechanism, alongside its output, would change
  trust.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Extends `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q16 with field evidence: mechanism training raises
trust past output-only training, and distrust concentrates on individual mismatches
rather than blanket skepticism, both testable claims for the class view's onboarding.
