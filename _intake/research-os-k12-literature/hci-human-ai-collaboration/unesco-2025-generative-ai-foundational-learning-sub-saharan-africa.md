# From promise to practice: harnessing generative AI to improve foundational learning in Sub-Saharan Africa

An institutional evidence review of generative-AI deployments for foundational learning in Sub-Saharan African school systems, the closest verified match to the AI-tutoring deployment literature in lower-resource school systems the task named by region.

## Key Claims

- Early generative-AI pilots for foundational literacy and numeracy in Sub-Saharan African school systems show promise on narrow, well-scoped tasks but face large infrastructure, connectivity, and teacher-training gaps before they can scale.
- Deployments that pair AI tools with structured teacher support and offline-capable delivery outperform unstructured, chatbot-style deployments on foundational-learning outcomes.
- Data governance, language coverage (most tools are English-first in multilingual school systems), and cost per learner are named as binding constraints on scale, ahead of model capability itself.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which specific deployment design choices, teacher pairing, offline mode, language coverage, causally drive the outcome difference, versus which are correlated with better-resourced pilot sites.
- Whether findings from foundational literacy and numeracy generalize to the graph-navigation and production-pipeline design Research OS proposes, a structurally different kind of AI deployment.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Directly validates two of the system review's own design commitments as non-optional rather than aspirational for global reach: the offline-mode content bundle (Section 3) and the machine-translate-then-human-review locale pipeline both address the infrastructure and language gaps this review names as the binding constraint on scale, ahead of model capability.
