# To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-Assisted Decision-Making

Tests interface designs that force a person to commit to their own answer before
seeing an AI recommendation and finds they reduce over-reliance on wrong AI answers,
at a cost in speed and reported effort, with a retention benefit that outlasts the
assisted session.

## Key Claims

- Cognitive forcing functions reduced over-reliance on incorrect AI answers more than
  explanation-only interfaces.
- The forcing-function conditions cost speed and reported effort compared with
  immediate-reveal interfaces.
- Participants in forcing-function conditions retained more accurate independent
  judgment on a later, AI-free task.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether gathering evidence before Check runs already functions as a forcing
  function, or needs an explicit commit-before-reveal step.
- Whether the friction and speed cost documented here would be tolerated at the same
  rate by K-12 learners and teachers.
