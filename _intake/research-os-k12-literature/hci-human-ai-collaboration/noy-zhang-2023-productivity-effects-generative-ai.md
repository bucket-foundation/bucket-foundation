# Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence

Tests generative AI assistance on a writing task, finding the largest quality gains
among lower-scoring participants, a narrowed spread of scores across the sample, and
a self-reported enjoyability cost alongside the measured gains.

## Key Claims

- AI assistance reduced completion time and raised output quality, with the largest
  gains among lowest-scoring participants.
- Gains concentrated among lower scorers, narrowing the spread of quality scores
  across participants rather than lifting everyone uniformly.
- Participants reported the AI-assisted work felt less enjoyable and more repetitive
  than the unassisted version.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether Organize's restructuring narrows quality spread across a class the way this
  study's assistance narrowed spread across writers.
- Whether the enjoyability cost would show up in K-12 learners and interact with a
  citation-payment incentive.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Extends overlap map question 1 with a companion finding to Doshi and Hauser (2024):
AI assistance narrows the gap between weaker and stronger writers even as it narrows
the diversity of ideas across writers, two distinct class-level effects the testbed's
per-student measures do not separate.
