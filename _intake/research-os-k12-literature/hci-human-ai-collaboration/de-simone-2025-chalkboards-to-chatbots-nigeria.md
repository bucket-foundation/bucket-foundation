# From Chalkboards to Chatbots: Evaluating the Impact of Generative AI on Learning Outcomes in Nigeria

A World Bank randomized evaluation of an after-school AI tutoring program in Nigerian secondary
schools, six weeks of structured access to a Microsoft Copilot-based tutor with a facilitator on
site. Students gained close to two years of typical learning progress on an English-proficiency
measure, an effect concentrated among lower-performing students and among girls. The report
credits both the tool and the structure wrapped around it, without fully separating the two.

## Key Claims

- Six weeks of structured AI tutor access produced close to two years of typical learning
  progress on English proficiency, larger than most in-school interventions the report reviews.
- Gains concentrated among lower-performing students and girls, narrowing the baseline gap.
- The scripted activity structure and on-site facilitator appear to matter alongside the tool
  itself.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the gain holds without a facilitator present or over a longer, unsupervised period.
- How much of the effect traces to the AI tutor's own responses versus the structure around it.

## How It Bears on Research OS

This is the closest real-world precedent for Research OS's own three-arm cognitive-offloading
testbed: a structured, facilitator-present AI tutoring deployment produced a large effect size
in a low-resource setting, but the report cannot separate the AI's own contribution from the
scripted structure around it. Research OS's own three-arm design is built to answer exactly that
attribution question.
