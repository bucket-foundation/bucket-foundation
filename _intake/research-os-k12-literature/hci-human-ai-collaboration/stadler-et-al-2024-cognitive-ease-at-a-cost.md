# Cognitive Ease at a Cost

LLM assistance during a scientific-inquiry task lowered students' reported mental
effort and lowered independently rated inquiry depth together, with the two
reductions correlated within the assisted group.

## Key Claims

- LLM-assisted students reported lower subjective mental effort than unassisted
  students on the same inquiry task.
- LLM-assisted students produced shallower inquiry outputs, rated by independent
  coders, despite the lower reported effort.
- Effort reduction and depth reduction were correlated within the assisted group.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a tool scoped to evidence-gathering without hypothesis generation breaks
  the effort-depth correlation found here for undifferentiated assistance.
- Whether requiring a minimum evidence count before submission maintains depth better
  than relying on effort as a proxy.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Is a precedent for testing whether a scoped tool avoids the effort-depth trade this
paper documents for undifferentiated assistance: since Locate and Quote run with no
model call, this paper's depth-coding method is a candidate outcome measure for the
transfer test named in `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q8.
