# Cognitive Offloading

A review naming cognitive offloading as the use of physical action, a reminder, a
calculator, a search query, to reduce the internal demand a task would place on memory
or computation, and framing it as a metacognitive strategy-selection process rather
than a single behavior.

## Key Claims

- Offloading is a decision: a person weighs internal-processing cost against the cost
  of the offloading action, then chooses.
- Offloading reliably improves performance on the specific task offloaded, across
  reminders, navigation, and arithmetic.
- Whether offloading changes retained capacity once the aid is removed, versus only
  behavior while the aid is present, is named as unresolved.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether repeated offloading changes retained capacity over months or years.
- What governs the decision to offload, and whether that decision process can be
  trained.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Research OS's three-arm testbed is a direct test of the capacity question this review
leaves open: whether a learner who offloads retrieval to the Locate tool but keeps the
check-against-evidence step retains more than one who offloads both. `RESEARCH-
QUESTIONS.md` Q8 and Q11 already ask a version of this question; this paper supplies
the general framework both sit inside.
