An EEG study of essay writing under LLM-assisted, search-assisted, and brain-only
conditions, finding the weakest neural connectivity and weakest later recall of one's
own content in the LLM-assisted condition.

## Key Claims

- LLM-assisted writing showed the weakest EEG-measured neural connectivity of the
  three conditions tested; brain-only showed the strongest.
- LLM-assisted participants later quoted their own prior essay content less
  accurately than participants who had written unassisted.
- Participants who wrote unassisted before using an LLM retained higher connectivity
  than participants who used the LLM first, an order effect.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the EEG connectivity measure predicts a specific, testable learning outcome.
- Whether the order effect generalizes to a hint-ladder design rather than a strict
  before-and-after ordering.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Adds a physiological measure to Bastani and colleagues (2025)'s behavioral cost,
pointing at the same conclusion Research OS's design encodes: unassisted practice
protects something an unrestricted assistant erodes. The order effect gives a testable
sequencing hypothesis: whether a learner should reach Understanding unassisted before
the AI workspace opens at Production.
