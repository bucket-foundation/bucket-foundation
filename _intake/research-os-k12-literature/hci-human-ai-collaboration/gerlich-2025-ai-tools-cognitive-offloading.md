# AI Tools in Society

A survey linking frequent AI-tool use to lower critical-thinking scores through a
cognitive-offloading pathway, strongest among younger and more educated respondents,
using a cross-sectional, self-report design.

## Key Claims

- Heavier reported AI-tool use predicted lower critical-thinking scores, mediated
  statistically by cognitive offloading.
- The offloading tendency was stronger among younger and more educated respondents.
- The cross-sectional, self-report design cannot establish that AI use causes the
  lower scores.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the mediation holds under a longitudinal or experimental design.
- Whether a performance-based critical-thinking measure would replicate the
  self-report pattern.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Serves as a design warning: Research OS's own evaluation should prefer unassisted
performance tests, the approach Bastani and Macnamara and colleagues (2024) take,
over this study's self-report exposure and outcome measures.
