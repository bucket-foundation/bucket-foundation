# From Chalkboards to Chatbots: Evaluating the Impact of Generative AI on Learning Outcomes in Nigeria

De Simone and a World Bank team evaluated an after-school generative-AI tutoring pilot delivered to
secondary school students in Edo State, Nigeria, built around a GPT-4 based chatbot used with
structured prompting and teacher facilitation rather than open, unsupervised access. Over a six-week
window, students in the program showed large gains on English-language learning outcomes relative to a
comparison group, a result the authors describe as comparable to roughly two years of typical learning
progress compressed into six weeks. The gains were uneven across the student population: girls and
students who attended more consistently saw larger benefits, and the authors are explicit that program
facilitation and prompt design likely did much of the work, above the chatbot alone. As a field study in
a resource-constrained school system rather than a lab setting or a well-resourced classroom, it is one
of the more consequential data points so far on whether generative-AI tutoring can work outside
wealthy-country conditions.

Key claims:
- Students in the program showed large English-language gains over a six-week pilot relative to a comparison group, described as comparable to roughly two years of typical progress.
- Gains concentrated among girls and among students who attended more consistently, unevenly across the participant pool.
- The program's structured prompting and teacher facilitation were central to the design and to the authors' explanation of the result, above unsupervised chatbot access.

Open questions:
- Whether the six-week gains persist over a longer horizon, or generalize from an after-school format into regular classroom instruction, is untested.
- The bundled facilitation and prompting design prevents the paper from isolating how much of the effect is attributable to the AI tool itself versus the human facilitation around it.
