# Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

Field-tests generative AI on real knowledge-worker tasks and finds gains concentrate
inside the AI's own capability frontier while assisted performance falls below a
no-AI baseline outside it, with the frontier invisible to users without a signal.

## Key Claims

- AI-assisted workers outperformed a no-AI control on tasks inside the AI's own
  capability frontier, a large and consistent effect.
- On a task placed outside that frontier, AI-assisted workers performed worse than the
  no-AI control.
- Participants could not tell from the interface alone which side of the
  frontier a task fell on.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the four-tool scoping keeps every learner task inside a knowable capability
  frontier.
- What interface signal would tell a learner or teacher which side of the frontier a
  query sits on.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Extends overlap map question 1 with an empirical case for scoping: the same
assistance that helps inside its capability frontier can perform below a no-AI
baseline outside it, and the frontier is invisible without an explicit signal.
