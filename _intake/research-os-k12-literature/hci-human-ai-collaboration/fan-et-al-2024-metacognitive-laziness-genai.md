# Beware of Metacognitive Laziness

Generative-AI access during a learning task reduced students' self-reported planning
and monitoring, which statistically mediated a drop in subsequent unassisted test
performance, with a larger effect for students with weaker prior self-regulation
skill.

## Key Claims

- AI access reduced self-reported planning and monitoring relative to a no-AI
  control, controlling for prior achievement.
- The reduction in planning and monitoring mediated part of the link between AI
  access and lower unassisted test performance.
- Students with weaker prior self-regulated-learning skill showed a larger drop in
  planning and monitoring under AI access, an equity-relevant interaction.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an explicit planning-and-monitoring scaffold prevents the effect without
  losing AI's benefits.
- Whether the equity interaction replicates in a K-12 population, given the study's
  university-student sample.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Names a mechanism distinct from answer substitution: metacognitive laziness targets
planning and monitoring, the behavior the Organize tool is built to scaffold rather
than replace. The equity interaction argues for measuring the three-arm testbed's
outcomes by prior self-regulation subgroup, not only by average effect. Extends
`RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md` Q11.
