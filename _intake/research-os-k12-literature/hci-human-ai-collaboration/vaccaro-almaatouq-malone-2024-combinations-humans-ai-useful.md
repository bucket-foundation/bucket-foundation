# When Combinations of Humans and AI Are Useful: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

> the canon entry is the citeable record.

A meta-analysis across many studies finding human-AI combination outperforms the
better of either alone on decision tasks but underperforms the better of either alone
on content-creation tasks, with task decomposability a strong moderator.

## Key Claims

- Human-AI combination outperformed the better of either alone on decision-making
  tasks but underperformed on content-creation tasks, averaged across studies.
- The decision-versus-content-creation gap was a stronger moderator than AI capability
  level or collaboration format.
- Combination gains were larger when a task split into separable subcomponents than
  when human and AI had to jointly produce one output.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a production, a claim with cited evidence, counts as content creation or as
  a decomposable decision task by this meta-analysis's own split.
- Whether separating retrieval work from claim-writing would move a production's
  outcome toward the decision-task pattern.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Complicates overlap map questions 1 and 10: combination helps on decision tasks but
hurts on content creation on average, arguing against assuming the workspace
automatically produces a complementary gain on a student's written claim.
