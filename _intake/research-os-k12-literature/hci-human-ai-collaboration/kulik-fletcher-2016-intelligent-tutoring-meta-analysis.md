# Effectiveness of Intelligent Tutoring Systems

A second, larger, more recent intelligent-tutoring-systems meta-analysis than VanLehn's, updating the expected effect size and identifying study-quality moderators Research OS's own pilot design should control for.

## Key Claims

- Across 50 controlled evaluations, intelligent tutoring systems raised test scores by about 0.66 standard deviations on average relative to conventional instruction, a robust but more modest figure than some earlier, smaller studies reported.
- Effect sizes were larger in studies with weaker methodological controls, non-randomized designs, researcher-made outcome measures, meaning the pooled estimate likely overstates the effect a rigorous randomized study would find.
- Intelligent-tutoring effects held across subject areas and across K-12 and higher-education populations, evidence the benefit is not specific to one domain.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether effect sizes from pre-LLM tutoring-system architectures transfer to a generative-AI-based tutor with a different error profile, hallucination risk instead of narrow-domain brittleness.
- What the true effect size is once study-quality moderators are fully controlled, a question the meta-analysis flags but cannot fully resolve from the available literature.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Directly informs the planned three-arm pilot's own design rigor requirement: this meta-analysis's own finding, that weaker study designs inflate effect estimates, is the reason the system review insists on a formal power analysis and an IRB-approved randomized design rather than an internal program evaluation, to avoid contributing another inflated estimate to this literature.
