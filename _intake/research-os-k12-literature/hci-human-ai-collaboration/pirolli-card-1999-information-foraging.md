# Information foraging.

> **Promoted to canon, 2026-09-10.** This record lives in

> the citeable record.

A formal theory of how people move through a mix of relevant and irrelevant information under a cost-benefit tradeoff, the closest existing model for how a learner should be expected to move through the spatial canvas workspace's mix of mastered, gap, and target nodes.

## Key Claims

- People adapt their information-seeking strategies to maximize the rate of valuable information gained per unit cost, an adaptation the theory models by analogy to optimal foraging theory in animal ecology.
- Information scent, the perceived proximity of a cue to the sought information, predicts navigation choices: people follow the strongest-scented path even when it is not objectively shortest.
- Foraging strategies shift with the structure of the information environment, patchy versus uniform, predicting different navigation patterns depending on how content is organized into clusters.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- How information scent applies to a graph-structured canvas rather than the hypertext and document-collection environments the theory was built on.
- Whether a system can improve a learner's scent-following accuracy by design, label wording, visual proximity, rather than only measuring it after the fact.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Gives the spatial canvas workspace a design lens beyond visual clarity: the mastered-lit, gap-dim, target-ahead rendering is an information-scent design, and if the labels and visual proximity of gap nodes do not carry a strong enough scent toward the target, a learner may forage inefficiently through the canvas even when the underlying frontier-backward routing computed an optimal path.
