# Science of science

This review matters to Bucket Foundation because it names the field Bucket's own claims about
itself belong to. The paper surveys how large bibliometric and career datasets reveal measurable,
repeated patterns in scientific careers, collaboration, and impact, and argues findings like these
should feed back into how science is funded and organized. That argument
applies directly to the Research OS: routing a student toward a specific gap node, identified from
patterns in the evidence graph, is a small working version of the same feedback loop the paper
calls for at national scale, from measuring how science works to changing how it gets done. It
also sets the bar Bucket's own tournament and gap-node output should meet: a claim that the ranking
surfaces something real is a testable science-of-science claim in its own right.

Key claims:
- Bibliometric and career data reveal measurable, recurring regularities across many scientists and institutions, beyond individual case histories.
- Machine-readable science metadata now supports predictive modeling of research trajectories and impact.
- The field argues for closing the loop between studying how science works and using those findings to shape science policy and institutional design.

Open questions:
- Predicting which individual paper or career will matter remains an open problem the review names directly.
- How findings from large, well-digitized fields generalize to smaller or newer fields with sparser metadata.
