# Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science

This paper matters to Bucket Foundation as a measured answer to a question the belief-fusion
engine has to answer by rule: how much should one published claim move a belief score before
anyone has tried to reproduce it. The Open Science Collaboration ran 100 replication attempts
against the original studies' own materials with higher statistical power than the originals had,
and found that around a third reproduced a significant effect in the same direction, with effect
sizes about half the original size on average. Neither the original study's p-value nor its
authors' own confidence predicted which ones would hold up. That result argues for treating a
single publication as a starting weight to be raised once something checks it
independently, which is the same logic the Research OS should apply to a K-12 transfer-proof: it
earns trust through a teacher's independent check against the original claim, the same mechanism a
replication provides.

Key claims:
- About 36 percent of 100 replications reached significance in the original direction, against 97 percent in the original studies.
- Replicated effect sizes averaged about half the originally reported size, even among significant replications.
- Original p-values and author confidence did not predict which studies would replicate.

Open questions:
- Whether a given failed replication reflects a false original finding, a context-dependent effect, or a flawed replication attempt.
- How the same replication protocol would perform outside psychology, given the field-specific sample.
