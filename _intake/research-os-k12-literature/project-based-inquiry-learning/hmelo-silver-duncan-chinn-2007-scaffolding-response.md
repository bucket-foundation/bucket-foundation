# Scaffolding and Achievement in Problem-Based and Inquiry Learning

A direct reply to Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006), arguing problem-based learning (PBL)
and inquiry learning (IL) are highly scaffolded approaches conflated by that paper with
unguided discovery learning.

## Key Claims

- Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006) are argued to have mistakenly grouped PBL and IL with
  unguided discovery learning, when both are in practice highly scaffolded.
- PBL and IL are effective precisely because they employ extensive scaffolding, reducing
  cognitive load, the same mechanism Kirschner and colleagues invoke against unguided
  approaches.
- PBL and IL address goals beyond content knowledge, including epistemic practices and soft
  skills such as collaboration and self-directed learning.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What specific, checkable criteria distinguish highly scaffolded PBL or IL from minimally
  guided instruction, past asserting the two are different.
- Whether Research OS's own four-tool constraint meets this paper's scaffolding bar, and
  which specific tool maps to which specific scaffolding function this paper names.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Resolves the Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006) complication conditionally: the four-tool
workspace's explicit constraint, no tool writes the answer, evidence committed to before
Check's verdict, teacher review at Production, is structurally closer to the extensive
scaffolding this paper defends than to unguided discovery learning. The condition is that
this paper's scaffolding criteria get checked against the workspace tool by tool rather than
assumed by category resemblance. Extends overlap-map questions 1 and 10 as the resolving half
of this batch's guidance debate.
