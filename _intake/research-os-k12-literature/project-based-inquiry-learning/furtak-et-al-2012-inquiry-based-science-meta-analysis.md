# Experimental and Quasi-Experimental Studies of Inquiry-Based Science Teaching

A meta-analysis of 37 experimental and quasi-experimental studies of inquiry-based science
teaching, distinguishing cognitive activity type from degree of student guidance, and finding
teacher-led activities outperformed student-led ones by a wide margin.

## Key Claims

- Pooling 37 studies from 1996 to 2006, inquiry-based science teaching produced an overall
  mean effect size of 0.50 relative to comparison instruction.
- Studies contrasting epistemic activities, or a combination of procedural, epistemic, and
  social activities, had the highest mean effect sizes.
- Teacher-led inquiry activities had mean effect sizes about 0.40 larger than student-led
  conditions.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Where a four-tool, AI-scoped workspace falls on this paper's teacher-led-versus-student-led
  axis, given the tools substitute for parts of a teacher's guidance role while a human
  teacher still reviews Production.
- Whether the roughly 0.40 effect-size gap between teacher-led and student-led inquiry is a
  ceiling a scoped AI tool can close, since the paper's own contrast never varied AI
  involvement.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Load-bearing for overlap-map questions 1 and 10: teacher-led guidance
outperforms student-led guidance within inquiry teaching as a whole, the empirical stakes
behind whether Research OS's four-tool workspace supplies guidance closer in kind to a
teacher's own scaffolding or to unguided student-led inquiry. Read together with Kirschner,
Sweller, and Clark (2006) and Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn (2007), this paper's
guidance-level finding is the quantitative anchor for their qualitative debate.
