# Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning

The founding statement of cognitive load theory: a novice learner's working memory is a
limited resource, and an unguided problem-solving search consumes that resource on the
search itself rather than on building the schema the task is meant to teach.

## Key Claims

- Conventional problem-solving practice, exemplified by means-ends analysis, requires a
  novice solver to hold the current problem state, the goal state, and the differences
  between them in working memory at once, a search process that imposes a heavy load
  unrelated to the schema the task is meant to teach.
- Two experiments, on algebra transformation problems and on geometry problems, each
  replicated across problem sets, compare students who solved conventional problems against
  students who studied worked examples of the same problem types.
- Students who studied worked examples solved subsequent transfer problems faster and more
  accurately than students who practiced conventional problem-solving on the same material,
  read as evidence that worked examples teach the underlying schema at lower cognitive load
  than search-based practice.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the algebra- and geometry-problem domains this paper's own experiments use
  generalize to open-ended, multi-source research tasks, where there is no single
  transformation-problem structure to hold in working memory.
- What a worked-example equivalent looks like for a research task rather than a mathematics
  problem, since the paper's worked-example intervention is specific to problems with a
  defined start state, goal state, and operator set.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the mechanism Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006)'s guidance argument and
Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn (2007)'s scaffolding reply both argue from: whether a
four-tool workspace helps or harms a novice learner turns on whether its own guidance reduces
the working-memory load an unguided search would impose. Extends overlap-map questions 1 and
10 as the mechanism-level foundation underneath both papers' own claims.
