# Automating the practice of science: Opportunities, challenges, and implications

A companion piece to Binz and colleagues (2025), evaluating automated-science approaches
broadly and naming ethical and practical consequences of automation.

## Key Claims

- Evaluates the current scope of automation in scientific practice and assesses recent
  automated-discovery approaches, treating automation as a broader category than
  LLM-based tools specifically, King and colleagues (2009)'s Automation of Science and
  Boiko and colleagues (2023)'s Coscientist among the kinds of systems it surveys.
- Discusses where the greatest opportunities for automation in scientific practice lie
  and what current bottlenecks limit further automation, treating opportunity and
  bottleneck as distinct questions rather than a single readiness score.
- Names ethical and practical consequences of automating scientific practice as a
  distinct discussion from the technical opportunities and bottlenecks, inviting
  researchers, policymakers, and other stakeholders to engage with the frontier of
  automated science rather than presenting it as a settled trajectory.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Where the engine's own campaign runs, hypothesis generation, calibration, and ranking
  over a fixed corpus, sit on this paper's own opportunity-versus-bottleneck map, a
  placement no current engine document performs.
- Whether the ethical consequences this paper names for automating scientific practice
  generally have a K-12-specific counterpart once a Research OS gap node routes a learner
  toward automated-hypothesis-generated territory rather than human-authored curriculum.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Broadens Binz and colleagues (2025)'s own LLM-specific debate to automation as a
category, giving King and colleagues (2009)'s Automation of Science, already in this
corpus, and the engine's own campaign machinery a place in a live 2025 discussion rather
than only a 2009 one: this paper's own opportunity-and-bottleneck framing, read against
the engine's own real-corpora calibration gap PR #48 closed, argues the engine's current
bottleneck is calibration accuracy on real data rather than an ethical limit, a
distinction relevant to which of this paper's own named consequences, practical or
ethical, the engine's own remaining open items (a conflicting-evidence stress test, an
Allen-relations check) actually address.
