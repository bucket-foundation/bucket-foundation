# The Automation of Science

An earlier, physically embodied precedent, the Adam robot scientist, for a fully automated hypothesis-generation-and-testing loop in biology, useful as a baseline for how far AI-for-science has and has not moved since 2009 relative to newer AI Scientist and Coscientist systems.

## Key Claims

- The Adam robot scientist autonomously generated hypotheses about yeast gene function, designed experiments to test them, physically executed those experiments using laboratory automation, and interpreted the results, a closed loop with no human hypothesis or experiment design in it.
- Adam's own generated and tested hypotheses were independently confirmed to include novel, previously unknown gene-function findings, rather than a re-derivation of already-published results.
- The system's scope was narrow by design, a well-characterized model organism, a bounded hypothesis space over gene function, a scope the authors argue was necessary to make full automation tractable at the time.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- How much of Adam's early narrowness was a necessary condition for full automation to work at all, versus an artifact of the tools available at the time, a question sharpened by comparing against later systems with far broader scope but less complete automation.
- Whether Adam's closed physical loop or a newer system's broader-but-partially-supervised loop represents the more reliable design pattern for automation, an open methodological question the two approaches answer differently.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Functions as a historical baseline against which the hypothesis engine's own textual-evidence loop can be read: Adam ran a fully closed physical loop in a narrow domain well before this corpus's newer AI-Scientist and Coscientist entries attempted a broader, less physically embodied version of the same idea, and the contrast underscores that the hypothesis engine's own loop, generate, fuse, rank, self-report, is closer to Adam's narrow-but-closed pattern than to a general-purpose research agent, a scope choice worth stating explicitly rather than leaving implicit.
