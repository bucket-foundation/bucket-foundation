# How should the advancement of large language models affect the practice of science?

A direct follow-up debate to Messeri and Crockett (2024): four groups of scientists stake
out contrasting positions on how LLMs should be used in science, with no single position
winning outright.

## Key Claims

- Structured as an opinion piece with four invited groups of scientists debating one
  question, how the advancement of LLMs should affect scientific practice, each group's
  position is presented alongside a response from each of the other groups.
- One group argues working with LLMs is not fundamentally different from working with
  human collaborators; another argues LLMs are often misused and overhyped and that their
  limitations argue for specialized, interpretable tools instead; a third emphasizes
  transparent attribution and responsible use; a fourth argues humans should retain
  responsibility for setting the scientific roadmap.
- No single position is adopted as the piece's own conclusion; the format itself argues
  the field has not converged on how LLM involvement in science should be governed or
  evaluated.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Which of the four positions this piece stakes out is closest to how the hypothesis
  engine itself is used inside Research OS, ordinary collaborator, overhyped and misused,
  requiring transparent attribution, or requiring the human to retain the roadmap, a
  self-classification no current engine document performs.
- Whether the disagreement this piece documents among working scientists has a
  counterpart disagreement among K-12 teachers using a Research OS workspace, a
  classroom-level version of the same unresolved question.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Extends Messeri and Crockett (2024)'s own illusions-of-understanding argument with a
field-level status report: rather than resolving whether LLM involvement helps or harms
scientific practice, this piece's own four-way disagreement among working scientists
argues the question Research OS already treats as open, whether Production's own
AI-scoped tools support or substitute for a learner's understanding, has no settled
answer even among the researchers the field looks to for guidance, corroborating
`PLAN-REVISION-3.md` section 2d's own OPEN status rather than resolving it.
