# Understanding Scientific Understanding

The book-length philosophical treatment of scientific understanding as a subject-relative achievement, a theory is understood by a scientist rather than held true on its own, providing the deeper philosophical grounding the Krenn et al AI-focused paper draws its own criteria from.

## Key Claims

- Scientific understanding is defined through the Criterion for Understanding Phenomena: a scientist understands a phenomenon when they can construct a qualitative explanation of it using an intelligible theory, without doing the full calculation, evidence they have internalized how the theory works, beyond producing correct answers.
- Intelligibility, the quality that lets scientists use a theory to construct such explanations, is not fixed but changes historically as scientific communities develop new conceptual and mathematical tools.
- The book argues against reducing understanding to prediction or explanation alone: a theory can predict correctly without being understood, and a theory can be understood in ways that shift as tools and training change.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an AI system can possess or convey intelligibility in this specific technical sense, or whether intelligibility is definitionally a property of a human scientist's own conceptual toolkit.
- How historical shifts in intelligibility should inform expectations for how an AI-human research system's own standards of understanding might change over time.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the philosophical foundation the Krenn et al AI paper's own criteria for understanding build on, and by extension the standard against which a Research OS Internalization-stage claim, can use the idea on a problem never seen before, should be read: the criterion of constructing a qualitative explanation without the full calculation is close in spirit to the Internalization stage's own transfer-item design, suggesting the stage targets understanding in this sense rather than mere correct-answer prediction.
