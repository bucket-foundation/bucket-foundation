# Assessment of Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences: A Meeting Report

Documents the specific missing-instrument problem the hypothesis engine's own docs cite by name: no validated, shared instrument exists for assessing whether a student production meets a research-grade evidence standard, the precursor question the hypothesis engine's own research-question map needs answered before it can run its production-holdout campaign.

## Key Claims

- A national meeting of researchers and practitioners in course-based undergraduate research found no widely shared, validated instrument for assessing the outcomes of course-based research experiences, despite years of programs claiming research-skill gains.
- Existing assessments were largely program-specific, self-report-based, or borrowed from unrelated contexts, limiting comparison across programs and making claimed gains difficult to verify independently.
- The report calls for coordinated development of shared, validated assessment instruments as a prerequisite for the field to make credible, comparable claims about what course-based research experiences produce.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- What a validated instrument for assessing a K-12 student production's own research-grade quality would need to measure, given this report's own finding that no such instrument yet exists even at the undergraduate level.
- Whether an AI pre-check, originality, canon-conflict, citation validity, can substitute for part of a validated human instrument, or whether it addresses a narrower slice of what research-grade means.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Is cited by name in the hypothesis engine's own docs/RESEARCH-OS-INTEGRATION.md as the source of the missing-instrument finding behind the question of whether a reviewed production's detectability and tier move the engine's rankings in a calibrated direction: that campaign is blocked on exactly the validated-instrument gap this report documents, applied one level down from undergraduate research to a K-12 production.
