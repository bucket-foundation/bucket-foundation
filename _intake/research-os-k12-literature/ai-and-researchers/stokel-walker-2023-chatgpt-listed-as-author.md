# ChatGPT listed as author on research papers: many scientists disapprove

Documents the exact provenance-and-attribution controversy any AI-assisted research or production pipeline has to resolve before an AI-touched claim can be credited or paid, directly relevant to how a Research OS production records AI involvement without crediting the AI as an author.

## Key Claims

- Several published papers listed a generative AI tool as a co-author, prompting objections from publishers and researchers on the grounds that an AI tool cannot take responsibility for a paper's accuracy or give informed consent to authorship, both requirements of standard authorship criteria.
- Major publishers and journals moved quickly to issue policies barring AI tools from author credit, while generally permitting disclosed use of AI tools as an assistive technology, provided that use is stated in the paper's own methods or acknowledgments.
- The controversy surfaced an unresolved definitional problem: existing authorship criteria, accountability, contribution, consent, were written for human collaborators and do not map cleanly onto a tool that meaningfully shaped a paper's content but cannot be held accountable for it.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether an AI tool's contribution can be credited in provenance metadata without extending it authorship standing, and whether that distinction holds up as AI contribution deepens.
- How disclosure norms for AI-assisted work should scale down to a K-12 student's own production, where the same accountability question applies but the author is a minor.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Is the direct precedent for why Research OS's own production schema keeps author_role as one of student, teacher, researcher, agent, a real but explicit category distinct from crediting an AI tool as a co-author of a student's own claim: the schema's own design, and the citation payment rail routing fees to a human author or their custodial ledger, never to a model, is a structural answer to the accountability gap this controversy names, crediting the tool's involvement in provenance metadata without extending it authorship or payment standing.
