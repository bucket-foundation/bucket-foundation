# On scientific understanding with artificial intelligence

Directly names the distinction between an AI system that predicts well and one that produces human scientific understanding, the exact gap between the hypothesis engine's calibrated ranking output and an explanation a researcher could learn from.

## Key Claims

- The paper proposes concrete criteria for when an AI system contributes to human scientific understanding, rather than only producing an accurate prediction: the AI's output must be interpretable, and a human must be able to internalize and independently apply the pattern the AI found.
- It surveys four categories of AI-for-understanding approaches, interpretable models, explaining black-box models, AI-guided experiments that reveal structure, and AI-generated concepts a human then verifies, noting each has a different relationship to human comprehension.
- The authors argue that prediction accuracy alone, no matter how high, does not constitute scientific understanding by the definition the paper adopts, a human's ability to use the finding to explain, predict, and act on new cases.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- How to operationalize a human can internalize the pattern as a measurable criterion rather than a philosophical standard, for a system whose outputs feed both AI ranking and human learning.
- Whether an AI system optimized purely for calibrated prediction, the hypothesis engine's own tournament ranking, can be retrofitted for understanding, or whether understanding requires a different design from the start.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Poses the sharpest test for the hypothesis engine's own self-report: hte.roles.self_report produces a calibration summary and a missing-mass estimate a machine can evaluate, but this paper's own criteria ask whether a human researcher, or a K-12 learner routed to a hypothesis engine's gap node, can internalize why a ranked hypothesis holds, beyond the fact that it is calibrated, a gap between the engine's shipped self-report and this paper's own bar for understanding.
