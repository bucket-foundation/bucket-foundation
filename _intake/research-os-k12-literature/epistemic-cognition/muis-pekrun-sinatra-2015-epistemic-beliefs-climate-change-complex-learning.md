# The curious case of climate change: Testing a theoretical model of epistemic beliefs, epistemic emotions, and complex learning

Tests a causal chain from epistemic belief through epistemic emotion to complex-learning
outcome in an authentic climate-change instructional unit.

## Key Claims

- Develops and tests a theoretical model linking students' epistemic beliefs about the
  certainty and simplicity of scientific knowledge to epistemic emotions such as
  surprise, curiosity, and confusion, and links those emotions in turn to complex learning
  outcomes in a climate-change instructional unit.
- Epistemic beliefs are found to predict epistemic emotions, which in turn predict
  outcomes on a complex-learning task requiring students to reconcile conflicting texts
  about climate change, evidence emotion is a mechanism through which epistemic belief
  affects learning rather than only a correlate of it.
- The climate-change domain is chosen deliberately as a case where scientific knowledge
  is presented to students as contested and evolving, a domain structurally similar to
  the kind of unresolved, evidence-weighing task a Research OS gap node represents.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the same epistemic-belief-to-emotion-to-learning pathway holds for a K-12
  learner working a routed Research OS node instead of a climate-change instructional
  unit, a domain this study was not built to test.
- Whether Check's own verdict, delivered without a designed emotional component, could
  be redesigned to elicit productive epistemic emotions such as curiosity or confusion
  rather than only a pass-or-fail signal, a lever this study's own model suggests but does
  not test as an intervention.

## Verification note

Selected as the closest verified 2015 paper by Muis matching the task brief's own
"classroom interventions that move epistemic beliefs" target. A search for a paper by
that exact description did not resolve to a distinct 2015 Muis record; this paper, testing
how epistemic beliefs relate to learning outcomes in an authentic classroom-relevant
complex-learning unit, is the closest verified match on the same theme, epistemic beliefs
as consequential rather than inert in a K-12-relevant instructional context. It is not an
intervention study that manipulates beliefs experimentally; it is a model-testing study of
how beliefs, emotions, and outcomes relate in situ.
