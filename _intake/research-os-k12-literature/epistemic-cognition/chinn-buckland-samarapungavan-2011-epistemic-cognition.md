# Expanding the Dimensions of Epistemic Cognition

Proposes a five-component, philosophically grounded framework for epistemic cognition,
arguing psychological models should integrate philosophical work on epistemology they have so
far left out.

## Key Claims

- Contemporary philosophy investigates many epistemological topics highly relevant to the
  psychological epistemic-cognition research program, but these topics have not yet featured
  in it.
- A five-component framework is proposed: epistemic aims and value; the structure of
  knowledge; the sources and justification of knowledge; epistemic virtues and vices; and
  reliable versus unreliable processes for achieving epistemic aims.
- The paper argues for a fine-grained, context-specific analysis within each component,
  rather than a single global epistemic-cognition score or stage.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether all five components apply at a K-12 grade band, or whether some, epistemic virtues
  and vices in particular, presuppose reflective maturity a grades 3-5 population has not yet
  reached.
- Which components a Production's citation-and-evidence structure already engages by
  construction, and which it leaves untouched.
