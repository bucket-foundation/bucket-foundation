# The Development of Epistemological Theories: Beliefs About Knowledge and Knowing and Their Relation to Learning

The field-founding review naming the fragmentation across independent research programs
studying beliefs about knowledge under the shared label epistemological belief.

## Key Claims

- Reviews multiple independent research programs investigating students' thinking and
  beliefs about the nature of knowledge and knowing, including how knowledge is defined,
  constructed, and evaluated, finding the programs use differing definitions, conceptual
  frameworks, and methodologies.
- Identifies nine theoretical and methodological issues the field needs to resolve
  before consensus on what epistemological theories are and how they relate to
  cognition, motivation, and learning becomes possible.
- Frames epistemological belief as plausibly domain-specific and developmentally
  structured rather than a single unidimensional trait, a framing later instruments
  (Schommer 1990; Kuhn, Cheney, and Weinstock 2000; Chinn, Buckland, and Samarapungavan
  2011) each operationalize differently.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether this corpus's own citation-and-evidence requirement, the closest thing
  Research OS has to an epistemic-cognition intervention, targets a construct any of the
  reviewed research programs would recognize as their own, or a hybrid this review's own
  nine open issues do not anticipate.
- Whether the field has since converged on any of the nine issues this review names, a
  question this 1997 review cannot answer and this batch's own later instruments
  (Barzilai and Zohar 2014; Greene, Azevedo, and Torney-Purta 2008) partially address by
  proposing their own syntheses.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Is the field-level caution `PLAN-REVISION-3.md` section 2d's own open status already
implies without naming: Sandoval (2005)'s formal-versus-practical epistemology gap and
Kuhn (1999)'s three-form meta-knowing model, both already in this corpus, are two of the
several independent research programs this review finds do not share a common
definition, so any future Production rubric that claims to build "epistemic
understanding" needs to name which of these traditions, not epistemic cognition as an
undifferentiated whole, it targets.
