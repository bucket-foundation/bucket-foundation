# Views of nature of science questionnaire

Builds and defends the Views of Nature of Science Questionnaire (VNOS), an open-ended
instrument paired with individual interviews, arguing forced-choice assessment cannot capture
the range of nature-of-science understanding meaningfully.

## Key Claims

- Helping K-12 students develop informed nature-of-science views has been a central science-
  education goal since the early 1960s, but assessing those views meaningfully remains
  unresolved.
- The VNOS is an open-ended instrument, used with individual interviews, developed to assess
  learners' NOS views where forced-choice instruments fall short.
- Calls to return to standardized, forced-choice NOS instruments for mass administration are
  argued to ignore three decades of research on NOS teaching and learning.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether a version of the VNOS could be adapted into a scalable Research OS transfer item,
  given its design is explicitly built against the mass-administration format such an item
  would favor.
- Whether any NOS-adjacent aspect the VNOS assesses is a construct Research OS should track
  at all, or sits outside the workspace's outcome scope.

## How It Bears on Research OS

Supplies the concrete instrument Sadler and colleagues (2010) and Sandoval (2005) both name a
need for without providing: a validated way to assess nature-of-science understanding
directly rather than infer it from Check's pass rate. The argument against forced-choice
instruments directly challenges any plan to fold a NOS-style measure into Check's automated,
closed-set grading, and argues such a measure would need a teacher-mediated, interview-
adjacent step the current pipeline does not carry.
