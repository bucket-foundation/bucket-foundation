# The development of epistemological understanding

Proposes and tests a four-level developmental trajectory of epistemological
understanding, realist, absolutist, multiplist, evaluativist, across age groups and
knowledge domains.

## Key Claims

- Proposes four qualitatively distinct levels of epistemological understanding a person
  can hold: realist (knowledge as a direct copy of reality), absolutist (knowledge as
  certain facts an authority possesses), multiplist (knowledge as freely chosen,
  unevaluable opinion), and evaluativist (knowledge as a judged, evidence-based claim
  among legitimate alternatives).
- Tests this progression across age groups spanning childhood through adulthood and
  across knowledge domains that differ in how much a claim's justification depends on
  external evidence versus personal judgment, for example factual claims versus
  aesthetic or values-based claims.
- Understanding of the subjective and objective dimensions of a knowledge claim is found
  to develop with age, and the specific level attained differs by domain, evidence
  against treating epistemological understanding as a single trait that develops
  uniformly across all types of knowledge claims.

## Research Questions It Leaves Open

- Whether the evaluativist level, the paper's own most developed position, is the
  implicit target of Check's own support-or-contradiction verdict, or whether Check's
  binary format is closer to the absolutist level's own certain-fact framing than to
  evaluativist judgment among legitimate alternatives.
- Whether the domain-specific finding, that the level of understanding a person shows
  differs by knowledge domain, means a K-12 learner's own routing state should be tracked
  per subject area rather than as a single cross-domain epistemic-cognition score.
