**Is standards alignment (NGSS/Common Core tagging) required for K-12 adoption, or a nice-to-have for a free direct-to-learner product?**

For institutional sale (school/district procurement), standards alignment is close to a hard requirement in practice, not law: district and state curriculum-adoption processes lean on standards-alignment review as their primary quality gate (EdReports.org exists specifically to evaluate K-12 instructional materials against standards and to guide "adoption steps" that states and districts use, per edreports.org, fetched live), and the existence of a whole commercial category (Academic Benchmarks/Instructure, EdGate/ExACT, 1EdTech CASE-certified vendors) selling nothing but standards-correlation data is itself strong market evidence that no district-facing sales motion gets far without it. For a free, direct-to-learner product with no school/district sales motion, standards alignment is a nice-to-have, not a blocker: nothing in NGSS's or Common Core's own usage terms requires third-party products to tag content for individual learners to use it, and Khan Academy, CK-12, and similar free direct-to-learner tools built large user bases before or without deep standards-correlation infrastructure. Bucket's stated K-12 product (frontier-backward routing over five learner states) can launch and prove the pedagogy without standards tagging; alignment becomes worth doing only when/if a district-facing or grant-funded distribution channel is pursued, at which point CASE (free, self-implementable) is the lower-cost path in and Academic Benchmarks/EdGate are the pay-to-skip-the-work path.
