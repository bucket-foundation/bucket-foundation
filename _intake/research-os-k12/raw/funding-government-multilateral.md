## Implications for Bucket

**Evidence requirements are the binding constraint, not mission fit.** Every US public funder reviewed (ED EIR, IES, NSF's AI Institutes) sits on top of the ESSA evidence-tier ladder (strong, moderate, promising, demonstrates-a-rationale) or an equivalent efficacy standard. Bucket, as described, has no completed evaluation of the five-state learner model (Access, Awareness, Understanding, Internalization, Production) and no published crosswalk from that taxonomy to a standard measurement Vocabulary funders already use (learning gains, time-to-mastery, standardized-test proxies, Bloom's/DOK levels). Until that crosswalk and a pilot dataset exist, Bucket qualifies at best for ESSA Tier 4 ("demonstrates a rationale"), which routes it toward early-phase EIR grants specifically, and that tier was not part of the current EIR cycle found in this research.

**IRB and data security are prerequisites most funders check before award, not afterthoughts.** Any research on minors' learning behavior, which frontier-backward routing and the five learner-states inherently require to validate, triggers IRB review, and Bucket is not itself an IRB-of-record. A university partner or a commercial IRB contract solves this; going without one blocks any federally funded study of the platform, not just the grant. FERPA and state student-data-privacy statutes apply to any K-12 platform collecting interaction data regardless of funding source, and IES/ED award processes include pre-award risk assessments that check for exactly this. None of this was addressed in Bucket's design brief as summarized, so treat it as unbuilt infrastructure, not a solved problem.

**Standards alignment differs by funder and none of them speak Bucket's native vocabulary yet.** NSF wants alignment to its learning-sciences research base and "broader impacts" language. IES/ED want alignment to state academic standards and SEER's Open Science and Intervention Research domains. DPGA wants alignment to its 9 indicators, the one place Bucket's current design (MIT license, CC0-intent spec) already lines up without extra work. Bucket's tier system across five learner states and seven canon branches has no published mapping to any of these frameworks. Building that mapping is a real, unbudgeted task, not a formality.

**The single largest design-versus-funder clash found is the payment mechanism.** Citation fees settled over x402 on Base, in USDC, to student authors, some of whom are minors without bank accounts, do not appear anywhere in the NSF, IES, ED, Horizon Europe, Erasmus+, UNESCO, UNICEF, or World Bank documentation reviewed. Federal grant cost-allowability rules (2 CFR 200) and standard nonprofit fiscal-sponsor accounting were not built with wallet-based settlement to minors in mind. This needs dedicated legal and fiscal research beyond this pass before any grant application references the citation-payment mechanism as a funded activity; it is flagged here as unverified terrain, not resolved.

**One clear design-funder fit exists and it is worth stating plainly.** Restricting AI to find, quote, check, and organize (no generation) matches UNESCO's published "human-centred approach to AI" framing and aligns with SEER's Open Science domain's emphasis on transparency and avoiding automated, opaque scoring. This is a genuine point of alignment, backed by the UNESCO and SEER pages reviewed, not an inference stretched to flatter the product.

**Realistic timelines run long across every US federal path.** IES's own FY2027 cycle shows roughly a one-year gap between letter-of-intent (Sept. 2026) and possible grant start (July-Sept. 2027). ED EIR runs a roughly seven-month cycle from notice to deadline within a fiscal year, but is gated on an evidence tier Bucket does not yet have. NSF AI Institute-scale awards, when a solicitation exists, run 12-18 months solicitation-to-award, and no new education-cohort solicitation is confirmed open; the existing four institutes are funded through 2028-2031. EU calls add an extra 6-plus months before submission just to identify and formalize an EU coordinating partner, since Bucket cannot lead a Horizon Europe or Erasmus+ consortium itself.

**The fastest route is DPG registration paired with a university-PI partnership, not a choice between them.** DPG registration is free, roughly 30 days, and immediately unlocks the credibility signal several downstream multilateral funders reference. It does not, on its own, solve the IRB, FERPA-compliance, or prior-track-record problems that federal reviewers weight heavily. A university partner solves those three problems simultaneously (IRB-of-record, existing eRA/SAM.gov registration, an evaluation track record) and is the standard on-ramp edtech nonprofits have used since ESSA's 2015 evidence tiers took effect. Neither path alone gets Bucket to a fundable NSF- or IES-scale application; both together do, on a realistic 12-24 month horizon.

**The 2025 federal contraction changes the base rate for everyone in this space, independent of anything Bucket does.** IES lost roughly $881 million across 169 contracts in February 2025 DOGE actions, the Department of Education's workforce was cut roughly in half, and the Office of Educational Technology was closed outright in March 2025. The historically strongest US line for this exact kind of work, IES's 84.305A Education Research Grants, is absent from the current active-competition list. Near-term US federal dollars for AI-in-education research have concentrated in the four already-funded NSF AI Institutes rather than flowed to new entrants. Any planning built on IES or ED EIR money materializing on a normal pre-2025 timeline should be treated as optimistic given the evidence gathered here.

**Research gaps to close before this brief is presentation-ready:** Estonia's AI Leap program details (funding amount, named tech partners, scope) could not be confirmed through official channels reached this session. Brazil's national AI-in-education strategy returned no usable data (site blocked by CAPTCHA). Sunbird/DIKSHA's Digital Public Goods registration status is commonly reported but unverified here. Horizon Europe's and Erasmus+'s specific call topics, budgets, and named 2024-2026 AI-in-education awards were not located within this pass's reach into the EU Funding & Tenders Portal. NSF's "NLI" program (successor to the archived DRK-12 line) needs its full solicitation text pulled before Bucket relies on it as a funding path.
