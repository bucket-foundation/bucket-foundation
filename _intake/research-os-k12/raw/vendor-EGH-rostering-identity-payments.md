## Paying minors: legal mechanics

A US nonprofit can legally pay a minor for content contributions; no federal law bars it. A minor generally lacks the legal capacity to sign a binding contract, though, and can disaffirm one during minority in most states, so the standard practice across the creator economy is to route payment through a parent or guardian rather than pay the minor directly. YouTube's own Partner Program requires a creator to be 18, or to have a legal guardian at least 18 manage the AdSense payment account on the creator's behalf, a workable template for Bucket's citation-payment flow (support.google.com/youtube/answer/72857). On tax treatment, a nonprofit is not the minor's parent, so the FICA/FUTA exemption the IRS grants a parent employing their own child under 18 in a sole proprietorship does not carry over; a nonprofit paying $600 or more a year to an independent contributor, minor or adult, follows standard 1099-NEC rules (irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/family-help). The cleanest custodial mechanism is a UTMA or UGMA account: nearly every state allows a third party to deposit money into an account titled in the minor's name with a parent or guardian as custodian, and the funds remain the minor's legal property even though the custodian controls them until the state's age of majority. A growing and unsettled area is state-level child-influencer law: California's Coogan Act, expanded in September 2024 to cover child social media content creators, requires 15 percent of a young performer's or creator's earnings be set aside in a blocked trust account (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coogan_Law). Illinois is reported as having passed the first dedicated child-influencer earnings statute, with Minnesota and Utah enacting comparable laws since, though the exact statute text and effective dates for those three were not independently reverified against primary legislative sources this session and should be confirmed before Bucket finalizes a payout design, since K-12 contributors will span all 50 states. Given that landscape, the lowest-risk design for Bucket is a YouTube-style guardian-as-payee model for any contributor under 18, paired with UTMA custodial-account support for parents who want the funds legally locked to the child, and a compliance review of the states with Coogan-style trust mandates before treating a minor's citation earnings as fully liquid at the point of payment.
