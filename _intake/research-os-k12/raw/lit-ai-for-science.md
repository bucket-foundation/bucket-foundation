## 1. Overlap Design: Student Productions as Evidence and Hypothesis Records

**The core move.** A student production in Research OS is a claim (a sentence asserting something), backed by one or more quoted, cited sources, produced while the student was in one of the five learner states. That triple, claim plus citation plus learner-state, already contains everything an evidence item needs except the extracted slots, and everything a hypothesis record needs except a placement.

**Minimum viable schema.** An evidence item derived from a student production needs:

- **Claim text**: the student's sentence, verbatim, not paraphrased by any downstream system.
- **Slots** (actor, action, object, place, mechanism, interval, stance): extracted the same way the engine extracts them from any other source, by the extraction ensemble, not hand-coded by the student. Marshall and Wallace's finding that no deployed extraction tool has been validated to replace human review at acceptable recall, and the Elicit feasibility study's finding that extraction quality degrades on interpretive fields, both argue the extraction step should run identically on student and non-student sources and be scored for confidence identically, no separate, laxer path for K-12 input.
- **Citation graph**: one or more (source, quoted span, resolved location in source) tuples. SciFact's rationale-sentence format and MultiVerS's full-document-context requirement both argue against trusting an isolated quoted line, the citation record needs the surrounding context, not just the matched span.
- **Learner state at time of production**: recorded as a field on the record, not discarded after grading. This is the one field with no analogue in the engine's existing evidence items, and it is the field that makes the record auditable as a student production rather than an anonymous evidence item.
- **Provenance chain**: student ID, timestamp, tool-assist log (what the AI was permitted to do: find, quote, check, organize, and which of those four it actually did). This is a nanopublication-style provenance block, Groth, Gibson, and Velterop's three-part structure (assertion, provenance, publication info) maps onto this directly, and nanopublications already solved the "who asserted this and on what basis" problem the engine needs solved here.
- **Belief inputs, not a belief score.** The student production should not arrive at the engine already carrying a subjective-logic opinion tuple. It should carry the raw signals the fusion step needs (extraction-ensemble confidence, citation-verification result, reviewer or teacher confirmation if any) and let the engine's existing fusion operators compute belief the same way they do for any other evidence item. Treating a student-contributed evidence item as a first-class input to the same fusion machinery, rather than a separate, pre-scored category, is what makes the citation-payment mechanism defensible: the payment is for evidence that earned belief mass through the same process any evidence earns it.

**Hypothesis records from student work.** A frontier node the engine surfaces as "routable backward" is, structurally, an underdetermined placement, a claim with insufficient evidence to fix its address in the Gödel space. A student routing backward from that node produces exactly the ABC-style connective evidence Swanson's model describes: they are searching for the B-term that would tie the frontier claim to something already placed. That means a student hypothesis record needs one more field an ordinary evidence item does not: a pointer to the frontier node it is attempting to close, so the engine can track which frontier nodes are attracting student traffic and which are not, itself a signal about which open questions are tractable for a non-expert to make progress on.

**What the literature says about whether this can meet research-grade standards.** The evidence is genuinely mixed, not uniformly reassuring.

On the positive side: Bonney et al. document citizen science projects moving past simple data collection into publication-grade contributions once task design is structured (classification, counting, well-bounded judgment calls). Cooper et al.'s Foldit result shows non-experts, through a well-designed interface, outperforming algorithmic solvers on a subset of hard problems, expertise was not the limiting factor, interface design was. Lintott et al.'s Galaxy Zoo shows that reliability-weighted aggregation of tens of thousands of volunteer classifications converges to a catalogue professional astronomers cite, which is the closest existing precedent for the engine's own belief-fusion approach applied to human contributors rather than LLM contributors.

On the cautionary side: Auchincloss et al., reporting on the CURE literature specifically (course-based research experiences, the closest existing analogue to Research OS), states plainly that most published CURE evaluations measure student learning outcomes, self-efficacy, retention, persistence, and that assessment of the scientific validity of the research products themselves is comparatively underdeveloped across the field. That is not evidence that student research products cannot meet research-grade standards. It is evidence that the field studying student research has not yet built the instrument to check. Research OS would be building novel ground here, not applying an established assessment, and should say so rather than assume CURE evidence already answers the question.

The pattern across both citizen science and CUREs: quality holds up on structured, well-bounded tasks (classify this, count this, extract this slot) and is untested or weaker on open-ended judgment tasks (assess this claim's validity, judge this hypothesis's novelty). Slot extraction from a quoted source is a structured task. Judging whether a student's claim deserves to become a frontier-closing hypothesis is an open-ended judgment task. The schema above routes the structured part (extraction) through the same automated pipeline as any evidence item, and routes the open-ended part (does this close the frontier node, does it deserve citation payment) through the engine's existing judge and meta-review roles rather than through the extraction ensemble or the student's own self-report. That split is where the literature's confidence actually sits.

## 2. Fifteen to Twenty Open Research Questions in AI-for-Science, Ranked

Ranked by how directly the question bears on the engine's own design choices, most load-bearing first.

1. **(Messeri and Crockett, 2024)** When an AI system fills the oracle, surrogate, quant, or arbiter role in a research pipeline, does its use expand the range of questions a field asks, or quietly narrow it while looking like expansion, and how would the field detect that narrowing from inside its own pipeline? *A K-12 Research OS could contribute data here directly: tracking which frontier nodes attract student routing over years is a longitudinal record of question-diversity the engine itself cannot generate alone, since its own generator role is one of the things under suspicion.*

2. **(Si, Yang, and Hashimoto, 2024)** Does an LLM-generated hypothesis judged more novel than a human expert's, by expert reviewers, actually produce a better outcome once someone executes the research, or does judged novelty and executed-outcome quality decouple? *A Research OS where students physically execute (find, quote, check) a subset of frontier hypotheses is one of the only affordable ways to generate real executed-outcome data at any volume, since the authors' own paper stopped short of execution for cost reasons.*

3. **(Krenn et al., 2022)** What would count as evidence that an AI system achieved scientific understanding, compression, parsimony, generalization to unseen cases, rather than merely producing correct predictions? *A K-12 system could contribute here only indirectly, by generating a large, labeled corpus of "explained versus merely predicted" claims through the check learner state, useful training signal for whatever eventually tries to measure this at scale.*

4. **(Kitano, 2021)** Can an autonomous AI system perform discovery indistinguishable in quality from the best human scientists, and would such a system's discoveries even resemble the form of human scientific reasoning, or constitute a genuinely different kind of science? *No meaningful K-12 contribution; this is a frontier-AI capability question the engine's own design does not bear on either way.*

5. **(Gottweis et al., 2025)** Does a tournament-based, self-play ranking of AI-generated hypotheses reliably identify which ones are worth real-world validation, beyond the handful of cases any one paper can afford to validate? *This is the question the engine's own ranking tournament exists to answer, and it is currently unanswered by anyone, including the engine; a K-12 population routing backward from frontier nodes at volume is a way to generate many more low-stakes validation attempts than any lab-scale co-scientist project can afford.*

6. **(Lu et al., 2024)** Can an automated reviewer substitute for genuine peer review of AI-generated research output, given the authors' own finding that their automated reviewer is an imperfect proxy? *A K-12 system with human teachers checking student-and-AI-collaborative productions is a source of paired (automated judgment, human judgment) data that would let anyone calibrate an automated-reviewer proxy against ground truth at a scale no single lab produces.*

7. **(Boiko et al., 2023)** How should autonomy be safety-bounded once an LLM agent controls physical lab hardware or real-world actuators, given the authors' own report that an unconstrained agent could attempt hazardous synthesis routes? *No K-12 contribution; this is a physical-safety-engineering question orthogonal to a text-and-citation platform.*

8. **(Romera-Paredes et al., and Novikov et al., FunSearch and AlphaEvolve)** Program-search discovery depends on an automatic, cheap, correct verifier existing for the target claim (a proof, a score function); what happens to this entire class of method in domains, most of history, most of biophysics, most of qualitative science, where no such verifier exists? *This is the single clearest place a K-12 Research OS could contribute conceptual, not computational, progress: every student check-state interaction where a human manually verifies a claim no automatic scorer could verify is a hand-labeled example of the verification the field does not know how to automate.*

9. **(Nosek et al., 2015, and the Open Science Collaboration, 2015)** Human science's reproducibility failures were structural, incentive-driven (publication bias, underpowered studies, no pre-registration); does inserting an AI hypothesis-generation step correct those incentive problems or compound them by adding a new, faster source of unreplicated claims? *A K-12 Research OS that requires pre-registration of a student's hypothesis before the check step, rather than after, is a small working test of whether the fix (registered-report structure) generalizes to a non-professional, high-volume setting.*

10. **(Bender and Koller, 2020)** Can a system trained only on textual form ever be credited with grasping meaning or communicative intent, and does that distinction matter for whether an LLM-authored hypothesis is a genuine claim about the world versus a fluent, unmoored string? *No direct K-12 data contribution; this is a question about model architecture and training, not about evidence at scale.*

11. **(Birhane, Kasirzadeh, Leslie, and Wachter, 2023)** Who is accountable when an AI-touched scientific claim turns out to be wrong or fabricated, given diffuse authorship across model developers, prompt authors, and reviewers? *A K-12 platform that logs exactly which of find/quote/check/organize the AI performed on each student production is a working answer to a version of this question at small scale, an audit trail precedent worth generalizing upward.*

12. **(Wang et al., 2023, Nature survey)** The shared bottleneck for AI-driven discovery across physics, chemistry, biology, and materials science is scarce, sparse, or biased data, not model capacity; how do discovery systems get built for that data regime instead of assuming internet-scale labeled data will exist? *A structured, high-volume stream of student-contributed, slot-extracted, provenance-tagged evidence is a direct, if modest, answer to the data-scarcity half of this question for whatever domains Bucket's canon covers.*

13. **(Marshall and Wallace, 2019, and the broader systematic-review-automation field)** Every deployed ML-assisted review tool still requires a human-in-the-loop step because none has been validated to fully replace dual human screening at acceptable recall; what is the actual minimum human oversight that preserves validity, and does it scale? *A K-12 Research OS with teacher-in-the-loop review of AI-assisted student extraction is itself an experiment in exactly this minimum-oversight question, run at a scale most systematic-review labs cannot afford.*

14. **(Jøsang's subjective logic literature, read against Mellers, Tetlock, et al.'s forecasting-tournament finding)** Forecasting accuracy gains came mainly from process, training, update cadence, team structure, not from the specific mathematical combination rule; does the same hold for hypothesis belief fusion, meaning the engine's fusion formula matters less than how often and how it is re-run? *A K-12 population re-checking and re-routing frontier hypotheses on a different cadence than the engine's LLM roles do is a natural experiment on exactly this question, if the platform logs update frequency against calibration outcome.*

15. **(Kuhn et al., and Groth, Gibson, and Velterop, on nanopublications)** A technically complete, provenance-tracked, machine-readable claim format has existed since 2010 to 2018 and never became how scientists publish by default outside a few pilot projects; what would make a machine-readable claim format the thing researchers actually produce, rather than a shadow layer alongside conventional papers? *This is a direct design question for Bucket itself: whether evidence items and hypothesis records become something researchers produce as a primary output, or stay a shadow layer behind conventional writing, is unresolved by the nanopublication precedent, and a K-12 population that produces evidence items as their primary output from the start, never having learned the conventional-paper habit first, is a genuinely new data point on this question.*

16. **(Cooper et al., Foldit)** What is the equivalent well-designed "game" interface for a knowledge-graph evidence-placement task, one that would let non-expert contributors match or beat algorithmic extraction the way Foldit players matched algorithmic protein folders? *This is close to Research OS's own design problem; the five learner states are a candidate answer, and their success or failure against algorithmic slot extraction is directly measurable by the platform.*

17. **(Chambers, 2013, on Registered Reports)** Pre-registering a hypothesis and method before the outcome is known measurably reduces publication bias in human research; does pre-registering a generated hypothesis's placement and scoring criteria before a judge model sees the outcome reduce the analogous bias, where a generator or judge sharing a base model could implicitly favor hypotheses it expects to score well? *A K-12 platform is not positioned to answer this for the engine's own LLM roles, but a policy of pre-registering student hypotheses' evaluation criteria before the check step runs is a small-scale test of the same principle.*

18. **(Bronk Ramsey, and Allen, on interval reasoning)** Does a Gödel-style address space for sequence hypotheses actually preserve all thirteen of Allen's interval relations, or does it implicitly force claims into a total order the underlying evidence only supports as a partial one? *No K-12 data contribution; this is an internal formal-verification question for the engine's own address scheme.*

19. **(Auchincloss et al., and the CURE-assessment literature generally)** How should the scientific validity of a student-produced research artifact be assessed, separately from whether the student learned something, given that the field's own meeting report calls this instrument underdeveloped? *This is the question Research OS is best positioned to answer of anything on this list, since building the citation-payment mechanism at all requires building exactly this instrument, an artifact-validity check independent of a learning check.*

20. **(Priem, Piwowar, and Orr, and Kinney et al., on open bibliographic infrastructure)** Open citation graphs (OpenAlex, S2AG) now cover most of the published literature; what remains unmapped is the graph of claims and their evidentiary support inside papers, not just which paper cites which, does an open, general-purpose claim graph analogous to OpenAlex become feasible, or does it stay fragmented across per-project systems like the engine's own? *A K-12 platform producing standardized, provenance-tagged evidence items at volume is one of the few plausible sources of the training and seed data such an open claim-graph project would need, if it does not want to bootstrap from nothing.*

## 3. What the Hypothesis Engine Should Learn from the Literature

- **Adopt discovery-date holdout as the default validation, not an occasional check.** AlphaFold's CASP precedent and MOLIERE's own evaluation method both point the same direction: the engine's calibration should run as a standing, blind, pre-registered benchmark against future discoveries, not a one-time internal test. Brier scores against discovery-date holdout are already the right metric; the missing piece is running it on a fixed, published cadence the way CASP and Metaculus tournaments do, so the calibration claim itself becomes falsifiable by outsiders.

- **Separate extraction confidence from verification confidence, always.** SciFact and MultiVerS both show that a matched quote is not the same as a verified claim, verification needs full-document context. Agrawal et al. show a generator's own confidence about its citations is not trustworthy. The engine's extraction ensemble and preservation critic should never share a confidence score; a citation that extracted cleanly but has not been context-checked needs a visibly lower belief input than one that has.

- **Borrow nanopublication provenance structure wholesale for evidence items.** Groth, Gibson, and Velterop's three-part format (assertion, provenance, publication info) is a solved problem the engine does not need to redesign. What nanopublications got wrong was adoption, not structure, Kuhn et al.'s report of stalled uptake outside a few pilots is a warning about incentives and workflow friction, not about the schema. The lesson is to make producing a properly provenanced evidence item the path of least resistance for both an LLM role and a student, not an extra compliance step.

- **Treat registered-report-style pre-registration as a bias control, not paperwork.** Chambers's finding that registering hypothesis and method before outcomes are known reduces publication bias applies directly to any pipeline where a generator and a judge might share incentives or a base model. A generated hypothesis's placement and scoring criteria should be locked before the judge role sees how well it scores, the same discipline Registered Reports impose on human researchers.

- **Treat forecasting-tournament process lessons as more important than fusion-formula tuning.** Mellers, Tetlock, et al. found training and update cadence drove accuracy more than the aggregation math. The engine likely gets more calibration improvement from re-scoring hypotheses more often, and training its critic and judge roles the way superforecasters are trained (explicit calibration feedback, team debate before scoring), than from further refining the subjective-logic fusion operators themselves, which Jøsang's formalism already specifies correctly.

- **Use Yager's Dempster-Shafer warning as an audit, not a rejection of belief fusion.** Combining several weak or conflicting evidence sources can manufacture false confidence under naive combination rules. The engine's fusion step should be stress-tested against adversarial or conflicting evidence sets specifically, not just validated on the easy case of converging evidence, before being trusted on genuinely disputed frontier hypotheses.

- **Take Krenn et al.'s understanding-versus-prediction distinction as a second scoring axis.** A subjective-logic belief score answers "how confident should we be this is true." It does not answer "does this claim compress or explain, as opposed to merely restate, the evidence." Those are different properties and the engine currently only measures the first.

## 4. What to Change Where the Evidence Contradicts the Current Design

- **The judge and meta-review roles concentrate exactly the authority Messeri and Crockett warn about, and Birhane et al. warn no one owns.** If an LLM judge decides which hypotheses rank high enough to enter the canon, and an LLM meta-review role oversees that judge, the accountability chain for a wrong or fabricated claim that makes it into canon has no named human at any point. Every hypothesis that crosses into canon status needs a named human sign-off logged against it, not just an LLM judge score, full stop, this is the single clearest change the literature demands.

- **Sharing a base model across generator, critic, and judge roles is a known failure mode, not a design detail.** Qi et al.'s zero-shot hypothesis proposer result degraded specifically when target facts were excluded from pretraining, meaning a generator and a judge drawn from the same model family share blind spots by construction, a judge cannot independently catch what it was trained the same way not to notice. If the engine's roles share a base model, that needs to change, or the judge role's independence claim needs to be dropped from how the system is described.

- **The ranking tournament has never been validated against ground truth outside a handful of cases, and treating it as production-ready is not supported.** Gottweis et al.'s own co-scientist validated three narrow biomedical cases. Si, Yang, and Hashimoto explicitly flag that judged novelty was never tested against executed outcomes. The engine's tournament output should be labeled as unvalidated ranking, not as a reliable proxy for which hypotheses deserve resources, until a discovery-date-holdout-style track record accumulates specifically for tournament rankings, not just for individual hypothesis claims.

- **The five learner states need an explicit artifact-validity instrument, separate from a learning-outcome instrument, before citation payments attach to student productions.** Auchincloss et al. state this instrument does not yet exist in the CURE literature generally. Building the payment mechanism without first building this check means citation fees could attach to productions whose research validity was never actually assessed, only their evidence of student learning was. This is not a minor gap, it is the load-bearing check the whole "student productions as first-class evidence" plan depends on, and it needs to exist before payment, not alongside it as an afterthought.

- **Extraction-ensemble output feeding directly into belief fusion, without a context-verification step, is not supported by the evidence-synthesis literature.** MultiVerS shows full-document context materially changes verification judgments versus isolated-sentence matching. If the engine's extraction ensemble hands slots straight to the fusion step without a separate context-check pass, that pipeline should be restructured to insert one, particularly before student-contributed evidence items are treated as fusion inputs at scale.

- **Nanopublication-style provenance already exists as a mature format and does not need to be reinvented as a bespoke schema.** If the engine's evidence-item schema diverges structurally from the (assertion, provenance, publication-info) pattern Groth, Gibson, and Velterop specified, that divergence should be justified explicitly or dropped in favor of the existing standard, adopting it also opens a path to interoperate with any other system that speaks nanopublications, which a bespoke schema forecloses.
