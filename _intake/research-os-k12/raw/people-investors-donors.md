## Implications for Bucket

**For-profit subsidiary or pure nonprofit.** The evidence from adjacent precedents points toward a dual structure, not a binary choice.

Khan Academy stayed a pure 501(c)(3) for 15+ years and it worked: it pulled $5M from Musk, $10M+ from Gates, $2M from Google, all as pure philanthropy, with zero equity given up. That model fits an organization whose product IS the free good and whose only revenue need is operating cost. Bucket's free-to-read tier is structurally identical to Khan Academy's whole product.

OpenStax took the same path (Gates, Hewlett, Michelson 20MM, Maxfield funded it from the start) and stayed nonprofit because its product, like Khan's, has no natural for-profit wedge, textbooks don't need equity capital, they need grant capital and Rice University's institutional backing.

Mozilla and OpenAI both split because they had a revenue-generating activity that a pure nonprofit legally cannot or practically should not hold. Mozilla Corporation exists because a nonprofit can't cleanly receive hundreds of millions a year from a Google search deal and remain compliant. OpenAI restructured (2019 capped-profit, October 2025 PBC) because frontier model training needs venture-scale capital that no foundation grant pool can supply, and a nonprofit can't issue equity. In both cases the for-profit is a subsidiary controlled by the nonprofit parent, not the other way around, and 100% of Mozilla Corp profit returns to the mission.

Bucket has a candidate for-profit-shaped activity: **the citation-payment rail itself.** x402 settlement, wallet infrastructure, and any B2B licensing of the graph-query API to ed-tech companies or AI labs looks exactly like the thing that needed Mozilla Corporation to exist. A knowledge graph that stays purely nonprofit can still take Gates-style and Overdeck-style grants for content and product work, while a thin for-profit subsidiary (owned and controlled by the nonprofit, no external shareholders unless that changes deliberately) could hold the payment infrastructure, sell API access to Owl Ventures- or Learn Capital-backed portfolio companies, and route 100% of profit back to the foundation, the same non-negotiable clause Mozilla enforces. This is not urgent before 501(c)(3) approval; it becomes relevant only once x402 volume is real enough to need a commercial contract counterparty.

The a16z-crypto/Paradigm/Coinbase Ventures/Variant/1kx tier is close to irrelevant to Bucket's near-term fundraising. All five run pure equity funds with no public-goods or grant program identifiable on their current public pages. They will not write Bucket a check unless Bucket spins up a for-profit entity and offers equity, which contradicts the mission. Their only real point of contact is the Optimism Foundation's RetroPGF/Retro Funding line and the Base Ecosystem Fund's application portal, both of which fund shipped, measurable public value, not pre-launch promise. Treat that whole crypto-VC block as a signal of "where developer and settlement-rail attention is going," useful for hiring and technical partnerships, not as a donor pipeline.

**Which donor networks are realistically open to a pre-501(c)(3) project.** Three kinds of funder will engage before formal tax-exempt status closes, and two kinds will not.

Open now: fiscal-sponsorship-friendly intermediaries and program-driven grantmakers built to seed early work. Renaissance Philanthropy exists specifically to build "thesis-driven funds" for donors who want to back exactly this kind of pre-institutional idea, and it has already moved $533M in two years across exactly this kind of early, unproven initiative; Kumar Garg's own career (Schmidt Futures, Obama-era OSTP, per training knowledge, unverified from a fetched source in this research) is built around backing scientific and educational public goods before they have conventional institutional cover. NewSchools Venture Fund runs an open, numbered application (1,600 applicants, 80+ funded in 2025) that explicitly reaches ventures, not only registered nonprofits. Emerson Collective explicitly states it operates both a nonprofit-partnership arm and a venture arm from one house, meaning a single relationship there could flex to either structure as Bucket's legal status evolves.

Closed or slow until formalized: the large family foundations with formal cycles. Gates Foundation, Overdeck ("not currently accepting unsolicited grant proposals," stated on their own site), and Bezos Family Foundation all run relationship-driven, program-officer-gated processes that essentially require an existing nonprofit wrapper, audited financials, and often a warm introduction before a first conversation. Chasing these pre-501(c)(3) wastes founder time; they become live only after formal filing and, realistically, after Bucket has a first cohort of grants or a fiscal sponsor's track record to point to.

**Five to contact first, with a specific ask.**

1. **Renaissance Philanthropy (Kumar Garg)**, ask: a seed grant or matchmaking introduction to fund the first year of canon-building and the citation-payment pilot, framed as exactly the kind of "field-advancing idea" their fund-building model exists to catalyze. Channel: renaissancephilanthropy.org, "Partner With Us."
2. **NewSchools Venture Fund (Frances Messano)**, ask: apply through the open 2026 funding portal (newschools.org/apply-for-funding) for an early-stage grant, positioning Bucket as an AI-in-education infrastructure play, one of their four named strategic areas.
3. **Emerson Collective (Laurene Powell Jobs)**, ask: an initial scoping conversation via info@emersoncollective.com about whether Bucket fits their nonprofit-partnership track or their venture track, given the org explicitly runs both and Bucket may eventually need both.
4. **CK-12 Foundation (Neeru Khosla)**, ask: not funding, but partnership. CK-12 already gives away free, standards-aligned K-12 content to 453M+ users; a direct content, distribution, or co-citation relationship is more valuable to Bucket at this stage than a cash ask, and it is the single closest existing nonprofit precedent to what Bucket is building.
5. **Optimism Foundation (Retro Funding / RetroPGF)**, ask: not a donor conversation but a funding-track application once feed402 and the x402 citation rail have shipped and produced measurable usage, RetroPGF rewards demonstrated public value after the fact, so this is a milestone-gated target, not a pitch-deck target: build first, apply after.

Everyone else on the list above is either a pure-equity fund with no nonprofit mechanism (Owl, Learn Capital, GSV, a16z, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, Bessemer, all the crypto VCs), a large foundation with a closed process best approached only after formal 501(c)(3) status and a track record (Gates, Overdeck, Bezos Family Foundation, Ballmer Group, Dell Foundation, Stand Together), or a target whose live web presence could not be verified in this research (GSV Ventures, Rethink Education, Transcend Network, which appears defunct, Griffin Catalyst, Dalio Education) and should not be prioritized until independently confirmed.
