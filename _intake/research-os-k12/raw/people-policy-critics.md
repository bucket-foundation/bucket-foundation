## Regulatory record

**EU AI Act.** Entered into force 1 Aug 2024. Prohibited practices and the AI-literacy duty applied from 2 Feb 2025 (confirmed live). GPAI obligations applied from 2 Aug 2025. Annex III lists as high-risk any system determining access/admission to education, evaluating learning outcomes, steering a student's learning process, assessing appropriate education level, or monitoring prohibited behavior during tests. My understanding from the Act's text is that Annex III high-risk obligations generally applied from 2 Aug 2026, already past as of this report, while the later 2 Aug 2027 date applies only to high-risk systems embedded as safety components in already-regulated products (Annex I), not standalone education systems. A live European Parliament summary fetched in this session described a uniform "36 months" (~Aug 2027) framing without this Annex I/III distinction. Treat the exact date split as unverified pending direct confirmation against the Regulation's Article 113, but do not assume Bucket has years of runway before EU high-risk obligations bite. Bucket's learner-state classification and frontier-backward routing (arguably "steering the student's learning process") are strong candidates for Annex III classification and should be assessed by EU counsel before EU deployment. digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu; europarl.europa.eu.

**FTC COPPA rule amendments (2025).** Confirmed: first COPPA Rule update since 2013. Requires separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties for targeted advertising; caps data retention to what is reasonably necessary (the FTC explicitly rejected "we need it to improve our algorithms" as a retention justification, directly relevant to any AI training on K-12 data); expands "personal information" to include biometric identifiers; tightens Safe Harbor program transparency. Effective 60 days after Federal Register publication, general compliance one year later. Ed-tech-specific clarifications were dropped to avoid conflict with pending Department of Education FERPA updates, so FERPA (not COPPA) remains the primary federal lever for school-procured tools, while COPPA governs direct-to-child, consumer-facing use, which is how Bucket is described ("free to any learner worldwide"). ftc.gov (press release URL 404'd live; content confirmed via secondary IAPP-style summary).

**State student-privacy laws.** SOPIPA (California, 2014): bars operators from selling student data or using it for targeted ads or non-educational profiling. New York Education Law 2-d: requires third-party contractors handling K-12 student data to have a Parents Bill of Rights, a data security/privacy plan, breach notification, and NIST-aligned security controls. Illinois SOPPA: requires a written operator-school board agreement, breach notification, parental transparency rights. All three require per-district or per-state contracting before an ed-tech vendor can legally process K-12 student data at scale in that state; this is the practical bottleneck for US school adoption (unverified live in this session due to fetch failures; content is standard, well-documented state ed-tech-privacy law).

**Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA).** Confirmed live: not enacted. Reintroduced 119th Congress by Sen. Blackburn, May 2025; House Energy and Commerce reached a bipartisan deal in June 2026 to potentially merge it into a broader KIDS Act, still pending. If enacted, would impose a "duty of care," ban addictive design features for under-18 users, require default-private settings and third-party audits. A related bill, COPPA 2.0 (raising COPPA's covered age from under-13 to under-17), reportedly passed the Senate unanimously in March 2026 per a live secondary source (unverified against the primary bill text). If COPPA 2.0 becomes law, it would sweep nearly all of Bucket's K-12 user base under COPPA's parental-consent regime, not just under-13 users.

**Utah and state minor-social-media laws.** Confirmed live: Utah's Social Media Regulation Act (age verification, parental consent, night-time access limits, ban on algorithmic recommendations to minors) is blocked by a federal preliminary injunction (Sept 2024) following a NetChoice First Amendment challenge; appeal was pending at the 10th Circuit with a hearing scheduled Nov 2025 (outcome unverified in this session). Not currently enforceable anywhere. Bucket's no-feed, no-algorithmic-recommendation design likely falls outside most states' "social media platform" definitions, but this depends on each state's statutory language and should be checked per state before assuming exemption.

**UK Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code).** Confirmed live: in force since 2 Sept 2020, ICO-enforced, 15 standards requiring highest-privacy-by-default, data minimization, and no manipulative "nudge" design for any "information society service likely to be accessed by children," with extraterritorial reach similar to GDPR. The Wikipedia summary fetched contains no explicit AI/chatbot language; ICO has issued supplementary generative-AI guidance since 2023 (unverified specific document in this session).

**UK Online Safety Act.** Confirmed live: age-assurance duties for adult content began 17 Jan 2025; children's risk-assessment deadline for services likely accessed by children was 24 July 2025 (passed); Ofcom's categorized-service codes and guidance were due early 2026. Any interactive feature letting students publish or comment into the knowledge graph likely triggers a children's risk-assessment obligation, regardless of the platform's educational framing.

**Child-influencer earnings laws.** Confirmed live: California AB 1880 (signed Sept 2024) extends the Coogan Act's 15%-earnings-trust requirement to minors who are direct-contract content creators (vloggers, streamers, social-media influencers). Illinois and Minnesota enacted comparable content-creator earnings-trust laws in 2024-2025, and Utah passed a similar law in 2024; bill numbers, exact thresholds, and effective dates for Illinois/Minnesota/Utah are unverified in this session (fetches blocked or 404'd; content reflects background knowledge of well-publicized 2024 state legislation). None of these laws, nor any US state law identified in this session, addresses a minor directly receiving a cryptocurrency or stablecoin micropayment on a public wallet. This is a genuine regulatory gap, not a solved compliance question.

**US Department of Education AI guidance and Office of Educational Technology status.** OET published "Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Teaching and Learning" (2023) and "Designing for Education with Artificial Intelligence: An Essential Guide for Developers" (2024), both aimed at setting human-in-the-loop, evidence-based, equity-focused expectations for ed-tech AI vendors (background knowledge, unverified live: tech.ed.gov redirected to www.ed.gov/ai/, which returned a 404 in this session, and the Department of Education underwent substantial 2025 restructuring under the current administration; OET's current staffing and operating status could not be confirmed live in this session and should be treated as unverified).

**State education AI guidance documents.** A majority of US states have issued K-12 AI guidance since 2023 (tracked historically by TeachAI.org and state departments of education); an exact current count could not be confirmed live in this session (multiple fetches 404'd) and should be treated as unverified. Typical content: human oversight requirements, reaffirmation of FERPA/COPPA/state-law compliance, AI-literacy curricula, and a ban on high-stakes decisions made by AI alone.

**FTC AI-in-education enforcement.** No FTC enforcement action specific to a K-12 education AI product was confirmed in this session. The closest analog is the FTC's September 2024 6(b) inquiry into AI chatbot companies' effects on children and teens (background knowledge, unverified live), which is an inquiry, not an enforcement action, and separate private litigation against Character.AI over a minor's death (2024, not FTC-initiated).

**2025-2026 executive orders on AI in education.** Confirmed live: Executive Order 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth" (April 2025), creates a White House AI Education Task Force chaired by OSTP, directs the Department of Education to issue AI-education grant guidance, NSF to prioritize AI education resources, Labor to expand AI apprenticeships, and Agriculture to use 4-H/Extension programs, plus a "Presidential AI Challenge." No second 2025-2026 executive order specifically imposing K-12 AI safety guardrails (as opposed to promoting adoption) was found in this session; the broader federal posture in this period has trended deregulatory (a separate January 2025 executive order revoked the prior administration's AI order in favor of reduced federal AI regulation, background knowledge, unverified live), which is consistent with states filling the guardrail gap rather than the federal government.

## Implications for Bucket

**Legal obligations Bucket must meet.**

United States: any user known to be under 13 triggers COPPA, requiring verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information, retention limited to purpose (with "AI improvement" explicitly rejected by the FTC as a retention justification), and parental review/delete rights. If a public school district adopts the teacher class view, Bucket becomes a FERPA "school official" and needs a data-use agreement per district, or a state-level master agreement via an NDPA-style template. State laws (SOPIPA, Ed Law 2-d, SOPPA, and equivalents in other states) require written operator-district agreements, breach notification, and a ban on selling or ad-targeting student data before any US district can legally procure the tool. The citation-payment feature falls under California AB 1880, and the comparable Illinois, Minnesota, and Utah child-earnings-trust laws, in any applicable state, and should be built to a trust/custodial-holding standard by default rather than retrofitted after a first payment.

European Union: GDPR's Article 8 age-of-consent regime (13-16 depending on member state) applies to any data processing tied to a student account. The EU AI Act's Annex III education category is a live compliance question, not a future one, given the Aug 2026 general-application timeline discussed above; Bucket's learner-state classification and frontier-backward routing should get an EU counsel opinion on high-risk classification before any EU-facing deployment, not after.

United Kingdom: the ICO Children's Code requires a documented Data Protection Impact Assessment demonstrating high-privacy defaults and no manipulative design, achievable given Bucket's non-feed, non-companion architecture but not automatic simply because the architecture is favorable. The Online Safety Act likely applies a children's risk-assessment duty to any feature letting students publish productions into the shared graph, since that is user-generated content accessible to other users.

**Critics' objections already answered by the stated design.** The ban on AI answer-generation, limiting it to find/quote/check/organize, directly answers the companion-AI and cognitive-shortcut objections raised by Turkle, Center for Humane Technology, and Cognitive Resonance (Riley). The MIT license, open-source code, and nonprofit structure answer the "black box run for private profit" objection common to Williamson, O'Neil, EPIC, and AI Now. Retaining the teacher in the class view rather than letting AI decide learning outcomes answers Broussard's "technochauvinism" critique of AI overclaiming its own competence.

**Objections the current design does not yet answer.** No named external audit process is described for the five-learner-states classification or the frontier-backward routing engine, which is exactly what Williamson and O'Neil would flag: an unaudited system sorting students is a governance gap regardless of intent. No piloted, independently observed classroom-implementation evidence is described, which is Reich's and Selwyn's central test; a mission statement is not outcome data. No named bias-review process exists for what counts as a "foundation" in a global, all-subject knowledge graph, which is Noble's direct critique. No described technical safeguard confirms the AI's find/quote/check function cannot fabricate a quote or misattribute a source, which is Bender's direct critique and the one most damaging if it fails in public, since it undercuts the platform's core credibility claim. And no response exists yet to the anti-commercialization objection that Fairplay and EPIC are likely to raise against paying minors through a novel payment rail.

**Is paying minors a reputational liability.** Yes, and it should be treated as one independent of its legal defensibility. It combines two patterns regulators and advocates already treat with elevated suspicion: money paid to minors (the reason Coogan-style trust laws now exist in four states) and a cryptocurrency-adjacent payment rail reaching minors (a rail with essentially no state or federal precedent for direct-to-child settlement, and one that consumer-protection regulators have been skeptical of generally). Even a single, well-structured citation micropayment is likely to be described in coverage as an AI platform paying children in cryptocurrency, a framing that does damage independent of whether Bucket's structure is legally sound. Parents and school district risk offices tend to treat any monetary relationship with a minor as an automatic extra-scrutiny flag, regardless of framing as "citation payment" rather than "influencer income." The credible mitigation path, not yet confirmed as built, is a custodial or parent-controlled wallet by default for any user under the local age of majority, a trust-style holding structure matching the pattern already set in California, Illinois, Minnesota, and Utah, explicit parental visibility into any payment, and a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal opinion obtained before payment is enabled, not merely before registration is enabled.

**Five voices to engage first.**

1. Ben Williamson. Ask for a written pre-launch critique of the learner-state classification and routing algorithm: who can audit it, what happens on misclassification, what independent evidence exists that it improves learning. Publish his response, favorable or not.

2. Emily Bender. Ask her to run an adversarial technical audit of the find/quote/check constraint against a real sample of K-12 queries, testing specifically for fabricated quotes or misattributed sources. This is the single test that most directly validates or breaks Bucket's core credibility claim.

3. Fairplay. Ask for a pre-launch review of the citation-payment-to-minors design specifically: wallet custody, parental consent flow, and comparison against the CA/IL/MN/UT earnings-trust pattern, before any real payment reaches a minor. If they decline to engage constructively, their likely public objection should still be treated as a launch-readiness item to resolve regardless.

4. Common Sense Media. Submit Bucket for a formal AI risk assessment and rating before any school-facing marketing. An unreviewed product is a bigger adoption obstacle in district procurement than a reviewed-and-criticized one.

5. Future of Privacy Forum / Student Privacy Compass. Co-develop or adopt a standard NDPA before any US district pilot, and get their read on whether collecting a minor's wallet or payment information triggers SOPIPA/Ed Law 2-d/SOPPA obligations beyond ordinary account data.
