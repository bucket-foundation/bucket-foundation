## Implications for Bucket

**What this set of funders rewards.** Every named grant found above went to an organization with one of two things: an established track record at scale (Khan Academy, Zearn, Communities In Schools, Boys & Girls Clubs, curriculum publishers) or a named research/district partnership producing measurable outcomes (LEVI's whole model is a build-measure-improve cycle; Reach University's teacher apprenticeships; City Teaching Alliance). Carnegie, Ballmer, and Overdeck all fund organizations with existing footprints in tens of thousands of classrooms, not early-stage platforms. The brief asked specifically about ESSA tiers and district partnerships as evidence categories: no page reached in this research named ESSA tiers explicitly, but the pattern across LEVI, Gates, and Arnold Ventures (whose entire education portfolio is framed around "what works" evidence) signals that quantified learning-outcome evidence, not architecture or mission statements, is what moves money at this level.

**What they avoid.** The single most repeated, explicit statement found across the largest checks on this list is refusal of unsolicited proposals: Overdeck ("not accepting unsolicited grant proposals"), Ballmer ("cannot accept unsolicited grant applications"), Arcadia ("we don't accept applications for funding"). The pattern holds for the biggest K-12 dollars in this space: Gates, Walton, CZI, and Bezos publish no open application channel either. A cold nonprofit without an existing relationship, a warm introduction, or an intermediary (Renaissance Philanthropy functions as exactly that intermediary for Walton and Schmidt money) has close to no path into most of the money on this list.

**Evidence funders require.** None of the pages reached this session named a specific ESSA tier requirement, RCT mandate, or district-count threshold in explicit terms. What surfaced instead was a strong implicit standard: LEVI's entire premise is iterative efficacy measurement against learning gains, and Arnold Ventures frames its whole grant portfolio around evidence hierarchies even outside K-12. Bucket has no published efficacy study, no ESSA tier, and no district partnership as of this research. That is a gap against the implicit bar these funders operate on, not a gap in this research.

**Where Bucket's design clashes with funder priorities.**

Paying minors in cryptocurrency, over x402 on Base, is the single largest unaddressed risk found in this research. None of the sources reached, across Gates, Walton, Overdeck, Ballmer, Hewlett, CZI, Mellon, or Carnegie, contain any language referencing direct payments to students, cryptocurrency, blockchain settlement, or wallet-based compensation for minors. Institutional K-12 funders operate inside a child-safety and COPPA-conscious frame; a mechanism that routes payment to a minor's wallet is more likely to register as a compliance question for a program officer before it registers as an innovation. This needs a direct legal review and a funder-facing explanation independent of the fundraising pitch itself.

The knowledge-graph architecture has a direct positioning problem with the single funder whose language matches it almost word for word: CZI describes its own "Knowledge Graph" linking curriculum data and learning science, and "AI Developer Tools For Education," as something CZI is building, not something CZI funds externally. Pitching Bucket's knowledge graph to CZI as a novel approach risks reading as a competing product pitch rather than a grant request.

The AI-limited workspace (AI restricted to find, quote, check, organize, not generate-for-the-student) is the one design choice that reads as aligned with, not against, current funder posture. Overdeck funds Khan Academy, whose Khanmigo product was built with explicit guardrails against the AI doing the student's thinking. CZI and Gates both frame AI language around "personalization" and teacher support rather than replacement. This is the strongest existing point of alignment found in the research and the one worth leading with in any funder conversation.

**Overall.** Of the funders named in the brief, the ones with both real thematic overlap and any confirmed open door are narrow: Renaissance Philanthropy (one fund with confirmed open calls) and, on unverified but high-confidence grounds, VELA Education Fund. Every other large check in this space (Gates, Walton, CZI, Overdeck, Ballmer, Hewlett, Arcadia) is either closed to unsolicited proposals or has no public application information at all. The practical path into this funding landscape runs through relationships and intermediaries, not published RFPs, and the crypto-payments-to-minors mechanism needs a resolved compliance answer before it goes in front of any of them.
