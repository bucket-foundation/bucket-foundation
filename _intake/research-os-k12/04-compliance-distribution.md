# Research OS for K-12: Compliance, Distribution, and School-Operations Plan

Bucket Foundation, bucket.foundation. Nonprofit, 501(c)(3) filing pending, held in the founder's personal capacity. Stack: Next.js/Vercel, Supabase, Dynamic auth, x402 micropayments on Base. This plan covers the regulatory, financial, and operational path to put a K-12 knowledge-graph product with AI-limited student tooling and citation payments for student work into schools worldwide, while doubling as a research testbed for a PhD application question: which cognitive offloading to AI harms learning.

Every claim below is sourced. Anything not backed by a fetched source in 2026 is marked **UNVERIFIED** and should be confirmed with counsel or the named vendor before it becomes a public claim or contract term.

## 1. US Federal Law

| Law | Applies when | Concrete product requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **COPPA** (FTC, 15 U.S.C. 6501-6506, 2025 amendments) | Any operator that collects personal information from a child under 13, or a mixed-audience service where age is unknown | Age screen before any data collection on a general-audience or mixed-audience surface. Verifiable parental consent (VPC) before collecting personal information from a known under-13 user, with separate VPC for any third-party disclosure tied to targeted advertising. No behavioral advertising to children, full stop. Data minimization: collect only what the activity requires. A named data retention and deletion schedule, deletion on parent request. If relying on the "support for internal operations" exception (for example, learner-state telemetry used only to route content), publish a notice describing what is collected for that purpose and confirming persistent identifiers are not reused for advertising. Compliance deadline for the 2025 amendments was April 22, 2026, which has passed, so any 2026 build must already meet the amended rule. |
| **FERPA** (20 U.S.C. 1232g, 34 CFR Part 99) | Any product that receives personally identifiable information (PII) from a K-12 school's education records | The product can operate under the **school official exception** only if the school retains direct control over use and maintenance of the data, the product has a legitimate educational interest, and PII is never redisclosed without consent. This means a signed data privacy agreement with each district naming Bucket Foundation as a school official, a defined purpose limited to delivering the product, and a contractual right for the district to audit and terminate access. Directory information (name, grade level) can be disclosed only under a district's own annual-notice policy; the vendor has no unilateral say in what counts as directory information. Using student data for the founder's own PhD research falls outside the school-official exception entirely (see Section 11) and needs its own legal basis. |
| **PPRA** (20 U.S.C. 1232h) | Any survey, analysis, or evaluation funded in whole or part by the US Department of Education that touches eight protected categories (political affiliation, psychological problems, sex behavior, illegal or self-incriminating behavior, critical appraisals of family members, privileged relationships, religious beliefs, income) | Relevant if learner-state assessments or onboarding surveys probe any of these categories, which they should not, by design. If any research instrument (see Section 11) touches these categories, districts must get prior written parental consent and give parents a right to inspect the instrument. Keep onboarding and workspace telemetry limited to academic content and skill state to stay outside PPRA's scope entirely. |
| **CIPA** (47 U.S.C. 254(h), FCC) | Binds schools and libraries that receive E-Rate or LSTA funding; it governs the network the product runs on rather than the vendor | Districts must certify an internet safety policy with content filtering that blocks obscene material, child pornography, and material harmful to minors, and they cannot disable filtering for users under 17. Practically: the product must function correctly behind district content filters and CIPA-mandated monitoring, and any external links (research citations, source material) should be filter-compatible or the district will flag the tool during pilot IT review. |

**No behavioral ads, ever.** SOPIPA, COPPA 2025, NY 2-d, Illinois SOPPA, and Colorado's HB 16-1423 all independently prohibit targeted advertising to students. Since the product is free and ad-free by design, this is a non-issue if it stays that way, but it forecloses any future ad-supported tier for the K-12 surface.

## 3. International

| Jurisdiction | Framework | What "free to any learner worldwide" requires |
|---|---|---|
| European Union | GDPR Article 8 | Age of consent for a child to agree to an "information society service" is 16 by default, lowered by member states down to a floor of 13 (UK, Spain, Ireland, and others use 13; Austria uses 14; Germany and several others keep 16). Below the applicable age, parental consent, verified by proportionate means, is required before account creation. Because the threshold varies by country and the product is worldwide, the product needs a jurisdiction-aware age gate that adapts per region. |
| United Kingdom | Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), ICO, under the DPA 2018 | Applies to any online service "likely to be accessed" by anyone under 18, which is a lower bar than "designed for children." ## 4. Accessibility

| Standard | Scope | Deadline | Product implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| WCAG 2.2 AA | Voluntary consensus standard, referenced by nearly every other rule below | Current version since October 2023 | Build to 2.2 AA even though most legal mandates still cite 2.1 AA, since 2.2 is backward-compatible and covers 2.1 plus newer mobile, cognitive, and low-vision criteria. One target, satisfies every downstream mandate. |
| ADA Title II (DOJ rule) | State and local government entities, which includes every public school district and public school, but not the nonprofit itself directly, districts will require it of vendors by contract | WCAG 2.1 AA. Original deadline April 24, 2026 (entities over 50,000 population) has been extended by an interim final rule to **April 26, 2027** for large entities and **April 26, 2028** for smaller ones (state education agencies also moved to April 26, 2027). | Public-school district pilots will start asking for a VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) and an accessibility conformance statement as a routine RFP attachment well before their own legal deadline, since districts want vendor risk retired early. Do not wait for 2027 to build this. |
| Section 508 | Federal agencies and, by flow-down, many federally funded programs | Currently keyed to WCAG 2.0 AA as the base technical standard, no update scheduled for 2026 in the sources reviewed | Meeting 2.2 AA automatically clears 508's current bar. |

**Spatial-canvas risk.** A knowledge-graph canvas UI is the highest-risk accessibility surface in the product: pan/zoom graph navigation, spatial node relationships, and drag interactions map poorly to screen readers and keyboard-only navigation by default. Concrete requirements: every node and edge needs a text-equivalent linear navigation path (tab order that mirrors the frontier-backward routing logic instead of raw canvas position), zoom and pan must have keyboard equivalents, color must never be the only encoding for learner state (use icon plus color plus text label for each of the five states), and focus indicators must stay visible against the canvas background in both light and dark themes. This is custom UI work that sits outside what a generic accessibility library covers out of the box; budget a dedicated accessibility engineering pass for the canvas before any district pilot.

## 5. AI-Specific Rules and Provider Constraints

**State guidance volume.** As of mid-2026, 37 states plus Puerto Rico have issued official K-12 AI guidance, and nearly 100 state bills touching student AI use were tracked this legislative session. Recurring themes across the strongest examples:

- **Human-in-the-loop grading requirements are emerging.** Oklahoma explicitly bars AI from being the primary basis for a grade. New York City's March 2026 guidance uses a red/yellow/green traffic-light framework across roughly 1 million students and 78,000 teachers, the most detailed K-12 AI policy issued by a US district to date.
- **Teacher PD is now a compliance line item.** Maryland's SB 720 requires the state to fund teacher PD on AI; Virginia directs its state education agency to build teacher-training guidance into its AI policy.
- **Federal posture is permissive but conditioned.** The US Department of Education finalized an AI grant-priority rule April 13, 2026, and Secretary McMahon's accompanying guidance confirms federal funds can support K-12 AI tools (adaptive learning, tutoring) provided the deployment protects privacy, engages parents, and teaches appropriate AI use.

**Product design consequence.** The "teacher-in-the-loop" language showing up in state guidance is not generic messaging, it is becoming a specific procurement checkbox. The teacher review queue for student productions entering the graph (Section 11 of the product design) should be framed explicitly in district-facing materials as the human-in-the-loop control point, since that is the exact language several states now require vendors to demonstrate.

**"Proof of learning" explainability.** No state or federal source found in this research pass mandates a specific explainability standard for an AI judgment of student mastery. **UNVERIFIED, treat as a gap to close proactively rather than a known requirement.** Given the EU AI Act's Annex III transparency obligation (informing the learner that an AI system is assessing them, and providing a basis for that assessment) and district appetite for defensible grading-adjacent decisions, the product should ship: (a) a plain-language rationale attached to every state transition ("moved from Understanding to Internalization because you correctly applied X in three independent contexts"), (b) a visible evidence trail linking the judgment to the specific student artifacts it was based on, and (c) a teacher override that is logged and does not require justification. This is good practice ahead of regulation in most US states and a likely hard requirement in the EU.

**LLM provider age minimums, and why this matters for the build.** Consumer terms of service for the major model providers are not built for a K-12-deployed product and cannot be the basis for how minors interact with the system:

| Provider | Consumer ToS minimum age | Relevant note |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic (Claude.ai / Claude apps) | 18 | Consumer terms flatly exclude minors, with an active detection mechanism for suspected minor accounts. |
| OpenAI (ChatGPT) | 13, with parental permission required 13-17 | Automated age-prediction in 2026 applies teen content restrictions even to accounts self-reported as adult. |
| Google (Gemini) | 13 standard account, under 13 only via supervised Family Link | Gemini Education add-on is gated 18+. |

None of these consumer products can sit behind a K-12 student's login directly. The correct architecture, confirmed against Anthropic's published developer policy, is an **API-key deployment where Bucket Foundation is the accountable developer**: Anthropic's Usage Policy permits organizations to serve minors through their own API-key-provisioned product provided the developer implements age verification, content moderation, and monitoring appropriate to a minor audience, and it forbids routing requests through personal Free/Pro/Max consumer credentials on a user's behalf. This confirms the architecture already implied by "AI limited to find/quote/check/organize": a constrained, tool-scoped API integration running under Bucket Foundation's own developer account and content-safety layer. Build a content-safety and red-team pass for the constrained toolset (find/quote/check/organize) before any minor-facing launch; the safety obligation sits with Bucket Foundation as the deploying developer.

## 6. Minors and Money

This is the least precedented part of the product. It needs a direct legal opinion before launch; treat this section as a starting map for that opinion. Four separate legal questions stack here.

**1. Is a citation payment to a minor "employment" under child labor law?** The FLSA's creative-work exemption (actors, performers in film/theater/TV/radio) covers *employed* minors in entertainment productions, which is a different fact pattern from an unsolicited academic submission earning a one-time citation fee with no schedule, no supervision, and no ongoing obligation. A citation payment more closely resembles a contest prize, a royalty, or an honorarium than wages, which would place it outside FLSA child-labor coverage entirely. **UNVERIFIED as applied**, this needs an employment-law opinion tailored to the exact mechanic (accepted production triggers an on-chain citation fee to the author), because a growing wave of state "kid-influencer" trust-account laws (following Illinois's 2024 extension of its Child Actor law to monetized content creators, with similar bills active in California, Minnesota, and Utah) were written for platforms that pay minors for content, and a plausible-but-unconfirmed reading could sweep in a citation-payment model. Get a written opinion before the first live payment to a minor.

**2. Minors cannot hold a KYC'd crypto wallet on Base directly.** Coinbase's terms require account holders to be 18 and US-resident, and the broader VASP ecosystem (reinforced by 2025's Financial Digital Integrity Act per the sources reviewed) enforces an 18-plus floor for opening a self-directed exchange or wallet account. x402 settlement on Base therefore cannot terminate at a minor's own wallet.

**3. UTMA custodial accounts can legally hold crypto**, since most states' Uniform Transfers to Minors Act statutes recognize digital assets as property a custodian can hold on a minor's behalf, subject to prudent-custodian standards and secure key custody. This is the correct legal container.

**4. Tax reporting.** For 2026, a minor's earned income above roughly $16,100 requires a return; unearned/investment-type income above $1,350 triggers "kiddie tax" treatment, with income between $1,350 and $2,700 taxed at the child's own rate and anything above $2,700 taxed at the parent's marginal rate. A citation payment is closer to earned/royalty income than investment income, but Bucket Foundation as payer should still issue the appropriate 1099 (likely 1099-MISC for royalties/prizes, or 1099-NEC if characterized as compensation for services, pending the Section 6.1 legal opinion) to the custodial account holder.

**Recommended design:**

1. **No minor ever holds a wallet key.** A citation event on a student production writes an attribution and payment-owed record to Bucket Foundation's ledger (on-chain metadata, off-chain settlement rail).
2. **Parent or legal guardian is the required custodian at signup.** Verifiable parental consent (already required by COPPA/state law for the account itself) is extended to cover a specific authorization: "I consent to my child's accepted work earning citation payments, to be held in a custodial account I control." No custodian on file means the citation still credits the student's public attribution and portfolio, but payment escrows at Bucket Foundation rather than disbursing, with a clear parent-facing prompt to complete custodial setup.
3. **Settlement path:** Bucket Foundation's own KYC'd, adult-controlled treasury wallet receives the x402 payment, then disburses in USD-equivalent (or lets the parent choose USDC) into a UTMA account the parent opens at a custodian that accepts digital assets, or a plain cash UTMA if the parent prefers, Bucket Foundation is not the custodian of record, the parent or a UTMA-compliant custodian is.
4. **A minimum threshold and quarterly batching** for payouts keeps per-transaction gas and admin cost sane and gives Bucket Foundation time to confirm 1099 obligations per family per year.
5. **School-held option for classroom pilots** where individual parental custodial setup is impractical: escrow accrues per-student, released to the family only after a custodial account is confirmed, never spent by the school.

Sources: [DOL Fact Sheet #43, FLSA child labor, nonagricultural](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/43-child-labor-non-agriculture), [Experian, UGMA/UTMA basics](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-are-ugma-and-utma-accounts/), [BatesonLaw, how UTMA accounts work, including crypto](https://batesonlaw.com/uniform-transfers-to-minors-act-account-how-custodial-accounts-work/), [Bitget, can minors legally use Coinbase 2026](https://www.bitget.com/academy/can-minors-legally-use-coinbase-or-other-crypto-platforms-in-america-2026-guide-and-new-regulations), [SmartAsset, kiddie tax 2026](https://smartasset.com/taxes/kiddie-tax), [Nolo, kiddie tax limits 2026](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/kiddie-tax-limits-shifting-unearned-30003.html).

## 7. Nonprofit Status

**What a pending 501(c)(3) can do today:** accept donations, run public fundraising, and apply for grants that do not condition eligibility on a current determination letter. Deductibility to the donor becomes retroactive to the organization's formation date once the determination arrives, provided Form 1023 or 1023-EZ was filed within 27 months of formation.

**What it cannot do:** solicit donations while describing the organization as "tax-exempt" or "a 501(c)(3)" before the letter arrives (disclosure of pending status is required), apply to most institutional grant-makers or district RFPs that require current 501(c)(3) status as a threshold condition, or rely on the determination if the 27-month filing window is missed.

**The founder's-personal-capacity structure is itself a flag.** Holding Bucket Foundation's IP and accounts in a founder's personal capacity while the entity is legally a pending nonprofit creates a private-inurement and related-party question the eventual determination review, and any serious district or foundation funder, will ask about directly. This should be resolved (formal incorporation, IP assignment into the entity, conflict-of-interest policy on file, as already flagged in the venture's own governance documents) ahead of the first paid district contract or grant.

## 10. Pilot Design as Research

This section is the direct bridge to the founder's PhD application (ETH AI Center, AI for Education and Human-AI Collaboration) and the question: which cognitive offloading to AI harms learning.

**IRB path.** The founder is not currently affiliated with a research institution, and most university IRBs require a faculty member of record as principal investigator for human-subjects research, and the bar is higher still for research involving minors. Two viable routes:

1. **University partner as IRB of record.** Approach a School of Education faculty member (a natural target: a potential PhD advisor connected to the ETH AI Center's education track, or, if the pilot runs in a US school, a domestic education-school faculty member willing to serve as PI or co-PI) and route the study through their institution's IRB. This is the standard path for an independent researcher building a product-as-testbed and gives the resulting data institutional and publication credibility that a self-run study lacks.
2. **Internal program evaluation.** If the goal were only product improvement, most of this data collection could run as internal quality-improvement work outside IRB purview. That path is explicitly **not** recommended here, since the founder's stated goal is a publishable causal claim for PhD applications, which requires the rigor, disclosure, and generalizability standards only an IRB-approved design provides.

**Conflict of interest, disclose it directly.** The founder is simultaneously the product's builder, the study's designer, and the person whose PhD application benefits from the result. This is a known category of research conflict (the "founder-as-researcher" problem). State it plainly in the IRB application and in the consent materials. A partner faculty PI who did not build the product strengthens the design considerably.

**Consent and assent.** Passive consent (opt-out) is not acceptable for minors in research; active parental permission is required, plus the child's own assent. Assent may be waived by the IRB for minimal-risk research using normal educational practice up through roughly 6th grade, but should be actively obtained (written or, for younger children, a simplified oral/pictorial format) for middle and high school participants, since this is a novel AI tool being introduced for the study rather than an already-established curriculum. Build separate consent language for three things: (a) using the product at all, (b) the research use of interaction data as its own category, since FERPA's "studies exception" (34 CFR 99.31(a)(6)) is a narrow, school-initiated pathway that does not cleanly cover a vendor's own dissertation research and should not be the sole legal basis here, and (c) the custodial payment mechanism from Section 6.

**Study design: three-arm comparison.**

| Arm | Description | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Constrained AI (the product) | Find/quote/check/organize only, no generative answer synthesis | Tests whether scoped-tool AI support changes the outcomes below relative to no AI |
| Full chatbot | Open-ended generative AI assistance on the same material | Tests the hypothesized failure mode: full chatbot access produces short-term ease but worse independent performance |
| No AI (control) | Same material and same knowledge-graph navigation UI, human-only research tools | Baseline |

**Primary outcomes:**
- Near-transfer performance on a task structurally similar to but not identical to the practiced task
- Retention at 8 weeks post-instruction on the same construct
- Independent problem-solving, scored from workspace process logs already native to the product (time-to-first-hint, unprompted backtracking, unaided completion rate) rather than a separate instrument, which is a direct advantage of running the study inside the product itself

**Sample size, rough sketch only.** A three-arm between-subjects design targeting a medium effect size (Cohen's d approximately 0.4-0.5) at standard power (0.80) and alpha (0.05) needs on the order of 60-70 students per arm in most published guidance, roughly 180-210 total, before attrition. A smaller expected effect (d approximately 0.3) roughly doubles that. **Treat this as a back-of-envelope heuristic only; commission a formal power analysis from a statistician once the expected effect size and clustering structure (students nested in classrooms nested in schools, which inflates the needed N considerably under a proper multilevel design) are set.**

**Data governance for research use.** Keep the research data use agreement legally separate from the operational school DPA. The research agreement should specify: de-identification standard before any data leaves the product's operational systems for analysis, a fixed retention and destruction schedule tied to the study's end, no re-identification without a defined and IRB-approved process, and explicit handling of the founder's dual role as vendor and researcher (an independent data-access reviewer, ideally the partner faculty PI, should hold a technical or procedural check on what the founder-as-researcher can access from live student accounts outside the consented research cohort).

Sources: [UMass IRB guidance on K-12 research](https://www.umass.edu/research/compliance/human-subjects/irb-guidance/research-k-12), [UCSF HRPP, children and minors in research](https://irb.ucsf.edu/children-and-minors-research), [UNM IRB guidance on research in K-12 schools](https://irb.unm.edu/library/documents/guidance/research-in-k-12-schools.pdf), [Manhattan College IRB guidelines on assent](https://content.manhattan.edu/academic-resources/irb-documents/Guidelines%20on%20Research%20with%20Children%20for%20Consent,%20Permission,%20and%20Assent.pdf).

## 11. Teacher Adoption Playbook

**Onboarding under 20 minutes.** Structure as: create account and verify identity (5 minutes), pick or import a standards-aligned target node for the first assignment (5 minutes), preview what a student sees in the workspace including the AI's scoped tools (5 minutes), set the review-queue notification preference (5 minutes). Anything requiring a training video longer than the onboarding itself will not survive first contact with a teacher's planning period.

**First assignment as a target node.** The single most important design decision for adoption: a teacher should be able to pick an existing curriculum standard or textbook chapter and have the frontier-backward routing engine generate the prerequisite path automatically, rather than building a graph node from scratch. Requiring teachers to author graph structure before they can assign anything is the single most likely adoption killer on this list.

**Review queue that saves time instead of adding it.** The queue where teachers approve student productions for citation and graph inclusion has to net-save time versus a teacher's current grading workflow or it will not survive a semester. Concretely: default-sort by confidence (show the AI's own uncertainty about whether a production meets the bar, lowest-confidence first, so teacher attention goes where it is needed), allow batch-approve for high-confidence items, and show the evidence trail (Section 5) inline so a teacher does not have to re-derive why the system flagged something.

**What kills adoption**, based on the patterns implicit in the state-guidance and procurement research above:
- Any workflow that requires the teacher to understand graph theory or knowledge-graph structure to use the product day one
- A review queue that takes longer than the grading it replaces
- Any perception that the AI is grading rather than assisting, given the explicit state-level "AI should not be the primary basis for a grade" language already showing up in guidance (Oklahoma, and implicitly NYC's framework)
- A privacy or consent flow that puts the burden of legal understanding on the teacher rather than the district or the product's own pre-negotiated NDPA
- Device or bandwidth requirements that exclude the teacher's actual classroom hardware (see Section 13)

**PD material.** Keep it to two artifacts: a 10-minute video walkthrough of the first-assignment flow, and a one-page "what your students' AI can and cannot do" handout, since teacher trust in the AI-limited design (find/quote/check/organize, no generation) is itself an adoption lever worth stating plainly and often.

## 12. Equity Plan

**Avoid the gifted-only trap directly.** Path 3 in Section 9 (gifted and pull-out programs) is fast and appealing precisely because gifted coordinators have more autonomy, but a product that only ever gets validated in gifted contexts will (a) produce a research sample that cannot support the founder's broader learning-science claims and (b) never test the accessibility, offline, and device-constraint scenarios most students face day to day. Deliberately pair every gifted-program pilot with at least one Title I pilot in the same cohort of outreach.

**Title I partners.** Title I schools are federally funded to serve high concentrations of low-income students and are a natural equity anchor; they also have existing federal compliance infrastructure (they already handle FERPA, CIPA, and PPRA obligations routinely) which lowers the district's own onboarding friction for a new vendor relative to a school with less compliance muscle.

**Offline mode.** A spatial knowledge-graph canvas is bandwidth-heavy by nature. An equity plan worth the name needs: a lightweight text/list fallback view for the same graph content that works on low-bandwidth connections, local caching of the current target node's prerequisite path for offline review, and a design assumption that AI-assisted features (find/quote/check/organize) may be unavailable offline while the graph and student workspace stay usable.

**Device constraints.** Design against the low end of common school hardware (Chromebooks, shared computer labs with time-limited sessions, occasionally shared devices needing fast account switching), not against the founder's own development machine. A canvas UI that assumes a trackpad or a large display will fail in a shared-Chromebook-cart classroom, which remains one of the most common K-12 hardware patterns nationally.

**Languages.** English-only at launch is acceptable for a first pilot, but the roadmap should name a second-language target (Spanish is the strongest choice for US Title I reach) before the district-MOU stage, since language access is both an equity requirement in many state guidance documents and a practical adoption blocker in a large share of Title I schools.

## 13. Provide vs Outsource

| Function | Provide in-house | Outsource | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal counsel (privacy, AI Act, employment/minors) | No | Yes | Outsource. This is specialist, jurisdiction-spanning legal work (US federal, US state x6, EU, UK, Switzerland, Chile, India) that a non-lawyer founder should not attempt to self-serve past the research stage this document represents. Retain counsel before the first district contract and before the first payment to a minor. |
| DPA / NDPA templates | Yes, adapt the SDPC NDPA | No | The NDPA is a public, standardized template designed to be self-served; legal review of the adapted version is still worth a paid pass, but the drafting itself does not need to be outsourced. |
| Consent management (age gate, VPC flows) | Yes, build in-house | Partially, consider a verified-parental-consent vendor (several FTC-approved COPPA VPC methods exist commercially) for the verification mechanic itself | Build the UI and data flow in-house; buy the actual identity/age verification mechanism from a compliance vendor rather than building novel age-verification technology, since getting VPC verification wrong is a direct FTC exposure. |
| Accessibility audit | Partially | Yes, for the formal conformance statement/VPAT | Build accessibility into the canvas UI from day one (Section 4) in-house, since it is core product work, but commission a third-party WCAG 2.2 AA audit and VPAT before the first district RFP, since districts trust a named third-party auditor more than a self-attestation. |
| Content safety / red-teaming of the AI toolset | Partially | Yes, for adversarial red-teaming | Build the scoped-tool architecture (find/quote/check/organize, no free generation) in-house as core product design, but commission an external red-team pass focused on minor-safety failure modes before any minor-facing launch, consistent with Anthropic's own developer guidance for organizations serving minors. |
| Security audit / SOC 2 | No | Yes | Always a third-party engagement by definition; the in-house work is readiness (Section 8), not the audit itself. |
