# Research OS for K-12: Vendor and Data-Source Map

Same content as `03-data-services-system-review.md`, folded in from the separate system-review pass on 2026-09-10; see `CHANGELOG.md` in this folder.

Bucket Foundation, bucket.foundation. Verified 2026-09-09 against each vendor's own pages unless a row says "unverified". Product under evaluation: one knowledge graph in tiers across all subjects from first facts to frontier laws; frontier-backward routing to what a learner already knows; five learner states per node (Access, Awareness, Understanding, Internalization, Production); student workspace with AI limited to find, quote, check, organize; teacher class view; student productions accepted into the graph earn citation payments over x402 on Base; free to any learner worldwide. Stack: Next.js on Vercel, Supabase, Dynamic auth, x402 on Base, feed402, Story Protocol, MIT-licensed code.

Recommendation codes used in every table:

- **PROVIDES**: Bucket builds and hosts this itself on open data.
- **OUTSOURCE**: pay a vendor; the terms or the engineering make in-house the wrong call.
- **INGEST**: free data Bucket copies and caches into its own graph, subject to the license column.
- **SKIP**: dead, out of scope, or blocked by terms.

Phase 0 and Phase 2 stacks with monthly cost, and the consolidated licensing-landmine list, are at the end.

Category A landmines:

- Elsevier ScienceDirect: full text pulled via the API cannot be shared with third parties under any current terms. Snippets up to 200 characters only. Full access requires an institution that already subscribes.
- Wiley: unverified, both TDM pages 403'd. Treat as noncommercial-research-only with no redistribution until read.
- Springer Nature: Meta and Full Text APIs sit under a TDM Reservation Policy. Only the Open Access API is safe to cache.
- Clarivate Web of Science: no free tier at all; redistribution terms are not public.
- JSTOR: Constellate output may not be redistributed; free reading drops to 10 articles per 30 days on 2026-09-01.
- Semantic Scholar: CC-BY-NC datasets cannot enter a paid x402 citation flow. Filter them out or keep them outside the monetized graph.
- Dimensions: its own terms bar bulk data and derivative products.
- CORE and bioRxiv: license is per item. Some harvested items remain all-rights-reserved; some preprints carry NC or ND terms.
- arXiv: metadata is CC0; PDFs are not mirrorable by default.

- HathiTrust: only about a third of the corpus is public domain; the rest is gated by copyright and geography.
- Library of Congress and Europeana rights are per item. Filter, never blanket-cache.
- Wolfram Alpha is a paid vendor, and its 2026 prices need a direct quote.

### Paying minors: legal mechanics

No federal law bars a US nonprofit from paying a minor for content. A minor lacks capacity to sign a binding contract and can disaffirm one in most states, so the creator economy routes payment through a parent or guardian. YouTube's Partner Program requires the creator to be 18 or to have a legal guardian manage the AdSense payment account ([YouTube help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72857)); that is a workable template. A nonprofit is not the minor's parent, so the FICA and FUTA exemption for a parent employing their own child under 18 does not apply; a nonprofit paying $600 or more per year to an independent contributor follows standard 1099-NEC rules ([IRS family help](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/family-help)). The cleanest custodial mechanism is a UTMA or UGMA account: a third party can deposit into an account titled in the minor's name with a parent as custodian, and the funds stay the minor's property until the state's age of majority. California's Coogan Act, expanded in September 2024 to child social-media creators, requires 15% of a young creator's earnings be set aside in a blocked trust ([Coogan Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coogan_Law)); Illinois, Minnesota, and Utah have comparable child-influencer statutes whose exact text and effective dates were not re-verified against primary sources this pass. Lowest-risk design: guardian-as-payee for every contributor under 18, UTMA support for parents who want funds locked to the child, and a state-by-state review of Coogan-style trust mandates before treating a minor's citation earnings as liquid at payout.

## Licensing landmines

Sources that cannot be cached into Bucket's graph or shown to minors without a license or a per-item filter:

1. Elsevier ScienceDirect: no third-party sharing of API full text; 200-character snippets only.
2. Wiley: unverified; assume no redistribution.
3. Springer Nature Meta and Full Text APIs: TDM reservation; only the Open Access API is safe.
4. Clarivate Web of Science: paid license only; redistribution terms not public.
5. JSTOR: no redistribution of Constellate output; 10 free reads per 30 days from 2026-09-01.
6. Dimensions DSL: terms bar bulk data and derivative products.
7. Encyclopaedia Britannica: terms ban scraping and AI use; syndication license or nothing.
8. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: exclusive publication right; link only.
9. CK-12: proprietary license, unread.
10. Semantic Scholar CC-BY-NC datasets: no monetized citation flow.
11. Khan Academy CC BY-NC-SA: links and embeds only; no framing, no paid walls.
12. OpenStax CC BY-NC-SA: noncommercial; verify per book.
13. MIT OpenCourseWare CC BY-NC-SA: noncommercial.
14. PhET CC BY-NC: nonprofits running paid services count as commercial.
15. MSC 2020 CC BY-NC-SA: taxonomy only; noncommercial.
16. AL-CPL dataset CC BY-NC-SA.
17. arXiv full text: metadata CC0, PDFs not mirrorable by default.
18. bioRxiv and medRxiv: per-preprint NC and ND terms.
19. PubMed abstracts: publisher copyright; PMC and Europe PMC CC-BY-NC slices: noncommercial.
20. CORE: per-item license, some all-rights-reserved.
21. HathiTrust: about two-thirds in copyright, geographically gated.
22. Internet Archive controlled digital lending items: borrow only.
23. Library of Congress and Europeana: per-item rights.
24. Jack Kruse corpus in the in-house gateway: citation-only, snippet plus link.
25. Google Gemini API: terms prohibit applications directed at under-18 users; an AI-layer landmine rather than a content one.
26. Google Classroom API: no reselling of Classroom data; Limited Use disclosure and OAuth security assessment.

Any content under a noncommercial license must stay outside the x402 citation-fee flow, because a paid citation is a commercial use.
