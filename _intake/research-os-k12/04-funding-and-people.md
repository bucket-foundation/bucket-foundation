# Research OS for K-12: Funding and People Map

Bucket Foundation, bucket.foundation. Compiled 2026-09-09 from eleven parallel research streams (philanthropy, government and multilateral funding, venture-funded companies, hackathons and web3 funding, nonprofit analogs, and six people slices: researchers, open-knowledge leaders, industry leaders, investors and donors, educators and districts, policy and critical voices). Each stream's full report, with every table row and citation, sits in `raw/`. The brief asked for an analysis unbiased toward Bucket's own assumptions; where the evidence cuts against the current design it says so here.

Most streams lost web-search quota early and fetched primary pages directly. Anything a stream could not confirm against a primary source is marked unverified, in this file and in `raw/`. Re-verify before any figure enters a grant application, a filing, or public copy.

## 1. Findings

1. Money in this category moves through relationships and intermediaries. Overdeck, Ballmer, Arcadia, and Walton state that they take no unsolicited proposals; Gates, CZI, and the Bezos Family Foundation publish no open door. Renaissance Philanthropy (Walton and Schmidt money, home of LEVI Math, LEVI Literacy, and an AI for Math Fund with confirmed open calls) is the live intermediary. [raw/funding-philanthropy.md]
2. Open, non-dilutive doors exist now: Tools Competition (Catalyst $50,000, Growth $150,000, Transform $300,000; 2027 cycle opening September 2026), Fast Forward (deadline 2026-09-18), NewSchools Venture Fund (open portal, 80+ ventures funded in 2025), Google.org Generative AI Accelerator ($30M across 20 organizations in 2025), NLnet NGI Zero (deadline 2026-11-03, fits feed402), Ethereum Foundation ESP (rolling), ETHGlobal bounties, and Digital Public Goods registration (free, about 30 days). [raw/funding-hackathons-web3.md, raw/funding-government-multilateral.md]
3. The US federal picture contracted in 2025: IES lost about $881M across 169 contracts, the Office of Educational Technology closed in March 2025, and the flagship IES research line (84.305A) is absent from the FY2027 competition list. The four NSF AI Institutes in education are funded through 2028 to 2031 with no new education cohort open. The realistic public path is a university PI partner plus DPG registration plus ED EIR early-phase when it reopens. [raw/funding-government-multilateral.md]
4. The venture market splits three ways. Teacher-workflow tools scaled through districts (Brisk reports 10,000+ districts; MagicSchool, SchoolAI). Consumer homework help collapsed under free chatbots (Chegg lost 38% in one day in May 2023 and cut 45% of remaining staff in October 2025; Brainly re-platformed). Citation tools monetize subscriptions and pay no authors (Elicit, Consensus, Undermind; Scite was absorbed by Research Solutions). The graveyard for graph-plus-content plays: Knewton ($157.5M raised, sold for under $17M), Boundless (sued by three publishers over arrangement, wound down by 2017), Metacademy (volunteer graph, site now unreachable). An all-subjects mastery graph is unproven rather than disproven; the author-payment mechanism has no evidence base in K-12 or research tooling. [raw/funding-vc-startups.md]
5. Nonprofit analogs show the scale ladder. At $1M every survivor is single-source (CK-12 and the Khosla family; PhET and Wieman's prize money plus a university host; LibreTexts and one $4.9M Department of Education award). At $10M the pattern is diversified philanthropy (Center for Open Science $10.2M, Our World in Data) or membership revenue (ORCID reached 95.7% earned revenue after about ten years and seven years of negative net assets; Crossref runs on member fees and is cutting them in 2027). At $50M and above the models are mass small-dollar giving (Wikimedia $205.4M in FY2025), a nonprofit-owned commercial subsidiary (Mozilla), or government contracts (Digital Promise peaked at $89.3M in 2021). No analog reached scale on micropayments; ResearchHub has distributed $1.5M lifetime. [raw/funding-nonprofit-analogs.md]
6. Crypto association is a revealed-preference negative among the funders that sustain Bucket's analogs. Wikimedia stopped accepting crypto donations on 2022-05-01 by community vote; Our World in Data rejected a $7.5M FTX Future Fund grant in 2022. Equity-shaped crypto programs (Base Ecosystem Fund, Base Batches, Coinbase Ventures) are structurally incompatible with a 501(c)(3). x402 itself shows real machine-to-machine traction (75.41M transactions, $24.24M volume, average about $0.32 as of August 2026, under Linux Foundation governance) with no adoption yet by a scholarly publisher, funder, or citation-infrastructure incumbent. [raw/funding-nonprofit-analogs.md, raw/people-open-knowledge.md]
7. Paying minors is the single largest unaddressed risk in every stream. Coogan-style earnings-trust laws now exist in California (AB 1880, 2024), Illinois, Minnesota, and Utah; no US law addresses crypto payments to a minor; Fairplay and EPIC are the likely public objectors; program officers will read a wallet payout to a child as a compliance question before an innovation. [raw/people-policy-critics.md, raw/funding-philanthropy.md]
8. The constrained AI rule (find, quote, check, organize) is the strongest point of alignment across every stream: UNESCO's human-centred framing, IES SEER open-science standards, Khan Academy, Anthropic, and Google's public positions, practitioner "humans in the loop" language (Washington OSPI, Peninsula School District), and critics from Turkle to the Center for Humane Technology. The constraint itself has never been tested in an RCT. Tutor CoPilot (assistive AI) supports it; the Harvard physics RCT (a permissive tutor beat active learning on short-term gains) challenges it. [raw/people-researchers.md]
9. The five learner states are original and unvalidated. ICAP (Chi), Bloom's revised taxonomy, SOLO, and Perkins's understanding performances are the comparators; researchers ask for a published mapping and a construct-validation study against transfer; critics (Williamson, O'Neil) ask who audits the classifier. Bloom's 2-sigma has never replicated at scale: VanLehn 2011 puts intelligent tutoring near 0.76 SD; Nickow, Oreopoulos, and Quan 2020 put tutoring near 0.37 SD across 96 RCTs. [raw/people-researchers.md]
10. Distribution runs through teachers and districts in every scaled case. School librarians and research-skills instruction are the natural entry channel: AASL published second-edition standards in 2025 and an active AI editorial line, National History Day already runs the find-cite-argue loop for grades 6 to 12, and Iowa City delivers K-5 AI lessons through librarians. The 2026 backdrop is hostile to student-facing AI: NYC banned AI tools for pre-K through grade 8 (2026-09-01), LAUSD banned most student AI, the AllHere collapse drew an FBI search warrant (2026-02-25), and Houston parents filed "AI slop" complaints. Any district pitch has to lead with governance and with what the AI cannot do. [raw/people-educators-districts.md]

## 4. Implications for Research OS and for Bucket

Keep: the constrained AI rule, the open-source nonprofit structure, canon grounding, the teacher class view, and the free-to-read commitment. Every stream found these to be the strongest points of alignment with funders, practitioners, and critics.

Change, in priority order:

1. Narrow the first proof. One subject, one grade band, one classroom, one librarian or social-studies team (Peninsula or Iowa City), with outcomes published in the open. No funded company has built an all-subjects graph and kept it current; the nearest precedent (Boundless) died in litigation and the nearest volunteer graph (Metacademy) decayed. Prove the loop end to end before claiming scope.
2. Enter through teachers and librarians. Every scaled K-12 product reached scale through a teacher or district channel; the student-direct companies are the ones free chatbots destroyed. Build the teacher class view and the librarian research-skills workflow first; treat "free to any learner worldwide" as the access policy, and the classroom as the distribution.
3. Validate the five states before deploying them as a classifier. Publish a mapping to ICAP, Bloom's revised taxonomy, SOLO, and Perkins's understanding performances; run a construct-validation study showing that a state predicts transfer and forward learning rate; publish the audit process for misclassification (who can contest a state, what happens on error).
4. Pre-register an RCT of the constraint. Arms: find-quote-check-organize AI, permissive explain-and-answer AI, and no AI, across matched graph nodes, scored on delayed unassisted post-tests. Partner with Stanford (Loeb, Demszky, Wang) or ASSISTments so the IRB, registrations, and evaluation record come with the partner.
5. Separate the two stories.
