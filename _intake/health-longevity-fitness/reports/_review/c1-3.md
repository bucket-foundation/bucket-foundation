# Cycle 1 — Layperson review: slice 3

Reviewer persona: smart, motivated layperson, no medical training. Reading these six
chapters as a reader who wants value and will put the book down if bored, lost, or
never told why a section matters.

## PATTERNS (read this first)

**P1 — Every chapter opens cold, behind a wall of admin.** All six files start with one
to four stacked gray blockquotes — "the gap this section fills," "cross-references (do
not duplicate)," "not medical advice," "graded per the manual's evidence tiers" —
*before* a single sentence aimed at me, the reader. By the time I reach anything I care
about, I've waded through editorial scaffolding written for the corpus, not for me. 07,
22, 23, 24 are the worst; 17 and 42 at least reach a "one-line verdict" reasonably fast.
**Top fix: lead every chapter with one or two sentences of human stakes ("Your kidneys
can lose half their function before you feel a thing — here's how to protect them"),
then drop the cross-ref/not-medical-advice boilerplate into a smaller block *below* the
hook.**

**P2 — "The three honesty rules" are invoked constantly but never restated where I am.**
The phrases "predictor ≠ lever," "cohort ≠ RCT," "something beats nothing," and
especially "Honesty rule #1 applies hard / bites hard" appear dozens of times across all
six chapters, each assuming I read and memorized a "Start Here" file I may never have
seen. The *idea* is great and is the most useful thing in the whole slice — but the
shorthand reads like an inside joke I'm not in on. **Top fix: the first time each chapter
uses "predictor ≠ lever," gloss it inline in five words ("the number warns you, it isn't
the thing you fix"); stop numbering them ("rule #1") since the number means nothing to a
reader.**

**P3 — Acronym and biomarker jargon fires before it's defined, usually in the up-front
summary table or verdict.** apoB, eGFR, VO₂max, LARC, GDMT, RAAS, SGLT2, GLP-1, MASLD,
FODMAP, DMT, CGRP, AHI, LES, DGBI, HFrEF/HFpEF — almost all get expanded *eventually*,
but the at-a-glance table at the top (which is where a skimmer lands) is where they hit
first, undefined. **Top fix: expand every acronym on first appearance including inside
tables, or add a one-line legend under each opening table.**

Cross-cutting strength to protect: the writing is genuinely vivid whenever it stops
doing corpus-admin and tells a story — bloodletting as a cure, Marshall drinking the
bacteria, "time is brain," "you cannot diet your way out of gout," "muscle contraction
*is* the lymphatic pump." More of that voice, sooner.

## sections/17-organ-systems-atlas.md

- **GOOD** §intro — "Most longevity writing optimizes a short list of organs — heart, brain, muscle — and treats the rest of the body as plumbing. This section is the plumbing, written honestly." → A real hook with a point of view; keep it. This is how every chapter should open.
- **GOOD** §5.2 iron — "the treatment is therapeutic phlebotomy — literally regular bloodletting" and the framing of iron as two-sided grabbed me; the donation angle is memorable. Don't touch.
- **GOOD** §7.3 — "Muscle contraction *is* the lymphatic pump." One sentence that makes the whole "drainage detox" debunk land. Keep.
- **LOST** §1.1 — "Lung function is the respiratory half of the **VO₂max** story" uses VO₂max as if known; it isn't expanded until §1.5, four sections later. → fix: gloss on first use ("VO₂max — the max oxygen your body can use, the single best fitness-mortality number").
- **SO-WHAT** opening blockquote — the chapter's literal first content is a routing table of which organs live in which *other* files ("cardiovascular → 07; nervous/brain → 08…"). That's filing-cabinet metadata, not a reason to read. → fix: move the "what lives elsewhere" note to the end, or shrink to one line, and open on the plumbing hook instead.
- **BORED** §System-by-system map table — a dense 7-row table with a "Tier of the lever" column full of bare codes (`cohort`→`rct`, `meta`, `debunk`) lands before I know what those tiers mean. I skimmed straight past it. → fix: add a one-line key above the table and defer the tier-code column to the prose, or the table reads as code.
- **LOST** §2.1 — "eGFR" appears in the map table and again here; it's spelled out ("estimated glomerular filtration rate") only at §2.1. The summary table at the very top uses it cold. → fix: expand at first table appearance.
