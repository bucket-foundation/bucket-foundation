# Cycle 1 — Layperson review — Slice 8

Reviewer: smart, motivated non-expert reading for personal value.

## PATTERNS

- **Acronym and jargon dumping before definition.** Across almost every chapter, terms land cold the first
  time and only get spelled out paragraphs later or not at all: `COM-B`, `BCT`, `SDT`, `TTM`, `WOOP`, `DOHaD`,
  `GDM`, `GBD`, `MR` (Mendelian randomization), `apoB`, `Lp(a)`, `HOMA-IR`, `CPET`, `DALY`, `QALY`, `CRF`,
  `Beveridge/Bismarck`. A layperson hits a wall every time. **Top fix #1: expand every acronym in plain words
  at first use, in-line, even when the academic name follows in parentheses.**
- **Inline citation clutter breaks reading flow.** DOIs, PMIDs, author-year strings, and `claim-id` slugs are
  jammed mid-sentence everywhere (e.g. `(intention-behavior-gap-experimental, doi 10.1037/...)`). For a reader
  (not a fact-checker) these are speed bumps in the middle of otherwise great prose. **Top fix #2: move DOIs/
  PMIDs/claim-slugs to footnotes or end-of-section refs; keep at most "Author Year" inline.**
- **Effect-size shorthand assumes statistics literacy.** `d ≈ 0.66`, `HR ~3x`, "small-to-medium," `~13%/MET`,
  "J-shaped/U-shaped," "monotonic" appear constantly with no plain-English anchor. The book respects me enough
  to give numbers — it should respect me enough to tell me what they *mean* the first few times. **Top fix #3:
  on first use, gloss Cohen's d ("d≈0.65 ≈ a large, clearly-noticeable effect"), "monotonic" ("straight-line:
  more = worse, no safe dip"), and MET once, then reuse freely.**
- **Cross-reference soup.** Sections constantly point to `§04 §4.3`, `Domain N`, `Domain I`,
  `bucket-canon/05-biophysics`, `06-evidence/SCHEMA.md`. A few are helpful; the density makes it feel like I
  need 6 other files open to read one chapter. Trim to the load-bearing ones.
- **GOOD pattern worth protecting:** every chapter has a punchy one-line thesis up front and a plain-English
  closing synthesis ("the whole chapter reduces to a sentence…", "read this if nothing else"). These are the
  best parts of the book and the reason a layperson keeps going. Keep them; lead with them harder.
