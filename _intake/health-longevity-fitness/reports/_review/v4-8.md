## 29-behavior-change §1

- claim: "a medium-to-large change in intention (d ≈ 0.66) produced only a small-to-medium change in behavior (d ≈ 0.36)" | cited: Webb & Sheeran 2006, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249

Number, direction, and framing all match. (research.manchester.ac.uk; semanticscholar record)

## 19-life-stages §3.1

- claim: "periconceptional folic acid cut neural-tube-defect recurrence by ~72%" | cited: MRC Vitamin Study, Lancet 1991, PMID 1677062
- **CONFIRMED** — RCT (1,817 high-risk women, 4 mg/day folic acid): 6 NTDs in folic-acid groups vs 21 in the others = 72% protective effect; trial stopped early. Number, population (recurrence/high-risk), and direction all match. (pubmed 1677062)

## 19-life-stages §9.2

- claim: "the early-palliative-care group lived longer, not shorter" | cited: Temel et al. 2010, NEJM, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1000678
- **CONFIRMED** — Early palliative care in metastatic NSCLC: median survival 11.6 vs 8.9 months (longer), with better QoL/mood and less aggressive end-of-life care. Direction and endpoint match. (nejm.org; wikijournalclub)

## 09-exposures §2.1

- claim: "quitting before age 40 avoids ~90% of the excess mortality" (+ "~10 years of life" lost) | cited: Jha et al. 2013, NEJM, doi:10.1056/NEJMsa1211128
- **CONFIRMED** — NHIS cohort (~216,000 adults): cessation at 35–44 (median 39) cut excess mortality ~90% and gained ~9–10 years vs continued smoking. Match. (nejm.org NEJMsa1211128)

## 09-exposures §3.2

- claim: "each 10 µg/m³ increase in long-term PM2.5 raised all-cause mortality ~4%, cardiopulmonary ~6%, and lung cancer ~8%" | cited: Pope et al. 2002, JAMA, doi:10.1001/jama.287.9.1132
- **CONFIRMED** — The paper's headline: "approximately a 4%, 6%, and 8% increased risk of all-cause, cardiopulmonary, and lung cancer mortality" per 10 µg/m³. (Table-2 average-exposure metric runs higher at 6/9/14%, but the manual quotes the paper's own summary figure.) Match. (PMC4037163)

## 09-exposures §1.2

- claim: "lowest all-cause mortality risk sits at ~100 g of pure alcohol per week (≈5–6 standard drinks)" ... "599,912 current drinkers across 83 studies" | cited: Wood et al. 2018, Lancet, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X
- **CONFIRMED (with one MINOR unit note)** — Threshold for lowest all-cause mortality ~100 g/week; 599,912 current drinkers, 83 prospective studies. Headline number, population, and study count all match. MINOR: 100 g pure alcohol ≈ 7 US standard drinks (14 g each), not "5–6"; the parenthetical drink-count slightly understates. Core claim unaffected.

## 33-public-health §1.2

- claim: "clean water ... Total ~half (≈43%), Infant ~three-quarters (≈74%), Child ~two-thirds (≈62%)" | cited: Cutler & Miller 2005, Demography, doi:10.1353/dem.2005.0002
- **CONFIRMED** — Original estimates: clean water explains ~43% of total, ~74% of infant, ~62% of child mortality decline in US cities ~1900–1936. The manual correctly flags the Anderson–Charles–Rees reanalysis (revises total to ~38%, direction intact). Match. (nber.org w25027; PRB)

## 33-public-health §2.1

- claim: "Whitehall I: lowest grade had ~3× the coronary mortality of the highest" | cited: Marmot et al., J Epidemiol Community Health 1978, doi:10.1136/jech.32.4.244
- **CONFIRMED** — Lowest grade (messengers) had 3.6× the CHD mortality of the highest (administrators); gradient persisted after adjusting for standard risk factors. "~3×" is accurate. (PMID 744814; unhealthywork.org)

## 33-public-health §4.4

- claim: "of the ~30 years of life expectancy gained in the 20th century, clinical medical care explains roughly 5 years (~⅙, ~15–18%)" | cited: Bunker, Frazier & Mosteller 1994 / Bunker 2001
- **CONFIRMED** — This is the standard Bunker estimate: medical care accounts for ~5 of ~30 years gained (small population effect despite large individual effects); ~half of that from the post-1950 slice. Consistent with the canonical citation and the 4-methods review (PMC6827626). Match.

## 04-individual-variation §2.1

- claim: "Mean VO2max gain was ~+19%, but the individual range ran from essentially zero to over +40–50%" | cited: Skinner et al. 2001, J Appl Physiol, doi:10.1152/jappl.2001.90.5.1770

## 04-individual-variation §5.3 / WHAT-TO-TRACK Tier A

- claim: "muscle-strengthening mortality association peaks around ~30–60 minutes per week" / "resistance activity ~10–17% lower mortality ... J-shaped" | cited: Momma et al. 2022, BJSM, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061
- **CONFIRMED** — Meta-analysis of 16 cohorts: muscle-strengthening activity 10–17% lower risk of all-cause mortality/CVD/cancer/diabetes; maximum benefit ~30–60 min/week, J-shaped for CVD. Both numbers and the J-shape match. (PMID 35228201; news-medical)
