# Cycle 3 (Practitioner) findings — slice 5

## PATTERNS

- **Intro ↔ closing-summary redundancy is the single biggest length win across the slice.** Five of six
  chapters open with a strong verdict/framing paragraph and then re-state the same framing in a closing
  "honest summary" / "honesty footer." The closes mostly earn their keep (they carry the graded list), but
  the *narrative* framing ("refuses two failures," "both things are true," "the pattern is the same and
  clean") is said twice in full. Tighten the second instance to a pointer, keep every graded claim. Biggest
  single hits: 43 (intro vs footer), 27 (intro verdict vs one-paragraph summary), 38 (intro vs closing note).
- **"Honest take" / summary boxes that restate the table immediately above them.** 27 is the worst offender
  — nearly every subsection has a table, then an "Honest take" paragraph that re-narrates the table's verdict
  column. These boxes add voice and the occasional caveat, so they mostly *stay*, but several open with a
  preamble that just re-labels the heading and can be trimmed.
- **Duplicate "named over-uses / claims-indexed" tables at chapter end.** 38 §5 and 40 §40.10 each rebuild a
  table (or dense paragraph) out of gradings already stated in the body. These are useful as an at-a-glance
  index — keep as signpost, but they should say "detail above" rather than re-argue.
- **Findability is already strong.** 27, 38, and 40 have genuine BLUF boxes and "read this part first"
  pointers — flag as GOOD and copy the pattern. The two that lack an up-front verdict are **39**
  (anesthesia — reader wades to §10 for the payoff) and, to a lesser degree, the interior of **34**.
- **Master "Recognize→Act" / "map at a glance" tables are the best signposts in the slice** (34 §1.1, 38 §0,
  43 §0, 40 §40.2) — all GOOD. Where a big table lacks a "how to read this" cue, add one line.

Rough total cuttable: **~1,300–1,600 words** without touching a single graded claim, number, tier, DOI,
caveat, cross-ref, `@@FIG@@`, or `[^footnote]`.
