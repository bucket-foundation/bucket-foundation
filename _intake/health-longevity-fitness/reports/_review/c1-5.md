# Cycle 1 — Layperson review — Slice 5

Reviewer persona: smart, motivated non-expert reading to live better. Six chapters:
27 (skin/teeth/ENT/eye), 43 (pediatric/congenital), 34 (emergency/first aid), 38 (surgery),
39 (anesthesia/critical care), 40 (imaging/radiology).

## PATTERNS (cross-chapter)

1. **The "do-not-duplicate" front-matter wall is the #1 momentum killer.** Five of six chapters open
   with a long blockquote about which OTHER section owns what (§11, §18, §19, §20, §21, §22, §25,
   §26, §31, §34…). As a reader I do not yet care about the corpus's internal turf map — I came for
   *this* topic. I started skimming inside the first 10 lines of 27, 38, 39, and 40 because the page
   opened with cross-reference bookkeeping instead of the subject. The actual hooks (often excellent)
   are buried 30–60 lines down. **Top fix: lead every chapter with the human hook/stakes, then move
   the "what this section does/doesn't cover" map to a short box AFTER the opening, or to the end.**

2. **Jargon and acronyms fire before they're defined**, repeatedly: NMA, DMFS, PASI, SCC/BCC, MAC,
   RPE, MGD, IOP (eventually defined), QALY, CPET, RCRI, ASA class, CRRT, EGDT, BOLD, LNT, ALARA,
   apoB, MCED, DCIS. Some get expanded later or never. A layperson hits a wall each time. **Top fix:
   expand every acronym on first use, in-line, even when it feels obvious to a clinician.**

3. **The grading shorthand (`cohort`, `rct`, `meta`, `mechanistic`, `predictor ≠ lever`) is used as
   if the reader already internalized it.** It's powerful once you get it, but it's referenced
   ("Honesty rule #1", "the three honesty rules — defined up front in Start Here") without a one-line
   reminder. A reader dropped into chapter 27 or 40 first won't know what `mechanistic`/`outcome`
   mean. **Top fix: a single in-chapter footnote or first-use gloss of the three rules + the tier
   words.** This is the highest-leverage clarity fix across the whole slice.

Where these chapters SHINE (don't break it): the "Recognize → Act" table in 34, the ORBITA paragraph
in 38, the "we still can't explain consciousness" honesty in 39, and the "image a question, not a
body" line in 40 are genuinely gripping, plain-language writing. The recipe is: concrete stakes +
one vivid fact + plain verdict. More chapters should open that way.

## sections/27-derm-dental-ent-eye.md

- **SO-WHAT / BORED** §opening (lines 3–11) — "This is the **clinical-conditions** companion to
  Section 11… Section 11 owns the anti-aging/prevention framing; this section owns the named diseases."
  The chapter opens cold on corpus turf-mapping. → fix: open with the line that's already sitting at
  line 17 ("the interventions that work are unglamorous and cheap… the ones that sell are mostly
  theater") — that's the hook; demote the §11 boundary note to a small box.
- **GOOD** §intro (line 28) — "the best-evidenced moves in this entire section are **fluoride**…
  **the Epley maneuver** (a 60-second physical maneuver that *cures* the most common vertigo)…" — a
  fantastic up-front payoff list. This is exactly the right way to start; it tells me why to read on.
- **LOST** §A.1 (line 54) — "`meta` (NMA)" and the whole `meta`/`rct`/`cohort` column appear with no
  legend. A layperson doesn't know NMA = network meta-analysis or what the tier words rank. → fix:
  one-line key above the first table ("`rct` = randomized trial, the strongest tier; `cohort` =
  observational; NMA = network meta-analysis").
- **LOST** §A.1 (lines 47–49) — "disease of the **pilosebaceous unit**, driven by… (1) follicular
  hyperkeratinization (a plugged pore/comedo)…" — four mechanisms in dense clinical Latin before the
  payoff. The parentheticals help; "hyperkeratinization" still stops a lay reader. → fix: lead with
  "acne has four causes — a plugged pore, too much oil, a skin bacterium, and inflammation — and each
  treatment hits one of them," then name the technical terms.
- **GOOD** §A.6 (line 203) — "the active ingredients that work are **cheap, generic, and decades
  old**… buys **packaging, scent, and texture**, not better skin." Crisp, useful, memorable.
- **BORED** §B–§D — the chapter is long and every subsection repeats the same shape (mechanism table
  → "Honest take:"). By Part C/D I was skimming to the bold "Honest take" lines. That's actually a
  decent skim path, but the prose between tables is where momentum dies. → fix: trim the connective
  prose; let the tables + "Honest take" carry it.
- **GOOD** §C.3 (lines 374–385) — the BPPV/Epley explanation ("displaced otoconia (calcium-carbonate
  'ear rocks')… a ~60-second bedside procedure that resolves BPPV in a single session") is a model
  of making a mechanism vivid and motivating. Keep.
- **LOST** §C.5 (line 415) — "CPAP unambiguously fixes the symptoms" — CPAP is never expanded
  (continuous positive airway pressure). → fix: expand on first use.
