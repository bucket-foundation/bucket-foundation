# Cycle 1 — Layperson Review — Slice 9 (conflicts register, practitioner claim-checker, go-deeper library)

## PATTERNS

Across all three files the writing *can* hook me beautifully (the practitioner chapter and the go-deeper intro are some of the best reader-facing prose in the book) — but the same three things keep yanking me out of the experience:

1. **Backstage plumbing leaks onto the page.** Every table I read is studded with machine identifiers I have no use for as a reader: claim-id slugs (`omega3-index-predictor-not-proven-lever`, `conflict-resveratrol-sirtuin`), file paths (`02-domains/*-claims.json`, `05-labs/LABS.md`), tool names (`agf-yt`, `agf-yt-mine`), field names (`evidence_posture`, `conflicts_with`), tier codes (`_(rct-surrogate)_`, `_(epi-critique+absence-of-rct)_`), and "maintained by Nucleus / converges on re-runs" footers. Individually trivial; in aggregate they make a finished book read like a database export and they train me to skim past the last column of every table.
2. **Acronym pile-up with no expansion on first use.** CGM, RCT, apoB, LDL, Lp(a), NAD+, NMN/NR, mTOR, IGF-1, VO₂max, CRF, TRE, FMD, UPF, SENS, SASP, BAT, GH, PSG, CPET — most are never spelled out. A motivated non-scientist can google two or three, but the *density* forces skimming exactly where the content is most valuable.
3. **The reference docs open cold with version/provenance metadata** instead of one line of "why you should care." The two files that do nail a hook (practitioner intro, go-deeper §0) prove it's a five-second fix, not a rewrite.

**Top 3 fixes overall:**
1. Pull the machine identifiers (claim-id slugs, file paths, tool names, "converges on re-runs" footers) out of the reader-facing tables — move them to a backstage appendix, an endnote, or drop them. Keep the plain-English question/verdict that's already there.
2. Expand every acronym on first use per chapter, or drop a 6-line glossary box at the top of each (CGM, RCT, apoB, NAD+, VO₂max, mTOR cover most of the pain).
3. Give each register/reference doc a single plain-language hook sentence *above* the table, the way the practitioner chapter and go-deeper §0 already do.

## 06-evidence/CONFLICTS-REGISTER.md

- **SO-WHAT** §intro (top) — "A clean, navigable summary of every conflict object in `CONFLICTS.md` (parsed from the inline JSON mirrors). **`CONFLICTS.md` is the source of truth**…" → I open this and the first thing I'm told is about *another file* and JSON mirrors. Nothing tells me what a "conflict" is in human terms or why I'd read 29 of them. Fix: lead with one sentence — "These are the 29 fights scientists are still having about your health, where confident advice on both sides outruns the evidence — learn to spot them and you stop getting whiplash from headlines."
- **LOST** §intro — "a conflict stays `open` unless a `meta`-tier source resolves it" → "meta-tier" means nothing to me here; the tier ladder isn't on this page. Fix: gloss it inline ("a top-tier evidence review — the strongest kind") or link to the tier explainer.
- **BORED** §summary table — the 29-row table with two dense columns (Side A / Side B) each carrying italic codes like `_(animal-primate)_`, `_(rct-surrogate)_`, `_(invitro+animal-refutation)_` → I started skimming around row 5. The codes read like compiler output and the two long columns are a wall. Fix: replace the parenthetical codes with a single small "strength" badge per side (e.g. "mouse", "human trial", "no trial yet") in plain words.
- **GOOD** §summary table, Conflict column — "**Sleep duration causality** — does short sleep cause mortality or is it reverse causation?" and "**Blue Zones data quality** — genuine longevity hotspots or artifacts of bad age records?" → the bold-name-plus-plain-question format is genuinely excellent; I can scan the whole list of *questions* and know exactly what's contested. Keep this; it's the spine that survives the clutter.
- **GOOD** §status legend — "A status is never 'closed'… resolution is recorded, not deleted." → clear, principled, and it taught me how to read the whole table. Good payoff at the end.
- **SO-WHAT** §"How this maps to claims" — "59 of the 197 graded claims carry a `conflicts_with` field linking them to these objects." → pure internal wiring; as a reader I get nothing from it. Fix: cut, or rephrase as reader value ("about 1 in 3 of the book's strong claims touches one of these live disputes — so a third of what you'll read here is genuinely unsettled").

## sections/46-practitioner-claims-vs-evidence.md

- **GOOD** §opening (lines 7-11) — "Every other section grades the *science*. This one grades the **people who sell the science**… the actual interface to longevity research." → fantastic hook. I immediately know what this chapter is and why it's the one I personally need most. Best opening in the slice.
- **GOOD** §"three honesty rules" (predictor≠lever, cohort≠RCT, mechanism≠outcome) → crisp, memorable, and they reappear as the chapter's backbone. This is the kind of thing a reader actually takes away.
- **LOST/BORED** §"Method, and its honest limits" — "**~98 transcripts** were pulled with `agf-yt`, mined with `agf-yt-mine`… the regex miner is tuned for the Kruse biophysics corpus and produced mostly false-positive concept matches" → this is internal toolchain confession that means nothing to me; momentum died here right after the great opening. Fix: keep the honest *limits* (it's a sample, transcripts were rate-limited, one analyst's calls) in plain language; move the tool names and regex-miner detail to a footnote.
- **GOOD** §"The Calibration Spectrum" ranking table → naming names from most-aligned (Galpin) to least (Sinclair) with a one-line verdict each is gripping and exactly what a reader wants. "The most corpus-aligned voice in any wave" / "The anti-Sinclair" land hard. Keep.
- **LOST** §Calibration table + throughout — "every strength/creatine/protein/hydration claim maps to a `REAL`-graded entry; already cited by name (`galpin-hydration-heuristic`)" and the entire last column of every later table is claim-id slugs (`cold-norepinephrine-thermogenesis-mechanism`, `conflict-carbohydrate-insulin-model`) → these slugs are noise to me and they clutter otherwise readable tables. Fix: drop them from the body or footnote them.
- **LOST** §failure-mode tables — acronyms fire before definition: "CGM glucose 'spikes'" (CGM never expanded to *continuous glucose monitor*), apoB, NAD+, NMN/NR, SIRT1, Nrf2, GH, BAT, CRF, UPF ("UPF drives overconsumption" — never spelled out as ultra-processed food). Fix: expand on first use; CGM and UPF especially, since they anchor whole rows.
- **GOOD** §"The single most-laundered claim" — cold → +250% dopamine → "therefore cold plunging durably improves mood, focus, and longevity" → concrete, vivid, and the "acute response measured *in the water*, not a durable trait change" line is a genuine aha. Best single explanation in the slice.
- **GOOD** §"Practical takeaway — how to listen to a health podcast" (five questions) → this is the payoff the whole chapter earns; portable, usable without the book open. The meta-rule "the boring core is almost always right; the branded frontier is where evidence gets out over its skis" is a keeper.
- **SO-WHAT** §closing (lines 254-260) — "New first-class objects produced by this wave: `conflict-carbohydrate-insulin-model`…" and footer "Cross-check maintained by Nucleus… No new evidence added. Converges on re-runs." → backstage notes that add nothing for a reader and deflate a chapter that just ended on a high. Fix: cut or move to a colophon.

## sections/06-go-deeper-library.md

- **GOOD** §0 "one-paragraph orientation" → the single best on-ramp in the slice. "don't smoke; build and keep cardiorespiratory fitness… Almost everything that gets *sold*… is a **correlate dressed up as a scorecard**" tells me what to do AND what to ignore before I spend a dollar. If anything, surface this even higher.
- **GOOD** §intro framing — "Inclusion is not endorsement; it's a pointer with a tier attached." and the per-entry "honest one-line note: what it's good for AND its bias." → this is the whole reason to trust the list; it's reader-respecting and distinctive. Keep prominent.
- **LOST** §intro tag table — "People are tagged with their `evidence_posture` from `01-people/figures.json`" and inline codes like `mainstream-rigorous`, `clinical-translator`, `frontier-contested`, `fringe-to-canon`, `practitioner-n1` → the *table* explaining the tags is good, but the raw code-strings then appear inline for the rest of the chapter and read like database values. Fix: use the friendly labels (rigorous-scientist, translator, communicator, contested, practitioner) in the body and retire the underscore codes.
- **LOST** §2 paper tables — heavy acronym load with no glossary: apoB, LDL-C, Lp(a), CRF, VO₂max, TRE, CR, FMD, IGF-1, mTOR, SASP, BAT, HSP, PSG, CPET, HbA1c. Fix: a 6-line glossary box at the top of §2, or expand the top offenders on first use.
- **BORED** §2 paper tables — the inline DOIs ("[10.1016/j.cell.2013.05.039](https://doi.org/…)") make every row visually noisy; my eye bounces off the link strings. It's defensible (verify-it-yourself is the point), but consider a cleaner "DOI →" affordance so the *why-it-matters* column stays the star.
- **LOST** §throughout — references to internal files as if they were chapters: `01-people/figures.json`, `05-labs/LABS.md`, `02-domains/*-claims.json`, `00-map/CANON-BRIDGE-PROPOSAL.md`. As a book reader these are dead-ends — I can't open a `.json`. Fix: rephrase as "see the People chapter" / "see the Labs section," or drop.
- **GOOD** §5 "The open questions to watch" → framing these as "where the field will actually change" gives me a reason to care about disputes instead of feeling lost in them; the free-radical-theory entry as "a template for healthy skepticism" is a lovely touch.
- **GOOD** §7 "Beginner → Advanced reading paths" → the (B) entry → (I) synthesis → (A) primary source structure is exactly what a motivated layperson wants and almost nobody provides. "Exercise & fitness (highest ROI — start here)" signposting is perfect. Keep and maybe promote it nearer the top.
- **GOOD** §1 honest caveats — the Matthew Walker entry ("⚠️ Read with the errata in hand… the famous 'short sleep = short life' is actually U-shaped") and the Blue Zones entry (pension fraud) model exactly the skepticism the book is teaching. These caveats are the best part of the library.
