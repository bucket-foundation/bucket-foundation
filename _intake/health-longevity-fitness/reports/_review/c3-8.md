# Cycle 3 — findability + length — slice 8

## PATTERNS

- **The "one-line verdict up front" box is the single best findability device in the slice — and it's applied
  unevenly.** `09-exposures` and `33-public-health` both open with a bolded **"The one-line verdict up front:"**
  box that hands a busy reader the whole answer in 3–4 lines. It works. The three chapters that *lack* it
  (`19-life-stages`, `04-individual-variation`, `29-behavior-change`) all already have a payoff summary table
  buried at the end (§10, §6, and §7 respectively) — so the fix is cheap: lift a 2–3 line BLUF to the top and
  point at the existing table. Biggest findability win in the slice.

- **Myth/verdict content is stated 2–3 times per chapter (prose verdict → end-of-chapter table → dedicated
  debunk section).** Worst offender is `29-behavior-change`: ego depletion, the 21-day myth, and "biohack your
  discipline" are each explained in §2/§3 prose, listed again in the §7 "Drop these" table, *and* re-explained
  in §8. `19` and `09` have milder versions (opener line echoed verbatim in a later "putting it in proportion"
  section). This is the largest pure-redundancy pool in the slice — roughly **500–700 words** cuttable across
  the six files without touching a single grade, number, or citation (the tables and footnotes keep them).

- **Openers that re-state themselves.** Several chapters say the thesis in the hook, then say it again in §1
  (`19` repeats "the arc you spend your adult life defending was partly drawn before you drew your first breath"
  and "the peak you build is the asset you spend" essentially verbatim; `33` previews its 4-point personal
  hierarchy in the intro then reprints it nearly word-for-word in §8.4). Keep the hook, thin the echo.

- **Tables mostly earn their place.** The big grading tables (`29` §7, `19` §10, `33` §3.4/§4.4, `04` §6,
  `WHAT-TO-TRACK` A/B/C) are the skim layer and should stay — they're the reason a practitioner can use these
  chapters. Two need a one-line "how to read / skim this" signpost; none need columns cut. `WHAT-TO-TRACK` is
  the cleanest file in the slice and the model the others' tables aspire to.

- **Best chapters (findability):** `33-public-health` and `09-exposures` — BLUF box + skim signposts done
  right. **Weakest:** `29-behavior-change` (longest file, most triple-stated content, no top BLUF box despite a
  strong one-liner) and `19-life-stages` (self-repeating opener, verdict lives only at the very end).

### Note on preservation
No cut above removes or weakens a graded claim, number/effect-size, evidence tier, citation/DOI,
caveat, cross-reference, `@@FIG:@@` marker, or `[^footnote]`. All redundancy cuts collapse *repeated prose*
whose grades/numbers survive in the end-of-chapter tables and footnote blocks, which are left intact. Rough
total cuttable: **≈900–1,100 words** across the slice (largest single pool: `29-behavior-change` §8 debunks
+ verdict/table overlap, ~350–400 words; `33` intro/§8.4 hierarchy duplicate, ~120; `19` opener/reassurance
echoes, ~100).
