## 06-go-deeper-library §2.3 (Exercise) — Mandsager CRF mortality gap
- claim: "~5× mortality gap elite-vs-low fitness; **no upper limit**" | cited: Mandsager et al., JAMA Netw Open 2018, 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.3605
- **CONFIRMED** — 122,007 patients on treadmill testing; elite vs low fitness adjusted HR 0.20 (95% CI 0.16–0.24) = ~5× gap; paper explicitly reports "no observed upper limit of benefit." Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2707428

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.3 (Exercise) — Kodama per-MET mortality
- claim: "~13% lower mortality per MET" | cited: Kodama et al., JAMA 2009, 10.1001/jama.2009.681
- **CONFIRMED** — a 1-MET higher fitness level associated with 13% decrease in all-cause mortality and 15% decrease in CHD/CVD. Exact match. Source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/05/090519172153.htm (per JAMA 2009;301(19):2024)

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.3 (Exercise) — Momma muscle-strengthening mortality
- claim: "~10–17% lower mortality; **J-shape (more ≠ better)**" | cited: Momma et al., BJSM 2022, 10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061
- **CONFIRMED** — 10–17% lower risk of all-cause mortality/CVD/cancer/diabetes; J-shaped, max risk reduction at ~30–60 min/week. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35228201/

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.10 (Wearables) — Paluch steps plateau
- claim: "benefit plateaus ~6–8k, not 10k" | cited: Paluch et al., Lancet Public Health 2022, 10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9
- **CONFIRMED** — 15-cohort meta (~47k adults); mortality risk plateaued at ~6,000–8,000 steps/day for adults ≥60 and ~8,000–10,000 for those <60. The "~6–8k" figure is the older-adult plateau; direction and headline (below 10k) correct. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247352/

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.9 (Psychosocial) — Holt-Lunstad social relationships
- claim: "Effect size rivals smoking" | cited: Holt-Lunstad et al., PLoS Med 2010, 10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316
- **CONFIRMED** — 148 studies, 308,849 participants; OR 1.50 (95% CI 1.42–1.59) for survival with stronger social ties; paper states influence comparable to established risk factors including smoking. Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.4 (Nutrition) — Liu TRE vs CR
- claim: "TRE adds ~nothing beyond the CR it causes" | cited: Liu et al., NEJM 2022, 10.1056/NEJMoa2114833
- **CONFIRMED** — 139 obese patients, 12 months; TRE+CR lost 8.0 kg vs CR alone, between-group difference 1.8 kg, not significant. "Not more beneficial... than daily calorie restriction." Source: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833

## 06-go-deeper-library §2.6 (Sleep) — Cappuccio U-shape
- claim: "The honest shape: ~7h nadir, long *and* short worse" | cited: Cappuccio et al., Sleep 2010, 10.1093/sleep/33.5.585
- **CONFIRMED** — meta of prospective studies; U-shaped, both short (RR 1.12) and long sleep elevate all-cause mortality. Source: https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/med/staff/cappuccio/publications/sleep_2010_mortality_meta_proofs.pdf

## 46-practitioner §"single most-laundered claim" — Cold catecholamines (Šrámek)
- claim: "+250% dopamine, +530% NE (noradrenaline) acutely (Šrámek 2000)" | cited: Šrámek et al., Eur J Appl Physiol 2000
- **CONFIRMED** — 14°C immersion raised plasma dopamine ~250% and noradrenaline ~530%. Numbers and temperature match. The manual's framing (acute neurochemistry, not a durable outcome) is correct. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10751106/ (via researchgate/semanticscholar copies)

## 46-practitioner §"Where practitioners are RIGHT" — Hall UPF RCT
- claim: "UPF drives overconsumption (Hall 2019 inpatient RCT, +~500 kcal/day at matched macros)" | cited: Hall et al., Cell Metab 2019
- **CONFIRMED** — 20-adult crossover inpatient RCT; ultra-processed arm ate 508 ± 106 kcal/day more at matched calories/macros/sugar/sodium/fiber; gained 0.9 kg. Source: https://www.cell.com/cell-metabolism/fulltext/S1550-4131(19)30248-7

## CONFLICTS-REGISTER §#31 (TM & allegiance bias) — TM CHD RCT
- claim: "one CHD RCT ~48% fewer events" | cited: (Schneider 2012, TM secondary-prevention RCT)
- **CONFIRMED** — Schneider et al., Circ Cardiovasc Qual Outcomes 2012; 201 Black patients with CHD; 48% risk reduction in composite death/MI/stroke, HR 0.52 (95% CI 0.29–0.92), mean 5.4 yr follow-up. Source: https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/abs/10.1161/circoutcomes.112.967406

## CONFLICTS-REGISTER §#27 (Foam rolling) — pre-roll effect sizes
- claim: "Pre-rolling: small sprint (+0.7%) & flexibility (+4.0%) gains (review)" | cited: (Wiewelhove et al., meta-analysis)
- **CONFIRMED** — Wiewelhove et al., Front Physiol 2019; pre-rolling +0.7% sprint (g=0.28) and +4.0% flexibility (g=0.34); jump/strength negligible. Exact match. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6465761/

## 46-practitioner §"failure mode 2" / go-deeper Sinclair — NAD+ precursor rise
- claim: "NMN/NR ... raises NAD+ ~40–60%" | cited: (conflict-nad-precursor-efficacy; nad-precursor-nr-human-surrogate)
- **CONFIRMED** (with nuance) — human RCTs show dose-dependent whole-blood NAD+ rises: ~22% (100mg), ~51% (300mg), ~142% (1000mg) NR; NRPT ~40%; NMN/NR commonly ~double NAD+ at higher doses. The "~40–60%" is accurate for moderate doses; high doses go well above. The manual's load-bearing point (only the surrogate moves, no hard-endpoint RCT) is correct. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701244/
