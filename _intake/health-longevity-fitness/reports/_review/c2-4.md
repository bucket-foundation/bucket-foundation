# Cycle 2 line-edit — slice 4 (25, 26, 08, 20, 35, 21)

## PATTERNS

- **"genuine / genuinely" is the single biggest tell across all six chapters.** House style
  bans "genuinely/truly/really" as intensifiers, and this slice leans on "genuine(ly)" as a
  crutch — often 8–12 times per chapter (e.g. "genuine triumph," "genuine advance," "genuine
  lever," "genuinely hard," "genuinely lifesaving," "genuine mechanistic departure," "genuine
  irony"). It's doing emphasis work the surrounding evidence already does. **Fix: delete it
  ~80% of the time** (the sentence is stronger without it) and swap the rest for a concrete
  word ("proven," "RCT-backed," "real"). This is the highest-priority sweep — it's mechanical
  and it recurs everywhere.
- **"actually" as filler.** "how well we can actually treat it," "what you actually *do*,"
  "where the lifespan gains actually came from," "what actually treats it," "exercise is the
  drug." Most instances can be cut with zero loss. Keep the few where it marks a real contrast
  with expectation (e.g. "not what intuition says").
- **Antithesis comma-splices are pervasive and are a house-style violation.** The book's honest
  voice loves "X, not Y" ("control and time, not cure"; "treat the mutation, not the address";
  "cancer is not a thing, it is a process"; "keep them alive, not conditioning all help on
  abstinence"). The style is load-bearing to the book's rhetoric, so I flag the most mechanical
  ones to convert to plain positive statements and leave the ones that carry real argumentative
  weight — but a reviewer should decide the global policy, because there are dozens. Listed
  representative cases per chapter below.
- **"real / really" doubling.** "real, RCT-supported lever," "real but rare," "real and
  measurable," "a real risk." "Real" is fine once; it's stacked so often it goes invisible.
  Vary it.
- **Good news on the banned-word list:** no "delve," "leverage," "seamless," "robust,"
  "it's worth noting," "at the end of the day." "Boost" appears only inside quoted debunks
  ("boost your immunity") — legitimate, leave it. So the cleanup is concentrated on
  genuine(ly)/actually/antithesis.
- **Rhythm:** these are dense, well-built chapters. The main rhythm issue is occasional
  paragraph-length run-ons in the chapter openers (25, 08) and inside the long "honest status"
  blockquotes. Flagged per chapter.

**Top overall fixes:** (1) global "genuine(ly)" sweep; (2) global "actually" trim; (3) decide
antithesis policy and convert the mechanical comma-splices; (4) break the two opener run-ons in
08 and 25.

## sections/25-oncology.md

- **[ANTITHESIS]** §opening — "Cancer is not a thing, it is a *process* — the corruption of the
  cell's own software." → "Cancer is a *process* — the corruption of the cell's own software."
  The "not a thing, it is" scaffold is the banned construction; the positive statement is
  punchier and the em-dash already does the work.
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §opening — "the honest biology of what it *is* — and of how well we can
  actually treat it — is rarely told straight." → drop "actually": "…and of how well we can
  treat it — is rarely told straight."
- **[ANTITHESIS/RHYTHM]** §25.1.1 — "It is not a foreign invader, not an imbalance of 'energy,'
  not a fungus — it is **your own cells running a corrupted copy of their own code.**" The
  triple-negation-then-positive is a lot of runway. Consider tightening: "It is **your own
  cells running a corrupted copy of their own code** — not a foreign invader, not an energy
  imbalance, not a fungus." (Leads with the positive; the negations become a crisp tail.) This
  one earns its contrast, so keep the contrast but front-load the truth.
- **[GOOD]** §25.1.1 — "A healthy multicellular body is a negotiated truce" and "Cancer is the
  **collapse of that truce**" — protect. Exactly the dry, vivid voice the book wants.
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §25.4.3 blockquote — "This is one of medicine's genuine triumphs and the
  counterweight to the alternative-oncology trope…" → "This is one of medicine's real triumphs…"
  or just "one of medicine's triumphs" (the survival numbers already prove it).
- **[FLAB]** §25.5 — "This is real and transformative — and the honest version names **who it
  helps and who it doesn't**." "real and transformative" is a stacked-adjective throat-clear;
  "This is transformative — and the honest version names who it helps and who it doesn't" is
  tighter.
- **[ANTITHESIS]** §25.5.4 — "**treat the mutation, not the address.**" Deliberate slogan and it
  lands; flag for the antithesis policy call but I'd keep it — it's earned wordplay, not a lazy
  comma-splice.
- **[GOOD]** §25.5.2 blockquote — "The marketing says 'cancer cured by the immune system'; the
  data say 'a real, sometimes durable cure for a substantial minority, nothing for the majority,
  and a new class of harms.'" — protect. Best sentence in the chapter.
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §25.6 — "The promise is enormous" then later "The promise is enormous — a
  single blood test…" appears twice in close range (bottom-line lead + §25.6 blockquote). Vary
  the second ("The upside is real —") to avoid the echo.
- **[FLAB]** §25.7 sugar row — "far subtler than 'sugar = fuel.'" fine; but "Oversimplified to
  the point of false" is doing good work — **[GOOD]**, keep.

## sections/26-infectious-disease.md

- **[ANTITHESIS]** §opening — "The single largest improvement in human lifespan ever recorded was
  not a drug or a diet; it was the conquest of infectious disease…" Semicolon softens it, but
  it's still the negate-then-assert shape. Optional positive rewrite: "The single largest
  improvement in human lifespan ever recorded came from the conquest of infectious disease
  through sanitation, clean water, vaccines, and antibiotics." Drops the "not a drug or a diet"
  entirely and reads cleaner.
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §1.1 — "demanding antibiotics for a cold or flu is not just useless, it is
  actively harmful." "not just X, it is Y" is the banned "not just… but" family. → "demanding
  antibiotics for a cold or flu is actively harmful, not merely useless" reads worse; better:
  "demanding antibiotics for a cold or flu is worse than useless — it's actively harmful."
- **[GOOD]** §2.2 blockquote — "the highest-leverage health interventions in human history were
  **boring, collective, and preventive** — a sewer, a clean well, a hand-washing protocol, a
  vaccine schedule." Protect. The concrete list is the voice at its best. (Note: "highest-
  leverage" uses "leverage" as a noun-adjective — house style bans "leverage" as a verb; the
  compound adjective "high-leverage" recurs all over the corpus as a term of art, so I'd leave
  it, but flag that a strict reader might object.)
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §4.4 — "monitored continuously through systems like VAERS/VSD … and
  post-marketing surveillance across hundreds of millions of doses. Honesty requires stating
  both halves:" — the line break mid-sentence ("across hundreds of millions of doses. Honesty
  requires…") is fine, but "Honesty requires stating both halves" is a throat-clear that repeats
  a move the chapter makes a dozen times. Consider cutting straight to the bullets.
- **[FLAB]** §5.2 HCV row — "a genuine medical miracle that arrived quietly." → "a medical
  miracle that arrived quietly." ("genuine" adds nothing to "miracle.")
- **[FLAB]** §5.1 RSV row — "are a genuine advance." → "are a real advance" or just "are an
  advance." Same for §6.2 "a genuine scientific triumph" → "a scientific triumph."
- **[RHYTHM]** §5.4 — "It is staggeringly common and under-recognized: a Global Burden of Disease
  analysis … estimated **~48.9 million cases and 11 million deaths in 2017 — roughly 1 in 5
  deaths worldwide.**" Strong. But the following sentence ("It can follow *any* infection …, it
  is a medical emergency where **every hour to antibiotics matters**, and the public barely knows
  the word.") is a three-clause pile-up. Break after "abdominal)": "It's a medical emergency —
  every hour to antibiotics matters — and the public barely knows the word."
- **[GOOD]** §8 close — "it was beaten not by a heroic cure but by a sewer, a clean well, a bar
  of soap, and a vaccine schedule." Protect (the "not by X but by Y" is technically the banned
  shape, but this is the chapter's thesis sentence and the list makes it sing — I'd keep it; flag
  for the policy call).

  amyloid antibodies) carry far weaker benefit-to-risk than their visibility implies." One
  60-word sentence carrying the whole thesis. Split at "unmonetizable": "…are mostly unsexy and
  unmonetizable. Meanwhile the marketed interventions — nootropics, brain-training apps, the new
  amyloid antibodies — carry far weaker benefit-to-risk than their visibility implies."
- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §1.2 — "**45% is a population counterfactual, not your personal odds.**" Good
  and keep — but the parenthetical right after, "(Honesty rule #1: predictor ≠ lever — forecasting

  already in the chapter. Trim to "(Honesty rule #1: predictor ≠ lever.)"
- **[FLAB]** §5.2 — "exercise for depression is a real, RCT-supported lever with a benefit profile
  most drugs would envy." → "a benefit profile most drugs would envy" is **[GOOD]** voice; keep,

- **[FLAB]** §6.1 — "the most genuinely exciting *new* lead." → "the most exciting *new* lead."

- **[DEADWEIGHT]** §6.2 — "represent decades of the amyloid hypothesis finally 'working'." The

- **[GOOD]** §6.3 heading — "Brain-training games: practice the task, not the brain." Antithesis,
