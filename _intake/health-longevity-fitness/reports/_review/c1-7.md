# Cycle 1 — Layperson Review — Slice 7 (Training / Modalities / Sports / Nutrition / Fasting / Recovery)

Reviewer persona: smart, motivated, no medical training. Reading these as a reader who wants
to live better and will quit if bored, lost, or never told why a section matters.

## PATTERNS (cross-chapter — read these first)

**P1 — Internal machine-scaffolding leaks onto the page and breaks immersion in EVERY chapter.**
The body text is studded with things written for the system, not the reader: claim-id codes in
backticks (`crf-vo2max-strongest-mortality-predictor`, `conflict-zone2-optimal-mito`), raw DOI
strings mid-sentence (`10.3390/nu15092116`), internal file paths (`02-domains/D2-supplements-claims.json`,
`mobility/INVENTORY.md`), version stamps ("v1.0 — 2026-06-29"), and a "Section maintained by Nucleus"
footer. To a layperson these read as intimidating noise that says "this wasn't written for you." It's
the single most consistent momentum-killer across all six files.
→ **Top fix #1:** strip claim-ids, DOIs, and file paths out of the running prose into superscript
footnotes / an endnote list. Keep the author names and years inline (those read fine); banish the codes.

**P2 — Acronyms and jargon used before they're defined (recurs in every chapter).**
Examples located below per-chapter: CARs, MET, MEV/MAV/MRV (02); "osteogenic" (44/45); mTOR, IGF-1,
isocaloric (03/36); zeitgeber (05); CTE used before expansion (45). A motivated layperson can follow a
real argument but stalls on an undefined term thrown in passing.
→ **Top fix #2:** expand every term on first use, or give each chapter a 4–6 line "terms in this chapter" box.

**P3 — Catalog fatigue: long runs of identical-format cards with no payoff up front.**
Sections 44 and 45 (and to a lesser extent 03 and 36) are 15–20+ repetitions of the same card
(Trains / Benefits / Risk / Learning curve / Equipment / Best for / Honest evidence). The first three
are interesting; by the tenth I'm skimming. The good synthesis (44 §11 "adherence beats optimality",
45 §7 "play reframe") is buried at the *end*, after the wall.
→ **Top fix #3:** front-load each catalog with a "if you only read one thing / the 3 that matter for you"
box so the reader banks the payoff before the reference grind.

**Repeated boilerplate:** every chapter opens with the identical italic line *"Graded per the manual's
evidence tiers; the three honesty rules — predictor ≠ lever, cohort ≠ RCT, something beats nothing — are
defined up front in 'Start Here.'"* By the third chapter it's wallpaper a reader's eye slides off. Vary it,
shorten it, or move it to a tiny margin tag.

## sections/02-training.md

- **[GOOD]** §2 the ladder framing — "You do not start at the barbell — you start where you can own the
  movement with clean form, and you climb." → This is the most motivating idea in the chapter; it tells a
  nervous beginner there's a door for them. Keep it, and consider promoting it to the chapter intro.
- **[GOOD]** §2.1 cue — "knees track over toes (let them go past — that's normal and healthy)" → myth-busting
  woven into instruction feels like a knowledgeable coach. More of this.
- **[LOST]** §1 table — "~13% lower all-cause mortality per 1-MET" → "MET" is never defined anywhere in the
  chapter; a layperson has no idea what a MET is, and it's load-bearing for the headline strength-of-evidence
  claim. → fix: define MET in one clause on first use ("a MET = the energy you burn at rest; +1 MET ≈ a
  meaningful jump in fitness").
- **[LOST]** §1 — "a trial proved CARs extend life" → "CARs" appears here with no expansion; it isn't spelled
  out until the §4.1 table ("Shoulder CARs") and even there the words "controlled articular rotations" never
  appear. → fix: expand CARs (controlled articular rotations) on first use in §1.
- **[LOST]** §6.2 — "(Israetel's MEV→MAV→MRV 'volume landmark' framing)" and later "near MRV" → MEV/MAV/MRV
  are used as if known; only MRV is loosely glossed ("ceiling past which you accumulate fatigue") much later
  in §8. → fix: spell out the three (minimum effective / maximum adaptive / maximum recoverable volume) at
  first mention.
- **[LOST]** §2 intro — "with good technique and 1–3 reps in reserve" → "reps in reserve" is used here but
  RIR/the concept isn't defined until §6.2. A beginner doesn't know "how many reps you could still do" is the
  meaning. → fix: gloss it in one phrase at first use.
- **[BORED]** §6.4 the three sample weeks — "Goblet squat 3×8 · RDL (light) 3×8 · Incline push-up 3×8–12 ·
  Band/inverted row 3×8–12 · Suitcase carry 3×30 m · 10-min mobility" → reads like dense code; the run of
  abbreviations across three programs is where I started skimming. → fix: keep the tables but add a one-line
  plain-English caption per program ("Three full-body days; everything you've met above").
- **[SO-WHAT]** §3 Cardio opens straight into "Two qualities matter and they are trained differently" with no
  restatement of why cardio is the highest-leverage capacity (that lived in §1's table). → fix: a one-sentence
  stakes line ("This is the engine that the strongest longevity number in the book rests on — here's how to
  build it").
