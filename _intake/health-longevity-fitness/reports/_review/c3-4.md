# Cycle 3 — Slice 4 findings (findability + length-cut)

## PATTERNS

**Cross-chapter patterns (biggest wins first):**

1. **The redundant "three honesty rules" preamble is re-explained in full in every chapter.** 08, 20, 21,
   25, 26, 35 each re-derive "predictor ≠ lever / cohort ≠ RCT / something-beats-nothing" with parenthetical
   glosses, sometimes twice in one chapter (35 opens with them in prose *and* has a §0 table restating the
   same). Across the slice these wind-ups cost ~250–350 words that a one-line cross-reference to a single
   canonical definition would carry. Biggest single length lever in the slice. **Tighten, don't delete** —
   the rules are load-bearing; the *re-teaching* is what's cuttable.

2. **Chapter openers bury the payoff behind an essay.** 08, 20, 25.1, 26, 21.1 all open with 1–2 paragraphs
   of throat-clearing before the reader hits anything actionable. Several chapters *already have* a perfect
   BLUF lower down (a "§N summary" table or "map at a glance") — the fix is usually "hoist a 2-line pointer,"
   not "write new content."

3. **The "honest summary" tables/lists are the best feature in the slice — and they make some upstream
   prose redundant.** 20 §10, 35 §9, 21 §8, 08 §7 each restate section conclusions the reader already read.
   These are GOOD as findability anchors; flag them as the place to point a BLUF to, and cut the *duplicated*
   sentences upstream where they merely pre-echo the summary.

4. **"Go deeper" annotations repeat the in-text grading verbatim.** Every chapter's reading list re-states
   tiers and caveats already given in the body (e.g. 08 item 2 ACHIEVE "primary endpoint null / subgroup
   48%," 35 item 3 Sordo "halves death"). Low-value duplication, but these carry the DOIs — **tighten the
   prose, never cut the citation.**

**Best chapters (need least work):** 21 (pain) — verdict-first one-screen headers in §6 are the model the
others should copy; 26 (infectious) — "Read this framing first" is a clean BLUF. **Worst (most cuttable):**
35 (addiction) — longest, most repetition between §1.2, §3.4, §7.1, §8, §9; and 25 (oncology) — dense
hallmarks/therapy prose with several 3-deep example piles.

Total rough cuttable: **~1,900–2,300 words** across the slice, zero graded claims/numbers/citations lost.
