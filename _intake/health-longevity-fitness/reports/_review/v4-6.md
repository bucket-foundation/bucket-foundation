## 41-pathology-lab-medicine §C.1 (MCED / Galleri)
- claim: "The PATHFINDER prospective study … showed … a positive predictive value around 38–43% … and no demonstrated mortality benefit yet" | cited: Schrag et al., *Lancet* 2023, DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01700-2
- **CONFIRMED** — PATHFINDER (Schrag, Lancet 2023) reported PPV 43.1% (95% CI 30.8–55.9) for the refined assay and 38% for the earlier version; specificity 99.5%, CSO accuracy 88%. No mortality-benefit claim (prospective cohort, not a mortality RCT). The 38–43% range and "no mortality benefit yet" are accurate. https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(23)01700-2/abstract

## 41-pathology-lab-medicine §B.5 (PSA / ERSPC)
- claim: "Population PSA screening does reduce prostate-cancer-specific mortality modestly … ERSPC, Schröder et al., NEJM 2009: ~20% relative reduction, but a large number-needed-to-screen and number-needed-to-treat" | cited: Schröder et al., NEJM 2009, PMID 19297566
- **CONFIRMED** — ERSPC 2009 (NEJM 360:1320) reported a 20% relative reduction in prostate-cancer death among men offered PSA screening, with a large NNS/NNT. Direction, magnitude, endpoint all correct. https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0810084

## 41-pathology-lab-medicine §A.4 (Bayes worked example)
- claim: "99% sensitive, 99% specific … disease present in 1 in 1,000 … of 1,098 positive results, only 99 are real. PPV ≈ 9%"
- **CONFIRMED (arithmetic)** — 100 true cases → 99 TP; 99,900 well → 999 FP; 99/(99+999)=9.0%. Internally correct; no external source needed.

## 28-pharmacology-full §B.1.3 (opioids / SPACE)
- claim: "the SPACE trial (Krebs et al., 2018) found opioids no better (slightly worse) than non-opioid therapy for chronic back/osteoarthritis pain over 12 months" | cited: Krebs EE et al., *JAMA* 2018, doi:10.1001/jama.2018.0899
- **CONFIRMED** — SPACE (JAMA 2018; n=240 veterans) found no significant difference in pain-related function over 12 months (BPI 3.4 opioid vs 3.3 non-opioid), pain intensity slightly favored non-opioids, and more adverse events with opioids. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2673971

## 28-pharmacology-full §F.1 (bioequivalence)
- claim: "the FDA standard requires the 90% confidence interval of the generic/brand ratio to sit within 80–125%"
- **CONFIRMED** — This is the standard FDA/regulatory average-bioequivalence rule (90% CI of the geometric-mean ratio for Cmax and AUC within 80.00–125.00%). Correct as stated.

## 28-pharmacology-full §E (placebo / SAMSON nocebo, cross-ref §10.2.3)
- claim: "SAMSON … ~90% of 'statin side effects' occurred on placebo months too" | cited: Wood 2020, NEJM, doi:10.1056/NEJMc2031173
- **CONFIRMED** — SAMSON (NEJM 2020; n=60 n-of-1) mean symptom intensity 8.0 no-tablet / 15.4 placebo / 16.3 statin; nocebo ratio (15.4−8.0)/(16.3−8.0)=0.89 ≈ 90% of the symptom burden reproduced on placebo; statin vs placebo not significant (P=0.39). https://www.ccjm.org/page/aha-2020/noceba-statins

## 10-medical-pharmacology §3 (SPRINT)
- claim: "SPRINT … intensive arm cut major cardiovascular events by ~25% and all-cause mortality by ~27%" | cited: SPRINT 2015, NEJM, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1511939
- **CONFIRMED** — SPRINT (NEJM 2015; n=9,361): primary CV composite HR 0.75 (25% reduction), all-cause mortality HR 0.73 (27% reduction). Correct. (A U-Utah press note rounds the CV composite to 24%; the NEJM primary result is 25%.) https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901281

## 10-medical-pharmacology §4 (ASPREE)

## 30-complementary-medicine §2 (Vickers acupuncture IPD meta)
- claim: "Vickers individual-patient-data meta-analysis (Arch Intern Med 2012; updated J Pain 2018) … 39 high-quality RCTs / ~20,827 patients … beats no-acupuncture by ~0.5 SD … beats sham by only ~0.2 SD … persist at 12 months with ~15% decay" | cited: doi:10.1001/archinternmed.2012.3654; doi:10.1016/j.jpain.2017.11.005
- **CONFIRMED** — The 39-trial / 20,827-patient figures are the 2018 J Pain update (the 2012 original was 29 trials / 17,922 patients — the manual cites both and correctly reports the updated counts). Effect sizes: vs no-acupuncture SMDs ~0.4–0.6 SD (OA 0.57, back/neck 0.51, headache ~0.4) → "~0.5 SD"; vs sham small (~0.15–0.2 SD); benefit largely retained at 12 months (~15% decay). All accurate. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3658605/

## 30-complementary-medicine §3 (peppermint oil / IBS)
- claim: "pooled RCTs show a risk ratio ~2.4 for global symptom improvement" for enteric-coated peppermint oil in IBS
- **CONFIRMED** — Meta-analyses report RR ~2.2–2.4 for global IBS symptom improvement vs placebo (Khanna 2014 RR 2.23; later pooled RR 2.39). "~2.4" is within the reported range. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24100754/

## 30-complementary-medicine §4 (Ayurvedic heavy metals)
- claim: "Saper et al. (JAMA 2008) … ~1 in 5 (US- and Indian-made) contained detectable lead, mercury, or arsenic … rasa shastra products worse (~40%)" | cited: doi:10.1001/jama.2008.629
- **CONFIRMED** — Saper 2008 (JAMA 300:915): 21% of ~193 internet-purchased Ayurvedic products contained detectable lead/mercury/arsenic; 40% of rasa shastra vs 17% of non–rasa shastra. Exact. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18728265/

## 30-complementary-medicine §3 (ginkgo / GEM)
- claim: "the large GEM (Ginkgo Evaluation of Memory) RCT (>3,000 elderly, ~6 years) found no reduction in dementia, Alzheimer's, or cognitive decline"
- **CONFIRMED** — GEM (DeKosky, JAMA 2008; n=3,069, age ≥75, median ~6.1 yr follow-up, EGb761 120 mg BID): no reduction in all-cause dementia or Alzheimer's. Correct. https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/the-ginkgo-evaluation-of-memory-gem-study

## 30-complementary-medicine §9.3 (CAM-instead-of-care mortality)
- claim: "Johnson et al. (JNCI 2018) … 5-year survival 55% vs 78% … HR 2.50 overall and HR 5.68 for breast cancer" | cited: doi:10.1093/jnci/djx145
- **CONFIRMED** — Johnson 2018 (JNCI 110:121; n=281 alternative-only vs matched conventional): 5-yr OS 55.3% vs 78.3%; HR death 2.50 overall; breast-cancer subgroup HR 5.68. Exact. https://academic.oup.com/jnci/article/110/1/121/4064136

## 32-biohacking-fringe §3 (HBOT telomeres)
- claim: "Hachmo et al. 2020: 35 healthy older adults, 60 sessions of HBOT, reported increased telomere length (~20%) and decreased senescent cells" | cited: doi:10.18632/aging.202188
- **CONFIRMED** — Hachmo 2020 (Aging 12:22445; n=35, age ≥64, 60 daily sessions): telomere length of T-helper/T-cytotoxic/NK/B cells increased >20%; senescent cells declined 10–37%. Single-arm, no control (the manual flags this). Exact. https://www.aging-us.com/article/202188/text

## 32-biohacking-fringe §6.2 (chelation / TACT)
- claim: "TACT (Lamas et al., 2013): a marginal reduction in a composite endpoint … statistically fragile … subgroup-driven (diabetics) … never independently replicated" | cited: doi:10.1001/jama.2013.2107
- **CONFIRMED (as characterized)** — TACT (JAMA 2013) reported a marginal primary-composite reduction (HR 0.82) with the benefit concentrated in the diabetic subgroup; widely regarded as fragile/unreplicated and not guideline-endorsed. The manual's cautionary framing matches the literature.
