## PATTERNS (cross-chapter)

**P1 — "The goal is not X, it is Y" antithesis for the ROS-signal inversion (highest priority).** The
book's central correction — stop chasing low ROS, keep the signal crisp — is stated several times as a
comma/semicolon-spliced antithesis. `01-foundations.md` §2.6: "the goal is not to minimize ROS; it is
to keep the signaling crisp and the damage repaired." Convert to a plain positive: "the goal is to
keep the signaling crisp and the damage repaired — minimizing ROS is the wrong target." The
`37-mitochondrial-health.md` §37.5 version already splits it into two separate sentences ("The goal is
**not** to minimize ROS. It is to keep…"), which is the compliant form — so make foundations match
that, or go fully positive. This is the single most repeated house-style snag in the slice.

**P2 — "Strength, not muscle mass, predicts mortality" antithesis, repeated across chapters.** Same
comma-spliced `X, not Y` in `00-start-here.md` ("*Strength*, not muscle size, predicts survival") and
`01-STATE-OF-THE-FIELD.md` ("Strength (and grip), *not muscle mass*, independently predicts
mortality"). Recast both positively and identically, e.g. "Strength (and grip) predicts mortality
independently of muscle mass." Fix them the same way so the recurring claim reads consistently.

**P3 — Banned intensifier "genuinely" (+ "really") appears repeatedly.** `00-atlas.md` "What
genuinely remains thin"; `37` "genuinely real and replicated," "a genuinely elegant targeting trick";
`37` §37.2.1 "'More mitochondria' is really *more cristae membrane*." Kill every adverbial
"genuinely"/"really" — the sentences are stronger without them. (Adjectival "a genuine tradeoff" is
fine; the adverb is the problem.)

**P4 — AI-tell "robust" used ~5× as filler.** `01-foundations.md` §4.3 "the robust way to raise
antioxidant capacity"; `37` §37.5.2 "The robust way to raise antioxidant capacity"; `12` §12.3.8
"That is robust **mechanism**"; `37` §37.6.1 "a robust *phenomenon*." Swap for a concrete word:
reliable / consistent / well-established / solid. Also watch "leverage" as a noun ("where the leverage
actually is," `37` §37.1/§37.4) — defensible as mechanical-advantage, but it reads as the AI tell;
"where the practical power is" is cleaner.

**P5 — The good motifs are deliberately repeated — protect them, fix only the antithesis inside one.**
"Being alive is a verb, not a noun" (foundations, twice) and "You are a colony" (foundations + `37`)
are intentional callbacks and should stay. The only edit: the ", not a noun" tail is a house-style
antithesis *and* redundant with the sentence that follows it, so drop it — "Being alive is a verb."
lands harder and the process explanation right after already carries the contrast.

**Top overall fixes:** (1) de-antithesis the ROS-goal line in foundations to match the clean 37
version; (2) unify the two "strength not mass" lines as positive statements; (3) delete every
adverbial "genuinely"/"really" and replace "robust"; (4) trim the redundant glosses flagged below
(State-of-Field "(forecasts risk)", foundations' interrupting hazard-ratio gloss). Best-reading
chapters: `00-start-here.md` and `01-STATE-OF-THE-FIELD.md` (tight, voice-forward). Densest/most to
prune: `01-foundations.md` and `37` (long, table-heavy — prose is strong but the antithesis tics
cluster there).
