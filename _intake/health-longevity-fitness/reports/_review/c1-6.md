# Cycle 1 — Layperson review, Slice 6

Chapters reviewed: 41 (pathology/lab), 28 (pharmacology full), 10 (medical pharmacology),
31 (regenerative frontier), 30 (complementary medicine), 32 (biohacking).

## PATTERNS

These repeat across every chapter in the slice. Fixing them once, as a house style, fixes the slice.

1. **Every chapter opens with an editorial "status" block written for the book's editors, not for me.**
   Before I get a single reason to care, I hit a version stamp, a "Companion data:" filename, a list of
   cross-referenced section numbers, and (in §41) a note that "An external cross-check flagged the earlier
   draft as *thin*." That is backstage paperwork. As a reader I start skimming before the chapter has
   started. **Pattern fix (my #1 overall):** lead each chapter with one plain sentence of stakes ("Why this
   chapter can change a decision you'll actually make"), and push the version / companion-file / cross-ref
   scaffolding into a small footer at the bottom.

2. **The book leans on a private vocabulary it never defines *in the chapter* — it assumes I read "Start
   Here" and "SCHEMA.md" and remember them.** Phrases like *predictor ≠ lever*, *mechanism ≠ outcome*,
   *cohort ≠ RCT*, "the schema forbids collapsing," and the backtick tier-codes (`rct`, `meta`, `cohort`,
   `animal`, `invitro`, `mechanistic`, `speculative`, `claim_type: outcome`) carry the whole argument, but a
   reader who opened to this chapter has no idea what they mean. **Pattern fix (my #2 overall):** the first
   time each shorthand appears in a chapter, gloss it in four or five plain words (e.g. "`animal`-tier — shown
   in mice, never tested in people").

3. **Statistical shorthand is dropped in raw.** "0.5 SD," "~0.2 SD" (§30), "CV" (§41 A.8), "MACE" (§10),
   "NNT/NNH" (defined only glancingly). A motivated non-statistician stalls on these. **Pattern fix (my #3
   overall):** gloss each on first use — "0.5 SD (a moderate, clearly-noticeable effect)," "MACE (heart
   attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death)."

4. **The teaching prose, when it gets going, is genuinely excellent** (the Bayes worked example, the
   four-beat regenerative pattern, the GLP-1 hook, the cancer "instead-of" number). The problem is almost
   never the substance — it is the cold editorial on-ramp and the undefined jargon gating access to it.
