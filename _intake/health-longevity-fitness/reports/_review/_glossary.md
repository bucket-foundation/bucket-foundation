# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the acronyms, biomarkers, evidence-tier codes, and technical
terms used throughout this manual. This is a lookup, not a lecture — one line each. For the
evidence-tier codes (the backtick tags like `rct`, `cohort`, `mechanistic`), see the block at
the top under **Evidence tiers**, then the alphabetical list below.

---

## Evidence tiers (strength-of-evidence codes)

The manual tags most claims with one of these, roughly strongest to weakest. The tag says *how
good the evidence is*, not whether the claim is true.

- **`meta`** — meta-analysis or systematic review pooling many studies; the strongest tier when the pooled studies are randomized.
- **`rct`** — randomized controlled trial; people are randomly assigned, so it can show cause, not just association.
- **`cohort`** — a large group followed over time; shows association and can't fully rule out confounding (the top observational tier).
- **`cross-sectional`** — a single-snapshot survey of a population; shows correlation at one moment, no time direction.
- **`case-control`** — compares people with a condition to matched people without it, looking backward for exposures.
- **`mechanistic`** — a plausible biological pathway (why it *should* work) without an outcome trial proving it does.
- **`animal`** — evidence from mice, rats, worms, or other non-human models; often fails to translate to people.
- **`in-vitro`** — cells or tissue in a dish; furthest from a whole living human.
- **`n=1` / `n-of-1`** — a single individual's tracked self-experiment; hypothesis-generating only.
- **`anecdotal`** — uncontrolled personal reports or testimonials; the weakest empirical tier.
- **`theoretical`** — derived from first principles or models, not yet measured.
- **`speculative`** — an informed guess flagged as such; lowest confidence, kept honest rather than hidden.
- **`consensus` / `guideline`** — expert or professional-body agreement, cited as authority rather than a single trial.
- **`ecological`** — population-level correlations (e.g. country vs country); prone to the ecological fallacy.

## A–Z

**ACR** — urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, an early marker of kidney damage.
**AFib (atrial fibrillation)** — an irregular, often rapid heart rhythm that raises stroke risk.
**Allostasis** — the body maintaining stability through change, actively adjusting set-points to meet demand.
**Allostatic load** — the cumulative wear-and-tear from chronic stress and repeated allostatic adjustment.
**AMPK** — a cellular energy sensor switched on by low fuel and exercise; promotes repair and fat-burning.
**Anecdotal** — evidence from uncontrolled personal reports; the weakest empirical tier (see Evidence tiers).
**apoB** — a count of the atherogenic cholesterol particles that actually clog arteries; the best single lipid risk marker and one of the few causal blood levers.
**Atherosclerosis** — plaque buildup inside artery walls, the process behind most heart attacks and strokes.
**Autophagy** — the cell's recycling process, breaking down and clearing damaged components.
**Biological age** — an estimate of how old your body seems from biomarkers, versus your calendar age.
**BMD (bone mineral density)** — how much mineral is packed into bone, measured by DEXA to gauge fracture risk.
**BMI (body mass index)** — weight over height squared; a crude population screen that misjudges muscular or older bodies.
**BP (blood pressure)** — the pressure of blood against artery walls; a top modifiable driver of stroke and heart disease.
**CAC (coronary artery calcium)** — a CT score of calcified plaque in heart arteries; a strong personalized heart-risk test.
**Caloric restriction** — eating fewer calories without malnutrition; extends lifespan in animals, unproven for human lifespan.
**Case-control** — a study comparing people with a condition to those without, looking back for exposures (see Evidence tiers).
**CGM (continuous glucose monitor)** — a wearable sensor tracking blood sugar in real time; useful in diabetes, oversold for the healthy.
**CGRP** — a nerve signaling molecule central to migraine; the target of newer migraine drugs.
**Chemiosmosis** — how cells make energy by pumping protons across a membrane and letting them flow back through ATP synthase.
**Circadian** — the body's roughly 24-hour internal clock governing sleep, hormones, and metabolism.
**CKD (chronic kidney disease)** — long-term loss of kidney function, tracked by eGFR and ACR.
**Cohort ≠ RCT** — a core manual rule: long-term observational data can look strong but carries bias a randomized trial would remove.
**Cohort** — a group followed over time; the top observational evidence tier (see Evidence tiers).
**Conflict object** — the manual's practice of keeping each genuine expert disagreement as its own logged item instead of smoothing it over.
**Confounding** — when a hidden third factor drives both the exposure and the outcome, faking a cause-effect link.
**COPD** — chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; progressive airflow obstruction, mostly from smoking.
**CRF (cardiorespiratory fitness)** — how well heart and lungs deliver oxygen during effort; measured as VO₂max, the strongest mortality predictor.
**CRP / hsCRP** — C-reactive protein, a blood marker of inflammation; the high-sensitivity (hs) version is used for cardiovascular risk.
**Cross-sectional** — a single-snapshot population survey (see Evidence tiers).
**Deprescribing** — the deliberate, supervised stopping of drugs that no longer help or now harm.
**DEXA / DXA** — a low-dose X-ray scan measuring bone density and body composition (fat and muscle).
**DMT** — a fast-acting psychedelic compound; discussed in the psychedelics and fringe sections.
**DOHaD** — Developmental Origins of Health and Disease; the idea that early-life (fetal, infant) conditions shape adult disease risk.
**Dose-response** — the pattern of how an effect changes with the size of a dose; steepest gains usually come at the start of the curve.
**eGFR** — estimated glomerular filtration rate, the main number for how well kidneys filter blood.
**Effect size** — how big an effect is (not just whether it exists); often reported as SMD, HR, or absolute risk change.
**Epigenetic clock** — an age estimate built from DNA methylation patterns; a predictor, not proven to be a lever.
**Ex vivo** — done on living tissue removed from the body; a step closer to reality than in-vitro.
**Four Horsemen** — the manual's shorthand for the four chronic killers that dominate late-life mortality: heart disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, and metabolic disease.
**FOXO** — a family of longevity-linked transcription factors that switch on stress-resistance and repair genes.
**Frailty** — an aging state of low reserve and vulnerability, marked by weakness, slowness, and exhaustion.
**FRAX** — a calculator estimating 10-year fracture risk from clinical factors and bone density.
**Gait speed** — walking pace; a cheap, powerful predictor of survival and function in older adults.
**GLP-1** — a gut hormone (and the drug class mimicking it, e.g. semaglutide) that curbs appetite and lowers blood sugar and weight.
**Grip strength** — how hard you can squeeze; a simple whole-body strength proxy that predicts mortality.
**GWAS** — genome-wide association study, scanning many genomes to link genetic variants to traits or disease.
**Hallmarks of aging** — the ~12 shared cellular processes (e.g. senescence, mitochondrial decline, telomere loss) that drive biological aging.
**Hazard ratio (HR)** — the relative rate of an event in one group versus another over time; HR 0.8 means 20% lower rate.
**HbA1c** — a roughly 3-month average of blood sugar, used to diagnose and track diabetes.
**HDL** — the "good" cholesterol particle; higher is loosely protective, but raising it with drugs hasn't cut risk.
**Healthspan vs lifespan** — healthspan is years lived in good function; lifespan is total years lived. The goal is compressing decline, not just adding time.
**Heritability** — the share of a trait's variation in a population explained by genetic differences.
**HIIT** — high-intensity interval training; short hard efforts with recovery, efficient for raising VO₂max.
**HOMA-IR** — a fasting glucose-and-insulin formula estimating insulin resistance.
**Hormesis** — a beneficial adaptive response to a low dose of a stressor (exercise, heat, cold) that would harm at a high dose.
**HR** — see Hazard ratio (also used for heart rate in some contexts).
**HRV (heart-rate variability)** — the beat-to-beat variation in heart rate; a trend marker of autonomic recovery, a predictor rather than a lever.
**IGF-1** — insulin-like growth factor 1; a growth-and-repair hormone whose lower levels are linked to longevity but higher levels to muscle and bone.
**IL-6 (interleukin-6)** — an inflammatory signaling molecule; elevated levels predict cardiovascular and aging risk.
**Immunosenescence** — the age-related decline and dysfunction of the immune system.
**In-vitro** — done in a dish with cells or tissue, furthest from a whole human (see Evidence tiers).
**Inflammaging** — the chronic, low-grade inflammation that rises with age and drives many age-related diseases.
**Insulin resistance** — when cells respond poorly to insulin, forcing higher levels and driving type 2 diabetes.
**LDL** — low-density lipoprotein, the main cholesterol-carrying particle causally tied to atherosclerosis; lower is better, earlier is better.
**Lifespan** — total years lived (contrast with healthspan).
**Lp(a)** — lipoprotein(a), a largely genetic particle that adds heart and stroke risk on top of LDL; worth measuring once in a lifetime.
**MACE** — major adverse cardiovascular events, a trial endpoint bundling heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular death.
**MASLD / NAFLD** — metabolic dysfunction-associated (formerly non-alcoholic) fatty liver disease; fat buildup in the liver tied to insulin resistance.
**Mechanism ≠ outcome** — a core manual rule: a plausible biological pathway does not prove a real-world health benefit.
**Mechanistic** — evidence from a biological pathway rather than an outcome trial (see Evidence tiers).
**Mendelian randomization** — using inherited genetic variants as natural randomization to test whether an exposure truly causes an outcome.
**Meta (meta-analysis)** — a pooled analysis of many studies; the strongest tier when the inputs are RCTs (see Evidence tiers).
**Methylation** — a chemical tag added to DNA that switches genes on or off; the basis of epigenetic clocks.
**Metabolic syndrome** — a cluster (belly fat, high BP, high sugar, bad lipids) that multiplies cardiometabolic risk.
**Mitochondria** — the cell's power plants, where oxygen and fuel are turned into ATP energy.
**Mitochondrial biogenesis** — the making of new mitochondria, a key training adaptation.
**Mitophagy** — the targeted cleanup of worn-out mitochondria.
**MOUD** — medication for opioid use disorder (e.g. buprenorphine, methadone), the evidence-based treatment for opioid addiction.
**mTOR** — a master growth switch; active it builds tissue, suppressed (e.g. by rapamycin or fasting) it triggers repair and cleanup.
**NAD⁺** — a coenzyme central to energy metabolism whose levels fall with age; NMN/NR supplements aim to raise it, with weak human outcome evidence.
**NNH (number needed to harm)** — how many people must take a treatment for one extra person to be harmed.
**NNT (number needed to treat)** — how many people must take a treatment for one to benefit; smaller means more effective.
**Nocebo** — a harmful effect produced by negative expectation, the mirror image of placebo.
**Non-responder** — a person who shows little or no measurable adaptation to an intervention others benefit from.
**NR / NMN** — nicotinamide riboside and nicotinamide mononucleotide, NAD⁺-precursor supplements marketed for longevity.
**NRF2** — a master switch that turns on the cell's own antioxidant and detox defenses under stress.
**Odds ratio (OR)** — the odds of an outcome in one group versus another; approximates relative risk for rare outcomes.
**Osteopenia** — bone density lower than normal but not yet osteoporosis.
**Osteoporosis** — porous, fragile bone with high fracture risk.
**Outcome** — a real health endpoint (death, heart attack, fracture) rather than a surrogate marker.
**OSA (obstructive sleep apnea)** — repeated airway collapse during sleep, causing oxygen dips and cardiometabolic harm.
**PAF (population attributable fraction)** — the share of disease in a population that would vanish if a risk factor were removed.
**Placebo** — an inert treatment; its measurable effect sets the bar a real treatment must beat.
**Polygenic** — a trait shaped by many genes each with small effect, rather than one.
**Polypharmacy** — being on many medications at once, raising interaction and harm risk, especially in older adults.
**Prediabetes** — blood sugar above normal but below the diabetes threshold; a warning stage.
**Predictor ≠ lever** — the manual's first rule: a number that predicts death isn't automatically something that, when changed, prevents death.
**Proteostasis** — the cell's maintenance of a healthy set of properly folded proteins.
**Proton-motive force** — the electrochemical gradient across the mitochondrial membrane that powers ATP synthesis.
**PSA** — prostate-specific antigen, a blood test for prostate cancer screening with real overdiagnosis tradeoffs.
**RCT (randomized controlled trial)** — the gold-standard causal design (see Evidence tiers).
**Redox** — reduction-oxidation, the shuffling of electrons cells use to move and store energy.
**Relative risk (RR)** — the risk in one group divided by another; RR 2.0 means twice the risk, but says nothing about absolute size.
**Responder** — a person who shows a clear measurable benefit from an intervention (contrast non-responder).
**Reverse causation** — when the outcome actually causes the apparent exposure, not the other way around (e.g. illness lowers activity).
**ROS (reactive oxygen species)** — reactive oxygen byproducts; damaging in excess, but also necessary signals for adaptation.
**Sarcopenia** — the age-related loss of muscle mass and strength.
**SASP** — senescence-associated secretory phenotype, the inflammatory soup that senescent cells leak into surrounding tissue.
**Senescence** — a state where a cell stops dividing but resists dying and secretes inflammatory signals (SASP).
**Senolytics** — drugs that selectively clear senescent cells; promising in animals, early in humans.
**SGLT2 inhibitors** — diabetes drugs (e.g. empagliflozin) that dump glucose in urine and protect heart and kidney.
**Sirtuins** — a family of repair-and-metabolism enzymes that depend on NAD⁺; the target of much longevity hype.
**SMD (standardized mean difference)** — an effect-size unit letting different studies' outcomes be compared on one scale.
**SNP** — single-nucleotide polymorphism, a common single-letter DNA variant between people.
**SSRI / SNRI** — serotonin (and norepinephrine) reuptake inhibitors, first-line antidepressant drug classes.
**Statin** — a cholesterol-lowering drug class that cuts LDL/apoB and cardiovascular events, among the best-evidenced preventive drugs.
**Surrogate endpoint** — a lab marker (like LDL or HbA1c) used as a stand-in for a real outcome; convenient but sometimes misleading.
**T2D (type 2 diabetes)** — high blood sugar from insulin resistance and beta-cell decline; largely lifestyle-driven and preventable.
**Telomere** — the protective cap on a chromosome's end that shortens with each cell division; a predictor of aging, not a proven lever.
**Time-restricted eating** — confining daily food intake to a set window; a fasting variant with modest, mostly weight-mediated effects.
**TMAO** — trimethylamine N-oxide, a gut-microbe-derived metabolite associated (contested) with cardiovascular risk.
**TNF (tumor necrosis factor)** — a potent inflammatory signaling protein, targeted by several autoimmune drugs.
**TSH** — thyroid-stimulating hormone, the main screening test for thyroid over- or under-activity.
**VO₂max** — the maximum oxygen your body can use at full effort; the single strongest mortality predictor and a treat-it-like-a-vital-sign fitness measure.
**Zone 2** — a moderate aerobic intensity (conversational pace) that builds mitochondrial and aerobic base.
