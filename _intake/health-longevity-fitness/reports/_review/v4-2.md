## 18-genetics-anatomy §A.2.1 / A.3 — APOE ε4 gene-dose risk
- claim: "one ε4 copy raises lifetime risk roughly **2–3×**, two copies (ε4/ε4 …) roughly **8–12×**, relative to ε3/ε3" | cited: Corder 1993 (doi:10.1126/science.8346443); Farrer 1997 meta (PMID 9343467, JAMA)
- **CONFIRMED (with minor note)** — Farrer 1997 (5930 AD cases / 8607 controls) reports, for Caucasians, roughly **3–4× in ε4 heterozygotes and ~9–15× in ε4/ε4 homozygotes** as case-control odds ratios, with a clean gene-dose gradient, larger effect in women, and attenuated effect in African-ancestry groups — all as the manual states. The manual's "2–3× / 8–12×" is framed as *lifetime-risk* multipliers (which run lower than case-control ORs) and is a commonly-cited range, so it is defensible, but note Farrer's ORs are somewhat higher at the top end (up to ~15× for ε4/ε4). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9343467/

## 18-genetics-anatomy §A.2.2 — HFE C282Y penetrance
- claim: "in the HealthIron cohort, only a minority of C282Y homozygotes (≈**28% of men, <2% of women**) developed iron-overload-related disease" | cited: Allen et al. 2008, NEJM (doi:10.1056/NEJMoa073286, PMID 18199861)
- **CONFIRMED** — Iron-overload-related disease documented in **28.4% of male and 1.2% of female** C282Y homozygotes. Exact match. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18199861/

## 18-genetics-anatomy §A.8 — DIETFITS diet × genotype
- claim: ">600 people randomized to healthy low-fat vs healthy low-carb … pre-genotyped … and **genotype did not predict which diet worked better**" | cited: Gardner et al. 2018, JAMA (doi:10.1001/jama.2018.0245, PMID 29466592)
- **CONFIRMED** — 609 adults; no significant diet–genotype interaction on 12-month weight loss (−5.3 kg low-fat vs −6.0 kg low-carb, NS). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5839290/

## 18-genetics-anatomy §A.8 — FTO and weight-loss response
- claim: "across 9,500+ people, *FTO* genotype **did not affect weight-loss response** to diet/exercise/drugs" | cited: Livingstone et al. 2016, BMJ (doi:10.1136/bmj.i4707, PMID 27650503)
- **CONFIRMED** — IPD meta of **9563 participants / 8 RCTs**; FTO minor allele did not alter response to diet, physical-activity, or drug-based interventions. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27650503/

- claim: "serum total and especially **free** testosterone decline gradually, **roughly ~1%/year** from around age 30–40" | cited: Harman et al. 2001 (BLSA), JCEM (doi:10.1210/jcem.86.2.7219)

## 13-endocrine-hormones §4.3 — DHEA replacement null
- claim: "**no beneficial effect on body composition, physical performance, insulin sensitivity, or quality of life**, despite restoring DHEA levels to young-adult ranges" | cited: Nair et al. 2006, NEJM (doi:10.1056/NEJMoa054629)
- **CONFIRMED** — 2-yr placebo-controlled RCT; "no evidence that either DHEA or low-dose testosterone is an effective antiaging hormone supplement"; no benefit on body composition, physical performance, BMD, or glucose tolerance. Source: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa054629

## 13-endocrine-hormones §4.1 / §11 — Testosterone Trials benefits
- claim: "real improvement in **sexual function/libido**, smaller effects on mood and walking, and **no meaningful cognitive benefit** (the memory sub-trial was null)" | cited: Snyder et al. 2016, NEJM (doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1506119)
- **CONFIRMED** — 790 men ≥65 with low T; moderate benefit on sexual function, some benefit on mood/depressive symptoms, no benefit on physical function; the paired Cognitive Function Trial (Resnick 2017) found no memory/cognition benefit. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5209754/

## 13-endocrine-hormones §6 — DPP/DPS diabetes prevention

- **CONFIRMED** — Both the US DPP (n=3234, 2.8-yr) and the Finnish DPS (n=522) independently reported a **58% reduction** in incident type 2 diabetes with intensive lifestyle intervention. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12819313/ ; DPP.

## 13-endocrine-hormones §10 — VITAL vitamin D

## 14-nervous-system §6.3 — SPACE trial (opioids)
- claim: "opioid vs. non-opioid … over 12 months and found **no benefit of opioids on pain-related function — and slightly *worse* pain in the opioid group**, plus more side effects" | cited: Krebs et al. 2018, JAMA (doi:10.1001/jama.2018.0899, PMID 29509867)
- **CONFIRMED** — No difference in pain-related function; pain intensity was significantly *better* in the non-opioid group (i.e., slightly worse in the opioid arm); more adverse effects with opioids. Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2673971

## 14-nervous-system §6.4 — neuropathic-pain NNT
- claim: "even first-line, the **numbers needed to treat are ~4–8** for 50% pain relief" | cited: Finnerup et al. 2015, Lancet Neurol (doi:10.1016/s1474-4422(14)70251-0)
- **CONFIRMED** — NeuPSIG meta used NNT for 50% relief; first-line agents cluster in roughly this band (TCAs ~3.6 at the strong end; SNRIs/gabapentinoids ~6–8). "~4–8" is a fair characterization. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4493167/

## 14-nervous-system §2.2 — vagus afferent fraction

- **CONFIRMED** — The ~80% afferent / ~20% efferent split for the vagus is standard textbook physiology; consistent with the literature.

## 15-immune-system §4.1 — sleep and cold susceptibility
- claim: "short/poor sleep predicts higher rates of the common cold (Prather 2015: **<6h ≈ 4× the cold risk of ≥7h**)" | cited: Prather et al. 2015, Sleep (doi:10.5665/sleep.4968)
- **CONFIRMED** — Vs >7h sleepers, OR for developing a cold was **4.50 for <5h and 4.24 for 5–6h**. "<6h ≈ 4×" is accurate. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26118561/

## 15-immune-system §4.3 — vitamin C and cold duration
- claim: "modest reduction in cold *duration/severity* (**~8% shorter in adults, ~14% in children**) only with consistent prophylactic intake … taking it *after* symptoms start does essentially nothing" | cited: Cochrane / Hemilä (doi:10.3390/nu9040339 cited in "Go deeper")
- **CONFIRMED** — Hemilä Cochrane review: regular supplementation shortened colds by **8% in adults and 14% in children**; therapeutic (post-onset) dosing showed no consistent effect. Exact match. Source: https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000980.pub4/full

## 15-immune-system §3.2 — CANTOS / canakinumab

## 11-body-systems §1.2 — sunscreen and photoaging (Hughes)
- claim: "~900 adults to daily vs. discretionary sunscreen for 4.5 years … daily group had no detectable increase in skin aging … i.e. **~24% less photoaging** than discretionary use" | cited: Hughes et al. 2013, Ann Intern Med (doi:10.7326/0003-4819-158-11-201306040-00002)

## 11-body-systems §1.2 — sunscreen and melanoma (Green)
