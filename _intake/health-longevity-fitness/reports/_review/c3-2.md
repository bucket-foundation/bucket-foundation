# Cycle 3 — Findability + Length (slice-2)

Reviewer: busy practitioner. Two jobs: (A) can I get the verdict fast? (B) cut redundancy/verbosity without losing a graded claim, number, tier, citation, caveat, cross-ref, `@@FIG@@`, or `[^fn]`.

## PATTERNS (cross-chapter + biggest length wins)

- **The honesty-rule gloss is re-defined in every chapter AND re-glossed within chapters — the single biggest cuttable redundancy.** The parenthetical "predictor ≠ lever (something that forecasts risk isn't automatically something that, changed, lowers it)" is spelled out in full in 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18 — and then *repeated in-line* a second/third time inside 13, 14, 15, 16. These tiers/rules are defined once in "Start Here" (11 and 14 both say so). **Rule: gloss each rule ONCE per chapter at first use; every later occurrence is just "predictor ≠ lever."** Across the six files this is ≈250–350 words with zero information loss. Never cut the *claim* that a rule applies to a specific finding — only the repeated definition.
- **Terminal "honest synthesis / summary" sections restate the body.** 13 §11, 14 §8, 15 §7, 16 §16.7, 18 Part-A closer + honesty footer each re-say what the chapter already said. These double as end-TLDRs so they *earn their place* — but several are padded and can tighten 15–30% by dropping re-explanation while keeping the ranked lists (the lists are the payoff a skimmer wants).
- **Findability is mostly GOOD.** 11 (one-line verdict box), 13 (master axis table §2), 15 (rules up front + boost/regulate reframe), 16 (rules table §16.0), 18 (one-line filter box) all front-load a verdict. **The one real gap: 14 (nervous system)** — 5,475 words, no bottom-line-up-front; the payoff is the 7-point §8 at the very end. Add a TLDR box up top pointing to §8.
- **In-line term re-glosses** (e.g. "hsCRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein…)", "VO₂max (a fitness/aerobic-capacity score)", "DEXA (the bone-density scan)") are re-expanded on nearly every reappearance. Gloss once per chapter; later mentions bare. Small individually, adds up.
- **Biggest single-file wins:** 18 (verbose Part-A prose, several 4-line windups) and 13 (§11 synthesis heavily overlaps §1–§10). These two files hold ~40% of the cuttable words in the slice.

Rough total cuttable across slice-2: **≈1,400–1,700 words**, all redundancy/verbosity — no graded content touched.
