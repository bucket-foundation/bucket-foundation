# Cycle 1 — Layperson Reviewer — Slice 1

Files reviewed: `sections/00-start-here.md`, `sections/00-atlas.md`, `sections/01-foundations.md`,
`sections/12-mechanism-bridge.md`, `sections/37-mitochondrial-health.md`, `00-map/01-STATE-OF-THE-FIELD.md`.

## PATTERNS (cross-chapter)

**P1 — Draft/machine scaffolding leaks into reader-facing text (highest impact, everywhere).**
A reader keeps tripping over things written for the build system, not for them: version/status
lines ("**Status:** v0.1 (Wave 2 synthesis) — 2026-06-28"), a *stale* note that "`01-foundations.md`
is **not yet written**" (it now exists and I just read it), raw file paths
(`02-domains/*-claims.json`, `bucket-canon/05-biophysics/...`), opaque one-letter domain codes
("(`crf-vo2max...`, E)"), `@@FIG:...@@` placeholders sitting in the prose, and sign-offs like
"Atlas maintained by Nucleus / the corpus is idempotent — re-running converges." None of this means
anything to a reader, and it makes the book feel like a database dump instead of a manual. It's
worst in `00-atlas.md`, `12`, and `37` because they open on it.

**P2 — Headline numbers contradict each other across chapters.** The atlas says
"**1007 graded claims** across 53 domain files"; State-of-the-Field says "The corpus is **197 graded
claims**." A smart reader notices, and trust takes the hit — if the book can't keep its own count
straight, why believe the effect sizes? Reconcile to one number (or explain why they differ).

**P3 — The densest reference asset is front-loaded before the readable version.** `00-atlas.md` is a
wall of node tables; `12-mechanism-bridge.md` leads with a 24-row × 8-column mega-table *before* the
narrative deep-dives that actually explain it. Neither warns you it's skim-able. A reader hits the
table, bounces, and never reaches the good prose behind it.

**P4 — A handful of load-bearing terms get used hard before a plain-language gloss:** VO₂max, apoB,
HRV, "redox," "U-shaped," "MET." Each needs one clause on first use in the front-matter chapters.

**P5 — `00-start-here.md` and `01-STATE-OF-THE-FIELD.md` substantially repeat each other** (same
three honesty rules, same lever ranking). Cover-to-cover that reads as déjà vu.

**Top 3 fixes overall:**
1. Strip every reader-facing build artifact: version/status lines, the stale "not yet written"
   note, internal file paths, the one-letter domain codes, `@@FIG@@` tokens, "maintained by
   Nucleus / idempotent." Move anything authors truly need into a hidden comment.
2. Reconcile the claim-count (and any other headline numbers) to one figure across all chapters.
3. Add a one-clause plain gloss for VO₂max / apoB / HRV / redox / U-shaped / MET on first use, and a
   one-line "you can skim this table; the explanation follows" signpost atop the big tables in §00-atlas
   and §12.1.

## sections/00-start-here.md  (the strongest chapter — protect it)

- **GOOD** §opening — "Most of the benefit in this entire 500-page manual comes from a short, dull,
  mostly-free list." → keep verbatim; this is the hook that earns the whole book.
- **GOOD** §DO list — "it's the one nobody sells you, which is exactly why it gets left off the list."
  Memorable and trust-building. Keep.
- **GOOD** §The minimum-effective week — the concrete Mon–Sun table is exactly what a motivated
  layperson wants; "the best program is the one you'll actually keep doing" lands. Keep.
- **LOST** §DO #2 / §MEASURE — "cardiorespiratory fitness (VO₂max)" carries the whole chapter but is
  never said in plain words. → fix: add one clause on first use, e.g. "VO₂max — how much oxygen your
  body can use at full effort, i.e. how fit your heart and lungs are."
- **LOST** §DO #5 / §MEASURE — "**apoB**" and "**Lp(a)**" appear as the headline blood levers with no
  plain gloss ("apoB (best lipid metric)" assumes you know what it measures). A reader knows "LDL =
  bad cholesterol" but not these. → fix: one clause — "apoB, a count of the cholesterol particles
  that actually clog arteries."
- **LOST** §DO #6 — "U-shaped risk" for sleep, unglossed. → fix: "(both too little *and* too much are
  worse)."
- **LOST** §MEASURE — "HbA1c + fasting insulin," "HRV" used without expansion. → fix: expand HRV
  ("heart-rate variability") and gloss HbA1c ("a 3-month blood-sugar average") on first use.

## sections/00-atlas.md  (reads as internal scaffolding, not a chapter)

- **BORED/SO-WHAT** §"Corpus at a glance" — "49 chapters · ~265,000 words · **1007 graded claims**
  across 53 domain files · **660 carded figures** · 37 first-class conflicts · 24 labs · 15 trials."
  This is build metadata; a reader does not care how many "domain files" or "carded figures" exist,
  and it's the second thing they hit. → fix: cut the file/figure counts; keep only what a reader feels
  ("hundreds of claims, each graded by how good the evidence is").
- **LOST** throughout — the tables route everything to opaque codes: "domain B," "domain C," "domain
  L," and the ✅/🟡/⚪ "coverage status." Coverage status is a project-management view (what the authors
  have/haven't finished), not reader content. → fix: drop the coverage column for readers, or relabel
  it as something a reader wants ("how settled the science is"), and spell out the domain letters or
  remove them.
- **BORED/LOST** §closing — "Atlas maintained by Nucleus... The corpus is idempotent — re-running
  research converges, it does not duplicate." Pure machine-speak; a lay reader has no idea what this
  means and feels shut out. → fix: delete from the reader-facing page.
- **LOST** §Layer 0 — "Bioenergetics — chemiosmosis, proton-motive force, ATP... mTOR, AMPK,
  sirtuins, IGF-1, NRF2, FOXO" dumped as a list with zero definition. As a *map* that points
  elsewhere it's defensible, but landing it at position 00 (right after Start Here) talks over the
  reader early. → fix: add a one-line "don't worry if these words are new — each links to a plain
  explanation" note at the top of Layer 0.
- **GOOD** §"The honest edge of the map" — naming exactly what the book does *not* cover
  ("Surgery & anaesthesia... not a dedicated chapter") is disarming and builds trust. Keep.

## sections/01-foundations.md  (dense but genuinely good — mostly needs signposting)

- **GOOD** §2 — "being alive is a verb, not a noun." Beautiful and sticky. Keep, and consider
  surfacing it even earlier.
- **GOOD** §1 — the Foundation-vs-Outcome table and "Hype is what floats in between without touching
  either." This is the intellectual payoff of the whole book stated cleanly. Keep.
- **GOOD** §2.7 — brown fat / UCP1 as "the cleanest everyday demonstration that Mitchell was right."
  A concrete, surprising proof a layperson can hold onto.
- **LOST** §2.2 — the formula `Δp = Δψ − (2.303 RT / F) · ΔpH` drops on the reader with R and F

  membrane*" and expand R/T/F.
- **LOST** §2.6 / throughout — "redox" is used constantly and load-bearingly; it's only ever
  expanded as "(redox chemistry)." → fix: one plain gloss on first use — "redox = moving electrons
  from one molecule to another; it's how cells shuffle energy."
- **BORED** §2.4–2.6 — a dense run of named enzymes/people (Krebs, Szent-Györgyi, NADH/FADH₂,
  Complexes I–IV) where a non-biologist may start skimming. The writing is good; the issue is no
  "breather" or signpost that the names are reference, not memorization. → fix: a one-line "you don't
  need to memorize these names — the one idea is: all fuels funnel to one hub" at the head of §2.4.
- **GOOD** §6.1 — the hormesis "right dose of the right stressor" frame, plus the honest "it becomes
  *unfalsifiable* when used to retro-explain any result." Models the book's whole posture.
