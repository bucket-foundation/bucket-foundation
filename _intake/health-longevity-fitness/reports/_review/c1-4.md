# Cycle 1 — Layperson review — Slice 4

Reviewer: smart, motivated non-specialist. Chapters: 25 (oncology), 26 (infectious disease),
08 (brain/cognitive), 20 (mental-health/psychiatry), 35 (addiction), 21 (pain/rehab).

## PATTERNS

1. **Cold mechanism-first openings vs. warm ones — the corpus does both, and the warm ones win.**
   The best chapters (26, 21, 35) open with stakes a human feels — "for nearly all of human history
   infection was *the* killer"; "the public model of pain is a plumbing model… the cost of believing
   it is enormous." The weaker openings (25, 08) lead with status-block metadata and cross-reference
   housekeeping before they tell me why I should keep reading. Chapter 25 buries its single most
   gripping line ("cancer is not a thing, it is a *process* — the corruption of the cell's own
   software") inside a grey status blockquote where a skimmer will miss it. **Fix: every chapter
   should open with one plain human-stakes sentence BEFORE the status/cross-ref apparatus; promote the
   buried one-liners out of the blockquotes into the first thing the reader sees.**

2. **Undefined shorthand jargon used before (or without) expansion.** Recurring across all six:
   `PAF`, `MCI`, `DUP`, `SMD`, `NNT`, `MOUD/MAT`, `CDR-SB`, `ARIA`, `R₀`, `U=U`, `CHS`, `MSI`,
   `ctDNA`, `EMT`, `nociplastic`. Some are defined, many are defined late or only by initials. A
   layperson hits an unexplained acronym and momentum dies. **Fix: define every acronym at first use
   in-line (one clause), and add a short glossary box to the chapters densest in them (08, 20, 25).**

3. **The "predictor ≠ lever / cohort ≠ RCT / something beats nothing" rules are referenced as if I
   already know them, but they're defined in a "Start Here" file I'm not reading.** All six chapters
   lean on these three rules by name (often as "honesty rule #1/#2"), and 25 even re-numbers them
   ("the three rules, applied up front") without restating what they are. A reader who lands on
   chapter 35 first is lost. **Fix: give each chapter a one-line restatement of the three rules in
   plain English at first invocation (e.g. "predicting a risk isn't the same as having a fix for
   it"), not just a pointer to Start Here.**

Top 3 fixes overall: (1) warm, human one-sentence hook at the very top of 25 and 08; (2) define
acronyms inline at first use everywhere; (3) restate the three honesty rules in plain words inside
each chapter, not by cross-reference.

## sections/25-oncology.md

- **[SO-WHAT]** opening status block — The chapter opens with three stacked blockquotes of metadata,
  discipline-justification, and cross-references before any reader-facing hook. The killer line
  ("cancer is not a thing, it is a *process* — the corruption of the cell's own software") is trapped
  inside that grey apparatus. → fix: lift that sentence to be the literal first line of the chapter
  body, above the status block.
- **[LOST]** §25.1.2 Hallmarks table — The 12-row hallmarks table throws `RAS`, `HER2/ERBB2`,
  `RB`, `TP53`, `BCL2`, `PI3K/AKT`, `TERT`, `VEGF`, `EMT`, `MYC`, `HIF1α`, `PD-L1`, `MSI` at me in
  one screen, most unexpanded. I can follow the *concept* column but the "driver" column is opaque.
  → fix: keep the table but add a one-line note that the gene names are examples the reader can skim
  past, and spell out EMT ("epithelial–mesenchymal transition") and MSI on first use.
- **[BORED]** §25.1.3 "Why this ties to the aging fundamentals" — Right after the dense hallmarks
  table comes another dense paragraph cross-linking telomeres, senescence, SASP, Haycock MR,
  Armitage–Doll. This is the spot I started skimming — two heavy passages back-to-back with no
  breather. → fix: shorten to its payoff sentence ("cancer and aging are two readouts of the same
  accumulating damage, which is why cancer risk rises so steeply with age") and move the citations to
  the Go-deeper list.
- **[GOOD]** §25.2.1 "gas pedals and the brakes" — The oncogene/tumour-suppressor accelerator-and-
  brakes metaphor is genuinely clear and memorable; "you can drug an oncogene far more easily than you
  can restore a lost brake" landed for me. Keep it.
- **[GOOD]** §25.5.2 / §25.7 honesty blockquotes — "The marketing says 'cancer cured by the immune
  system'; the data say 'a real, sometimes durable cure for a substantial minority, nothing for the
  majority, and a new class of harms.'" This is exactly the level the whole book should hit. Keep.
- **[SO-WHAT]** §25.6 MCED — Strong content, but it never plainly tells *me, the reader*, the
  one practical thing until the very last clause ("Do not pay out-of-pocket for it expecting it to
  save your life"). → fix: surface that one-line verdict to the top of the section as the hook.

## sections/26-infectious-disease.md

- **[GOOD]** opening — "For nearly all of human history it was **the** killer — the thing that
  determined whether a child reached five." Best opening in the slice; immediate human stakes, no
  jargon. This is the model the cold-opening chapters should copy.
- **[GOOD]** §1.1 selective toxicity — "A penicillin can shatter a bacterial cell wall while leaving
  your cells untouched precisely because your cells *have no cell wall to attack*." One sentence and I
  finally understand why antibiotics don't work on colds. Excellent.
- **[LOST]** §4.2 herd immunity — "R₀ ≈ 12–18" appears with only the phrase "one of the most
  transmissible diseases known" nearby; R₀ is never defined. A layperson doesn't know what the number
  means. → fix: gloss it inline ("R₀ — how many people one case infects in a fully susceptible
  population").
- **[LOST]** §5.2 chronic viral table — "U=U: undetectable = untransmittable" is expanded (good) but
  "PrEP," "ART," "DAAs," "HPTN 052" land fast and close together; HPTN 052 is just a trial code to me.
  → fix: drop the bare trial codes from the body (keep them in Go-deeper) and expand ART/DAA on first
  use.
- **[BORED]** §5.5 + §6 + §7 — By the everyday/vector-borne table, the pandemic section, AND the
  hygiene section, the chapter is long and the energy flags; lots of tables in a row. → fix: this is
  the one chapter that could lose ~15% length without losing substance — tighten §6.3 (pandemic
  preparedness reads like a policy memo) and merge the two §7 tables.
- **[GOOD]** §2.2 epidemiologic-transition reframe — "the highest-leverage health interventions in
  human history were boring, collective, and preventive — a sewer, a clean well, a hand-washing
  protocol." Memorable and true-feeling. Keep.

## sections/08-brain-cognitive.md

- **[SO-WHAT]** opening status block — Like 25, it opens cold: status metadata + companion-data
  pointers + a cross-reference list before the hook. The actual hook ("what actually protects the
  brain as it ages, and what is being sold to you that does not?") is good but sits below the
  apparatus. → fix: promote that question to the first line.
- **[LOST]** §1.1 the whole table hinges on "PAF" — "population-attributable fraction" is defined,
  but the definition is a dense italic sentence right above a 14-row table, and the table's last
  column says "the honest tier" referencing the three rules I may not know. → fix: add a plain-words
  gloss ("PAF = if we wiped this risk out everywhere, how much dementia would vanish") and a one-line
  restatement of predictor≠lever.
- **[LOST]** §6.2 amyloid drugs — `CDR-SB`, `ARIA-E`, `ARIA-H`, `APOE4` arrive in a burst. "0.45
  points on an 18-point scale" is the crux but I need to be told what CDR-SB *is* first (a dementia
  severity score). → fix: one clause defining CDR-SB before the numbers; spell out ARIA once.
- **[GOOD]** §6.2 lecanemab honesty — "The '27%' is a relative slowing of decline, not improvement.
  Everyone still got worse; the drug group got worse slightly less slowly." This is the clearest
  explanation of relative-vs-absolute benefit in the slice. Keep verbatim.
- **[GOOD]** §6.1 shingles-vaccine natural experiment — the date-of-birth-cutoff explanation ("people
  on either side of that cutoff are essentially identical except for vaccine access") makes a hard
  statistical idea (regression discontinuity) genuinely intuitive. Keep.
- **[BORED]** §6.4 nootropics list — bulleted lion's mane / ginkgo / omega-3 entries each end in a

  translate the tag into a plain verdict word ("verdict: thin / null / null") so the eye gets the

## sections/20-mental-health-psychiatry.md

- **[GOOD]** §0 "A word on tone" — "Underselling treatment talks people out of care that would help
  them; overselling it sets up disillusionment that talks them out of care next time." Strong, human,
  earns trust up front. Keep.
- **[LOST]** §1.3 the Cipriani result — "the pooled standardized mean difference was about 0.30 — a
  small-to-moderate effect" then "barely above the ~0.2 threshold often cited as a minimal clinically
  perceptible difference." SMD is never explained in lay terms — I don't know what 0.30 *means* for a
  real person. → fix: add one plain sentence ("an SMD of 0.3 means the average treated person does
  better than ~62% of placebo patients — real, but most people won't feel it dramatically").
- **[BORED]** §10 "honest summary" — a 10-item numbered recap that largely restates the chapter's own
  section headers. After a long chapter this felt like reading it twice. → fix: cut to the 4-5
  highest-value takeaways, or make it a scannable one-line-per-condition table.
- **[GOOD]** §3.2 lithium — "a rare and precious thing in psychiatry: a treatment with a direct
  anti-suicide signal." The framing made me care about an old, unglamorous drug. Keep.
- **[SO-WHAT]** §6.4 psychedelic frontier — Excellent content, but the load-bearing fact (FDA
  *rejected* MDMA therapy in 2024) is the third bolded thing in a long bullet; a skimmer sees

  the rejection, then explain the promising results and why they weren't enough.
- **[LOST]** §5.3 "relative age effect" — introduced as a named effect mid-sentence; clear from

### Note on what NOT to break

Chapters 26, 21, and 35 are the strongest reader experiences in this slice precisely because they
open with human stakes and pay off their honesty framing with quotable one-liners. Whatever editing
pass hits 25 and 08 should make them look more like 21, not flatten 21 toward 25. The "honesty
blockquote" device (used well in 25 §25.5.2, 08 §6.2, 35 §7.3) is the book's best recurring move —
keep it, just make sure the plain-English verdict leads it rather than trailing it.
