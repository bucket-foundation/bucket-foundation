## 02-training.md §1 (Four Capacities)
- claim: "~13% lower all-cause mortality per 1-MET" (CRF). | cited: [^crf-met] crf-per-met-mortality-meta
- **CONFIRMED** — Kodama et al. 2009 (*JAMA* 301:2024) meta (33 studies, ~102,980 participants): a 1-MET
  higher CRF = **13% lower all-cause mortality** and 15% lower CVD events. Number, direction, endpoint all match.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19454641/

## 02-training.md §1 / §6.1 (Strength → mortality)
- claim: "Resistance activity → ~10–17% lower mortality"; "cohort sweet spot ~30–60 min/week … benefit is J-shaped". | cited: [^momma] Momma 2022 (BJSM)
- **CONFIRMED** — Momma et al. 2022 (*BJSM* 56:755, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061): muscle-strengthening
  activity = **10–17% lower** all-cause mortality/CVD/cancer/diabetes; maximal risk reduction at
  **~30–60 min/week**, **J-shaped** (higher volumes lose benefit). Exact match.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35228201/

## 02-training.md §3.2 / §6.1 (VO₂max intervals)
- claim: "Norwegian 4×4 … 4 min at ~90–95% max HR, 3 min easy, ×4 … beat moderate continuous for VO₂max (Helgerud 2007, MSSE)". | cited: [^hiit-med]
- **CONFIRMED** — Helgerud et al. 2007 (*Med Sci Sports Exerc* 39:665): 4×4 min @ 90–95% HRmax with 3-min
  recovery raised VO₂max ~7–9%, significantly more than isocaloric moderate/threshold work. Protocol and
  direction match. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/

## 44-exercise-modalities.md §7.3 (Tai chi — Li 2018)
- claim: "therapeutic tai chi *outperformed* conventional multimodal and stretching exercise for fall reduction in high-risk older adults". | cited: [^taichi-falls] Li 2018 JAMA Intern Med

## 36-fasting.md §5 (Fasting-mimicking diet — Wei 2017)
- claim: "3 monthly FMD cycles in ~100 generally healthy adults improved body weight, blood pressure, fasting glucose, IGF-1, and CRP, largest in those with elevated baseline risk". | cited: [^wei] Sci Transl Med 2017
- **CONFIRMED** — Wei et al. 2017 (*Sci Transl Med* 9:eaai8700; n=100, 3 monthly 5-day cycles): reduced body
  weight/trunk fat, BP, fasting glucose, IGF-1; CRP/inflammation improved chiefly in the elevated-baseline
  subgroup — exactly the "largest in higher-risk" nuance the chapter states. Surrogate endpoints, as flagged.
  https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.aai8700

## 36-fasting.md §4.4 (Liver-flush "stones" — Lancet 2005)
- claim: "The classic analysis: a *Lancet* 2005 letter, **Sies & Brinkmann**, showing the 'stones' were saponified oil with no actual gallstone constituents." | cited: inline
- **MINOR** — The mechanism and source are right: *Lancet* 2005;365:1388 ("Could these be gallstones?")
  showed the passed "stones" were **saponified oil** (potassium carboxylate soaps), no cholesterol/bilirubin/
  calcium. But the **co-author's surname is wrong**: the authors are **Sies & Brooker** (Christian D. Sies,
  Michael J. Brooker), not "Sies & Brinkmann." Fix: change "Sies & Brinkmann" → "Sies & Brooker."
  https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140673605663738/fulltext
