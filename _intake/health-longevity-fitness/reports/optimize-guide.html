The one idea behind this guide

The interventions with the largest, most replicated effects on how long and how well you live are behavioral and unglamorous. They are not for sale, which is exactly why they get crowded out by supplements and gadgets. This guide puts them first, illustrates the foundational movements, and labels everything by what the evidence has actually earned.

Three rules that govern every recommendation here (1) Predictor ≠ lever — a number that predicts death isn't automatically something that, when changed, prevents it. (2) Cohort ≠ proof — you can't randomize fitness or sleep over decades, so the strong-looking numbers carry bias. (3) Something beats nothing — the steepest gains are at the start of every dose-response curve; optimization past that is real but smaller than advertised.

Part 1 — The movement program

Four capacities carry almost all the mortality signal: cardiorespiratory fitness, strength, the ability to move/balance, and not sitting all day. A week that touches all four beats any single "optimal" protocol.

A. Strength — the two patterns that matter most

Strength (and grip), not muscle mass, independently predicts mortality — and the dose-response is a J-curve: benefit peaks around 30–60 minutes per week, then flattens. Two compound patterns cover most of the body: a squat (knee-dominant) and a hip hinge (hip-dominant). Add a push, a pull, and a carry and you have a complete program.

Squat — knee-dominant lower-body strength
Hip hinge — the posterior-chain pattern

Tier A resistance-training-mortality-meta · grip-strength-mortality-pure (PURE, n=139k) · dexa-strength-not-mass-predicts-mortality. Practical: 2–3 sessions/week, progressive load, the 5 patterns (squat / hinge / push / pull / carry).

B. Cardiorespiratory fitness — the single strongest lever

VO₂max is the strongest mortality predictor in preventive medicine (~5× elite-vs-low, ~13% per MET, no observed ceiling of benefit). You raise it two complementary ways, and the evidence does not support one being uniquely magic:

  • Zone 2 (conversational pace), ~150–180 min/week — builds the aerobic base efficiently. Tier B caveat the "uniquely optimal for mitochondria" claim is overstated (conflict-zone2-optimal-mito).
  • VO₂max intervals / HIIT, 1–2× week — efficiently lifts the ceiling (hiit-crf-cardiometabolic-meta).
  • Just move & break up sitting — the steepest, least-confounded gain is sedentary→active (physical-activity-dose-response-mortality). Steps help, but the benefit plateaus ~7–8k, not 10k.

C. Balance & capacity — free tests that are also the training

Two bodyweight tests independently predict all-cause mortality — and practicing them is the intervention. They cost nothing and double as your progress metric.

10-second one-leg stand (HR 1.84 if you can't)
Sit-to-rise — strength + balance + flexibility

Tier A one-leg-stance-10s-mortality (Araújo 2022) · sit-to-rise-mortality (Brito 2014). Both are predictors — but the capacities they test (balance, lower-body strength, mobility) are trainable and worth training directly.

D. Mobility & breath — the supporting tier

Mobility keeps the range you need to keep moving; slow breathing shifts autonomic balance. Both are worth doing; both are honestly Tier B (real acute effects, longevity outcome unproven).

90/90 hip rotations — counters chronic sitting
Box breathing — ~6 breaths/min raises HRV acutely

Part 2 — Lifestyle habits

Outside the gym, a handful of habits carry effect sizes that rival or exceed anything in a supplement stack — and one of them (social connection) is almost never on the list because nothing is sold for it.

HabitWhat the evidence supportsTier
Don't smokeThe single largest modifiable mortality factor; the "longevity genes" of GWAS are largely the genetics of not doing harmful things.A
Protect social connectionAmong the largest psychosocial mortality associations — effect size rivals smoking cessation (Holt-Lunstad). Purpose/ikigai adds independently.A
Sleep ~7 h, regularlyU-shaped mortality with a ~7 h nadir; regularity and timing carry independent weight. Protect it like training.A
Lower lifetime apoB / LDLOne of the only causal blood levers; cumulative exposure means earlier is better. Diet + (if indicated) medication.A
Keep metabolic healthProtein-adequate, fiber-rich, mostly-whole-food diet; manage visceral fat & glucose. The pattern matters more than the brand-name diet.A
Protein adequacy (esp. older adults)Supports muscle & survival in the elderly — but a genuine mid-life vs late-life tradeoff with mTOR. Not "more is always better".B
Morning light, dim eveningsStrong circadian mechanism; bright AM / dim PM. (Blue-blocking glasses specifically show no clear benefit — the behavior, not the product.)B
Early time-restricted eatingEarly-window alignment helps insulin/BP — but most of the benefit is the calorie restriction it causes. Late 16:8 is null.B
Sauna / heatDose-response to mortality & dementia — from one Finnish cohort, no RCT. Promising. Infrared ≠ traditional evidence.B
Cold plunge "for longevity"Great for mood/alertness; the metabolic outcome data used hours of mild cold, not a 3-min plunge. Enjoy it, don't oversell it.C
Safety — non-negotiable Never do Wim Hof / breath-hold breathing in or near water (shallow-water blackout drowns people). Cold immersion carries cardiac risk — never combine with breath-holds. Heat/sauna: hydrate, avoid with alcohol, pregnancy, or uncontrolled cardiovascular disease. Loaded spine work: keep it neutral, progress slowly. When in doubt, a clinician beats a protocol.

Test yourself — the high-signal, mostly-free panel

Measurement should be functional first. These predict more, and cost less, than the commercial age-tests and continuous monitors.

The bottom line, in one paragraph

Build and keep cardiorespiratory fitness and strength; move more and sit less; sleep ~7 h regularly; keep apoB/LDL low across life; eat a protein-adequate, mostly-whole-food pattern; and protect your relationships. Train the four capacities — cardio, strength, balance, mobility — and use the free functional tests (VO₂max, grip, gait, sit-to-rise, the 10-second stand) as both training and scorecard. Treat sauna, breathwork, early eating windows and light hygiene as worthwhile trends. Treat cold plunges, NAD⁺, glucose monitors for the healthy and biological-age tests as experiments you enjoy, not protocols you depend on. The dull list is the powerful list — and it is almost entirely free.
