# 19 — Life Stages: Health Across the Whole Lifespan

> **Manual section, v1.0 — 2026-06-28.** Companion graded claims in `02-domains/lifestages-claims.json`.
> The rest of this manual is, by default, **adult-focused**: it tells a 30-to-70-year-old how to train,
> eat, sleep, and dose drugs. But health varies enormously across the lifespan, and the highest-leverage
> interventions in a whole human life often land *before that window even opens* — in the womb, in the first
> 1000 days, and in the bone and metabolic "bank account" built during childhood. This section is the
> **time axis** the manual was missing: it walks the lifespan from conception to death and asks, at each
> stage, *what actually matters, what the evidence says, and what is folklore.*

> ## ⚠️ This is not medical advice
>
> Pregnancy, fertility, paediatric, and end-of-life decisions are unusually consequential and unusually
> personal. Everything below is an **index of the evidence**, written so you can ask sharper questions of a
> clinician — not a protocol to self-apply. Several stages here (fertility supplements, "detox for two,"
> anti-aging hormones in elders) are sold hard on weak evidence; those are flagged explicitly.

## How the three honesty rules cut across the lifespan

**Predictor ≠ lever:** low birthweight predicts adult heart disease (Barker) but no single postnatal fix
reverses the programmed risk; a frailty score is a readout of accumulated deficit, not a dial — the lifespan is
full of powerful predictors whose levers are upstream (maternal/early-life) or multifactorial, not a pill.
**Cohort ≠ RCT:** almost the entire developmental-origins (DOHaD) field is observational — you cannot randomize
a fetus to famine — so causal arrows come from natural experiments (Dutch Hunger Winter, sibling designs);
pregnancy and paediatric *interventions* (folate, aspirin, GDM treatment) do have RCTs, and that is where the
confident recommendations live. **The floor moves with age:** "any movement beats sedentary" holds at every age,
but in youth the floor is building the peak, in old age defending independence (the resistance-training
inversion, §04 §4.3), and at the very end comfort and meaning, where *less* intervention is sometimes
higher-value.

**Cross-references (read alongside):** **age-specific training** (youth=build, midlife=defend, 65+=the
resistance inversion) is owned by `reports/sections/04-individual-variation.md §4` — this section does **not**
restate the training prescriptions, it provides the *biological stage context* they sit in. **Menopause and
andropause** as endocrine inflections are owned by `reports/sections/13-endocrine-hormones.md §4` and
`02-domains/N-womens-longevity.md`. **Sarcopenia** definition/diagnosis is owned by Domain E

## 1. The shape of a human life — and why "adult advice" is incomplete

Picture the trajectory of physiological capacity — VO2max, bone mineral density, muscle mass, kidney
function, immune competence — across a lifespan. For almost every system it is the same arc: a **steep climb
to a peak in the 20s–30s, a plateau, then a long decline.** Two facts about that arc reorganize how to think
about health:

- **The peak you build is the asset you spend.** Peak bone mass is banked by the late 20s and drawn down for
  the rest of life; a higher peak means more margin before the fracture threshold. The same logic applies to
  VO2max, muscle, and cognitive reserve. **The interventions with the longest leverage happen on the way up**,
  in childhood and early adulthood — a stage most "longevity" content ignores because its audience is already
  middle-aged.
- **The *start* of the arc is set before birth.** Organ number (nephrons, pancreatic β-cells, cardiomyocytes),
  metabolic set-points, and stress-axis calibration are substantially fixed in fetal and early life — the
  **developmental origins** story below. The arc you spend your adult life defending was partly *drawn* before
  you drew your first breath.

@@FIG:L10-capacity-arc@@

This is why an adult-only manual is incomplete: the two ends of life (developmental and geriatric/terminal)
operate by **different rules** than the defend-the-peak middle, and the single highest-leverage health
decisions in a population — maternal nutrition, childhood activity, deprescribing in elders, honest
end-of-life planning — live at those ends.

## 2. Developmental Origins (DOHaD) — the Barker hypothesis

This is the deepest and least-known idea in the section, so it goes first: **the environment in the womb and
in early infancy programs adult disease risk, decades before the disease appears.**

### 2.1 The hypothesis and its evidence

In the 1980s the British epidemiologist **David Barker** noticed that the regions of England with the
**highest infant mortality** in the early 1900s were the same regions with the **highest coronary heart
disease** mortality two generations later — and that, individually, **lower birthweight predicted higher
adult heart disease, hypertension, stroke, and type 2 diabetes.** The "**Barker hypothesis**" (also called
the **fetal origins** or, broadened, **Developmental Origins of Health and Disease — DOHaD**) proposes that a
fetus undernourished at a critical window **adapts** its physiology to expect a poor environment, and that
this adaptation becomes a liability if later life is one of plenty (Barker, *Lancet* 1996,
`10.1016/s0140-6736(96)04547-3`; the definitive modern review is Gluckman, Hanson, Cooper & Thornburg,
*NEJM* 2008, `10.1056/NEJMra0708473`).

The mechanistic refinement is the "**thrifty phenotype**" (Hales & Barker, *Diabetologia* 1992,
`10.1007/BF00400248`): a fetus that senses scarcity down-regulates growth and reprograms its metabolism to be
fuel-sparing — fewer pancreatic β-cells, more insulin-resistant tissues, a "store every calorie" set-point.
In a matching scarce world that is adaptive; dropped into a modern calorie-rich world it is a **predictive
adaptive mismatch** that drives obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease.

### 2.2 The cleanest natural experiment: the Dutch Hunger Winter

Because you cannot randomize fetal nutrition, the strongest causal evidence comes from a tragedy used as a
natural experiment. During the **Dutch Hunger Winter (1944–45)**, a sharp, time-limited, well-documented
famine hit a previously well-nourished population. Cohorts of people conceived or gestating during the famine
have been followed for **70+ years** (the work of **Tessa Roseboom** and colleagues). The findings are a
DOHaD textbook:

- Famine exposure **timing matters**: exposure in *early* gestation (when organs are forming) produced
  different adult outcomes (more obesity, altered lipids, more cardiovascular disease) than exposure in *late*
  gestation (more glucose intolerance) — consistent with **critical windows** rather than a single dose-effect.
- Effects persisted into **late-life brain and metabolic health**, decades after a famine that lasted months.
- Some signatures appeared **epigenetic** — altered DNA methylation at metabolically relevant genes (e.g.
  *IGF2*) measured six decades later, a candidate molecular memory of the exposure.

**Honest grading:** DOHaD is `cohort`/`mechanistic` — strong, replicated, biologically coherent, but
fundamentally **observational** (Rule 2). The famine cohorts and animal models make the causal story
compelling, but you cannot read a personal "your birthweight = your destiny" verdict off it. Birthweight is a
**predictor**, not a fixed sentence (Rule 1): adult lifestyle still moves the same risks, and most low-birth-
weight people are healthy. The hypothesis's value is **population-level and upstream** — it relocates the
highest-leverage cardiometabolic intervention to *maternal and early-infant nutrition*, a generation before
the statin.

### 2.3 The first 1000 days and epigenetic programming (tie to fundamentals)

The practical distillation of DOHaD is the "**first 1000 days**" frame (conception → ~2nd birthday): the
window in which growth, organ development, the microbiome, and the stress and metabolic axes are most
**plastic** and therefore most **programmable** — for good or ill. Adequate maternal nutrition, avoidance of
toxic exposures (alcohol, smoking, severe stress), breastfeeding where possible, and responsive early
caregiving are the levers with the longest reach in a human life.

**The canon tie-in.** Programming is the body implementing a **control-theory** principle: a developing system
**sets its operating parameters from early inputs** because, evolutionarily, early conditions predicted later
ones. The molecular substrate is **epigenetics** — heritable-through-cell-division changes in gene *expression*
(DNA methylation, histone modification) without changes in the DNA *sequence*. This is the same
**information-vs-substrate** distinction that runs through `bucket-canon/04-information` (the program is not the
hardware) and the gene-regulation foundations in `bucket-canon/05-biophysics`: the genome is the score, the
epigenome is which notes get played, and early-life environment is a conductor that can re-mark the score for
life. DOHaD is, at root, **a developmental control system reading its environment and committing to a
set-point** — and the reason "predictor ≠ lever" bites so hard here is that by adulthood the set-point is
largely *committed*.

## 3. Pregnancy & Perinatal — evidence-based vs old wives

Pregnancy is the one stage where the manual's usual "lifestyle levers" act on **two** bodies at once, and it
is unusually polluted with folklore. Here the honest line between **graded intervention** and **myth** is
sharp, because — unlike DOHaD — many pregnancy questions *do* have RCTs.

### 3.1 The micronutrients that are genuinely load-bearing

| Nutrient | What the evidence says | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Folate (folic acid)** | The single best-evidenced perinatal supplement. The **MRC Vitamin Study** (RCT, *Lancet* 1991, PMID 1677062) showed periconceptional folic acid **cut neural-tube-defect recurrence by ~72%.** Must start **before conception** (the neural tube closes by ~week 4, often before pregnancy is known) — which is why population **fortification** of flour is a major public-health win. | `rct` — strong |
| **Iodine** | Iodine builds fetal thyroid hormone, which builds the fetal brain. Deficiency is **the leading preventable cause of intellectual impairment worldwide.** Even *mild-to-moderate* maternal deficiency in a developed country (UK) predicted **lower child IQ and reading scores** (Bath et al., ALSPAC, *Lancet* 2013, `10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60436-5`). | `cohort` (mild deficiency) / `rct`+public-health (overt) — strong |
| **Omega-3 (DHA/EPA)** | A Cochrane review (Middleton et al. 2018, `10.1002/14651858.CD003402.pub3`) found supplementation **modestly reduces preterm (<37wk) and early preterm (<34wk) birth** and low birthweight. Real but **modest**; the heavily-marketed "DHA makes a smarter baby" claim is **not** robustly supported by the cognitive endpoints. | `meta` for preterm birth (modest); cognition `mixed` |
| **Iron / Vitamin D / others** | Correct **documented deficiency** (iron for anemia; vitamin D where deficient). The pattern is the manual's signature: **replace a real deficiency, don't mega-dose a sufficiency** (cross-ref §13, §03). | deficiency-only |

**The honest synthesis:** a standard **prenatal vitamin started pre-conception** (folate + iodine the
load-bearing pieces), plus correcting documented deficiencies, captures essentially all the evidence-based
supplement benefit. The rest of the prenatal-supplement aisle is mostly marketing.

@@FIG:Y04-prenatal@@

### 3.2 Exercise in pregnancy — the myth is "rest"

The durable old-wives' advice was to *rest* and avoid exertion. The evidence inverts it: for
**uncomplicated pregnancies**, regular **moderate exercise is safe and beneficial** — it reduces gestational
diabetes, excessive weight gain, and likely pre-eclampsia and cesarean risk, without raising the risk of
preterm birth or harming the fetus (the consensus of ACOG and equivalent guidelines; `meta`/guideline tier).
The real precautions are specific and narrow: avoid **supine** positions late in pregnancy, contact/fall-risk
sports, scuba, and overheating; and high-risk obstetric conditions need individualized clearance. **The default
for a healthy pregnancy is to keep moving, not to stop** — a clean case of folklore (rest) refuted by evidence
(activity).

### 3.3 The two big pathologies, and what's evidence-based

- **Gestational diabetes (GDM).** Pregnancy is a physiological insulin-resistance stress test; when the
  pancreas can't keep up, GDM results — raising risks of macrosomia, birth complications, and (a DOHaD link)
  the child's later metabolic risk. The landmark **ACHOIS trial** (Crowther et al., *NEJM* 2005,
  `10.1056/NEJMoa042973`) showed that **treating GDM** (diet, glucose monitoring, insulin as needed)
  **reduced serious perinatal complications** — establishing that screening and treatment are worthwhile, not
  just labelling. `rct` — strong.
- **Pre-eclampsia.** A dangerous hypertensive disorder of pregnancy (the maternal side of placental
  dysfunction). The big evidence-based prevention is **low-dose aspirin** in high-risk women: the **ASPRE
  trial** (Rolnik et al., *NEJM* 2017, `10.1056/NEJMoa1704559`; companion analyses PMID 28888591) showed
  aspirin **markedly reduced preterm pre-eclampsia** when started in the first trimester in screened
  high-risk women. This is now standard guideline care. `rct` — strong.

### 3.4 "Old wives" vs evidence — the honest ledger

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Eating for two" (double calories) | **Myth.** Energy needs rise only modestly (~+340–450 kcal/day in 2nd/3rd trimester). Excess gain raises GDM and macrosomia risk. |
| "Rest, don't exercise" | **Myth** for uncomplicated pregnancy — moderate exercise is protective (§3.2). |
| "Detox / cleanse for the baby" | **Myth and potentially harmful.** No evidence; some "detox" products are unsafe in pregnancy. |
| "Avoid all caffeine" | **Overstated.** Moderate intake (≤~200 mg/day) is generally considered acceptable by most guidelines. |
| "No alcohol is the safe level" | **Evidence-based.** No threshold is established as safe; abstention is the guideline. |
| "Take folate before you're pregnant" | **Evidence-based and load-bearing** — the neural tube closes early (§3.1). |

## 4. Fertility & Reproduction — the honest data

Fertility is a market drenched in hope, and therefore in overclaiming. The honest version is partly hard
(the **age** data) and partly humbling (the **supplement** data).

### 4.1 Age and fertility — the biology, both sexes

- **Female fertility declines with age, and the decline is real, gradual, then steep.** Cycle-by-cycle
  conception probability falls measurably from the late 20s, more steeply after ~35, and sharply after ~40 —
  driven by **declining oocyte *quality* (aneuploidy) more than quantity** (Dunson et al., *Hum Reprod* 2002,
  `10.1093/humrep/17.5.1399`, which quantified the age decline in both the *level* and the *duration* of the
  fertile window). The egg pool is **fixed at birth and non-renewing** — there is no training it back. This is
  one of the section's hardest "predictor that is also nearly destiny" facts. `cohort` — strong.
- **Male fertility also declines with age — later and more gradually, but really.** Sperm count, motility, and
  especially **DNA integrity** worsen with paternal age (large clinic series: SCSA-defined DNA fragmentation
  rises with age, *Fertil Steril* 2020, `10.1016/j.fertnstert.2020.03.028`), and advanced paternal age is
  associated with modestly higher rates of some offspring conditions. The "men can father children at any age"
  story is **half-true** — possible, but with rising sperm-quality and offspring risks. `cohort` — moderate.

@@FIG:L11-fertility-decline@@

### 4.2 IVF and egg freezing — what the numbers actually are

The honest framing fights two opposite distortions: clinic marketing that oversells success, and fatalism
that ignores real options.

- **IVF success is dominated by *egg age*, not the year you do it.** Live-birth rates per cycle fall steeply
  with the age of the eggs used — high in the early 30s, much lower by the early 40s with a woman's own eggs.
  Donor-egg (younger eggs) cycles largely **erase the recipient's age effect**, which is itself proof that
  **egg quality is the binding constraint.**
- **Egg freezing (oocyte vitrification) is a real but probabilistic insurance policy, not a guarantee.** Outcomes
  depend heavily on **age at freezing** (younger = better) and **number of eggs stored** (more = better), and a
  large fraction of women who freeze never return to use them. The technology works (Cobo et al., *Fertil Steril*
  2021, `10.1016/j.fertnstert.2021.02.006`), but the honest counseling is in **probabilities and egg-counts**,
  not promises — and freezing at 38 buys far less insurance than at 32. `cohort`/registry — solid, but
  individual outcome is probabilistic.

### 4.3 The fertility-supplement industry — honest grade

A large supplement market sells **CoQ10, DHEA, inositol, antioxidant blends, "egg-quality" and "sperm-boost"
formulas.** The honest evidence:

- **Female subfertility antioxidants:** the Cochrane review (Showell et al. 2020,
  `10.1002/14651858.CD007807.pub4`) found **low-quality evidence and no clear, reliable benefit** of antioxidant
  supplementation on live birth or clinical pregnancy in subfertile women — the evidence is too weak to
  recommend. `meta` — **low certainty / largely null.**
- **Male subfertility antioxidants:** the Cochrane review (de Ligny et al. 2022,
  `10.1002/14651858.CD007411.pub5`) found the evidence **of very low quality** and could not confirm a reliable
  effect on live birth. `meta` — **low certainty.**
- **CoQ10, DHEA, inositol** in specific contexts (e.g. inositol in PCOS, DHEA in poor ovarian responders) have
  *some* small-trial signals but **remain low-certainty and context-specific**, not general "fertility boosters."

**The honest reading:** correct genuine deficiencies, stop smoking, manage weight and alcohol, treat
underlying conditions — those have real fertility leverage. The **supplement-as-fertility-cure story is sold
with far more confidence than the trials support** (Rule 1: a hopeful mechanism is not a proven outcome). The
biggest non-negotiable lever — **age** — is the one no supplement moves.

## 5. Childhood & Adolescence — building the "health bank account"

This is the **build-the-peak** stage, the highest-leverage and most-neglected window in the lifespan arc (§1).
The framing that organizes it: childhood and adolescence are when you make **deposits** into health accounts
you will draw down for 60+ years.

### 5.1 Peak bone mass — the account with the longest maturity

**~90% of adult bone mass is laid down by ~age 18–20, and the peak is reached by the late 20s** — after which
it can only be defended, not substantially added (Weaver et al., National Osteoporosis Foundation position
statement, *Osteoporos Int* 2016, `10.1007/s00198-015-3440-3`). The determinants in youth — **weight-bearing
and impact exercise, adequate calcium and vitamin D, and avoiding smoking/under-fueling** — *build a higher
peak*, which means **more margin before the osteoporosis fracture threshold** decades later (cross-ref §04 §4.1
and the menopausal bone-loss slope, §13/Domain N). A childhood spent loading the skeleton is, literally, the
asset menopause and aging will spend. `meta`/position-statement — strong.

The same "build now, spend later" logic applies to **muscle, cardiorespiratory fitness, and motor skill**:
movement competence and an aerobic base built young are easier to *defend* than to *rebuild* in midlife
(cross-ref `crf-vo2max-strongest-mortality-predictor`).

### 5.2 Puberty and the adolescent brain

- **Puberty** is the endocrine ignition of the reproductive (HPG) axis (§13): a cascade of growth, sexual
  maturation, and — relevant to the bone account — the **adolescent growth spurt** during which bone accrual
  is fastest. Timing varies widely and is normal across a broad range.
- **The adolescent brain is under construction until the mid-20s.** The maturational order is the key fact:
  the **limbic/reward system matures *early*** (driving sensation-seeking and reward sensitivity) while the
  **prefrontal cortex — judgment, impulse control, long-horizon planning — matures *last*, into the mid-20s.**
  This **developmental mismatch** is the neurobiological reason adolescence carries elevated risk-taking; it is
  not a character defect but a stage. The practical reading: adolescents need **scaffolding** (structure,
  sleep, guardrails) precisely while their internal regulators are still being wired, and the *brain's*
  plasticity here is itself a deposit — skills, habits, and exposures in adolescence shape adult cognition and
  mental health. `mechanistic`/`cohort` (developmental neuroscience).

### 5.3 Sleep and screens — the modern adolescent problem

Two facts collide in adolescence. First, **puberty shifts the circadian clock *later*** (a biological phase
delay in melatonin onset — cross-ref §13 §9 and Domain I): teenagers are genuinely not sleepy at 10pm and not
naturally awake at 6am — which is the strongest physiological argument for **later school start times.** Second,
**screens make it worse.** A systematic review (Hale & Guan, *Sleep Med Rev* 2015,
`10.1016/j.smrv.2014.07.007`) found that **screen time is consistently associated with worse sleep** (later
bedtimes, shorter duration) across children and adolescents in the large majority of studies, via displacement
of sleep time, bright-light melatonin suppression, and arousal. Interventions to reduce children's screen use
modestly improve sleep (*J Sleep Res* 2021, `10.1111/jsr.13130`).

**Honest grade:** the screen→sleep association is `cohort`/`cross-sectional` and largely correlational
(causal direction is partly bidirectional), but it is consistent, mechanistically plausible, and the
low-risk lever — **protect adolescent sleep duration and timing, get screens out of the bedroom, align school
start times to teen biology** — is well worth pulling. Adolescent sleep is itself a deposit: it underwrites
the brain construction in §5.2.

## 6. Adulthood — peak, then defend (cross-reference)

The long middle of life — the manual's default audience — is covered in depth elsewhere; this section only
places it on the time axis. The one-line trajectory:

- **Young adult (20s–30s) — build the peak.** Maximal trainability, peak bone accrual, the highest fitness
  ceiling you will ever have. Build strength, VO2max, and metabolic health while it is cheapest. (§04 §4.1.)
- **Midlife (40s–50s) — defend it.** Sarcopenia and **power loss begin** (power declines faster than strength);
  for women this overlaps the **menopausal transition** (the single highest-leverage decade for the bone/strength
  levers); for men, the gradual **andropause** testosterone slope begins. Midlife is the cheapest time to defend
  the peak and start tracking functional biomarkers while they are still easy to move. (§04 §4.2; §13 §4;
  Domain N.)

## 7. Geriatrics & Frailty — the stage with its own rules

After ~65, and especially ~75+, the body operates by **different rules**, and generic adult advice
under-serves it. The organizing concepts are **frailty**, **sarcopenia**, **polypharmacy**, **falls**, and
**cognitive aging** — and the goal that ties them together is the **compression of morbidity** (§8).

### 7.1 The frailty phenotype (Fried)

**Frailty** is not the same as "being old" or "having diseases" — it is a distinct, measurable state of
**diminished physiological reserve** that leaves a person vulnerable to catastrophic decline from a minor
stressor (an infection, a fall, a new drug). **Linda Fried** operationalized it as the **frailty phenotype**
(*J Gerontol A* 2001, `10.1093/gerona/56.3.m146`): a person is frail meeting **≥3 of 5** criteria —

@@FIG:N06-frailty-pentagon@@

1–2 criteria = "**pre-frail**" (an intervention window); 0 = robust. The phenotype **predicts** falls,
hospitalization, disability, and death — and it is a **cohort**-validated readout (Rule 1: it is a powerful
*predictor*; the *lever* is the multi-domain work below, not a "frailty drug"). A competing model, the
**deficit-accumulation Frailty Index** (Rockwood), counts the *proportion* of a long list of deficits present;
the two capture overlapping reality. `cohort` — strong, foundational.

### 7.2 Sarcopenia (cross-reference)

The muscular engine of frailty is **sarcopenia** — the age-related loss of muscle **strength** (better than
mass as the defining criterion) and function (EWGSOP2: Cruz-Jentoft et al., *Age Ageing* 2019,
`10.1093/ageing/afz046`; cross-ref Domain E `sarcopenia-strength-defining-ewgsop2`). The **lever is the §04
§4.3 inversion**: resistance training, **power** work, adequate **protein (HIGHER in older adults, ~1.0–1.2+
g/kg)**, and balance — which work **even in frail nonagenarians** (Fiatarone). This section does not restate
that prescription; it flags that **sarcopenia is the modifiable core of frailty**, the one part of the frailty
syndrome with a strong, proven, non-pharmacologic lever.

### 7.3 Polypharmacy and deprescribing

A defining and under-recognized geriatric hazard: older adults accumulate medications across decades and
specialists, and at some point **the drugs themselves become a leading cause of harm** — falls, confusion,
hospitalization, interactions — a problem amplified by age-altered pharmacokinetics (the aging kidney and
liver clear drugs more slowly) and the **prescribing cascade** (a side effect of drug A treated with drug B).

**Deprescribing** — the planned, supervised **reduction or stopping** of drugs whose harms now outweigh their
benefits — is a real, evidence-informed clinical discipline (Scott et al., "Reducing inappropriate
polypharmacy: the process of deprescribing," *JAMA Intern Med* 2015, `10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0324`).
Structured tools — **STOPP/START** criteria and the **Beers list** of potentially inappropriate medications in
older adults — flag the usual suspects (long-acting benzodiazepines, anticholinergics, certain NSAIDs and
sedatives). **The honest lever, and it is a strong one:** in geriatrics, the highest-yield "intervention" is
often **subtraction, not addition** — a periodic medication review asking *"is this still doing more good than
harm for this person now?"* This is the geriatric face of "predictor ≠ lever" inverted: sometimes the lever is
**removing** a thing. `cohort`/expert-consensus; deprescribing RCTs are growing and broadly favorable for
specific drug classes.

### 7.4 Falls

**Falls are a leading cause of injury, disability, loss of independence, and death in older adults** — and
they are **not** an unavoidable part of aging. They are multifactorial (weakness, balance loss, vision,
postural hypotension, environmental hazards, and — see §7.3 — **medications**), and they are **preventable**:
structured **strength + balance** programs reduce fall *rate* (World Falls Guidelines, Montero-Odasso et al.,
*Age & Ageing* 2022, `10.1093/ageing/afac205`; cross-ref §04 §4.3). The practical stack is unglamorous and
effective: **train balance and leg power, review and deprescribe falls-risk drugs, correct vision, address
postural blood pressure, and remove home hazards.**

### 7.5 Cognitive aging

The honest frame separates **normal** from **pathological**. **Normal cognitive aging** includes slower
processing speed and weaker word-finding/"tip-of-the-tongue" and working memory — while **crystallized
knowledge and vocabulary are largely preserved or improve.** This is *not* dementia. **Pathological** decline
(Alzheimer's and related dementias) is a disease process, not an inevitability of years. The evidence-based
**risk-reduction** levers overlap the whole manual — the **Lancet Commission** modifiable dementia risk factors
(hearing loss, hypertension, physical inactivity, diabetes, smoking, depression, social isolation, education,
air pollution, etc.) — and the strongest single behavioral lever is the one this manual keeps returning to:
**physical activity and cardiorespiratory fitness** (cross-ref §08 brain/cognitive section and Domain Q).
**Cognitive reserve** — built by education, complex work, and engagement — is the brain's version of the bone
account: more reserve means more buffer before symptoms cross the threshold.

## 8. The goal that ties geriatrics together — compression of morbidity

The organizing aspiration of the whole geriatric stage, and arguably the whole manual, is **James Fries's
"compression of morbidity"** (*NEJM* 1980, `10.1056/NEJM198007173030304`). The idea: if the **onset of chronic
disability** can be pushed *later* faster than the age of death moves, then the **period of sickness at the end
of life is compressed** into a shorter window — more years of healthy function, fewer years of decline.

@@FIG:L12-compression-morbidity@@

This is the precise, rigorous statement of the manual's recurring theme: **healthspan, not lifespan, is the
target.** It reframes every geriatric lever above — strength to defend independence, balance to prevent the
falls that start the terminal cascade, deprescribing to remove drug harm, cognitive reserve to delay
disability — as instruments for **squaring the curve**: staying functional almost to the end, rather than
adding low-quality years. (Cross-ref the female "health-survival paradox" — `health-survival-paradox`, Domain N
— which is the same point with a sex lens: the population that already has the *years* needs the *function*.)

## 9. End of Life & Palliative — honest framing

The last stage is the one the wellness industry refuses to discuss, which is exactly why an honest manual must.
The governing distinction is the manual's own: **healthspan vs lifespan** — and at the very end, **the highest-
value intervention is sometimes *less* intervention.**

### 9.1 The limits of intervention

Every lever in this manual has a domain of validity, and near the end of life many of them **invert**. Aggressive
disease-directed treatment can, in advanced terminal illness, **add suffering without adding meaningful time or
function** — more hospitalizations, more procedures, less time at home, worse quality of remaining life. The
honest, evidence-based reframing is that **quality of death** is a real and measurable outcome, and that the
goal shifts from *extending the curve* to *comfort, dignity, agency, and meaning* in the time that remains. This
is not defeat; it is matching the intervention to the stage (Rule 3, at the far end: the floor is comfort).

### 9.2 What the evidence actually shows — palliative care is not "giving up"

The single most important evidence point corrects the deepest misconception. In a landmark RCT, **early
palliative care** integrated alongside standard oncology for metastatic lung cancer patients (Temel et al.,
*NEJM* 2010, `10.1056/NEJMoa1000678`) improved **quality of life and mood**, reduced aggressive end-of-life
treatment — **and the early-palliative-care group lived *longer*, not shorter.** Palliative care is **symptom-
and-meaning-focused care, not the abandonment of care**, and the data show it can deliver *both* better quality
*and* (at least non-inferior, sometimes superior) quantity. `rct` — strong, and counter-intuitive.

### 9.3 Advance directives and advance care planning

The instrument that lets a person's values govern their end-of-life care is the **advance directive** (a living
will + a designated healthcare proxy/durable power of attorney). The evidence that planning *works*: a
randomized trial of **advance care planning** in elderly inpatients (Detering et al., *BMJ* 2010,
`10.1136/bmj.c1345`) found it **improved end-of-life care, increased the likelihood that patients' wishes were
known and followed, and improved satisfaction while reducing stress, anxiety, and depression in surviving
relatives.** The honest takeaway: the conversation and the document are a **high-value, low-cost, evidence-
backed intervention** that most people defer until it is too late to have it. `rct` — supports.

### 9.4 The honest synthesis on the end

The end of life exposes the manual's core thesis most starkly: **more is not always better, and lifespan is not
the only — or even the primary — good.** A life optimized purely for *length* can end in a long, low-quality
decline; a life optimized for *healthspan and meaning* aims at the compression of morbidity (§8) and, when the
end comes, at a death that matches the person's values. The same evidence discipline that debunks a fertility
supplement or an anti-aging hormone applies here: **palliative care, honestly graded, outperforms aggressive
intervention on the outcomes that matter at the end** — and saying so is not pessimism, it is the same honesty
the rest of the manual demands.

## Go deeper

A short, honestly-annotated reading list. Grades flag where a source is a hypothesis (DOHaD, compression of
morbidity) versus settled trial evidence.

1. **Gluckman PD, Hanson MA, Cooper C, Thornburg KL. "Effect of in utero and early-life conditions on adult
   health and disease."** *NEJM* 2008, `10.1056/NEJMra0708473` — the definitive modern review of DOHaD/the
   Barker hypothesis; read *with* **Barker** (*Lancet* 1996, `10.1016/s0140-6736(96)04547-3`) and the
   **Hales & Barker thrifty-phenotype** original (*Diabetologia* 1992, `10.1007/BF00400248`) and the **Dutch
   Hunger Winter** cohort work (Roseboom). **Tier: cohort/mechanistic — strong but observational** (you cannot
   randomize a fetus).
2. **MRC Vitamin Study (folic acid, NTD prevention)** (*Lancet* 1991, PMID 1677062) + **Bath et al. iodine/ALSPAC**
   (*Lancet* 2013, `10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60436-5`) + **Middleton et al. omega-3 Cochrane**
   (`10.1002/14651858.CD003402.pub3`). The evidence base for which prenatal nutrients are actually load-bearing.
   **Tier: rct (folate) / cohort (mild iodine) / meta (omega-3, modest) — strong for folate+iodine.**
3. **Crowther et al. ACHOIS (GDM treatment, *NEJM* 2005, `10.1056/NEJMoa042973`)** + **Rolnik et al. ASPRE
   (aspirin for pre-eclampsia, *NEJM* 2017, `10.1056/NEJMoa1704559`).** The two big perinatal-pathology RCTs that
   define evidence-based obstetric prevention. **Tier: rct — strong.**
4. **Dunson et al. "Changes with age in the level and duration of fertility" (*Hum Reprod* 2002,
   `10.1093/humrep/17.5.1399`)** + the antioxidant Cochrane reviews (**Showell 2020, `10.1002/14651858.CD007807.pub4`**;
   **de Ligny 2022, `10.1002/14651858.CD007411.pub5`**). Together: age is the dominant, unmovable fertility lever,
   and the supplement industry is **low-certainty / largely null.** **Tier: cohort (age) — strong; meta (supplements)
   — low certainty.**
5. **Weaver et al. — National Osteoporosis Foundation position statement on peak bone mass** (*Osteoporos Int* 2016,
   `10.1007/s00198-015-3440-3`). The evidence that ~90% of bone is banked by ~18 and the childhood levers that
   raise the peak — the "build the account" foundation. **Tier: meta/position statement — strong.** Pair with
   **Hale & Guan screen/sleep** (*Sleep Med Rev* 2015, `10.1016/j.smrv.2014.07.007`) for the adolescent sleep story.
6. **Fried et al. "Frailty in older adults: evidence for a phenotype"** (*J Gerontol A* 2001,
   `10.1093/gerona/56.3.m146`) + **Scott et al. deprescribing** (*JAMA Intern Med* 2015,
   `10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0324`) + **World Falls Guidelines** (*Age & Ageing* 2022, `10.1093/ageing/afac205`).
   The geriatric core: measure frailty, subtract harmful drugs, prevent falls. **Tier: cohort + expert consensus —
   strong/foundational.**
7. **Fries JF. "Aging, natural death, and the compression of morbidity"** (*NEJM* 1980,
   `10.1056/NEJM198007173030304`) + **Temel et al. "Early palliative care for metastatic NSCLC"** (*NEJM* 2010,
   `10.1056/NEJMoa1000678`) + **Detering et al. advance care planning RCT** (*BMJ* 2010, `10.1136/bmj.c1345`). The
   bookend goals: compress morbidity, then meet the end with palliative care and a plan — better quality *and*
   (Temel) better survival. **Tier: hypothesis (Fries, theoretical/cohort) → rct (Temel, Detering) — the RCTs are
   strong and counter-intuitive.**

> **Honesty footer.** This section refuses two opposite errors. The first is **adult-centrism** — pretending
> the defend-the-peak middle is the whole story, when the longest-leverage health decisions in a life are made
> before birth, in childhood, and at the very end. The second is **the wellness industry's two favorite lies at
> the edges of life**: that you can supplement your way past the age wall in fertility, and that more
> intervention is always better at the end. The honest lifespan view is that the rules *change* with the stage —
> build early, defend the middle, subtract and protect function late, and meet the end with planning and
> palliative honesty rather than denial. Grade accordingly.
