### John Snow

|---|---|

| **primary works** | *On the Mode of Communication of Cholera* (1855); the Broad Street pump investigation (1854) |

**Field contribution.** Founded modern epidemiology: by mapping cholera deaths and tracing the 1854 Soho outbreak to a single contaminated water pump on Broad Street — years before the cholera bacterium was identified — he proved waterborne transmission against the dominant miasma (bad-air) theory and demonstrated evidence-driven disease control by removing the pump handle.

**Why indexed.** The origin of evidence-based public health and spatial/observational epidemiology: a method (find the pattern, infer the cause, intervene) that long predated and outpaced knowledge of the pathogen itself.

**Built on.** vital-statistics record-keeping (William Farr's data)

**Influenced / downstream.** all of epidemiology; the population-vs-individual reasoning later formalized by Geoffrey Rose

**Canon bridge.** method-tier (causal inference from observational pattern)

**Disputed / known unknowns.** The "pump handle ended the outbreak" story is partly mythologized — the epidemic was already waning — but the inferential method is the durable contribution.
