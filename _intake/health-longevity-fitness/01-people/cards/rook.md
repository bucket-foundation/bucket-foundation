**Field contribution.** Reframed Strachan's hygiene hypothesis into the "old friends" / biodiversity model: it is not childhood infections but co-evolved commensal and environmental microorganisms (soil, gut, skin) that train regulatory immunity. Their loss in urban industrialized life — not lack of pathogens — drives the rise in allergic, autoimmune, and chronic-inflammatory disease.

**Why indexed.** The mechanistic correction that turned a puzzling epidemiological pattern into a coherent immune-regulation framework; load-bearing for microbiome, inflammaging, and "rewilding" claims (separating the supported core from the overreach).

**Built on.** Strachan's hygiene hypothesis; regulatory-T-cell immunology; microbiome science

**Influenced / downstream.** microbiome-immune-education research; environmental-microbial-exposure interventions

**Canon bridge.** immune-environment hypothesis lineage

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Causal human interventions ("rewilding" the microbiome to prevent disease) remain largely unproven; the framework outruns the trial evidence.
