**Field contribution.** With Richard Doll, carried the British Doctors Study through 50 years of follow-up, quantifying that lifelong smoking costs ~10 years of life and that cessation — especially before age 40 — recovers most of the excess risk. Separately, co-developed the large-simple-trial and meta-analysis methodology that disciplines how treatment effects are estimated.

**Why indexed.** The foundational magnitude estimate for the single largest modifiable mortality exposure, and a methodological foundation (big numbers, simple designs, pooled analysis) for distinguishing real effects from noise across the corpus.

**Built on.** Doll & Hill's 1950s smoking-lung-cancer work; statistical methods of randomized trials

**Influenced / downstream.** Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' Collaboration; modern meta-analytic epidemiology; tobacco-control policy

**Canon bridge.** method-tier (large-trial/meta-analysis) + exposure magnitude

**Disputed / known unknowns.** None on the smoking findings; "Peto's paradox" (why large animals don't get more cancer) is a separate open question he named.
