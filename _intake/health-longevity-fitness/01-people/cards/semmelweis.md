### Ignaz Semmelweis

| | |
|---|---|
| **id** | semmelweis |
| **lifespan** | 1818-1865 |
| **era** | 19th c. |
| **region / tradition** | Vienna General Hospital, Austria-Hungary — obstetrics |
| **domains** | P (Clinical & population prevention) |
| **evidence_posture** | `mainstream-rigorous` |
| **primary works** | *Die Ätiologie, der Begriff und die Prophylaxis des Kindbettfiebers* (1861); chlorinated-lime handwashing data (1847) |

**Field contribution.** Demonstrated, from clinic mortality data, that physicians carrying "cadaverous particles" from autopsies to the delivery ward caused fatal puerperal (childbed) fever, and that handwashing with chlorinated lime cut maternal mortality dramatically — a decade before germ theory explained why.

**Why indexed.** The original demonstration that the cheapest intervention (hand hygiene) can be the most powerful — and the canonical case study in how correct evidence is resisted ("the Semmelweis reflex") when it lacks a mechanism and indicts the practitioners.

**Built on.** comparative ward mortality statistics

**Influenced / downstream.** Lister's antisepsis; modern hand-hygiene and infection-control doctrine

**Canon bridge.** method-tier (evidence-before-mechanism)

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Contemporaries rejected him partly for his combative communication and lack of a causal theory; vindication came posthumously via Pasteur/Koch.
