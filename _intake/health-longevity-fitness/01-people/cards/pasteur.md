### Louis Pasteur

| | |
|---|---|
| **id** | pasteur |
| **lifespan** | 1822-1895 |

| **primary works** | swan-neck-flask refutation of spontaneous generation (1860s); rabies and anthrax vaccines (1880s) |

**Field contribution.** Co-founded germ theory: showed with controlled swan-neck-flask experiments that microbes do not arise spontaneously, established that specific microorganisms cause fermentation and disease, invented pasteurization, and developed the first laboratory-attenuated vaccines (anthrax, rabies).

**Why indexed.** Foundational to all microbiology and infectious-disease medicine — the conceptual shift from miasma/spontaneous-generation to specific microbial causation that makes modern sanitation, sterilization, and vaccination possible.

**Built on.** earlier microscopy (Leeuwenhoek); Semmelweis/Snow's epidemiological hints

**Influenced / downstream.** Koch (postulates), Lister (antisepsis), all of vaccinology and microbiology

**Canon bridge.** canon-adjacent 05-biophysics / foundations of biology

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Priority and method disputes (e.g. with Pouchet on spontaneous generation, Toussaint on anthrax attenuation) are historically documented; the core results stand.
