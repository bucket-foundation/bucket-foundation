| **primary works** | *Cognitive reserve (Lancet Neurol / Neuropsychologia)* (2002–2012) |

**Field contribution.** Formalized the cognitive-reserve construct — the observation that, for a given amount of brain pathology, people with more education, occupational complexity, and cognitive engagement show fewer clinical symptoms — distinguishing brain "reserve" (hardware) from "reserve" as more efficient/flexible network use.

**Why indexed.** Explains why equal Alzheimer's pathology yields unequal dementia, and underpins the "use it or lose it" and lifelong-cognitive-engagement framing in dementia prevention — while honestly flagging that reserve is largely an observational predictor, not a proven lever.

**Built on.** clinicopathological discordance observations; epidemiology of education and dementia

**Influenced / downstream.** dementia-prevention frameworks (Livingston/Lancet Commission); lifestyle-and-cognition research

**Canon bridge.** cognitive-aging construct

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Reserve is hard to measure directly and confounded by reverse causation and socioeconomic factors; whether deliberately "building reserve" prevents dementia is unproven.
