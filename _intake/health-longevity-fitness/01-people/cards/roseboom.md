**Field contribution.** Leads the long-running Dutch Hunger Winter cohort — adults conceived during the 1944-45 Dutch famine — providing the cleanest quasi-causal natural experiment for the developmental origins of health and disease: timing-specific prenatal undernutrition produces distinct adult cardiometabolic, reproductive, and late-life brain outcomes, with persistent DNA-methylation signatures (e.g. at IGF2) detectable 60-70 years later.

**Why indexed.** Converts Barker's fetal-origins hypothesis from association into a sharp natural experiment; demonstrates that a window-of-development exposure can set lifelong disease risk — the highest-leverage cardiometabolic lever sitting a generation upstream.

**Built on.** Barker's developmental-origins hypothesis; Hales-Barker thrifty-phenotype model

**Influenced / downstream.** DOHaD field; epigenetic-programming research; maternal-nutrition public health

**Canon bridge.** life-stages / developmental-origins mechanism

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Methylation-to-phenotype causality is still partly inferential; generalizing acute-famine findings to ordinary nutritional variation requires caution.
