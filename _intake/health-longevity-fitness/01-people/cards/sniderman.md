**Field contribution.** Argued that apolipoprotein B (apoB) - a count of atherogenic particle number, one apoB per LDL/VLDL/IDL/Lp(a) particle - is a more accurate index of cardiovascular risk than LDL-cholesterol concentration, and defined the 'discordance' framework in which apoB and LDL-C diverge and apoB tracks outcomes better.

**Why indexed.** The principal architect of the case that particle number, not cholesterol mass, is the causal lipid exposure to measure - the conceptual root of the apoB-over-LDL-C debate.

**Built on.** Fredrickson lipoprotein phenotyping

**Influenced / downstream.** ference, apoB-guideline debate, Attia (apoB advocacy)

**Disputed / known unknowns.** His position that apoB should replace LDL-C as the primary clinical target is accepted in principle but only partially adopted in guidelines, which still lead with LDL-C for cost/availability reasons.
