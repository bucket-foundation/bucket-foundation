**Field contribution.** NIDA director and principal architect of the brain-disease model of addiction — a three-stage circuit framework (binge/intoxication, withdrawal/negative affect, preoccupation/anticipation) grounded in dopamine, prefrontal, and stress-system imaging — and lead author of the canonical review of cannabis harms.

**Why indexed.** The default scientific and policy frame for addiction, which the corpus grades honestly against its choice/behavioral-economics critics (Heyman, Hart, Robins) — the necessary anchor for the disease-vs-choice debate.

**Built on.** Koob's allostasis/dark-side model; dopamine reward neuroscience (Schultz, Berridge)

**Influenced / downstream.** US addiction-treatment-as-chronic-disease policy; medication-for-opioid-use-disorder advocacy

**Canon bridge.** addiction-neuroscience framework

**Disputed / known unknowns.** The strong brain-disease framing is contested by natural-remission epidemiology (Robins) and choice models (Heyman, Hart); the truth is likely a context-and-biology synthesis.
