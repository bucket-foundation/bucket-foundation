### Geoffrey Rose

| **primary works** | *Sick individuals and sick populations (Int J Epidemiol)* (1985); *The Strategy of Preventive Medicine* (1992) |

**Field contribution.** Articulated the prevention paradox and the population-vs-high-risk distinction: because most cases of disease arise from the large number of people at modest risk (not the small high-risk tail), a small downward shift in the whole population's risk distribution prevents more disease than intensively treating high-risk individuals. Distinguished the causes of *cases* from the causes of *incidence*.

**Why indexed.** The single most important conceptual tool in population prevention — and the lens that places almost all individual consumer longevity products (which optimize the high-risk tail or the worried-well) at the weakest leverage point.

**Built on.** cardiovascular epidemiology; distribution-of-risk-factor data

**Influenced / downstream.** Frieden's Health Impact Pyramid; population-level salt/tobacco/BP policy; all of modern preventive strategy

**Canon bridge.** method-tier / population-prevention theory

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Population strategies can impose small costs on many for diffuse benefit (the paradox's ethical edge); the high-risk vs population choice is context-dependent, not absolute.
