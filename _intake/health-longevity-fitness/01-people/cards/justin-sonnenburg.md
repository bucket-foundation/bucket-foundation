**Field contribution.** Developed the microbiota-accessible-carbohydrate (MAC) framework — that dietary fiber the host cannot digest is the primary fuel for a diverse gut microbiota — and showed experimentally that fiber deprivation collapses diversity (transgenerationally in mice), while a randomized human trial found high fermented-food intake increases microbiota diversity and lowers inflammatory markers.

**Why indexed.** Anchors the strongest mechanistic-and-trial case in the diet-microbiome-health chain: fiber and fermented foods as levers on gut ecology and inflammation, separated from the broader (thinner) "psychobiotic" overreach.

**Built on.** gnotobiotic mouse microbiology; Bäckhed/Gordon gut-microbiome ecology

**Influenced / downstream.** fiber and fermented-food nutrition science; microbiome-diversity-and-inflammaging research

**Canon bridge.** outcome-tier (microbiome → metabolic/immune health)

**Disputed / known unknowns.** Long-term hard-outcome (mortality/disease) effects of fiber/ferment-driven diversity changes in humans are not yet established; much mechanism is murine.

**Tags.** `microbiome` `fiber` `MAC` `fermented-foods` `diversity`
