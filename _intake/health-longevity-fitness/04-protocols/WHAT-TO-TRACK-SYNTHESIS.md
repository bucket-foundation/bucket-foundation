# What To Actually Track & Do — the evidence-tiered capstone

Almost everything sold as longevity is a correlate dressed up as a scorecard, a mouse
result that never made it to humans, or a dose that doesn't match the one that was
studied. Strip that away and the evidence points at a short, boring, powerful list. This
page sorts every lever you can *do* and every number you can *measure* by how strongly the
evidence actually backs it — Tier A first, hype last.

Three honesty rules govern the whole page:

1. **Predictor ≠ lever** — something that forecasts risk isn't automatically something
   that, once changed, lowers it. Grip strength predicts death, but squeezing a gripper
   won't save you. The same holds for gait speed, HRV (heart-rate variability), hsCRP (an
   inflammation blood marker), and biological-age clocks: they flag risk without being the
   thing that, when improved, *causes* lower mortality. The causal, modifiable levers are a
   short list.
2. **Cohort ≠ RCT** — a cohort is a population you watch over time; an RCT (randomized
   controlled trial) is the strongest evidence tier, where a coin-flip assigns the
   treatment. Almost every longevity "fact" is observational — you can't randomize fitness,
   sleep, or smoking over decades — so healthy-user and reverse-causation bias inflate the
   strong-looking numbers.
3. **Doing something beats nothing is the most reliable signal in the whole corpus.** The
   steepest dose-response gains are at the *low* end (sedentary → some). Optimization past
   that is real but smaller and noisier than the marketing implies.

_Not medical advice; this is a synthesis of already-graded claims, not new evidence._

## PART 1 — WHAT TO DO (the levers, by confidence tier)

### TIER A — Well-established (large cohorts + converging RCT/mechanism; do these first)

| Lever | Why it's Tier A |
|---|---|
| **Don't smoke** | The single largest modifiable mortality factor; dominates lifespan GWAS (gene-wide association studies) — the "longevity genes" they find are largely smoking + cardiometabolic genes.[^p1a-smoke] |
| **Build & keep cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max — your aerobic-capacity ceiling, the oxygen you can use at max effort)** | Strongest exercise-mortality association in preventive medicine (~5x low-vs-elite; ~13% lower risk per MET, one unit of resting metabolic effort). No observed upper benefit limit. Treat CRF as a vital sign.[^p1a-crf] |
| **Resistance-train for strength** | Strength (not muscle mass) independently predicts mortality; resistance activity ~10–17% lower mortality. Note the **J-shaped dose-response — benefit rises then dips if you overdo it** (peaks ~30–60 min/week).[^p1a-strength] |
| **Just move more (any intensity); break up sitting** | Steepest dose-response at the sedentary→active end; device-measured, least-confounded signal in the domain.[^p1a-move] |
| **Lower lifetime apoB / LDL** (apoB — a blood test counting the cholesterol particles that lodge in artery walls; better than standard LDL) | One of the few **causal** levers — genetics + epi + RCT converge; cumulative exposure matters, so earlier is better.[^p1a-apob] |
| **Sleep ~7 hours, regularly** | U-shaped mortality — too little and too much are both worse — with a ~7h low point (nadir); regularity/timing matter.[^p1a-sleep] |
| **Keep a healthy metabolic profile** (glucose/insulin, visceral fat) | Diabetes ~2x vascular risk; HbA1c (your ~3-month average blood sugar) predicts even in the non-diabetic range; visceral fat predicts beyond BMI.[^p1a-metabolic] |

### TIER B — Promising (real but smaller / surrogate-endpoint / dose-uncertain)

| Lever | Status / honest caveat |
|---|---|
| **Zone 2 + VO2max-targeted intervals (HIIT — high-intensity interval training)** | Improves CRF/cardiometabolic surrogates efficiently. The "Zone 2 is *uniquely* optimal for mitochondria" claim is an over-extrapolation (open conflict).[^p1b-zone2] |
| **Sauna (heat)** | One Finnish *men's* cohort, dose-response to mortality/dementia — but unexcluded healthy-user bias; no RCT.[^p1b-sauna] |
| **Protein adequacy + leucine-threshold dosing (esp. older adults)** | Supports muscle/strength; but a **mid-life vs late-life tradeoff** with IGF-1 (a growth-signalling hormone) via the protein/mTOR (cell-growth) pathway conflict. Not "more protein = always better".[^p1b-protein] |
| **Circadian-aligned eating / early time-restricted eating (TRE)** | The surviving TRE signal is *early-window circadian alignment*, small; **most TRE benefit is just calorie restriction.**[^p1b-tre] |
| **Light hygiene (bright AM / dim screens PM)** | Solid mechanism (melatonin action spectrum, screen-light circadian delay). Outcome data thinner. Note blue-blocking glasses show no clear benefit.[^p1b-light] |
| **Caloric/dietary restriction (modest)** | CALERIE (only long-term human CR RCT): ~12% calorie restriction, surrogate endpoints, only one clock moved. Modest and honest.[^p1b-cr] |
| **Slow breathing / HRV-supportive practice** | RCT-rich but surrogate/subjective, short, small. Real acute autonomic effects; longevity outcome unproven.[^p1b-hrv] |

### TIER C — Speculative / hyped / unproven (interesting, not actionable as "do this for longevity")

*These are listed because they're heavily marketed — the point is to show where the hype
outruns the evidence. None is actionable as "do this for longevity" yet.*

| Lever | Why it's Tier C |
|---|---|
| **Senolytics (D+Q, fisetin)** (senolytics — drugs meant to clear worn-out "senescent" cells; D+Q = dasatinib + quercetin) | Striking in mice; human evidence = tiny pilots. Not established in healthy humans.[^p1c-senolytics] |
| **NAD+ boosting (NR/NMN)** (NAD+ — a coenzyme that falls with age; NR/NMN are supplement precursors) | NAD+ declines with age (mechanism), but human RCTs show surrogate changes only; no demonstrated longevity benefit.[^p1c-nad] |
| **Metformin / rapamycin for healthy people** | Metformin = cohort signal + TAME *design* (not yet run); rapamycin = mouse lifespan + one immune RCT. Off-label for longevity is unproven.[^p1c-metformin] |
| **Partial epigenetic reprogramming** | Mouse only; not a human intervention.[^p1c-reprog] |
| **Cold plunge for metabolic/longevity benefit** | Rich mechanism (brown fat / norepinephrine), but the only human metabolic *outcome* used **prolonged mild cold (hours)**, not the brief plunge that's sold. Dose↔evidence mismatch.[^p1c-cold] |
| **CGM / "glucose spikes" for healthy people** (CGM — a continuous glucose monitor, the wearable that tracks blood sugar) | No outcome RCT in non-diabetics; variability has no proven outcome meaning.[^p1c-cgm] |
| **Seed-oil avoidance** | Polarized & low-rigor; higher-tier evidence runs the other way; certainty exceeds evidence on both sides.[^p1c-seedoil] |

## PART 2 — WHAT TO MEASURE (highest-signal first)

### TIER A — Measure these (high signal; cheap or causal)

| Measure | What it actually tells you | Honest caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **VO2max** (CPET — a lab test of your max oxygen use; or a hard field test) | Strongest single mortality predictor | Consumer *estimated* VO2max is a trend tool, not calibrated |
| **Grip strength + gait speed + chair-rise + balance** | Integrated organ-system reserve; rival expensive panels | Biomarkers, not levers; reverse causation |
| **apoB** (one draw) | The best lipid risk metric; **causal** | Subsumes LDL-C/non-HDL; act on it |
| **Lp(a)** (an inherited, mostly-fixed cholesterol particle; measure ONCE in a lifetime) | Causal, genetic, ~stable; flags high-risk ~20% | No approved Lp(a)-specific drug yet (2026) |
| **HbA1c + fasting insulin/HOMA-IR** (HOMA-IR — an insulin-resistance score from fasting glucose + insulin) | Glycemic control + earliest metabolic warning | Insulin assays vary between labs; HbA1c distorted by RBC disorders |
| **DEXA for BMD** (DEXA — a low-dose X-ray scan; BMD — bone mineral density) (where indicated) | Reference test for bone/fracture risk | Misses most fractures by BMD alone → pair with FRAX (a 10-year fracture-risk calculator) |

Footnotes for the Tier A measures: VO2max,[^p2a-vo2max] the functional battery,[^p2a-capacity]
apoB,[^p2a-apob] Lp(a),[^p2a-lpa] glycemic panel,[^p2a-glycemic] and DEXA.[^p2a-dexa]

### TIER B — Useful with caveats (track trends, don't over-read)

| Measure | Use it for | Don't use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **hsCRP** | Inflammation *marker*; risk stratification | A treatment target (it's not causal)[^p2b-hscrp] |
| **DEXA body composition** | Fat distribution / visceral fat trend | Judging longevity via *lean mass* (function beats mass)[^p2b-dexabc] |
| **Resting/overnight HRV** | Within-person recovery/autonomic trend | Cross-person or cross-device comparison; absolute risk[^p2b-hrv] |
| **Consumer sleep tracker** | Total sleep time + timing regularity | Sleep STAGING ("deep sleep %"), diagnosing disorders[^p2b-sleeptracker] |
| **IGF-1** | Context dial (anabolism/cancer tradeoff) | A "minimize for longevity" target (U-shaped)[^p2b-igf1] |

### TIER C — Don't over-invest (low validity or unproven personal value)

| Measure | Why caution |
|---|---|
| **Epigenetic / "biological age" tests** | Predict at population level; **not a validated surrogate**, first-gen clocks noisy → a single number can't tell if your protocol "works"[^p2c-bioage] |
| **CGM for non-diabetics** | Validated for diabetes (time-in-range); no outcome evidence in the healthy; sensors disagree[^p2c-cgm] |
| **Microbiome "age"/uniqueness tests** | Composition-age correlations aren't causal; commercial[^p2c-microbiome] |

## The capstone in one paragraph (read this if nothing else)

The evidence is lopsided toward a short list of **boring, powerful, mostly-functional levers**: don't smoke,
build and keep **cardiorespiratory fitness** and **strength**, **move more**, **sleep ~7h regularly**, keep
**apoB/LDL** low across life, and keep a **healthy metabolic profile**. The single causal, modifiable blood
lever is **apoB/LDL** (plus measure **Lp(a)** once). The highest-signal *measurements* are **functional**
(VO2max, grip, gait, chair-rise, balance) and a few **causal/early-warning blood markers** (apoB, Lp(a), HbA1c,
fasting insulin). Almost everything that gets *sold* — biological-age clocks, CGM for the healthy, consumer
HRV/sleep-stage numbers, senolytics/NAD+/rapamycin for healthy people, cold plunges, seed-oil panic — is
either a **correlate dressed up as a scorecard**, a **mouse result not yet in humans**, or a **dose that
doesn't match the studied dose**. Spend your attention on Tier A; treat Tier B as trends; enjoy Tier C as
experiments, not protocols.
