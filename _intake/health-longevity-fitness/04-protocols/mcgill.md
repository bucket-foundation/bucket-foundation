## The framework
- **Spine stability via stiffness, not range:** the lumbar spine is best protected by building **endurance and
  co-contraction stiffness** around a neutral spine, while **minimizing repeated end-range lumbar flexion**
  (McGill's disc-herniation work links cumulative flexion-under-load to disc damage).
- **Find your pain trigger, remove it first** ("the McGill method": assess provocative motions/postures,
  remove the cause, then build capacity). Avoid stretching an already-unstable painful back into more motion.

## The recipe — the "Big 3"
Three exercises chosen (from McGill's EMG/spine-load work) to train the core's endurance/stiffness 360° around
the spine while keeping **low compressive load on the lumbar discs**:

1. **Modified curl-up** — hands under the lumbar lordosis, one knee bent; lift head/shoulders only a few cm,
   no lumbar flexion. (Trains anterior core without spinal flexion.)
2. **Side bridge (side plank)** — trains quadratus lumborum / obliques / lateral chain; regress to knees.
3. **Bird dog** — quadruped opposite arm/leg reach; trains back extensors + multifidus + coordination with a
   neutral spine.

**Dosing:** done daily; reps built as **descending pyramids of brief isometric holds** (e.g. 8/6/4 holds of
~8–10 s) rather than long single holds or high reps — endurance over max force. Preceded by the **cat-camel**
(motion, not a stretch, to reduce spine viscosity) and a neutral-spine hip-hinge pattern.

## Honest framing notes
- **Evidence tier:** McGill-style stabilization for chronic non-specific low-back pain has **small RCT +
  clinical** support showing pain/function improvement **comparable to conventional physiotherapy** (not a
  decisive superiority); the *spine-sparing biomechanics* (low disc load, anti-flexion) are **mechanistically
  well grounded** in his lab work. → graded `mcgill-big3-back-stability`.
- It is a **back-pain management + spine-protection** protocol, **not** a longevity intervention — don't
  inflate it into a mortality/healthspan claim.
- The "avoid all spinal flexion forever" popular reading is **stronger than McGill's own nuance** (flexion
  tolerance is individual; the target is *cumulative* end-range loaded flexion, not all bending).
- **COI:** books/courses/consulting (backfitpro).
