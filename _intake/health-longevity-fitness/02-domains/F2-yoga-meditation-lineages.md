# F2 — Yoga & Meditation Lineages (mind-body traditions)

> **Wave 5 deepening (2026-06-27).** Domains **F (Movement/mobility/flexibility)** and **G (Breath)**
> bridge into **I (autonomic/recovery)** through the mind-body traditions. This file maps the major
> yoga and meditation *lineages* — their origin, what each emphasizes, and **what is actually measurable**
> — so the graded evidence (`I-claims.json`) and the conflicts (`CONFLICTS.md`) attach to a named tradition
> rather than a vague "meditation helps" blob.
>
> **Hard rule carried over from `SCHEMA.md`:** a lineage / a teacher's name is **provenance, not evidence**.
> The tradition tells you *what was practiced*; the grade lives on the primary study, not on the guru. Most
> of this literature is small, unblinded, expectancy-prone, and (for TM specifically) entangled with
> movement-funded research groups — tier accordingly. See the publication-bias note at the bottom.

## Part 1 — Yoga lineages

Modern "yoga" in the health literature almost always means **postural yoga (asana practice)**, a 20th-century
synthesis. The classical textual root (Patanjali) is overwhelmingly *not* about postures — a frequently
laundered conflation worth keeping explicit.

| Lineage | Origin | Emphasis | What's measurable | Key figure(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Patanjali's Yoga Sutras** *(classical root)* | ~2nd c. BCE–4th c. CE, India (compiled) | The "eight limbs" (ashtanga): ethical restraints (yama/niyama), posture (asana — a single line: "steady, comfortable seat"), breath control (pranayama), sense-withdrawal, concentration (dharana), meditation (dhyana), absorption (samadhi). A **meditative/philosophical** system, NOT a stretching manual. | Almost nothing directly — it is a textual/philosophical foundation. Its *downstream practices* (pranayama, dhyana) are what get measured. | **Patanjali** (compiler) |
| **Hatha yoga** *(medieval physical tradition)* | ~11th–15th c. India (Hatha Yoga Pradipika, ~15th c.) | Physical purification: asana, pranayama, bandhas (locks), mudras, kriyas — body as the vehicle for energetic/contemplative ends. The trunk from which modern postural styles branch. | Flexibility, balance, autonomic tone, BP — via its modern asana descendants. | (textual; Svatmarama compiled the Pradipika) |
| **Krishnamacharya lineage** *(the modern root)* | Early–mid 20th c., Mysore, India | "Father of modern yoga." Fused Hatha asana with gymnastics, breath-synchronized movement, and therapeutic individualization. Teacher of Iyengar, Pattabhi Jois, Desikachar, Indra Devi — i.e. nearly all globally exported styles trace to him. | (as for descendants) | **T. Krishnamacharya** |
| **Iyengar yoga** | mid-20th c.→, Pune (B.K.S. Iyengar) | **Precision of alignment + props** (blocks, straps, bolsters, wall ropes), long holds, therapeutic sequencing. Most *clinically studied* style — props make it accessible to patients, so it dominates yoga-as-medicine RCTs. | Flexibility/ROM, balance, back pain, BP, mood — the style behind much of the trial evidence. | **B.K.S. Iyengar** *(already carded)* |
| **Ashtanga (Vinyasa) yoga** | mid-20th c.→, Mysore (Pattabhi Jois) | Fixed, demanding sequences of postures linked breath-to-movement (vinyasa) with ujjayi breath, bandhas, drishti (gaze). Athletic, fast, fixed-series. Parent of "power yoga." | Cardiorespiratory load, strength, flexibility; higher injury exposure than gentle styles. | **K. Pattabhi Jois** |
| **Vinyasa / "flow" yoga** | late-20th c.→, West (derived from Ashtanga) | Continuous breath-linked flow, but *creative/variable* sequencing rather than a fixed series. The dominant modern studio style. | Aerobic/strength load varies hugely with the teacher; hard to standardize for research. | (diffuse; descends from Jois) |
| **Yin yoga** | late-20th c.→, West (Paulie Zink → Paul Grilley, Sarah Powers) | Long passive holds (3–5 min) targeting connective tissue/fascia and joints rather than muscular effort; meridian/Daoist framing. | ROM, relaxation/parasympathetic shift; the "fascia release" mechanism claim is unproven (cf. foam-rolling conflict). | Grilley, Powers |
| **Kundalini yoga** | brought West 1969 by Yogi Bhajan (3HO) | Dynamic breathwork (e.g. breath of fire), repetitive movement, mantra, meditation aimed at "raising energy." Highly stylized; **organizationally contested** (3HO/Yogi Bhajan abuse allegations). | Some breath-effect signals; grade independent of the organization, which has serious credibility problems. | Yogi Bhajan *(disputed org — index with caution)* |
| **Restorative yoga** | late-20th c.→ (Iyengar-derived, Judith Lasater) | Fully supported, passive postures held for minutes; explicit down-regulation. | Parasympathetic/HRV shift, stress, sleep — the gentlest, most "recovery" of the styles. | Lasater |

**Yoga takeaway.** The thing usually *measured* is a movement + breath + attention bundle, dominated in the
literature by Iyengar-style interventions (props → patients → RCTs). Its honest signals are **autonomic
(HRV/vagal), blood-pressure, flexibility/balance, and stress/mood** — all already graded in `I-claims.json`
(`yoga-hrv-vagal-increase`, `yoga-blood-pressure-meta`, `yoga-stress-mood-rct-review`), and all at **weak-to-
very-low evidence tiers** despite being "systematic reviews," because the underlying trials are small,
heterogeneous, unblinded, and frequently from a small number of Indian research groups.

## Part 2 — Meditation lineages

| Tradition | Origin | Emphasis | What's measurable | Key figure(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vipassana / Insight** | Theravada Buddhist (ancient); modern lay revival via S.N. Goenka, U Ba Khin, IMS (Goldstein/Kornfield/Salzberg) | Non-judgmental moment-to-moment observation of sensation/mind ("seeing things as they are"). Open-monitoring style. | Cortical thickness (Lazar 2005, cross-sectional), attention, stress reactivity. The contemplative-neuroscience workhorse. | Goenka; (research: Lazar, Davidson) |
| **MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)** | 1979, Jon Kabat-Zinn, UMass Medical Center | **Secularized** 8-week clinical program: body scan, sitting meditation, gentle Hatha yoga. Deliberately stripped of religious framing to enter medicine. The bridge that made meditation a research and clinical object. | The MOST studied meditation intervention: anxiety/depression/pain (moderate evidence), immune/vaccine response, BP, cortisol — but mostly small, active-control-poor. | **Jon Kabat-Zinn** |
| **MBCT (Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy)** | 1990s–2000s (Segal, Williams, Teasdale) | MBSR + cognitive therapy, targeted at **depression relapse prevention**. | Depression-relapse rates — the meditation intervention with the *best* RCT support (NICE-endorsed for recurrent depression). | Segal, Williams, Teasdale |
| **Transcendental Meditation (TM)** | 1950s–60s, Maharishi Mahesh Yogi | Silent mantra repetition, 20 min ×2/day, effortless "transcending." Highly **commercialized + trademarked**; research heavily produced by movement-affiliated institutions (Maharishi University). | BP (AHA gives TM a qualified nod; other techniques none), the Wallace/Benson "hypometabolic state," and a contested CVD-events RCT. **Funding/affiliation bias is the central caveat.** | Maharishi Mahesh Yogi; (research: Schneider, Wallace) |
| **Relaxation Response** | 1970s, Herbert Benson (Harvard) | Benson's **de-religioned, generic** distillation of TM: a repeated word/phrase + passive attitude elicits a physiological "relaxation response" (↓O2 consumption, HR, BP). An attempt to extract the physiology from any one tradition. | Reduced metabolic rate, heart rate, sympathetic tone (Wallace & Benson 1971). The mechanism layer under "meditation relaxes you." | **Herbert Benson** |
| **Loving-kindness / Compassion (metta)** | Theravada/Tibetan Buddhist; modern via Salzberg, Davidson's lab (compassion training) | Cultivating directed benevolence/compassion. A "constructive" family distinct from focused-attention/open-monitoring. | Positive affect, vagal tone (Fredrickson/Kok), prosocial measures; smaller literature. | Salzberg; (research: Davidson, Fredrickson) |
| **Zen (zazen) / Soto-Rinzai** | Mahayana Buddhist, China→Japan (~6th–12th c.) | Seated "just sitting" (shikantaza) or koan work; posture and breath as anchors. | EEG signatures, attention; less RCT-driven than MBSR/TM. | (Dogen, lineage) |
| **Yoga Nidra / iRest** | tantric/Hatha root; modern clinical iRest (Richard Miller) | "Yogic sleep": guided, lying, systematic body/awareness rotation toward deep relaxation. | Sleep, PTSD symptoms, relaxation; small evidence base, military-adjacent trials. | Miller |

**Meditation takeaway.** The two highest-credibility points are: **(1)** MBSR/MBCT have the best *psychological*
evidence (Goyal 2014 meta: *moderate* evidence for anxiety, depression, pain; *low/insufficient* for nearly
everything else, and **no** evidence of superiority over active treatments like drugs or exercise); **(2)** the
*physiological* relaxation response (Wallace & Benson 1971) is real but is a mechanism, not an outcome. The
**biological-aging / telomere** claims and the **TM blood-pressure / cardiovascular-event** claims are the two
places where the literature most outruns its evidence — both are carded as conflicts.

## Contemplative neuroscience — the measurement layer

- **Richard Davidson** (Wisconsin) — founder of "contemplative neuroscience"; the Davidson & Kabat-Zinn 2003
  MBSR study (brain-asymmetry shift + improved influenza-vaccine antibody response) and long-term Tibetan-
  adept gamma-EEG work. The lab that made meditation a hard-neuroscience object.
- **Sara Lazar** (Harvard/MGH) — the 2005 cortical-thickness finding in experienced insight meditators (cross-
  sectional; can't separate practice effect from self-selection).
- **Elizabeth Blackburn & Elissa Epel** (UCSF) — telomere/telomerase biology applied to meditation
  (Jacobs/Epel 2011 retreat→telomerase; Epel 2009 hypothesis). Blackburn is a Nobel laureate for telomerase;
  *that* credibility does **not** transfer to the small meditation studies, which are the over-claimed link.

## Publication-bias / honest-grading note (load-bearing)

1. **Small, unblinded, expectancy-prone.** You cannot blind someone to whether they are meditating; wait-list
   controls inflate effects vs active controls. Goyal 2014 found effects shrink toward null against active
   comparators.
2. **Allegiance / funding bias is acute for TM.** A large share of TM cardiovascular research originates from
   Maharishi-affiliated institutions; independent replication is thinner, and the AHA statement (Brook 2013)
   explicitly limits its endorsement to TM with a *qualified* class-IIB/level-B "may be considered" — and
   recommends *against* other meditation techniques for BP on insufficient evidence.
3. **Telomere claims are the canonical over-reach.** Telomerase "activation" in a 3-month retreat is a
   surrogate-of-a-surrogate; it is not demonstrated lifespan or healthspan change. Grade as `nequals1`-adjacent
   `rct`-small, never as an aging outcome.
4. **Bundle problem.** Yoga = movement + breath + attention; you cannot attribute a yoga HRV effect to
   "meditation," nor a seated-meditation immune effect to "yoga." Keep the lineage attached so the bundle is visible.
5. **Lineage ≠ evidence.** An ancient pedigree (Patanjali, Theravada) is cultural provenance, not biological
   validation. Index the practice; grade the study.
