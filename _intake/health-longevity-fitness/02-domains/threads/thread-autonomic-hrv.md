# Thread — Autonomic Balance / HRV (the shared readout)

> **Status:** v0.1 (Wave 1 cross-cut) — 2026-06-27. Weaves G/H/I claims by reference. HRV is the
> one number that breath, cold, sleep, and stress all move — which makes it the corpus's most
> *connective* biomarker and its most *over-interpreted* one. Keep biomarker ≠ intervention sharp.

## What connects
Heart-rate variability is a non-invasive index of **vagal / autonomic regulation**, and it is the
common downstream readout of four otherwise-separate practices:

- **Breath (G):** slow breathing (~6/min, ~0.1 Hz resonance) raises HRV and shifts toward
  parasympathetic dominance (`slow-breathing-autonomic-hrv`); prolonged exhalation increases vagal
  output via respiratory sinus arrhythmia (`exhalation-vagal-mechanism`); cyclic sighing and
  diaphragmatic breathing lower arousal/cortisol (`cyclic-sighing-mood-arousal-rct`,
  `diaphragmatic-breathing-stress-cortisol`). Breath is the most *direct* lever on HRV.
- **Cold (H):** cold exposure provokes a large acute sympathetic surge (norepinephrine +530%,
  `cold-norepinephrine-thermogenesis-mechanism`); the autonomic response and post-exposure
  parasympathetic rebound are part of the cold-as-autonomic-training claim.
- **Sleep/recovery (I):** HRV is *the* autonomic recovery biomarker
  (`hrv-autonomic-recovery-biomarker`); chronically low HRV associates with stress and higher
  CV/all-cause risk; it is the wearable-era readout of recovery state.
- **Stress (I→B):** HRV is the measurable face of allostatic load
  (`allostatic-load-stress-mediators`) — autonomic tone is how chronic stress becomes a number.

The shared mechanism is **vagal/baroreflex control of the sinoatrial node**; the UP-link is to
canon `05-biophysics` (autonomic physiology, baroreflex; adjacent to Hodgkin–Huxley excitability).

## Where it's SOLID
- **Slow breathing raises HRV / vagal tone** — well-established `mechanistic`; the ~0.1 Hz
  resonance frequency aligning with the baroreflex is reproducible.
- **HRV indexes autonomic flexibility**, and chronically low HRV tracks higher CV/all-cause risk
  (`mechanistic`/`cohort`). The *association* is solid.
- **Extended exhalation increases vagal output** (RSA) — sound physiology grounding the
  physiological-sigh and coherent-breathing practices.

## Where it's HYPE
- **HRV is a biomarker, not an intervention.** "Raise your HRV" is not itself a validated health
  outcome (`hrv-autonomic-recovery-biomarker` flags this explicitly). Moving the number is not the
  same as buying health.
- **Cross-person comparison is weak.** HRV is noisy, method-, posture-, and age-dependent, and only
  interpretable *within a person over time* — "my HRV beats yours" is close to meaningless.
- **Wearable HRV optimization** can become a vanity metric divorced from outcomes; the autonomic
  surge from Wim Hof is an *acute stress response*, not evidence of durable upgrade
  (`conflict-wim-hof-mechanism`).
- The acute, subjective, short-duration nature of the breathwork RCTs (mood/cortisol/HRV
  surrogates, small healthy samples) means the *outcome* evidence is thin even where the *mechanism*
  is firm.

## Open questions
1. Does HRV-guided training / HRV-biofeedback produce real outcomes, or is it a vanity metric?
   (named Wave-2 gap in G and I)
2. Is the autonomic-flexibility → mortality link causal, or does low HRV merely *reveal* underlying
   illness (reverse causation, as with grip strength and VO2max)?
3. Do breath, cold, and sleep raise HRV through one shared vagal pathway, or distinct routes that
   converge on the same readout? (decides whether HRV is a unifying *mechanism* or just a shared
   *dial*)
