Johann Goethe was a literary celebrity by the age of 25 and was ennobled by the Duke of Saxe-Weimar, Carl August in 1782 after first taking up residence there in November of 1775 following the success of his first novel, <i>The Sorrows of Young Werther</i> (available on Librivox). Goethe was a member of the Sturm und Drang literary movement. This group of writers believed that literature should shock the audience in order to produce extreme emotions as a counterbalance to the rationalist movement which promoted detachment in the sciences. This is the first part of his autobiography, which he called <i>Truth and Fiction</i> or <i>Truth and Poetry</i> depending on the translator. Goethe wrote poems, such as, <i>The Sorcerer's Apprentice</i>, as well as plays, novels and some scientific works. He wrote the play <i>Faust</i> which is also available on Librivox. He was an Anti-Enlightenment writer.
