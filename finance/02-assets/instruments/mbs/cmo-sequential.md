# CMO Sequential Pay

ID `I-mbs-cmo-seq`. As of 2026-11-27.

Parent: [mbs](../../mbs.md). Index: [mbs instruments](README.md).

## Structure
A CMO is a multiclass bond backed by agency pass-throughs or loans; the issuer splits cash flow into two or more tranches with different maturities, and a REMIC is the common legal form, per FINRA. Grade A. In a sequential structure, all principal goes to the first tranche until it is retired, then to the next. Ginnie Mae describes sequential-pay structures as redistributing principal across tranches with different expected maturities. Grade A.

## Purpose
Ginnie Mae states the economic purpose of an agency REMIC is cash flow prioritization; collateral credit is unchanged. Grade A.

## Issuance
Agency single-family REMIC issuance in March 2026: Ginnie Mae $23.06B in 11 deals, about 48.7% of the total; Freddie Mac $13.49B; Fannie Mae $10.79B. Through April 2026 Ginnie Mae guaranteed more than $80B of single-family REMICs, near 40% of its single-family MBS issuance. Grade A.

## Worked Example
Balances and rates in the example are illustrative inputs chosen to show the arithmetic. $300M of collateral split into tranche A $100M, B $100M, C $100M. If the pool returns $20M of principal in a month, A receives all $20M and B and C receive interest only. After A is paid down to zero, principal flows to B.

## Reporting
CMO trades became subject to TRACE dissemination on 20 Mar 2017. Grade A.
