Teaches: Ernest Chan (2025 interview) for beginners: what algorithmic trading is, how AI changes the skill mix, where LLMs help and fail, how to start with a simple strategy, and why not to quit a job to trade.
Claim: Algorithmic trading means a strategy encoded in a program so decisions follow rules instead of human discretion, and hand-entered execution still counts. || you have a strategy that is that can be encoded in a program
Claim: With generative AI the code is the easy part, and the scarce skill is deep understanding of the market and its nuances rather than math genius or superb programming. || you do not need to be a math genius
Claim: Chan has found it very hard to get a machine to generate a good trading idea he had not thought of, since model ideas come from what is on the internet; strategies are conceived by humans and refined by LLMs. || most of the ideas that machine generate are what they read on the internet
Claim: A year earlier ChatGPT could not follow an exact strategy specification, and now it can turn one into code. || now they understand it
Claim: Start with a simple strategy, get a first-pass Sharpe slightly above the risk-free rate, then improve through risk management, parameter optimization or AI regime detection. || you do not start with a complicated strategy to improve it
Claim: A basic PC is enough to test most strategies, since nobody is training a language model with hundreds of billions of parameters. || rudimentary computing resource is quite sufficient
Claim: Chan advises against quitting a job to trade full time: it violates the rules of risk management and automation, and a good strategy should run on its own. || The two are a contradiction
Architecture: none (interview).
