Teaches: NPTEL IIT Roorkee lecture on forward pricing for consumption assets: why reverse arbitrage fails when holders value physical possession (convenience yield), a wheat example, a currency forward valuation, forwards on dividend-paying stocks and forward interest rates.
Claim: Convenience yield is the benefit of keeping the physical asset, which is why holders will not sell it to capture reverse arbitrage. || The preference, the desire, the temptation to retain physical possession of the asset
Claim: The worked example has wheat at 1,200 rupees per quintal, 150 storage cost paid quarterly in advance, a 1,425 one-year forward and a 10 percent risk-free rate, from which the convenience yield per annum is solved. || Storage costs work out to rupees 150 per quintal payable quarterly in advance
Claim: A long and a short currency forward at different prices cancel the dollar leg and leave a profit of 15 rupees at maturity, worth its present value today. || The value of the strategy as on 31st December is 15 rupees
Claim: Forward prices on dividend-paying stocks subtract the present value of dividends still to come before maturity. || this dividend payment will be accounted for
Claim: Forward interest rates follow from investing for three years either at the 3-year spot rate or by rolling shorter periods. || A will be equal to P or P 0 into 1 plus S03 to the power 3
