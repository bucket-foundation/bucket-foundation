Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on portfolio theory: portfolio mean as a weighted average, variance as a sum over covariances, diversification, correlation effects on the mean-standard deviation frontier, and the tangency portfolio.
Claim: Portfolio expected return is a weighted average of the stock returns, but portfolio variance is not a weighted average of variances; it contains n squared terms and depends on covariances. || the variance of the portfolio is not a simple weighted average of the variances
Claim: Diversification works because covariances outnumber variances, so correlations matter more than individual variances in a large portfolio. || there are a lot more covariances than there are variances
Claim: A 50-50 mix of General Motors and Motorola earns the average return but has less risk than the average, giving a portfolio that beats General Motors on both return and risk. || everybody in this room should prefer that dot to General Motors
Claim: With correlation of minus one a combination exists with zero risk, and as correlation rises the bullet-shaped frontier collapses toward a straight line. || You get straight lines of different stripes
Claim: Monthly standard deviation scales to annual by the square root of 12 under no correlation across months. || What's the square root of 12
Claim: As the number of stocks grows, average variance stops mattering and the leftover undiversifiable risk is set by average covariance; in a crisis correlations jump because everyone sells at once. || everybody is going to be selling at the same time
