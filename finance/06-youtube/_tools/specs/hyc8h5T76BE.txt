Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on the 2008 Lehman failure and leverage, real versus nominal returns, and the start of fixed income: coupon bond cash flows, pure discount bonds and STRIPS.
Claim: Leverage amplifies loss: a 500,000 dollar home bought with 100,000 down loses half of the buyer's equity on a 10 percent price fall, and a zero-down buyer loses more than everything. || you've lost 50-- half of your assets just got wiped out with only a 10% move
Claim: A bank leveraged 16 to 1 can lose a large share of its capital on a 5 to 10 percent fall in asset value. || If you leverage that 16 to 1
Claim: The 1987 crash fell about 20 percent in one day, and market makers who were leveraged lost their capital bases. || the US stock market lost approximately 20% of its value
Claim: Inflation measures purchasing power and differs from time value of money; the real return is approximately the nominal rate minus inflation. || equal to the nominal rate of return minus the inflation rate
Claim: A 10 percent nominal gain with 10 percent inflation leaves real wealth unchanged. || your wealth hasn't changed in real terms
Claim: Fixed income markets dwarf equity markets in size but trade less often, and complex instruments such as CDOs trade even less. || These numbers-- they dwarf the stock market
Claim: A bond's market value comes from discounting each cash flow at market-derived rates, and STRIPS are coupon government bonds split into separately traded discount bonds. || separate trading of registered interest and principal securities
