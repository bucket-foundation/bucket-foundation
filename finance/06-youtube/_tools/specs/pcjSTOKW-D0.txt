Teaches: Flirting with Models interview with Dean Curnutt (Macro Risk Advisors) on reflexive volatility episodes: 1987, carry trades, pre-2008 correlation recycling and credit, AIG collateral, the 2018 VIX product blowup and the GameStop gamma squeeze.
Claim: The 1987 crash did not come from nowhere: the VIX reconstruction reached 150 on the day but was above 40 in the week before, a one-day fall of more than 20 percent without precedent. || the VIX on the '87 crash got to 150
Claim: Carry trades state that the world tomorrow will look like today, and post-crisis low-rate promises made them extremely crowded. || A carry trade is really successful, and it effectively states
Claim: Before 2008 the Fed believed credit derivatives dispersed risk, with the IG CDX contract at 20 basis points, while exposures to credit were a one-way train. || the heroism of central banking
Claim: AIG's AAA rating let it post no collateral, and losing it forced growing postings, which Curnutt calls a poison chalice. || AAA counterparties don't post any collateral ever
Claim: Before the 2018 VIX product blowup, advisers showed that a rise in the front future from 12 to 18 would force about 130,000 VIX futures purchases in one day, more than daily volume. || you got about 130,000 VIX futures or more to buy in one day
Claim: Curnutt's rule of thumb that nothing bad happens in markets when realized volatility is below 15, and the lesson that regulation changes who holds risk after a blowup. || Nothing bad happens in markets when realized vol is below 15
Claim: Whether to be long or short volatility is hard to answer; backtests show a variance risk premium but he values finding cheap optionality. || most of the back test will tell you there's a VRP
