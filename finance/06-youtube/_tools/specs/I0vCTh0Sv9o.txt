Teaches: Student presentation of chapter 2 of Lopez de Prado's Advances in Financial Machine Learning: the four financial data types, standard bars (time, tick, volume, dollar), information-driven bars (imbalance, runs), the ETF trick and PCA roll for futures, and CUSUM event sampling.
Claim: Start from raw data, because other people's processed datasets only reproduce what they already found. || one will most likely find find what others have already found
Claim: Four data types: fundamental (low frequency, lagged, often back-filled), market, analytics (derived, little added value) and alternative (satellite, search, chat), where the edge is largest. || alternative data such as satellites
Claim: Time bars should be avoided because markets do not process information at a constant interval and time-sampled series show heteroskedasticity, serial correlation and non-normal returns. || time sample series have poor statistical properties
Claim: Dollar bars beat tick and volume bars because outstanding shares change through splits and mergers while traded value stays comparable. || the best methodology to deal with with all this stuff is dollar bars
Claim: Tick, volume and dollar imbalance bars sample when order flow diverges from expectation, aiming to detect informed trading as early as possible. || the objective here is to detect information as early as possible
Claim: The ETF trick turns a multi-product dataset into one series that behaves like a total-return ETF so only cash-like products need modeling. || resembles the total return cash a ETF
Claim: Event-based sampling with a CUSUM filter flags when an observation moves away from the target mean, then lets the model learn only around those events. || detect a shift in the mean of value of a measured quantity
Architecture: none (book chapter summary; bars then labeling pipeline per Lopez de Prado).
