Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on finishing CAPM and applying it to capital budgeting: use cash flows not accounting earnings, depreciation enters only through taxes, beta sets the discount rate, with a publishing and an oil example.
Claim: Realized returns against beta look linear but with a flatter slope than the market risk premium, which motivates the Black CAPM using a zero-beta portfolio instead of a risk-free rate. || the Black CAPM says that there is no risk-free rate
Claim: Capital budgeting must use project cash flows, because accounting earnings serve other purposes than decision making. || you should use cash flows, not accounting earnings
Claim: Depreciation affects a project only through taxes: project tax equals the tax rate times operating profit minus the tax rate times depreciation. || So your depreciation doesn't actually affect the profitability of the particular piece of equipment
Claim: With a beta of 1.29, a 5 percent risk-free rate and a 6 percent market premium, the lecture computes a 12.7 percent cost of capital for a publisher. || it turns out that the cost of capital is 12.7%
Claim: Oil production is a high-beta activity, so the example uses a 20 percent discount rate against a 5 percent risk-free rate for after-tax profit of 20 dollars a barrel over 3 million barrels. || The industry discount rate of oil production is 20%
Claim: Whether to take on distress risk is a question of magnitude: bankruptcy costs versus the value of the upside. || What are the bankruptcy costs versus the value of the upside
