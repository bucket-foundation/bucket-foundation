Teaches: Flirting with Models interview with Charles McGarraugh (Altis) on a two-layer systematic stack: an upstream signal layer producing prediction curves and a downstream strategy layer that builds portfolios, plus regime change, trend following after QE, and productizing as ETFs.
Claim: The design splits an upstream signal layer from a downstream strategy layer, and the speaker names the drawback that rigid components may reduce the spanning set of the design space. || a drawback of anything that's I guess rigidly componented
Claim: The signal layer ingests market data (and later alternative data) and outputs prediction curves, which the strategy layer turns into portfolios. || the output being these prediction curves
Claim: McGarraugh argues QE compressed risk premia and made active trading returns small relative to transaction cost, a reason trend following did poorly after COVID. || the reason why trend following did so poorly
Claim: His team prefers an accurate estimate of measured risk and avoids ridge bumps or correlation clustering that inflate idiosyncratic risk. || it's important to have an accurate representation of our best guess of what the measured risk really is
Claim: He faults discretionary traders for ignoring factor exposures and quants for arrogance in the model, over-reliance on data and underestimating fat tails. || an arrogance of belief in the model
Claim: Priorities are incremental gains in execution, risk modeling and latency over speculative redesign, and the ETF route aims at a diversifier with positive expected return for return-stacking advisers. || incremental improvements to execution, to risk modeling, and just to latency
Architecture: Two layers: signal layer (market and non-market data to prediction curves) feeding a strategy layer (curves to portfolio under measured covariance).
