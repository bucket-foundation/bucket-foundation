# Ses 4: Present Value Relations III & Fixed-Income Securities I | MIT OpenCourseWare | hyc8h5T76BE | 1.20 h

Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on the 2008 Lehman failure and leverage, real versus nominal returns, and the start of fixed income: coupon bond cash flows, pure discount bonds and STRIPS.

Claims:
- Leverage amplifies loss: a 500,000 dollar home bought with 100,000 down loses half of the buyer's equity on a 10 percent price fall, and a zero-down buyer loses more than everything. "you've lost 50-- half of your assets just got wiped out with only a 10% move" [0:06:02]
- A bank leveraged 16 to 1 can lose a large share of its capital on a 5 to 10 percent fall in asset value. "If you leverage that 16 to 1" [0:08:03]
- The 1987 crash fell about 20 percent in one day, and market makers who were leveraged lost their capital bases. "the US stock market lost approximately 20% of its value" [0:56:07]
- Inflation measures purchasing power and differs from time value of money; the real return is approximately the nominal rate minus inflation. "equal to the nominal rate of return minus the inflation rate" [0:40:00]
- A 10 percent nominal gain with 10 percent inflation leaves real wealth unchanged. "your wealth hasn't changed in real terms" [0:38:20]
- Fixed income markets dwarf equity markets in size but trade less often, and complex instruments such as CDOs trade even less. "These numbers-- they dwarf the stock market" [0:48:01]
- A bond's market value comes from discounting each cash flow at market-derived rates, and STRIPS are coupon government bonds split into separately traded discount bonds. "separate trading of registered interest and principal securities" [1:08:08]
