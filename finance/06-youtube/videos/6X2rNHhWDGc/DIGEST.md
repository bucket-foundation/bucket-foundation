# How Investors Fall Into Bias Traps: Masters in Business with Richard Thaler and Alex Imas | Bloomberg Podcasts | 6X2rNHhWDGc | 1.41 h

Teaches: A Masters in Business interview with Richard Thaler and Alex Imas on the updated "Misbehaving" anomalies, defaults in retirement plans and biases in markets.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- Thaler says economics developed a norm that models with smarter agents are better models, which pushed out behavioral assumptions. "if the agents in my model are smarter than the agents in your model then my model's better" [0:14:16]
- Imas says intro microeconomics textbooks still follow the 1973 pattern, starting from perfect competition. "open up a textbook" [0:10:09]
- The update covers 30 years of research since 1992 and two thirds of the new book is new material. "twothirds of the book ended up being brand new" [0:24:05]
- About $4.7 trillion sits in target-date and balanced funds, and 40% of that reached them through the default setting. "there is about $4.7 trillion with a t trillion dollars in those funds of which 40% according to recent research was the default setting" [0:27:01]
- The disposition effect, documented by Shefrin and Statman in 1985 and Odean in 1999, is selling winners and holding losers. "the disposition effect" [0:32:46]
- Imas views limited attention as the larger principle: stocks covered in the news attract buying, from retail and institutions alike. "limited attention" [0:34:29]
- Employees held company stock in their 401(k)s and kept buying it, which is own-company bias. "you shouldn't be taking your discretionary spending and buying more Enron stock" [0:37:17]
- Swedish pension savers held mostly Swedish stocks despite Sweden being 1% of world GDP. "Sweden is 1% of world GDP" [0:37:48]
- In a jar-of-coins auction the average bid is below the value but the winning bid is above it, which is the winner's curse first found by Arco engineers. "the winning bid is always above $100 if you have enough people" [0:55:28]
- Thaler asks how often an earlier NFL draft pick at a position beats the next pick, and the guess offered is below 50%. "They know less than nothing" [1:04:03]
- Warning: Behavioral finance leans on available data sets such as Odean's three years of retail accounts, which limits what the field can learn. "you can't have a field evolve looking at three years of retail traders" [1:23:05]
