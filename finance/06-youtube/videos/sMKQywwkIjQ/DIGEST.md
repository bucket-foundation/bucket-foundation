# Ses 18: Capital Budgeting II & Efficient Markets I | MIT OpenCourseWare | sMKQywwkIjQ | 1.33 h

Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on capital budgeting rules (NPV, payback, profitability index, IRR) and the start of efficient markets, using the 1986 Challenger disaster stock reaction as an event study.

Claims:
- NPV is the right criterion, and the lecture compares it against payback, profitability index and IRR. "NPV is always the right thing to do" [0:03:02]
- Payback ignores cash flows after the cutoff and has no notion of scale, while the profitability index is a gross rate of return that also says nothing about project size. "There's nothing that tells you about scale" [0:16:02]
- IRR is the discount rate that sets NPV to zero, and a k-th order cash flow polynomial can have up to k roots including complex ones, so IRR can be ambiguous when cash flows change sign. "A kth order polynomial has powers of IRR that are up to order k" [0:41:02]
- IRR is used heavily by venture capital and had been gaining on NPV in corporate surveys, which the lecturer says reflects practice rather than theory. "the venture capital community the only one that really focuses almost" [0:49:03]
- The efficient markets hypothesis underpins CAPM and NPV maximization as shareholders' goal and relies on rational investors. "we need this efficient markets hypothesis to justify most of what" [1:10:01]
- In the Challenger event study, minutes after the explosion the stock of Morton Thiokol, the booster maker, fell far more than the other contractors, long before the official finding. "Within minutes of the explosion" [1:18:02]
