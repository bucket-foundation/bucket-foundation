# The Global Minotaur: The Crash of 2008 and the Euro-Zone Crisis in Historical Perspective | Columbia University | iVxaTC7Qp44 | 1.57 h

Teaches: Yanis Varoufakis presents the thesis of his book The Global Minotaur: after 1971 the US deficit absorbed the world's surpluses and Wall Street recycled the capital back, a mechanism that broke in 2008 and left the eurozone without support. Justin Fox (Harvard Business Review) responds and a Columbia audience asks questions. The thesis is the author's interpretation and Varoufakis is a polemical speaker (his Greek political context and bailout critique appear throughout).

Claims:
- The first postwar phase (to 1971) rested on the US recycling its own trade surpluses to Europe and Japan through grants and investment; Nixon's August 1971 move acknowledged there were no more surpluses to recycle. "a term by which the first postwar" [0:09:51]
- In the second phase the US expanded its deficit and absorbed the net exports of Germany, Japan and later China; those profits flowed to Wall Street, which Varoufakis puts at $3 to $5 billion every working day for 20 years in constant prices. "billion every working day for 20 years" [0:16:30]
- Wall Street used the inflow to mint private money (CDOs and similar paper) until the pyramids collapsed in 2008 and, in his metaphor, mortally wounded the "Minotaur". "the collapse of those pyramids mortally wounded" [0:18:38]
- The euro was built on the premise that all members could behave like Germany, which fails by arithmetic since not every country can be a net exporter; US demand hid the flaw until 2008. "up the euro zone up until 2008" [0:22:56]
