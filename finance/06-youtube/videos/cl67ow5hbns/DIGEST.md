# Aydin Senkut: Chasing Demand, Not Hype, at Felicis | Capital Allocators with Ted Seides | cl67ow5hbns | 1.02 h

Teaches: Felicis founder Aydin Senkut on 20 years of early-stage investing: demand-led picks, the 100x revenue test, robotics sizing, exit engineering and fund size. Venture investing, not trading.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- Felicis keeps an early focus even with a fund near $1 billion, with 91% of investments at seed and Series A. "91% of our investments are seed and series a" [0:01:38]
- The firm looks for companies that can plausibly show at least 100x revenue growth from the investment point. "at least 100x revenue growth from the point we invest in" [0:03:18]
- Self-reported: Supabase, bought at the 2021 valuation peak, took 2 to 3 years for the market to catch up and has grown about 3,000x in business performance since. "the company's business performance has grown like almost 3" [0:05:21]
- Robotics sizing: 190 million low-skilled jobs in the US, Western Europe and Japan at an assumed $20 to 25k a year. "190 million low" [0:08:30]
- A 0.2% share of that labor market would equal $10 billion in revenue. "0 2% of that market would equal to 10 billion in revenues" [0:08:55]
- Valuations now jump 10x instead of 2x between rounds, which pressures investors to be early, with the risk that a better and more durable company arrives later. "valuations jumping 10x instead of 2x between the rounds" [0:12:55]
- A founder survey, run for the last 10 to 12 years, asks which elements founders value most. "we do a founder survey" [0:19:46]
- Benchmarking: a company at 3x growth can sit at the 58th percentile of its vintage, which gives founders context. "58th percentile" [0:26:57]
- Warning: neocloud and inference companies that build infrastructure on debt pay about 10% higher interest, so finances matter. "building their infrastructure on debt" [0:45:25]
- Rule of thumb: acquirers keep buying companies priced at 1 to 2% of their market cap. "1 to 2% of their market cap" [0:48:24]
- Without a revenue scale of 1 to 10 billion, fewer IPOs make exits unlikely, which pushed the firm to look for companies that can reach that scale. "you can never go against the market" [0:54:06]
- Felicis spends 10 to 20% of its time understanding and engineering exits, through secondaries or rescues. "10 to 20% of our time understanding visualizing and engineering exits" [0:57:33]
- In one rescue the firm recovered only about 40 to 50 cents on the dollar, which he says beat the alternative. "40 50 cents on the dollar" [0:56:22]
- He argues a 50-company fund beats a 25-company fund because obvious-only picks dilute returns. "returns are going to get diluted a little bit" [0:36:59]
