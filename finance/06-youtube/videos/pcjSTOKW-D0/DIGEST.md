# Dean Curnutt – The Reflexivity of Equity Volatility (S6E16) | Flirting with Models | pcjSTOKW-D0 | 1.30 h

Teaches: Flirting with Models interview with Dean Curnutt (Macro Risk Advisors) on reflexive volatility episodes: 1987, carry trades, pre-2008 correlation recycling and credit, AIG collateral, the 2018 VIX product blowup and the GameStop gamma squeeze.

Claims:
- The 1987 crash did not come from nowhere: the VIX reconstruction reached 150 on the day but was above 40 in the week before, a one-day fall of more than 20 percent without precedent. "the VIX on the '87 crash got to 150" [0:07:32]
- Carry trades state that the world tomorrow will look like today, and post-crisis low-rate promises made them extremely crowded. "A carry trade is really successful, and it effectively states" [0:14:21]
- Before 2008 the Fed believed credit derivatives dispersed risk, with the IG CDX contract at 20 basis points, while exposures to credit were a one-way train. "the heroism of central banking" [0:28:25]
- AIG's AAA rating let it post no collateral, and losing it forced growing postings, which Curnutt calls a poison chalice. "AAA counterparties don't post any collateral ever" [0:35:05]
- Before the 2018 VIX product blowup, advisers showed that a rise in the front future from 12 to 18 would force about 130,000 VIX futures purchases in one day, more than daily volume. "you got about 130,000 VIX futures or more to buy in one day" [0:42:25]
- Curnutt's rule of thumb that nothing bad happens in markets when realized volatility is below 15, and the lesson that regulation changes who holds risk after a blowup. "Nothing bad happens in markets when realized vol is below 15" [0:49:34]
- Whether to be long or short volatility is hard to answer; backtests show a variance risk premium but he values finding cheap optionality. "most of the back test will tell you there's a VRP" [1:10:26]
