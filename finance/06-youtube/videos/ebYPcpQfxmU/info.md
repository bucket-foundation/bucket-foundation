## Description

Is equity long-short dead in three years? Michael Watson of Hedgineer comes back on Odds on Open to argue that it is, at least in its current form. He says AI will automate the process alpha behind fundamental long/short pods: coverage initiation, earnings prep, relative valuation, multiples and consensus tracking. Ethan Kho and Michael cover what that means for multi-manager hedge funds, pod shops, single managers and prop trading firms. Topics include alpha decay, netting risk and PM compensation, center books, and why misaligned incentives in IT and infrastructure leave some multis behind. Michael also explains why crowding, short-term momentum and AI factor exposure are driving trillion-dollar names during de-leveraging events.

Michael walks through how an institutional AI research stack works: one long-running agent per name, earnings and inter-quarter monitoring, relative valuation across a coverage universe, and portfolio optimization agents that feed into sizing and risk management. He explains why context engineering is the one lever a PM controls, and why students and analysts should be writing evals to stand out in hedge fund recruiting. The episode ends with the career questions: what happens to junior analysts, whether hedge fund headcount shrinks by 2028, why family offices are growing, and how senior analysts can turn sector expertise into asset creation instead of only asset investing. This episode is for hedge fund analysts, PMs, quants, traders, allocators and MFE/MBA students who want to understand AI in investing, portfolio construction and the future of the hedge fund business.
