Teaches: Robert Shiller's lecture on futures as standardized exchange contracts. It covers the Dojima rice market, margin and daily settlement, the fair-value storage formula, contango and backwardation, the oil price history from 1871, and stock index futures with cash settlement. Data quoted is from March 18, 2011.

Claims:
- Futures quotes are the working price because contracts are standardized in grade, place and date; the spot market is hard to read. "the future but they quote them because" [0:22:02]
- Margin example: $5,000 in an account can control about $100,000 of rice at a 3% to 5% requirement; daily settlement debits the account when the price moves against the position and the broker closes out the account if margin runs out, so counterparty risk is removed. "have $5 000 and the margin requirement" [0:33:52]
- Fair value for commodities: futures price = (1 + r + s) x spot, where r is total interest to maturity (about 0.075 for 1.5 years at 5%) and s is storage cost, so contango is the normal shape and futures converge to spot at expiry. "like 0 075 because the total interest" [0:42:24]
- Oil history: Texas Railroad Commission stabilized prices in the 1940s to 1960s; the 1979 to 1980 crisis raised prices about sixfold; each spike came with a recession; oil reached over $140 in 2008. "1979 80 is the second oil crisis" [1:03:52]
- For the S&P 500 the formula is (1 + r - y) x spot with a dividend yield y of about 2% and interest near 0%, settled in cash; the curve only reflects fair value, so it does not forecast the market. "we have about a 2% dividend yield" [1:10:38]
