## Description

Jeff deGraaf of Renaissance Macro Research explains how market trends, investor behavior and real yields shape his approach to protecting capital. After a sharp selloff and rebound, he separates the evidence of a durable turn from the temptation to buy simply because prices have fallen.

Jeff joins Justin Carbonneau and Jack Forehand to discuss how he combines technical analysis with the economic cycle. He walks through RenMac's bubble signal in semiconductors, the signs he looks for in a market washout, and why a strong long-term story can become difficult to hold through a deep drawdown.

The conversation also explores what credit spreads and market breadth reveal, how AI financing is changing the risk profile of major tech companies, and why higher real bond yields create alternatives to stocks. Jeff explains how those signals inform his use of cash, commodities and sector tilts, including healthcare, and closes with the case for following trends rather than trying to call every top and bottom.

Topics covered:
- Why volatility can persist through both sharp declines and powerful rallies
- What a liquidation event looks like when traditional diversifiers fall together
- How RenMac's bubble signal identified fragility in semiconductors
- The sentiment, volume and volatility changes Jeff deGraaf watches for a washout
- How ETFs and factor trading influence the behavior of individual stocks
- Why oversold conditions mean different things in uptrends and downtrends
- Combining the market cycle clock with price confirmation and sector leadership
- What equity volatility, credit spreads and the RenMac Thrust reveal about market conditions
- AI financing, real yields and the competition between stocks and bonds
- Using cash, commodities and disciplined risk management in portfolio construction
