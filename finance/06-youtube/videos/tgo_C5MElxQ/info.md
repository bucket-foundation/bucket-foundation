## Description

Sam Klar of GMO explains the investment case for American reindustrialization and why owning the S&P 500 may leave investors with less exposure to it than they expect. The opportunity extends beyond building factories to the businesses that supply, power, connect and maintain them.

Sam joins Justin Carbonneau and Jack Forehand to explore why national security, corporate supply-chain decisions and public policy could support a long-term revival of U.S. industry. He discusses China's manufacturing rise, what COVID revealed about the risks of offshoring, and why he sees reshoring as a trend that reaches beyond any single administration.

Turning that trend into investment returns requires more than identifying companies with the right story. Sam explains how he combines business quality and valuation, using examples including Martin Marietta, Clean Harbors and MYR Group. We also discuss automation, inflation, AI's competition for investor attention, and the growing gap between a technology-heavy U.S. stock index and the domestic industrial economy.

Drawing on his work managing the GMO Domestic Resilience ETF (DRES), Sam walks through four areas of opportunity: manufacturing and automation, transportation and logistics, energy and materials, and defense. He also addresses what could go wrong, how he evaluates downside risk, and why good analysis needs to be paired with patience and temperament.

Topics covered:
- Why U.S. industrial production and GDP growth began to diverge
- How China's subsidies and supply-chain leverage shape Sam Klar's investment thesis
- The defense, corporate and public-policy forces behind reindustrialization
- Why designing and manufacturing products together can support innovation
- Tracking reshoring through jobs data, company research and site visits
- Finding quality businesses at sensible prices beyond the most crowded AI trades
- How automation could offset labor constraints and change manufacturing costs
- Why S&P 500 ownership differs from exposure to the U.S. industrial economy
- Building a portfolio across manufacturing, logistics, energy, materials and defense
- Assessing losses, relative underperformance and the temperament to invest long term
