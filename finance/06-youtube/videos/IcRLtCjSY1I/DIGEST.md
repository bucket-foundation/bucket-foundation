Teaches: A QuantInsti explainer on factor-portfolio optimization and three alternatives to mean variance: Kelly and risk parity optimization.

Claims:
- Factor portfolio optimization is the same mean variance problem with a covariance matrix from the factors plus a residual variance matrix, each with its own lambda, "we have a source of covariance matrix from the factors". [0:00:02]-[0:01:04]
- Mean variance is still a useful method because even when its assumptions are wrong the answer is more often than not close to the best solution, "closer to the perfect solution". [0:01:04]-[0:02:05]
- Kelly optimization maximizes the log of terminal wealth, which belongs to the constant relative risk aversion family, "maximize your terminal wealth". [0:02:05]-[0:03:07]
- Kelly dominates other strategies in long-run wealth and speed, but it is the most aggressive on risk and runs a higher risk of ruin in the short run, "higher risk of ruin in the short run". [0:03:07]-[0:04:08]
- Risk parity makes every asset's risk contribution equal, and the speaker says it has no theoretical justification, "there's no theoretical justification". [0:04:08]-[0:05:11]
- Risk parity uses only risk and no returns, which makes it resistant to model error because risk is measured better than returns, "better model to measure risk than returns". [0:05:11]-[0:06:13]

Framework: Each method changes the utility function: mean variance trades mean against variance, Kelly maximizes log wealth, risk parity equalizes risk contributions, "equating what you call the individual risk contribution". [0:04:08]-[0:05:11]
