# What impacts a Quant Strategy? [Panel Discussion] - Sep 24, 2020 | QuantInsti Quantitative Learning | Ne_S7iDuLi0 | 1.16 h

Teaches: QuantInsti 10-year anniversary panel with Nicolas (Factor Research), Radovan (caption spelling "quantum media") and Laurent (Tokyo short seller), moderated by Vivek Krishnamurti, on where alpha comes from and how to build, run and retire quant strategies.

Claims:
- Nicolas argues 99% of investors should not seek alpha. SPIVA scorecards show most mutual funds trail the benchmark over 5, 10 and 15 years, and equity market neutral hedge fund indexes from 2008 to 2019 "looks like a pancake" [0:03:06]-[0:05:08].
- Factor investing is alternative beta with "no free lunch for example" in value, which has not performed over ten years. Pure alpha sits in niche, capacity-limited markets, and his firm traded Japanese REITs so profitably that the prime broker shut them down [0:05:08]-[0:07:10].
- Radovan says old alpha strategies are now "they are so cheap and they are so" accessible that they count as beta. He points to roughly 300 US stocks above two and a half billion dollars outside the S&P 500, and to futures outside the main 60 to 70, where he cites research with Sharpe ratios 50 to 100 percent higher [0:07:10]-[0:12:14].
- Laurent says "63 percent of active managers on the mutual fund side have underperformed" (speaker's figure, unverified), and argues long-short investors are paid for the short side [0:13:14]-[0:16:17].
- Nicolas starts from theory, back tests over decades across asset classes with transaction and shorting costs, and "you look at 100 strategies and maybe 10 look okay". Tables hide drawdowns that charts reveal, and a 30% drawdown makes investors redeem [0:18:20]-[0:23:30].
- Radovan says back testing exists because "you want to break your strategy", and a strategy must be explainable in three sentences to his mother [0:27:35]-[0:29:38].
- Laurent says "there are two strategies one is mean reversion and the other one is trend following". Trend following is a low win rate lottery ticket and mean reversion has a left tail; he blends them by taking quick profit and letting a remainder run [0:32:41]-[0:35:42].
- On retiring strategies Nicolas says "the golden rule stick to the model" and retire only after research shows a structural change. Radovan says a single-style mandate never retires its strategy, while a multi-strategy fund can cut laggards [0:36:43]-[0:46:52].
- Nicolas says most asset classes are "kind of the same bet" on economic growth, and he seeks strategies that gain from uncertainty or volatility but finds few [0:53:56]-[0:56:57].
- Radovan advises to "use the academic paper as uh as inspiration" rather than as truth, and to look in smaller, less efficient markets [1:03:17]-[1:04:19].

Framework: Strategy selection runs as theory first ("we would like to start with a theory"), then adversarial back testing across assets with costs, then fit to client mandate and investor patience, then a retirement rule tied to the mandate [0:17:17]-[0:46:52].

Worked:
- Nicolas's team traded statistical arbitrage on US real estate stocks and MLPs, then Japanese REITs, with enough alpha that the prime broker ended the relationship [0:05:08]-[0:07:10].
- Laurent describes shorting Japanese consumer finance in 2005 or six on valuation: it paid only after a long wait, and he calls short on valuation treacherous [0:48:53]-[0:50:54].
