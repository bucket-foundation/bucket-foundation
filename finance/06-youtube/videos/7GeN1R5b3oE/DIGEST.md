# The Science Behind The Markets | Masters in Business with David Booth | Bloomberg Podcasts | 7GeN1R5b3oE | 1.15 h

Teaches: A Masters in Business interview with David Booth, founder of Dimensional Fund Advisors, on the CRSP data origin of factor investing, index funds, fee-only advisers and behavior in drawdowns.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- Booth says finance became a science once the University of Chicago built the CRSP database starting in 1926, which allowed testable hypotheses. "you need testable hypotheses" [0:02:29]
- The first index fund request in the early 1970s was an S&P 500 fund because the market could understand and track it. "an S&amp;P 500 index fund" [0:09:22]
- DFA is not plain market-cap indexing; it combines indexing with systematic factor tilts. "it's not simple market cap based indexing" [0:20:39]
- DFA focuses on what trades easily each day and uses execution and volatility as sources of better pricing. "We buy 10,000 stocks on any given day" [0:24:48]
- The future is unknowable, and uncertainty creates opportunity. "embrace that uncertainty" [0:29:05]
- In 2020 the S&P fell 34% in one quarter and then rose 69% for the rest of the year. "Was less than a quarter down 34%" [0:31:33]
- Acting on a forecast everyone already knows is a waste of time; he learned this around 1998. "it's already in the price" [0:32:14]
- His advice for tough markets is to return to first principles and the plan. "Go back to first principles" [0:31:52]
- The adviser line quoted: the problem is the client, and education is how DFA addresses it. "I've got investments with client problems" [0:47:27]
- DFA launched in 1981 and offered its first ETF in 2020. "DFA launched in 1981, in 2020 was your first ETF" [0:48:07]
- DFA uses three to five factors where traditional indexers use one, with plain-vanilla funds for clients who want no tilt. "we're going to use three or 4 or 5 factors" [0:50:53]
