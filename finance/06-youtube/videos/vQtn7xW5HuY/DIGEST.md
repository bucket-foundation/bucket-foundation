# The truth about private equity valuations, benchmarks, and returns | Enterprising Investor Podcast | CFA Institute | vQtn7xW5HuY | 0.60 h

Teaches: An Enterprising Investor podcast interview with a private markets benchmarking specialist on why private equity benchmarks disagree, how marks compare with exit prices, secondary-market discounts and what an allocator should demand. Industry opinion backed by the guest's own data set.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- The guest says the spread between public and private valuation multiples is the highest in the history of the performance data her firm tracks. "is at its highest that it's ever been" [0:00:05]
- Two benchmarks that supposedly measure the same thing can differ by several hundred basis points. "differ by several hundred basis points" [0:03:13]
- Private equity benchmarks lack transparency into underlying constituents, so investors cannot easily pick one. "there is no transparency into the underlying constituents" [0:04:06]
- Comparing a top quartile manager on IRR with a bottom quartile one on TVPI or multiple of invested capital is not meaningful, since the answer changes with the measure. "not a very meaningful comparison" [0:07:31]
- MSCI's June 2026 report found nearly 75% of buyout portfolio companies were exited above their last NAV, which the guest reads as a sign of manager conservatism. "nearly 75% of buyout portfolio" [0:10:18]
- The test is how often managers exit above the mark held 6 or 12 months before the sale. "6 months prior to transaction or 12 months prior to transactions" [0:11:52]
- Her data set holds more than 70,000 underlying investments going back to the early 2000s. "more than 70 000 underlying investments" [0:13:13]
- In 2020 and 2021 most transactions occurred significantly above NAV, a premium explained by market sentiment and sector performance. "most of the transactions were happening significantly above the nav" [0:14:18]
- Public and private valuations diverged from late 2022 when public markets revalued and recovered quickly. "started happening in the later half of 2022" [0:15:24]
- Warning: investors confuse smooth accounting marks with observable prices or true intrinsic value. "the biggest mistake i see investors make" [0:17:38]
- She is not impressed with the backtesting results of models that offer daily or weekly private valuations. "i'm not impressed with the back testing results" [0:20:35]
- A fund with a $100 million NAV and an $80 million secondary bid is a test case: bids test the mark when there is no transaction volume. "you're really testing the mark that you have" [0:23:28]
- Secondary discounts are hard to put into benchmarks because transactions are episodic and bespoke. "these transactions are still pretty episodic" [0:24:21]
- A better benchmark should equalize the unit of risk between public and private markets, as the S&P 500 methodology does for public stocks. "same unit of risk equalized between public and private markets" [0:26:11]
- Her view of risk: it is not the volatility of quarterly marks but the illiquidity risk of the allocation. "is not actually the volatility of quarterly marks" [0:00:16]
