# Stacie Mintz – Turning Qualitative Fundamentals into Quantitative Factors (S7E33) | Flirting with Models | byV7GtcQGww | 0.78 h

Teaches: Flirting with Models interview with Stacie Mintz of PGIM Quantitative Solutions on 30 years of a behavioral quant equity strategy: in-house risk models, signal counts, backtest judgment and LLM risks.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- The strategy launched in 1996 on behavioral anomalies such as overconfidence, loss aversion and anchoring to a purchase price. "being loss averse or anchoring to the price that they bought a stock at" [0:04:29]
- The team replaced the off-the-shelf Barra risk model with its own in 1999 because the model unwound intended growth exposure and left the portfolio imbalanced. "was only seeing the value piece as risk and unwinding it" [0:07:03]
- Owning the risk model allows client customization and easy integration of new ideas. "having our own risk models really helped us with customization for clients" [0:07:41]
- In the 2007 quant quake the team's risk model acted as a diversification engine for idiosyncratic risk instead of predicting which stocks were less risky. "would be more of a diversification engine" [0:09:27]
- Models carry roughly 25 to 40 signals per region, which she calls concepts with several components each. "maybe 25 to 40 signals" [0:24:54]
- She prefers a count in the 40s to the 400s because each signal needs meaningful weight and an economic underpinning. "the number is more in the 40s as opposed to" [0:25:39]
- Risk control is built into the alpha model by adjusting each factor for a better risk and return trade-off. "we have an element of risk control built right into the alpha model" [0:24:03]
- Manager discretion on the models is kept limited because the models are built to be resilient over time. "manager discretion needs to be pretty limited" [0:33:56]
- A strategy rooted in fundamentals should not necessarily work in 2020, and a backtest that works there is out of step with the philosophy. "it wouldn't necessarily work in 2020" [0:36:25]
- Warning: the scariest backtest is one that does well every year. "the scariest thing is a backtest that does well every year back in time" [0:36:49]
- She wants a backtest with periods of weakness so the team knows the model's weaknesses ahead of time. "i like to see a backtest in time that does have periods of weaknesses" [0:36:32]
- Histories for new factors and data sets are shorter, where the team once wanted 20 years of data. "we wanted to see 20 years of data" [0:37:07]
- On LLMs, she advises against using them through the whole model and manages the risk in the research process. "don't just jump in and use llms through your whole model" [0:38:31]
- LLM risks listed: hallucinations, a black box with unknown model versions, and look-ahead bias from training data. "the risk of a look ahead bias having the model trained on" [0:42:44]
