# Lecture 1, Part III: Bond “Mathematics” | MIT OpenCourseWare | NZ3Mva95UsQ | 0.36 h

Teaches: Vasily Strela on interest and bond mathematics for MIT 18.S096: compounding to e, discounting by no-arbitrage, coupon bond pricing, yield, the yield curve and inversion, duration and convexity.

Claims:
- Continuous compounding is the limit of splitting the year into m periods, and the speaker credits Bernoulli's 1683 paper on interest with the discovery of e. "that's how e was discovered and defined" [0:04:12]
- A claim on $1 in one year is worth 1/(1+r) today because any other price lets someone borrow and lock in a sure profit; this is discounting. "So for no arbitrage" [0:06:52]
- A bond is priced by discounting all coupon and notional cash flows; yield is the single flat rate that reproduces the market price, found numerically for coupon bonds. "So you back out one flat interest rate, which will give you the price of the bond" [0:12:20]
- Price and yield move inversely because the rate sits in the denominator of the discount factor. "the yield and price are in inverse relationship" [0:13:35]
- An inverted US yield curve preceded recessions in 2007 and 2019, and the lecturer treats it as generally predictive. "The year before the recession" [0:17:05]
- Bond prices are nonlinear in yield; duration is the first derivative scaled by price and convexity is the second derivative. "they're called durations because they're usually scaled by price" [0:19:51]
