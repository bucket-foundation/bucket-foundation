In this episode, we’re joined by Martin Schmalz, Professor of Finance, Economics, and Real Estate at the University of Oxford’s Saïd Business School, for a wide-ranging conversation about index funds, common ownership, investor behavior, and wealth management. Martin’s research challenges us to look beyond the benefits of indexing for individual investors and consider what happens when everyone makes the same rational choice. Could the growth of index funds affect asset prices, expected returns, and competition between companies?

We explore the equilibrium effects of indexing, how common ownership might influence competition and consumer prices, and what existing antitrust laws could mean for large institutional shareholders. Martin also explains why wealth creation and wealth preservation require different mindsets, why wealthy investors can struggle to embrace passive investing, and how delegated investment management can help people stick with an investment plan when markets become uncomfortable. We finish with a discussion of family offices, the compounding impact of costs, and Martin’s thoughts on why thinking like an economist might not always make us happier.
