# John Gu - Crypto Market Making & The Cold Start Problem (S7E30) | Flirting with Models | S1r6Jpdeomk | 1.15 h

Teaches: John Gu, founder of Caladan, on market making for new crypto token launches: how to quote with no price history, the two paid contract structures (loan plus option versus retainer), inventory and portfolio risk, spoofing, venue and ADL risk, DeFi pools, and the extension into structured products for token treasuries.

Claims:
- Caladan began from the Korean "kimchi premium" (20% to 50% above offshore prices, hard to trade because of capital controls) and short-term cross-exchange arbitrage; Gu expected the trades to disappear. "between 20 to 50% premium to exchanges" [0:09:45]
- At launch the mid matters less than the bid and ask: quote wide and small, narrow gradually, and tune the mid from trading feedback. Exchanges and the project communicate a reasonable price range and the market anchors on it. "a mid and just quote really wide" [0:16:57]
- Caladan is a passive price taker; the work is nearly always money-losing on its own, so projects pay the market maker. "us we're very passive we're price takers" [0:17:43]
- Markets bifurcate: one path becomes self-sustaining organic trading, the other leaves only market makers with tightly controlled inventory and insider manipulation risk. "of potentially insider manipulation and that becomes" [0:21:25]
- Retainer contracts at about $2,000 to $5,000 a month put P&L risk on the project. "know $2 000 $5 000 a month" [0:29:27]
- Inventory deployment is path dependent; they sell embedded delta spaced out in time and keep reserves to buy back on drops. "do is sell the delta the embedded" [0:32:13]
- Risk is split into a market component and an idiosyncratic one; small and new tokens are almost all idiosyncratic, so pricing is skewed from own inventory with tight bounds, and holding periods are short. "inventory and we just have tighter bounds" [0:35:37]
- Spoofing example: someone who traded 10% to 20% of ES manipulated it while profiting in related markets; table selection of tokens and venues is the main defense in an adversarial environment. "traded 10 20% of the es market" [0:37:14]
- ADL risk grows with profit; manage it by trading out of large winners and limiting notional against open interest. "this particular thing relative to open interest" [0:44:31]

- Quote wide and small first; let flow tune the mid; manage inventory by delta hedging an option or by contract; split risk into market and idiosyncratic; choose venues and tokens with edge. "to manage the inventory using the option" [0:29:02]
