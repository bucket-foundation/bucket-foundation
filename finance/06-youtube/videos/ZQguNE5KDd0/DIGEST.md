Teaches: Flirting with Models interview with Roni Israelov on volatility risk premium strategy construction, the 2007 quant quake dispersion across managers, informed trading with short-term signals, tail hedging, tax-loss harvesting frontiers and terminal wealth dispersion.

Claims:
- In the 2007 quant quake, managers who cut their books during the four-day losses missed the rebound, and results diverged widely across managers by their response. "you saw incredible dispersion actually across quant managers" [0:15:20]
- Adding a short-term signal to a long-term model makes the optimal strategy trade more aggressively; trading costs rise slightly but exposure to the long-term signal also increases. "you actually increase your exposure to your long-term signal" [0:31:38]
- A beta-neutral, delta-hedged volatility risk premium strategy can be built to be unconditionally uncorrelated with the market, with attractive historical returns and Sharpe ratio. "it's possible to construct a VRP strategy that is unconditionally uncorrelated to the underlying market" [0:47:28]
- A tail-hedged or capital-efficient fund that is sized for a good stand-alone return risks catastrophic loss, such as down 60 percent if equities fall 20 percent, which few would allocate to if told. "if equities are down 20%, this fund is going to be down 60%" [0:55:20]
- Moving tax-loss harvesting from monthly to daily gives more harvest yield but proportionally more tracking error, with the two frontiers nearly overlapping, so the benefit is small. "those frontiers are nearly on top of each other" [1:03:35]
- Frontiers across objectives look similar across assets and asset classes, so calibration can be treated as a panel rather than asset-by-asset. "the efficient frontiers across these objectives look pretty similar across assets" [0:23:19]
