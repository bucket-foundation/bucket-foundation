# Forwards Pricing: Consumption Assets | IIT Roorkee July 2018 | 2C5iRNWGUJw | 0.58 h

Teaches: NPTEL IIT Roorkee lecture on forward pricing for consumption assets: why reverse arbitrage fails when holders value physical possession (convenience yield), a wheat example, a currency forward valuation, forwards on dividend-paying stocks and forward interest rates.

Claims:
- Convenience yield is the benefit of keeping the physical asset, which is why holders will not sell it to capture reverse arbitrage. "The preference, the desire, the temptation to retain physical possession of the asset" [0:06:04]
- The worked example has wheat at 1,200 rupees per quintal, 150 storage cost paid quarterly in advance, a 1,425 one-year forward and a 10 percent risk-free rate, from which the convenience yield per annum is solved. "Storage costs work out to rupees 150 per quintal payable quarterly in advance" [0:06:20]
- A long and a short currency forward at different prices cancel the dollar leg and leave a profit of 15 rupees at maturity, worth its present value today. "The value of the strategy as on 31st December is 15 rupees" [0:13:32]
- Forward prices on dividend-paying stocks subtract the present value of dividends still to come before maturity. "this dividend payment will be accounted for" [0:20:11]
- Forward interest rates follow from investing for three years either at the 3-year spot rate or by rolling shorter periods. "A will be equal to P or P 0 into 1 plus S03 to the power 3" [0:27:33]
