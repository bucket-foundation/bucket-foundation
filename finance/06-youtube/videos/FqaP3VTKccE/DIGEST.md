# Cliff Asness: A Brief and Biased Survey of Quantitative Investing | Mizzou Business | Trulaske College of Business | FqaP3VTKccE | 1.17 h

Teaches: AQR founder Cliff Asness explains what quantitative equity investing is, rebuts common misconceptions, and walks through value, momentum, carry and defensive (low risk) styles, the out-of-sample tests behind them, the August 2007 quant crisis, and why he expects fees on simple factor strategies to fall.

Claims:
- A good quant model is not a black box: any holding or short has a precise stated reason, and quants mostly use the same inputs as fundamental managers (cheapness, growth, momentum, risk). "call a quantitative manager a black box" [0:09:16]
- Quants mostly trade value (buy cheap, sell expensive) and 6 to 12 month momentum, holding a stock for about a year; high frequency trading is a separate activity AQR does not do. "is even about 6 to 12 months" [0:16:00]
- Value works because cheap stocks get a little too cheap, and cheap companies are often worse companies; value and momentum often point opposite ways, so a combined model buys the 14th cheapest stock that is recovering and skips the cheapest one still falling. "14th cheapest stock in the universe because" [0:21:55]
- A quantitative model goes long about 400 stocks with the characteristics it likes and shorts 400 with the opposite, so it does not bet on an individual company. "400 stocks that in aggregate have the characteristics it likes" [0:23:31]
