# Ultimate Guide to Portfolio Management | Quantitative Trading Strategies | By Prodipta Ghosh | QuantInsti Quantitative Learning | u5UUjHZQCFk | 1.32 h

Teaches: A QuantInsti webinar by Prodipta Ghosh on portfolio management as a strategy of strategies: why picking the top backtested strategy fails, portfolio weights, the St. Petersburg paradox and risk premium, CAPM, factor investing and the breadth versus skill trade-off.

Claims and warnings. Figures and results are self-reported by the speakers and unverified.

- Questions about the best strategy depend on each person's goals, levels and risk appetite, so even a perfect model of the world cannot answer them in general. "we have all different risk appetite" [0:03:19]
- Four strategies backtested through July 2018 (Bollinger, moving average crossover, RSI) are ranked by Sharpe ratio, and the top pick is invested in from there. "typically we will pick a strategy based on his sharpe ratios" [0:07:03]
- Self-reported: the chosen strategy showed about 14.77% return in the backtest, then suffered a drawdown of close to 11% after investing. "you have a huge draw down and that drawdown is on campus and closer to 11%" [0:07:51]
- A year of extra investing left returns roughly where the backtest said, which the speaker uses to show backtested winners do not carry forward. "the retires scaled to a certain level is not much different" [0:08:24]
- A strategy is a buy-and-hold or trading rule; portfolio management is a strategy for strategies concerned with risk and uncertainty. "portfolio management on the other hand is a major strategy" [0:10:58]
- Portfolio value is the weighted stream of returns, for example 10 stocks at 10% each on $100,000. "portfolio value is the weighted underlying stream of regions" [0:13:38]
- St. Petersburg game: the pot doubles per head and the game ends on the first tail, which raises the question of how much one should pay to play. "how much one should pay to play this fair game" [0:24:17]
- Risk premium: with a 3% safe return against an 8% expected return from equity, the premium is 5%. "the risk premia in the market with respect to that particular investment that is equity is 8 minus 3" [0:29:21]
- Weekly returns are scaled with an annualization factor of 52, while daily data use 252. "scaled them with with a analyzation factor to 52" [0:38:48]
- Adding a risk-free asset at 4% puts each investor's portfolio somewhere on a line between the risk-free asset and the market portfolio. "somewhere along this line" [0:43:43]
- CAPM: expected return is the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium. "risk free rate plus is peter that is some kind of contribution to the market portfolio" [0:48:10]
- Factor investing, popular after 2008, replaces the single market premium with a series of risk premia. "it became very popular after 2008" [0:50:46]
- CAPM says an investor should seek the assets with highest expected return, because systemic risk can be diversified away and is not rewarded. "you don't get paid for the systemic risk" [0:56:21]
- Breadth versus skill: a manager of 10 stocks needs a skill level above 70% hit rate to beat the market with 95% confidence, while a 500-stock systematic manager needs only 55 to 57%. "it has to go way above 70%" [0:59:19]
- The key differentiator is the square root of the number of independent bets. "the square root of m and n is the main differentiating factor" [1:01:04]
