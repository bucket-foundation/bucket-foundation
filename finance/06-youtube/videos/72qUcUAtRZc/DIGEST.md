# 14. Guest Speaker Maurice "Hank" Greenberg | YaleCourses | 72qUcUAtRZc | 1.18 h

Teaches: Hank Greenberg, former AIG chief executive, describes how he built AIG from a failing unit, its compensation and risk culture, and his account of the 2008 collapse (credit default swaps, collateral calls, the Fed loan). He is an interested party and says he feels bitter, so the crisis account is one-sided. Questions from students follow.

Claims:
- He built AIG by dropping agents at American Home, writing large commercial risks backed by Lloyd's reinsurance, then buying National Union and New Hampshire Insurance and forming AIG in 1967. "'60s and by 1967 i created aig" [0:10:36]
- AIG ran a 19% expense ratio against about 30% for most insurers (self-reported). "we ran our company with an expense ratio of 19%" [0:13:38]
- Pay rules: nobody earned more than $1 million in salary, nobody had a contract, and bonus shares set aside through the private Starr companies vested at retirement as golden handcuffs at no cost to AIG shareholders. "certain number of aig shares set aside" [0:24:20]
- AIG grew from a $300 million market cap at its public offering to close to $200 billion, with about 15% held by the Starr companies at the end. "market cap was close to $200 billion" [0:22:54]
- AIG Financial Products wrote more credit default swaps in the nine months after he left than in the prior seven years (his claim). "more credit default swaps in the nine months after i left" [0:34:18]
- Credit default swaps changed from paying on default to requiring collateral on any loss in value; with no exchange there was no price discovery, so collateral calls ran AIG out of cash once it lost its AAA rating. "price discovery because there was no exchange" [0:36:34]
- The New York Fed lent AIG $85 billion at 14.5% interest and took 79.9% of the equity. "borrowed $85 billion from the new york fed at 14 5% interest" [0:41:14]
- His reform list: CDS pay only on default, trade on an exchange, issuers hold insurance-style reserves; investment banks should not bet against clients; "better regulators, not more regulation". "reserves if i was going to issue" [1:02:36]


Worked:
- Incentives tied to long-term performance and shares forfeited on exit kept people, and he says very few left. "lot of money very few people left" [0:24:39]
- Enterprise risk monitoring of market and credit risk, with a mirrored computer system for the derivatives unit. "we monitored both market risk and credit" [0:27:44]
- The CEO is the top risk manager and culture starts at the top. "greenberg the culture starts at the top" [1:10:05]
- China life license took from 1975 to 1992 of repeated visits, and an agent commission model was introduced there. "that if a security in this case" [0:34:37]

Failed:
- The parent AIG was left without a bank holding company license while Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Hartford received one. "things so goldman sachs and morgan stanley" [0:40:20]
- Rating agencies gave AAA to diversified mortgage packages and the SEC ignored 30 to 40 times borrowing against capital and stayed silent on mark-to-market rules, in his view. "leveraging their capital 30 and 40 times" [0:46:28]
- Boards failed to see what was happening after he left, and many directors folded under Spitzer's pressure. "recognize what was happening after i left" [0:57:00]
- Treasury under Rubin declined an exchange and regulation for credit default swaps, per his account. "an exchange and regulating credit default swaps" [0:36:57]
- Government intervention picked who to save; he calls the AIG take of 92% an unlawful taking (opinion). "leader he has very little experience internationally" [0:59:34]
