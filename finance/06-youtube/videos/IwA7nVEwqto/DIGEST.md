# Ses 10: Forward and Futures Contracts II & Options I | MIT OpenCourseWare | IwA7nVEwqto | 1.33 h

Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on futures and options: margining and daily settlement, cost-of-carry pricing for forwards and futures, hedging a 1 million dollar portfolio with S&P futures, and calls and puts as insurance.

Claims:
- A futures contract starts at zero net present value and is marked to market daily, with gains and losses paid through a margin account that removes the credit risk built into forwards. "forward contracts contain enormous amounts of credit risk" [0:13:06]
- A crude oil contract covers 1,000 barrels and an initial margin of 4,050 dollars controls the full notional, which is why leverage is the chain saw of finance. "each contract is for 1,000 barrels" [0:02:00]
- Forward and futures prices follow no-arbitrage cost of carry: spot times (1 + r) plus storage cost minus convenience yield; gold has almost no storage cost so futures equal spot times (1 + r). "the convenience yield" [0:32:08]
- An S&P 500 futures contract has a 250 dollar multiplier, so four contracts mimic a 1 million dollar index position, and selling five contracts hedges part of a 5 million dollar portfolio faster and cheaper than selling stocks. "you need four of those contracts" [0:46:19]
- A call payoff is max of S minus K and zero, a put gives the right to sell at K, and a put is an insurance contract on a stock whose premium is the price of the put. "A put option is nothing more than an insurance contract on the value of a stock" [1:12:05]
- Insurance via puts is available but costly when implied volatility is high, so the question is whether it is worth the price. "The implied volatility is up by at least an order of magnitude" [1:17:00]
