# Ses 11: Options II | MIT OpenCourseWare | rMsu4v-UlkA | 0.98 h

Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 lecture on option payoff diagrams (long and short calls and puts, straddles, spreads), implied volatility and the VIX, equity as a call on firm assets, and the history of option pricing from Cardano to Black-Scholes-Merton. The pricing derivation is postponed to the next session.

Claims:
- A call has an unlimited upside and a downside capped at the premium paid; its payoff is kinked at the strike, unlike the straight-line payoff of a stock or future. "straight line it's kinked at the strike" [0:06:27]
- A long call plus a long put at the same strike pays off when the market moves a lot in either direction, a bet on volatility; when volatility is high the premium is large and the V shifts down. "a lot because volatility is very high" [0:22:01]
- Buying a call at 50 and selling one at 60 is cheaper than the plain call because the short call funds part of it, at the price of giving up the upside above 60. "price going above 60 so you're giving" [0:26:39]
- Lo says any payoff can be approximated by a sequence of calls and puts. "be approximated by a sequence of calls" [0:28:57]
- Inverting an option price gives implied volatility; the VIX does this for at-the-money S&P 500 options. He cites historical S&P volatility of 15 to 20% against an intraday VIX high of 89% the prior week and 71% in class. "volatility reached an interdaily high of 89%" [0:32:38]
- Equity is a call on firm assets with the debt's face value as strike, and debt is a short put plus borrowing; capital structure arbitrage trades the gap between modeled and market debt and equity values. "capital structure arbitrage says that this equation" [0:37:18]
- Black and Scholes were rejected by about five journals until retitled around corporate liabilities; Merton delayed his own paper so both appeared the same year; Bachelier's 1900 thesis had already used Brownian motion and the heat equation. "heat equation you actually use the same" [0:50:56]


Worked:
- Spreads and straddles let a holder bet on volatility or a range rather than direction. "are not more volatile than this range" [0:23:33]
- Implied volatility inverted from option prices is used daily and published as the VIX. "invert the market price for the volatility" [0:31:10]
- Heat-equation mathematics from physics solved the option pricing problem. "had never heard of the heat equation" [0:55:38]
