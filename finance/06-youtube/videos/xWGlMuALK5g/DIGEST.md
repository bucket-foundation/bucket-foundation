# Cliff Asness on Why Most Investors Quit At the Exact Wrong Time | How I Invest Podcast | xWGlMuALK5g | 1.50 h

Teaches: AQR's Cliff Asness explains why good strategies fail in practice (clients leave after about three bad years), how AQR combines value, momentum, macro and arbitrage modules, how it screens signals against data mining, and why he favors gearing up diversified, equal-risk bets.

Claims:
- Optimal hockey play is pulling the goalie about 5 minutes out when down one and 10 plus minutes out when down two; coaches decline because of reputational incentives, and active managers face the same pull [0:01:31]-[0:04:32].
- About three years is as long as many institutional clients hold an underperforming strategy, and "statistical time" in a 100-year backtest is longer than "behavioral time" [0:05:17]-[0:06:48].
- At institutional scale he sees no 2 to 3 Sharpe strategies, so even diversified books have hard stretches; AQR copes by pre-educating clients, researching improvements and staying open-minded without caving [0:09:05]-[0:12:57].
- The 2001 paper "Do hedge funds hedge?" found hedge fund indices about half hedged (beta 0.4 to 0.5), correlation over 0.8 to the market and about zero alpha, using imperfect index data [0:30:28]-[0:32:00].
- AQR stacks four layers: individual stock long-short on value and momentum, hedged macro, directional macro, and arbitrage such as merger arbitrage; a study by Mitchell and Pulvino found merger arbitrage managers, after high fees, do not beat a lower-fee rules-based book that takes every deal [0:35:49]-[0:43:25].
- A signal with a 50.02% hit rate would probably not pass; testing a thousand random series yields one that looks amazing, so AQR demands long-term evidence plus economic sense [0:49:30]-[0:51:48].
- With machine learning the data versus story mix moved from about 50/50 to about two thirds data, and natural language models read earnings calls better than word lists [0:47:13]-[0:52:34].
- Trend following has positive convexity: he says it made money in nine of ten big equity drawdowns and broke even in the tenth; self-reported and unverified [0:57:58]-[0:58:45].
- Gearing up equalizes risk across asset classes: equal dollars in equities, bonds and commodities lets commodities dominate, so scale bonds up and commodities down [1:04:03]-[1:07:07].
- A high-volatility version of an alternative needs fewer dollars; AQR now offers both volatility levels with fees scaled in proportion [1:10:57]-[1:16:16].

Framework: Signal needs long-term evidence plus an economic story, modules (stocks, macro, directional, arbitrage) combine into products, risk is equalized by gearing up, and client education decides who stays long enough to be paid.

Worked:
- Stock and country value plus momentum was the Goldman group starting point in 1994, building on his dissertation, whose data ended in 1990 [0:35:49]-[0:37:21].
- Profitability and low beta additions improved the factor models; he admits profitability lacks a good theory [0:44:55]-[0:55:37].

Failed:
- Value had a hard stretch from 2018 through 2020 and drove repeated tests of the intangibles argument; price to sales did about as badly as price to book [0:11:25]-[0:16:48].
- Market up, you down is the hardest quadrant to hold for clients [0:27:26].
- Off-the-run versus on-the-run Treasury arbitrage is skipped because the left tail is too big; gearing plus illiquidity is the "death combination" [1:07:54]-[1:09:24].
