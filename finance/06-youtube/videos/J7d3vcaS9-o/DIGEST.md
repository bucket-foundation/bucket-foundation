# Ses 14: Portfolio Theory II | MIT OpenCourseWare | J7d3vcaS9-o | 1.34 h

Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on portfolio theory: portfolio mean as a weighted average, variance as a sum over covariances, diversification, correlation effects on the mean-standard deviation frontier, and the tangency portfolio.

Claims:
- Portfolio expected return is a weighted average of the stock returns, but portfolio variance is not a weighted average of variances; it contains n squared terms and depends on covariances. "the variance of the portfolio is not a simple weighted average of the variances" [0:06:08]
- Diversification works because covariances outnumber variances, so correlations matter more than individual variances in a large portfolio. "there are a lot more covariances than there are variances" [0:12:00]
- A 50-50 mix of General Motors and Motorola earns the average return but has less risk than the average, giving a portfolio that beats General Motors on both return and risk. "everybody in this room should prefer that dot to General Motors" [0:36:00]
- With correlation of minus one a combination exists with zero risk, and as correlation rises the bullet-shaped frontier collapses toward a straight line. "You get straight lines of different stripes" [0:56:08]
- Monthly standard deviation scales to annual by the square root of 12 under no correlation across months. "What's the square root of 12" [0:23:06]
- As the number of stocks grows, average variance stops mattering and the leftover undiversifiable risk is set by average covariance; in a crisis correlations jump because everyone sells at once. "everybody is going to be selling at the same time" [1:02:00]
