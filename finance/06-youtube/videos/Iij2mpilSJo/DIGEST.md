# 3. Technology and Invention in Finance | YaleCourses | Iij2mpilSJo | 1.26 h

Teaches: Finance as engineering. Shiller reviews the central limit theorem and fat tails, then uses inventions (limited liability, Chinese township enterprises, inflation-indexed bonds, Chile's Unidad de Fomento, swaps, credit default swaps) to show how risk pooling, framing and human factors drive adoption.

Claims:
- The central limit theorem makes averages of independent, identically distributed variables with finite variance approach the normal curve; the fat-tail caveat is that the variance assumption can fail. Paul Pierre Levy first developed fat-tailed theory and taught Mandelbrot. "you have independent identically distributed random variables" [0:08:32]
- Since 1970 finance gained options exchanges, financial futures, swaps and electronic trading, none of which existed then. "in 1970 there were no options exchanges" [0:15:06]
- New York's 1811 corporate law let anybody start a corporation by filing papers with the government. "anybody can start a corporation just file the papers" [0:42:17]
- Township and Village Enterprises in China: 12 million by 1985 (as he recalls), and most industrial output by the mid-1990s; the village shared in profits, which protected entrepreneurs where contract courts were weak. "there were 12 million tves in china" [0:49:41]
- The first indexed bond was issued by Massachusetts in 1780 and was forgotten in the US until 1997 (Larry Summers); indexed bonds reached over 10% of US debt and sat near 6% at the time of the lecture. He argues 100% should be indexed. "over 10% of the u s debt" [0:57:23]
- Chile created the Unidad de Fomento in 1967, an inflation-indexed unit of account; one UF was 21,468 pesos at the time, prices having risen about 50-fold since 1977. Rents and alimony are quoted in UFs. "the dollar sign for pesos 21 468" [1:02:52]
- US consumer prices rose 22-fold since 1913, about 3% a year. "consumer price index in 1913 it's 22" [1:06:00]
- Swaps are contracts to exchange cash flows (David Swensen is credited with the invention at Salomon Brothers in the early 1980s); ISDA lobbied for the legal framework. A credit default swap has a protection buyer paying regular premiums to a seller who pays on a defined credit event. "david swensen's name the credit default swap" [1:11:09]

- The Rollaboard suitcase, invented by Robert Plath in 1991, is used as an example of a delayed invention. "it was 1991 and he called it the rollaboard" [0:34:52]

Worked:
- Limited liability funneled capital to New York companies. "in a company and it's limited liability" [0:46:14]
- Index units of account protected Chilean contracts from inflation. "important because without it chileans wouldn't index" [1:03:30]
- A currency swap fixes the conversion rate between euros and dollars in advance, which is useful for risk management. "we decide in advance on what the conversion is" [1:09:18]

Failed:
- The credit default swap market failed in the crisis and AIG was bailed out; Shiller attributes it to new, poorly understood risk and defends the invention. "failure of the credit default swap market" [1:15:11]
- Inventions often vanish or wait decades (indexed bonds from 1780 to 1997, gimlets), and a psychological barrier blocks complicated contracts. "1997 it's amazing why did the invention" [0:55:40]
