# Strategic Python Trading with Michael Harris | Techniques for Market Regime Changes | QuantInsti Quantitative Learning | uk6L0gfHKYg | 0.22 h

Teaches: Michael Harris on regime changes in the S&P 500 between momentum and mean reversion, shown through moving-average and mean-reversion strategies and his "mersion" indicator, in a QuantInsti session.

Claims:
- A long/short strategy on S&P 500 daily data, long after an up day or a price above the two-day moving average, earned an annualized return of 27.6% against about 9% for buy and hold from 1941 to 1999, "annualized return of 27.6%" (speaker's figure, unverified). [0:00:08]-[0:01:11]
- Harris says the result looks like a refutation of market efficiency but is a way to identify regime shifts and not a practical strategy, "this refutes the efficient market hypothesis". [0:01:11]-[0:02:14]
- Long-only moving-average rules with periods from two to 20 beat buy and hold, with t statistics as high as 8.5, "all moving averages from with the period from two to 20". [0:03:17]-[0:04:19]

- The market moved from momentum to mean reversion in daily returns, which Harris attributes to a crowded trade driven by reflexivity, "reflexivity that resulted in a". [0:04:19]-[0:05:21]

Framework: Markets alternate between a momentum regime, where simple momentum rules profit, and a mean-reversion regime, where those rules fail; the "mersion indicator" measures the relative strength of the two in daily returns. [0:08:26]-[0:09:27]
