Teaches: Andrew Lo's MIT 15.401 session on finishing CAPM and applying it to capital budgeting: use cash flows not accounting earnings, depreciation enters only through taxes, beta sets the discount rate, with a publishing and an oil example.

Claims:
- Realized returns against beta look linear but with a flatter slope than the market risk premium, which motivates the Black CAPM using a zero-beta portfolio instead of a risk-free rate. "the Black CAPM says that there is no risk-free rate" [0:10:30]
- Capital budgeting must use project cash flows, because accounting earnings serve other purposes than decision making. "you should use cash flows, not accounting earnings" [0:30:27]
- Depreciation affects a project only through taxes: project tax equals the tax rate times operating profit minus the tax rate times depreciation. "So your depreciation doesn't actually affect the profitability of the particular piece of equipment" [0:40:17]
- With a beta of 1.29, a 5 percent risk-free rate and a 6 percent market premium, the lecture computes a 12.7 percent cost of capital for a publisher. "it turns out that the cost of capital is 12.7%" [1:00:21]
- Oil production is a high-beta activity, so the example uses a 20 percent discount rate against a 5 percent risk-free rate for after-tax profit of 20 dollars a barrel over 3 million barrels. "The industry discount rate of oil production is 20%" [1:10:24]
- Whether to take on distress risk is a question of magnitude: bankruptcy costs versus the value of the upside. "What are the bankruptcy costs versus the value of the upside" [0:50:21]
