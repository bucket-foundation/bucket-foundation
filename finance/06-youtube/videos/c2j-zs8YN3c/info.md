## Description

Mean reversion is widely used in quantitative trading, where prices or returns move away from typical levels and later shift back. These strategies work well alongside trend systems, especially in range-bound markets. The concept relies on stationarity, with the mean acting as a reference point, and z-scores showing how far values deviate before reverting.

This video covers 10 categories of mean reversion, including:
-Price Level Reversion: Prices moving around a dynamic mean, such as in Bollinger Band–based methods.
-Return Reversion: Short-term recoveries after sharp price swings driven by sentiment or liquidity shifts.
-Relative Value Reversion: Convergence between related assets, often seen in pairs trading.
-Volatility Reversion: The tendency of volatility measures like the VIX to drift back toward long-term averages.
-Regime-Based Approaches: Separating mean-reverting phases from trending periods, since behaviour changes during volatility spikes or macro events.
-Machine Learning Methods: Models that estimate the likelihood and pace of reversion using multiple features instead of fixed rules.

Mean reversion reflects the market’s regular cycle of stretching, correcting, and settling back toward balance.
