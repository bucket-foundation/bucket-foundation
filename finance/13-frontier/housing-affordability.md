# Housing Affordability

## Question
Is US housing structurally unaffordable, and will prices, rates or incomes adjust to restore the historical norm?

## Case That It Is Structural
- The NAR Housing Affordability Index read 100.7 in October 2026, meaning a median-income family has exactly the income to qualify for a median-priced home. [empirical: FRED FIXHAI, 2026-11-27, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FIXHAI]
- The 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.21% on 2026-11-25. [empirical: FRED MORTGAGE30US, 2026-11-27, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US]
- Harvard JCHS reports 22.7 million renter households, 49%, cost-burdened in 2024, a record, and 20.7 million owner households cost-burdened. [empirical: JCHS State of the Nation's Housing 2026, as reported by NLIHC and Novogradac, fetched 2026-11-27, https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/state-nations-housing-2026]

## Case That It Is Adjusting
- The median sales price fell from $413,500 in Q2 2026 to $393,300 in Q3 2026. [empirical: FRED MSPUS, 2026-11-27, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MSPUS]
- Case-Shiller US national index slipped from 336.779 in August to 335.754 in September 2026. [empirical: FRED CSUSHPINSA, 2026-11-27, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CSUSHPINSA]
- JCHS notes rising vacancies and slightly lower rents in some regions in Q1 2026. [empirical: JCHS 2026, url above]

## What Would Settle It
- The affordability index holding above 120 for a year, its level before the 2022 rate rise.
- Housing completions outpacing household formation in JCHS data.
