# Prediction Market Legality

## Question
Are sports and event contracts on CFTC-registered exchanges such as Kalshi federally regulated swaps that preempt state law, or gambling that states may ban?

## Case For Federal Preemption
- The Third Circuit held in April 2026 that Kalshi contracts are outside New Jersey gambling law. [empirical: Al Jazeera, 2026-09-25, fetched 2026-11-27, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/us-court-rules-against-kalshi-says-states-can-regulate-prediction-markets]
- The CFTC sued Illinois, Arizona and Connecticut, claiming exclusive jurisdiction under the Commodity Exchange Act, and later sued Wisconsin, Utah, Louisiana, Minnesota and New York. [empirical: PYMNTS 2026; CFTC complaints OGC_UtahComplaint102626 and OGC_LouisianaCompliant111326, fetched 2026-11-27, https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/cftc-sues-to-stop-states-from-regulating-event-contracts/ and https://www.cftc.gov/media/14936/OGC_LouisianaCompliant111326/download]
- A New Jersey federal judge let Kalshi keep offering sports contracts while its suit proceeds. [empirical: Front Office Sports, Nov 2026, fetched 2026-11-27, https://frontofficesports.com/article/kalshi-wins-preliminary-ruling-new-jersey-sports-contracts/]

## Case For State Authority
- The Sixth Circuit, in a unanimous opinion by Judge Gibbons on 2026-09-25, held Ohio and Tennessee may apply gambling law to event contracts. [empirical: Al Jazeera, 2026-09-25, fetched 2026-11-27, url above]
- The Ninth Circuit in August 2026 held Kalshi contracts subject to Nevada gambling law. [empirical: same source]
- The Second Circuit denied Kalshi and Coinbase injunctions against New York and Connecticut pending appeal. A Connecticut judge ruled the sports contracts never qualified as swaps. [empirical: CDC Gaming Reports and Crowdfund Insider, 2026, fetched 2026-11-27, https://cdcgaming.com/brief/second-circuit-allows-new-york-connecticut-to-enforce-laws-against-kalshi-coinbase/ and https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/08/296440-connecticut-judge-rules-kalshi-sports-contracts-never-qualified-as-swaps/]
- New York sued Polymarket on 2026-09-24. [empirical: Al Jazeera, url above]

Status on 2026-11-27: a circuit split stands, more than 20 suits are pending, and no Supreme Court petition was confirmed in fetched sources.

## What Would Settle It
- A Supreme Court ruling on whether CEA exclusive jurisdiction covers sports event contracts.
- Congress amending the CEA, or a CFTC rule defining gaming contracts under section 5c(c)(5)(C).
