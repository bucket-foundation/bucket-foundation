# Factor Zoo

## Question
Of the hundreds of published cross-sectional return predictors, how many are real and survive out of sample?

## Case That Most Factors Are False
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu survey hundreds of published factors and argue a new factor needs a t-ratio above 3.0 once multiple testing is priced in. [empirical: NBER w20592 abstract, 2026-11-27, https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592] DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv059, Review of Financial Studies, 2015. [empirical: Crossref, 2026-11-27, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1093/rfs/hhv059]
- Hou, Xue and Zhang replicate 452 anomalies with NYSE breakpoints and value weights: 65% fail a t-hurdle of 1.96, 96% of trading-friction anomalies fail, and 82% fail the multiple-test hurdle of 2.78. DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhy131, Review of Financial Studies, 2020 issue, online 2018-12-10. [empirical: Crossref, 2026-11-27, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1093/rfs/hhy131]

## Case That Most Factors Replicate
- Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen build a Bayesian model of factor replication and find most factors replicate, cluster into 13 themes, and work out of sample in data covering 93 countries. DOI 10.1111/jofi.13249, Journal of Finance, 2023. [empirical: Crossref, 2026-11-27, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/jofi.13249]

## What Would Settle It
- Agreement on the null: frequentist t-hurdles versus Bayesian shrinkage give different answers on the same data.
- Post-publication decay measured on a common, pre-registered universe.
- Live fund returns net of costs on the 13 JKP themes.
