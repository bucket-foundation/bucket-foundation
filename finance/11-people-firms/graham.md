## Profile

<!-- voice-ignore-next 3 -->
> Benjamin Graham (; né Grossbaum; May 9, 1894 – September 21, 1976) was an English-American financial analyst, economist, accountant, investor and professor.
> He is widely known as the "father of value investing", and wrote two of the discipline's founding texts: Security Analysis (1934) with David Dodd, and The Intelligent Investor (1949).
> His investment philosophy stressed independent thinking, emotional detachment, and careful security analysis, emphasizing the importance of distinguishing the price of a stock from the value of its underlying business.

## Documented Methods

<!-- voice-ignore-next 6 -->
> Graham laid the groundwork for value investing at mutual funds, hedge funds, diversified holding companies, and other investment vehicles.
> He also advocated the creation of index funds decades before they were introduced.
> Therein, Graham showcased his emerging thoughts and methods on value investing, such as his method of calculating goodwill.
> Warren Buffett describes The Intelligent Investor (1949) as "the best book about investing ever written." Graham exhorted the stock market participant to first draw a fundamental distinction between investment and speculation.
> With that perspective in mind, the stock owner should be unconcerned with erratic fluctuations in stock prices, since in the short term the stock market behaves like a voting machine, but in the long term it acts like a weighing machine (i.e. its true value will be reflected in its stock price in the long run).
> Graham distinguished between defensive and enterprising investors.

## Published Figures

<!-- voice-ignore-next 3 -->
> Graham's investment performance was approximately a ~20% annualized return over 1936 to 1956.
> The overall market performance for the same time period was 12.2% annually on average.
> Graham's largest gain was from GEICO, in which the firm Graham-Newman purchased a 50% interest in 1948 for $712,500.
