## Deep Read

Source: open-access NBER Working Paper 17464, the 2011 version, revised, of the paper published in the Journal of Financial Economics 110(3), 2013. Read 2026-11-27.

### Research Question

How does uncertainty about which policy a government will choose affect stock prices, volatility, correlations and risk premia, and does that depend on the state of the economy?

### Data and Sample

The core is a general equilibrium model. The empirical checks use the Baker, Bloom and Davis (2012) policy uncertainty (PU) index, US stock returns, realized volatility and correlation measures, and five measures of economic conditions, monthly from January 1985 to December 2010.

### Method

Firms' profitability depends on government policy. A government chooses at a known date whether to replace the current policy with one of several new policies, weighing investor welfare against a political cost that is uncertain to investors. Investors learn about the current policy's impact and about political costs (Proposition 1 gives the choice rule). The authors derive the state price density, the SDF (Propositions 2 and 3), stock prices and returns (Propositions 4 and 5) and announcement returns (Proposition 6), calibrated in Table 1. Empirically: regressions of PU on economic conditions (Table 2), of volatility and correlation on PU (Table 3), with PU times economic conditions interactions (Table 4), and of future market returns on PU and its interaction with economic conditions, equation (62), with Newey-West errors (Table 5).

### Main Results

Theory: the government replaces policy when the economy is weak, which acts as an implicit put on the market. Political uncertainty is a priced risk whose premium is larger in weak economies; it lowers the value of the put; and it makes stocks more volatile and more correlated, more so when the economy is weak. Announcement returns depend on how much the policy choice surprises investors, so returns alone do not judge policy quality.

Table 2: all 10 slope estimates of PU on economic conditions are negative; 8 are significant at 5 percent. PU is higher in weak economies.

Table 3: volatility and correlation rise with PU in all eight regressions, all significant.

Table 4: the PU effect on volatility is stronger when the economy is weak in 18 of 20 significant estimates; for correlation, 5 are significant.

Table 5: unconditionally, PU does not predict future returns with statistical significance at any horizon. The interaction of PU with economic conditions is negative in all 15 specifications, 6 significant at 5 percent; at the 12-month horizon it is significant at 10 percent under all five measures. A fitted equity premium from dividend yield, T-bill rate and term spread rises with PU.

### Robustness

Several measures of economic conditions, horizons of 1 to 12 months, and the fitted equity premium as an alternative dependent variable.

### Stated Limits

The authors call their empirical work simple; 26 years of monthly returns give low power for the risk premium tests, and the unconditional premium is not detected.

### Retail Systematic Trader

Policy uncertainty raises volatility and correlation, most in weak economies, and the market's reliance on a government put fades when the political path is unclear. A retail book can use the public Economic Policy Uncertainty index as a risk input: when it spikes in a weak economy, expect diversification across stocks to work less and size positions down. The return-prediction evidence is weak, so it fits risk management better than timing.
