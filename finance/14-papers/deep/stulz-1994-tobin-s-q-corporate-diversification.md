## Deep Read

Source: open-access NBER Working Paper 4376, June 1993, the version published by Lang and Stulz in the Journal of Political Economy 102(6), 1994. Read 2026-11-27.

### Research Question

Are diversified firms valued below specialized firms, measured by Tobin's q, and can industry mix, size, R&D or financing constraints explain the gap?

### Data and Sample

Compustat Industry Segment and Business Information files, including research files, 1978 to 1990. Firms with average total assets above $100 million, about 1,400 a year (Table 3). Tobin's q follows the Lindenberg and Ross method. 192 firms that add segments and 320 that cut from five or more to four or fewer (Table 7).

### Method

Yearly regressions of q on dummies for having at least two, three, four and five segments, measuring the marginal q effect of each added segment (Table 3). A "chop-shop" industry adjustment: each division gets the average q of single-segment firms in its 3-digit SIC code, weighted by division assets, giving a pure-play q; the discount is pure-play q minus actual q (Table 4). Regressions add size, R&D, access to capital markets and other q determinants (Tables 5 and 6). Firms that change their segment count are compared with those that do not (Table 7).

### Main Results

Table 3: moving from one to two segments lowers q every year; the coefficient is minus 0.14 (t = minus 2.60) in 1978, minus 0.45 in 1981, minus 0.71 in 1982 and minus 1.01 (t = minus 5.92) in 1983. Further segments add smaller, mostly insignificant reductions.

Table 4, 1984: the unadjusted discount relative to single-segment firms is 0.62 for two-segment firms, 0.62 for three, 0.75 for four and 0.86 for five or more. After industry adjustment, mean discounts are 0.35 (t = 5.80), 0.43, 0.43 and 0.49 (t = 11.23); median discounts are 0.24 to 0.38, all with sign-test p-values below 0.01.

The industry-adjusted discount is positive and significant every year. Firms with five or more segments have mean and median q below one every year. Controls for size, R&D and capital market access shrink the discount without removing it.

Table 7: firms that diversify had lower q than other single-segment firms before they added segments, consistent with firms seeking growth through diversification after exhausting internal opportunities.

### Robustness

Several diversification measures (segment counts and Herfindahl indices), medians and nonparametric tests, firms with a constant number of segments, each year of the 1980s, and controls for size, R&D and financing constraints.

### Stated Limits

Segment reporting is self-defined and may bias comparisons; the authors can give only indirect evidence that reporting biases or unmeasured advantages of diversified firms do not drive the result. The evidence is less clear on whether diversification causes the low q or low-q firms choose to diversify.

### Retail Systematic Trader

Conglomerates trade at a discount to the sum of their parts, and firms that diversify do so after their growth slows. A screen can flag firms announcing diversifying acquisitions as weaker candidates and treat spin-offs and focus-increasing restructurings as events that can close the discount. The paper measures valuation levels without testing future returns, so a trader needs a separate return test before building a strategy on it.
