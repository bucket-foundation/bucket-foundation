## Deep Read

Source: open-access ECGI Finance Working Paper 446/2015, October 2016, the version published in the Journal of Finance 72(4), 2017. The extracted text overlays a second ECGI paper's cover page; the body is the Lins, Servaes and Tamayo paper. Read 2026-11-27.

### Research Question

Did firms that built social capital through corporate social responsibility earn higher stock returns when trust in corporations collapsed in the 2008 to 2009 financial crisis?

### Data and Sample

1,673 US non-financial firms with CSR ratings in the MSCI ESG Stats database, formerly KLD, measured before the crisis. Crisis window August 2008 to March 2009. Financial firms are excluded because of government support. Micro-caps below $250 million in 2007 dollars are dropped. Returns and controls are winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles. Regional trust comes from the World Values Survey.

### Method

Cross-sectional regressions of crisis-period raw returns and abnormal returns on pre-crisis CSR, the scaled strengths minus weaknesses across community, diversity, employee relations, environment and products. Controls include size, book to market, momentum, leverage, cash, profitability, idiosyncratic risk, Fama-French and momentum loadings and two-digit industry dummies. Panel B of Table II uses CSR quartile dummies; Panel C adds governance controls. Table III repeats the test in the Enron and WorldCom scandal window, October 2001 to March 2003. Later tables test regional trust, the post-crisis recovery and operating outcomes.

### Main Results

Table II, Panel A: a one standard deviation rise in CSR, 0.381, goes with 2.25 percentage points higher raw returns and 4.15 points higher abnormal returns during the crisis.

Table II, Panel B: firms in the top CSR quartile beat the bottom quartile by 5.52 percentage points in raw returns without firm controls and 4.53 with them; for abnormal returns the gaps are 9.85 and 7.27 points.

Table III, Enron and WorldCom window: the linear CSR measure is positive and insignificant; a dummy for positive CSR gives 7.3 points higher raw returns and 9.5 points higher abnormal returns.

High-CSR firms gained more where regional trust is high. They also showed higher profitability, margins, sales growth and sales per employee during the crisis. No return difference appears in the recovery after March 2009.

### Robustness

Governance controls, CSR quartiles, factor-model abnormal returns, firm and time fixed effects in panel tests, and the second trust shock in 2001 to 2003.

### Stated Limits

Two caveats: unobserved time-varying firm differences could drive the results, and CSR is one of several channels for building social capital that the paper does not compare.

### Retail Systematic Trader

The result is conditional: high-CSR firms outperform only in a crisis of trust and show no premium in normal times or the recovery. A retail book can treat a CSR or ESG tilt as cheap crash insurance on the equity sleeve, with little expected cost in calm markets. The evidence rests on two episodes, so it does not justify a standalone ESG factor bet.
