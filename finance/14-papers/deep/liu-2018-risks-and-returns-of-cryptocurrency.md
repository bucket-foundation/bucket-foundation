## Deep Read

Source: open-access NBER Working Paper 24877, August 2018, the version published in the Review of Financial Studies 34(6), 2021. Read 2026-11-27.

### Research Question

Do Bitcoin, Ripple and Ethereum returns load on the factors that price stocks, currencies, commodities and macro risk, and which factors predict crypto returns?

### Data and Sample

CoinDesk prices: Bitcoin from January 1, 2011, Ripple from August 4, 2013, Ethereum from August 7, 2015, all to May 31, 2018, at daily, weekly and monthly frequency. Google search volumes, Twitter post counts for "Bitcoin" from Crimson Hexagon, Bitcoin wallet users from blockchain.info, and mining cost proxies. Comparison assets: CRSP stocks, bonds, currencies, gold, silver and other commodities, and macro series.

### Method

Summary statistics by frequency (Tables 1 to 4). Regressions of crypto returns on CAPM, Fama-French 3 and 5 factor, Carhart, and other stock factors (Tables 5 to 7), on currency returns (Tables 8 to 10), commodities (Tables 11 and 12) and macro factors (Table 13). Time-series momentum: regressions of future returns on current returns, and quintile sorts of weekly returns (Tables 14 to 17, with an out-of-sample cutoff test in Table 17). Attention: Google and Twitter measures, and the ratio of "Bitcoin hack" to "Bitcoin" searches. Valuation: price over wallet users as a price-to-"dividend" ratio. Supply: mining cost proxies.

### Main Results

Table 1: Bitcoin's mean daily return is 0.52 percent with 5.55 percent volatility, a daily Sharpe ratio of 0.09; monthly mean 21.6 percent, volatility 69.5 percent, skewness 4.32, kurtosis 25.4.

Tables 5 to 13: loadings on stock market, currency, commodity and macro factors are small and mostly insignificant.

Table 14: a one standard deviation rise in today's Bitcoin return predicts 0.33 percent higher return the next day (coefficient 0.06, t = 2.96). Weekly coefficients are 0.19, 0.22 and 0.21 at one to three weeks ahead.

Table 15: sorting weekly Bitcoin returns into quintiles, the top quintile earns 11.22 percent the next week, Sharpe 0.45, and the bottom quintile 2.60 percent, Sharpe 0.19. Table 16 shows a smaller but significant effect from 2013 on.

Attention: a one standard deviation rise in Google searches for "Bitcoin" predicts 2.3 percent higher two-week-ahead returns; Twitter counts predict 2.5 percent higher one-week returns; a one standard deviation rise in the hack-search ratio predicts 2.75 percent lower returns the next week.

Price over wallet users, realized volatility (for Bitcoin and Ethereum) and mining costs do not predict returns.

### Robustness

Subsamples from 2013, out-of-sample quintile cutoffs from the first two years (Table 17), and three coins. Ethereum momentum is weaker.

### Stated Limits

The sample is short, seven years for Bitcoin and under three for Ethereum, and dominated by a few booms. The authors do not model trading costs or exchange risk.

### Retail Systematic Trader

Crypto momentum at one to four weeks is the actionable result: hold after strong weeks, step aside after weak ones. The lab's crypto trend replication tests a version of this. Crypto's low loading on stock factors makes a small allocation a diversifier in normal times. Results come from 2011 to 2018; returns of that size have not persisted, and the trader should model exchange fees, funding rates and the post-2018 decline in momentum strength before sizing.
