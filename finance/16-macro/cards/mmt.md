# Modern Monetary Theory

ID `M-mmt`. As of 2026-11-27. Section: monetary theory.

## What It Is
A school built on the state theory of money, chartalism, and Abba Lerner's functional finance. Main source used here: L. Randall Wray, "From the State Theory of Money to Modern Money Theory", Levy Institute Working Paper 792, March 2014. Grade A.

## Claims
Quoted from WP 792. Grade A.
- The sovereign state "can ensure at least some acceptability for its IOUs by imposing taxes and other monetary obligations."
- So long as it promises no conversion "at a fixed rate to precious metal or foreign currency, it can never be forced into involuntary default."
- "The limit on state spending is not arbitrary deficit or debt ratios but rather real resources offered for sale for the state's currency."
<!-- voice-ignore-next 1 -->
- "the main problem with excessive spending by the state is inflation, not risk of default and insolvency."
- Lerner: government "should increase taxes only if inflation threatens," and sell bonds "only if there is downward pressure on interest rates, pushing them below the central bank's target rate."

## Where It Meets Plumbing
MMT's account of bond sales draining reserves to hold the overnight rate matches the pre-2008 corridor. Under the [ample reserves framework](ample-reserves-framework.md) the Fed pays [IORB](iorb.md) instead, so bonds and reserves are near substitutes at the short end.

## Test Against 2026
The constraint MMT names, inflation, binds: CPI 4.08% with a $2.0 trillion FY2026 deficit. MMT's prescription is a tax rise; the Fed's response was a rate hike in Sept 2026. Compare [fiscal-theory-price-level](fiscal-theory-price-level.md) and [fiscal-dominance](fiscal-dominance.md).
