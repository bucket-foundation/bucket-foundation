# Ample Reserves Framework

ID `M-ample-reserves-framework`. As of 2026-11-27. Section: central bank operations.

## What It Is
The operating regime the FOMC chose on 30 Jan 2019: the Fed keeps reserves plentiful enough that small changes in supply do not move overnight rates, and it controls rates through IORB and ON RRP. The pre-2008 corridor system needed daily open market operations to fine-tune a scarce reserve supply.

## Evidence
- 30 Jan 2019 statement: "an ample supply of reserves ensures that control over the level of the federal funds rate ... is exercised primarily through the setting of the Federal Reserve's administered rates, and in which active management of the supply of reserves is not required." Grade A.
- 11 Oct 2019, after the September repo spike: the Fed would "purchase Treasury bills at least into the second quarter of next year in order to maintain over time ample reserve balances at or above the level that prevailed in early September 2019", described as "purely technical measures" that "do not represent a change in the stance of monetary policy." Grade A.
- 28 Oct 2026 directive repeats the pattern: bill purchases "to maintain an ample level of reserves." Grade A.

## Current State
Reserves $2,979.7B on 18 Nov 2026; ON RRP near zero; SOFR 3.95% above IORB 3.90%. The system sits near the boundary between abundant and ample.

## Market Reaction
Reserve management bill purchases are read by some as QE; the Fed frames them as technical. Bill yields fall and repo spreads narrow when purchases start.
