# Bank of England 2014: Money Creation in the Modern Economy

ID `M-boe-2014-money-creation`. As of 2026-11-27. Section: monetary theory.

## What It Is
Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1 article by Michael McLeay, Amar Radia and Ryland Thomas of the Bank's Monetary Analysis Directorate. A central bank stating in plain terms how deposits come into being.

## Claims
Quoted from the PDF. Grade A.
- "Whenever a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit in the borrower's bank account, thereby creating new money."
- "Rather than banks receiving deposits when households save and then lending them out, bank lending creates deposits."
- "In normal times, the central bank does not fix the amount of money in circulation, nor is central bank money 'multiplied up' into more loans and deposits."
- On the money multiplier: "it is not an accurate description of how money is created in reality." Central banks set "the price of reserves, that is, interest rates."
- Bank deposits "make up the vast majority, 97% of the amount currently in circulation" of broad money.

## Limits on Money Creation
Three sets of constraints: banks' own limits, from profitable lending, risk control and regulation; the behavior of households and companies, who can destroy money by repaying debt; and monetary policy, "the ultimate limit on money creation."

## QE
<!-- voice-ignore-next 1 -->
QE "is intended to boost the amount of money in the economy directly by purchasing assets, mainly from non-bank financial companies," first raising those companies' deposits.

## Why It Matters Here
It underpins the [endogenous-money](endogenous-money.md) view and explains why [bank-reserves](bank-reserves.md) work as a price-set settlement asset under the [ample reserves framework](ample-reserves-framework.md). It cuts against the textbook [quantity theory](quantity-theory.md) mechanism, in which the central bank fixes the money stock.
