# Debt Deflation

ID `M-debt-deflation-fisher`. As of 2026-11-27. Section: business cycles.

## What It Is
Irving Fisher, "The Debt-Deflation Theory of Great Depressions", Econometrica 1933. Two primary factors, "over-indebtedness to start with and deflation following soon after." Grade A.

## Mechanism
Fisher's nine links: (1) debt liquidation and distress selling; (2) contraction of deposit currency as bank loans are paid off, and slower velocity; (3) a fall in prices; (4) a greater fall in net worth and bankruptcies; (5) a fall in profits; (6) cuts in output, trade and employment; (7) pessimism and loss of confidence; (8) hoarding and slower velocity; (9) disturbances in interest rates, a fall in nominal rates. The paradox: "The more the debtors pay, the more they owe."

## Link to Other Cards
Link (2) is the BoE 2014 point run in reverse: repaying loans destroys deposits; see [boe-2014-money-creation](boe-2014-money-creation.md). Fisher wrote that reflation interrupts the chain, which is the [lender of last resort](../../01-money-history/CRISIS-PATTERNS.md) lesson.

## Current Read
US inflation runs above 4%, so the deflation leg is absent in Nov 2026.
