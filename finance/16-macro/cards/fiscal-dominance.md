# Fiscal Dominance

ID `M-fiscal-dominance`. As of 2026-11-27. Section: fiscal.

## What It Is
Thomas Sargent and Neil Wallace, "Some Unpleasant Monetarist Arithmetic", Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis Quarterly Review, Fall 1981. Grade A.

## Claims
- When monetary policy dominates, "the monetary authority can permanently control inflation in a monetarist economy, because it is completely free to choose any path for base money."
- When "fiscal policy dominates monetary policy," the fiscal authority sets all current and future deficits, and the monetary authority "must try to finance with seignorage any discrepancy between the revenue demanded by the fiscal authority and the amount of bonds that can be sold to the public."
- If deficits "cannot be financed solely by new bond sales, then the monetary authority is forced to create money."
- In one example, "tighter money today leads to higher inflation not only eventually but starting today."
