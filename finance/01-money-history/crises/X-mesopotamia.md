# X-mesopotamia: Mesopotamian Debt Cancellations

Event card, c. 2400 to 1600 BCE. Grades: A primary or lab-computed, B secondary, C snippet or single unchecked source.

## What Happened
Mesopotamian kings proclaimed debt cancellations. The *misharum* edicts of Old Babylon cancelled grain debts and freed people sold into debt bondage. The Edict of Ammisaduqa is the one *misharum* text that survives almost complete.

## Causes and Mechanics
Farmers borrowed grain against the next harvest at fixed interest. A bad harvest or war turned debt into bondage and shrank the free cultivator base the palace taxed and conscripted. Kings reset the ledger at accession and in crises; the edicts had to be repeated because debts rebuilt each time.

## Systematic Trader View
The signal is the debt stock relative to the harvest. When private debt outgrew the output that served it, the ruler wrote it down. Modern analogue: write-downs come from the sovereign when debt service crowds out the tax and military base.
