# X-south-sea: South Sea Bubble

Event card, 1720. Grades: A primary or lab-computed, B secondary, C snippet or single unchecked source.

## What Happened
The South Sea Company took over £31 million of British state debt by swapping it for shares. The share price ran from about £128 in January 1720 to £1,000 in August and fell to about £100 by December.

## Causes and Mechanics
Annuitants exchanged state debt for shares at market price. A higher price meant fewer shares per pound of debt, leaving surplus shares for the promoters, who had every reason to pump. The Bubble Act of June 1720 blocked rival unchartered companies; the price broke weeks after the August peak.

## Systematic Trader View
The issuer profited from its own share price, so the price was the product. A rule of thumb that held: when a firm's earnings come from issuing stock at a premium, sell. Exit came within six weeks of the peak.
