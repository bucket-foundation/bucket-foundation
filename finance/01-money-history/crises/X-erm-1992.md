# X-erm-1992: ERM Crisis, Black Wednesday

Event card, 16 September 1992. Grades: A primary or lab-computed, B secondary, C snippet or single unchecked source.

## What Happened
Britain left the European Exchange Rate Mechanism on 16 September 1992 after selling reserves and raising rates failed to keep sterling in its band. The Treasury later put the day's cost at £3.3 billion.

## Causes and Mechanics
Sterling joined the ERM in October 1990 at DM 2.95, a rate that needed German-level interest rates during a UK recession. Speculators shorted sterling against a fixed parity; the Bank of England bought £2 billion an hour and lifted Bank Rate from 10% to 12%, then announced 15%, and gave up that evening.

## Systematic Trader View
A peg defended with rate hikes during a recession has asymmetric payoff: the short loses a little if the peg holds and gains a lot if it breaks. Emergency rate hikes that fail to move the price are the exit signal for defenders and the entry for the short.
