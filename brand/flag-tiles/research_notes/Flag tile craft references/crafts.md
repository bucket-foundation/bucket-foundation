# Regional craft references for flag tiles

Research date: 2026-10-22.

## Country pairings

### Takeaway

Sixteen country tiles can share one flag construction and vary their surrounding material treatment. Each pairing names a place, community, or production tradition; a country can support additional editions.

### Inferences

- Material and process provide a shared design vocabulary: lacquer gloss, woven relief, ceramic glaze, and fused enamel. These are proposed translations for digital tiles.
- Place and community names belong in the detail view alongside the source. The country label identifies the flag; the craft label identifies that edition's reference.

### Gaps

- The Portuguese object record supports a Portuguese origin. Its workshop or city of manufacture is unknown.
- These sources establish craft attribution. They provide no permission from a maker or community to reproduce a particular pattern.

## Tile translation

### Takeaway

Keep the flag face fixed and put craft expression in the rim, sidewall and material response. The proposals use new low-detail marks and process cues.

### Cited Findings

- Kente patterns can communicate rank and social meaning, and batik includes ceremonial designs. These associations support recording motif provenance before reuse. [Smithsonian kente record](https://www.si.edu/object/wrapper%3Anmafa_EJ10571), [UNESCO Indonesian Batik](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indonesianbatik-00170).
- The documented Limoges example is a reliquary, while the Fez zellij reference came from a house. Object context is part of the reference. [V&A champlevé enamelling](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/how-was-it-made-champlev%C3%A9-enamelling), [Batha Museum panel](https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=object%3BISL%3Bma%3BMus01_C%3B31%3Ben).

### Inferences

All treatments in this table are design proposals. Keep the original flag artwork, proportions and orientation on an uninterrupted inset face. Keep cultural ornament outside that face, including its emblems and writing.

| Country | Material translation | Motif boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portugal | A white ceramic rim with a blue grid at the edge and shallow glaze pooling. | Invent the edge rhythm. Exclude copied saint panels and devotional scenes. |
| Japan | A rounded lacquer body with a quiet polished highlight and an unchanged red disc. | Use the roiro finish cue. Exclude shrine emblems, family crests and copied maki-e compositions. |
| Mexico | A ceramic rim with pooled colour and a small original dot rhythm. | Keep the flag's coat of arms intact. Exclude copied religious tile scenes and workshop marks. |
| India | A fabric-like outer rim with stamped dots and small registration variation. | Keep the Ashoka Chakra intact. Exclude sacred scripts, deity imagery and copied workshop blocks. |
| Ghana | Shallow strip seams and woven relief around the flag. | Use plain strip construction. Reserve named kente patterns and rank-coded combinations for maker-led editions. |
| Morocco | Cut-piece ceramic joints on the outer rim, with low-contrast grid relief. | Keep the flag's pentagram intact. Exclude copied sacred calligraphy and mosque panel compositions. |
| Türkiye | White glazed ceramic with a small raised red edge accent. | Keep the crescent and star intact. Exclude calligraphy and copied mosque tile panels. |
| Italy | An opaque white ceramic edge with a few original brush dots. | Exclude copied devotional scenes and heraldic devices. Keep brushwork outside the tricolour. |
| France | A copper-toned edge surrounding a smooth enamel face. | Exclude reliquary figures and cross decorations. Keep metal partitions outside the tricolour. |
| Netherlands | White ceramic with a narrow blue brush-wave edge. | Exclude Royal Delft marks and copied commissioned compositions. Keep the flag palette unchanged. |
| Finland | A translucent glass rim with a matte inner bevel and restrained refraction. | Create a new outline. Exclude branded vase silhouettes, maker marks and borrowed Sámi ornament. |
| Peru | Plain woven ridges with a stitched edge around the flag. | Reserve named Taquile designs and ceremonial garment patterns for community-led editions. |
| South Korea | A grey clay sidewall under brushed white slip and shallow incised edge marks. | Keep the taegeuk and trigrams intact. Exclude copied ritual vessel decoration or historic bureau seals. |
| Brazil | A glazed ceramic rim divided into small modules. | Create a new grid using unpainted cells. Reserve Athos Bulcão motifs and mural arrangements for licensed work. |
| Indonesia | A cotton-like rim with original wax-resist dots. | Reserve named court, ritual and lineage patterns for maker review. Keep the red and white flag fields solid. |
| New Zealand | A plain terracotta sidewall and neutral glazed face informed by Coromandel studio material. | Use no Māori motif adaptation in the default tile. A carving edition requires a Māori-led commission and agreed permissions. |

### Gaps

- This pass does not establish fabrication specifications for physical tiles. Material safety, tactile readability and durability need prototype testing if the scope expands beyond screens.
- Country flag geometry and permitted variants need their own source records. The craft references establish surface direction.

## Cultural rights

### Takeaway

The default set can use process cues and new marks. Named cultural patterns need provenance and an agreed scope of use before entering a production library.

### Cited Findings

- New Zealand's Intellectual Property Office explains collective ownership and responsibilities in Māori perspectives. It also states that use of Māori elements may require consent from traditional owners. [IPONZ Māori culture and intellectual property](https://www.iponz.govt.nz/get-ip/maori-ip/maori-culture-and-intellectual-property/).
- UNESCO describes producer communities, family transmission and social meaning in kente, and describes community life and collective decision-making in Taquile. [UNESCO Kente](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/craftsmanship-of-traditional-woven-textile-kente-02130), [UNESCO Taquile](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/textile-art-of-taquile-00166).

### Inferences

- Commission a named practitioner for editions that adapt a recognizable cultural pattern. Record attribution, compensation, approval and allowed reuse in the asset record.
- Use the New Zealand terracotta edition as the default. Keep Māori carving as a separate commission with its own authorship and permissions.
- The companion JSON contains prototype material tokens. Its colours and pattern labels are design decisions. Historical colour specifications and reproduction permissions require separate records.
- Describe digital work as inspired by a named process. A rendered texture carries no claim that a tile was made by a practitioner or certified workshop.

### Gaps

- No maker, community or rights holder has reviewed these proposed editions.
- UNESCO inscription pages returned limited page bodies during direct fetches. Their indexed text supplied the cited craft descriptions; other museum and institutional pages were opened for their source text.
- Smithsonian and Iittala direct fetches failed during this pass. Their indexed object and process descriptions supplied the cited facts. Museum images remain reference material; none were copied into the tile library.
