# Creators and live rooms should fund Polingual

Polingual should run a freemium subscription priced near **$12.99 a month, $69.99 a year, $119.99 for a family or group plan and a $199 to $249 lifetime tier**, keep Polingual Play's daily games free with paid archives and hosting inside that subscription, keep Bucket free with light rewarded ads, and grow through creators paid **25% of net proceeds** and live lobbies where the host pays and guests join free. The reason is arithmetic. At category medians a paid install turns into a subscriber 2.6% of the time, so Apple Ads in the US cost about **$112 per paying subscriber** against about **$59 of first-year net revenue** on the annual plan, while a creator on a 25% share costs about **$15 per annual subscriber** and is paid only after the subscriber pays. The money to build this comes first from non-dilutive sources that are open now, led by the NSF SBIR Learning and Cognition topic at up to $2M with zero equity, then from a pre-seed round pitched to Reach Capital, Owl Ventures and GSV as a live team game for languages, a lane less crowded than AI speaking tutors. The ICP, people who already speak two languages and are learning a third, numbers roughly 100M in the EU alone and gathers in r/languagelearning, HelloTalk, Tandem and polyglot YouTube, which is where the creator list comes from. The stack that ships live mobile games fastest for this team keeps native SwiftUI, Compose and Next.js screens, adds Rive for animation and moves game rules into one shared Rust core. The strongest combination is creators plus a RevenueCat-linked affiliate tool plus the 25% share aimed at the ICP, and the dated work runs through May 2027.

Two earlier reports set the frame this one builds on. The games market report ([Polingual Play games market](01-play-games-market.md)) concluded that Polingual Play should own the shared-screen room and that subscription carries revenue while portal ads buy reach. The expansion map ([Polingual expansion map](02-expansion-map.md)) ranked the lobby inside the subscription, venue nights, Teacher Pro, career vocabulary and sponsored intercomprehension nights as the top five moves and laid out a 12-month build sequence. Those conclusions stand except where the game tech section revises the engine choice.

## Price the annual plan at $69.99 and let hosts pay

### Subscriptions carry language apps and ads pay cents

Ad money cannot fund a learning product at Polingual's scale. Casual ads-only games earn **$0.01 to $0.05 per daily user** and hybrid ads plus in-app purchases reach $0.15 to $0.50 ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)). At $0.03, 10,000 daily players earn about $110K a year. Duolingo's Q3 2026 letter shows the shape of a mature language business: **$270.0M of $309.0M revenue came from subscriptions**, ads were flat at $20.8M and in-app purchases fell 38% to $6.9M ([Duolingo Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). With **13.1M payers out of 153.1M monthly users**, Duolingo converts 8.6% of its audience and earns roughly $82 to $84 a year per payer. The NYT Games pattern fits Polingual Play: core Wordle stays free and archives, stats and Wordle Bot sit behind a **$50 a year** games subscription, with a $120 family plan ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/09/the-new-york-times-launches-a-family-subscription-with-separate-wordles-for-everyone/)).

Category medians set the starting price. RevenueCat's 2026 education data puts the median at **$9.99 a month and $44.99 a year**, the highest yearly median of any category, with 6.5% download-to-trial and $0.30 revenue per install by day 14 ([RevenueCat Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)). Speak charges about **$19 a month** and grew from about $15M to about $100M annualized revenue between mid-2024 and November 2025 ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/compare/babbel-vs-speak); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/topics/25/11/48975908/this-1-billion-ai-startup-backed-by-openai-khosla-ventures-just-hit-100m-revenue-racing-to-beat-duolingo)), which shows a large segment pays twice the median for practice that feels like progress. Duolingo Super Family sells near **$119.99 a year for six people** ([Mindomax](https://www.mindomax.com/duolingo-cost)) and Babbel's all-languages lifetime plan lists at **$299.99** ([Copycatcafe](https://copycatcafe.com/babbel)). Education apps earned 17% of 2025 revenue from one-time purchases, more than most categories ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)), which supports a lifetime tier for polyglots who dislike stacking subscriptions.

### Learners pay little for apps and a lot for tutors

Most people interested in language apps will not pay: **67% prefer a free version or free trial**, and those willing to pay $15 or more a month lean toward a human tutor ([CivicScience](https://civicscience.com/?p=37961)). Paddle's analysis of Busuu and Duolingo data puts personal willingness to pay near **$6.31 a month**, with business use above twice that ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/blog/busuu-duolingo-pricing)). Tutoring sets a higher ceiling: italki community tutors charge $5 to $15 an hour and professional teachers $10 to $40 ([learnenglish.life](https://learnenglish.life/guides/how-much-does-italki-cost/)), so a learner with one $20 lesson a week spends over $1,000 a year, and a $69.99 app that complements tutoring looks cheap against it. Geography moves the number. Adapty's price index puts Italy and Spain at **2.3x the US**, France at 2.0x, Germany at 1.9x and Mexico and Brazil at 0.4x, and localization tests win 62.3% of the time ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). Polingual's multilingual ICP skews toward Europe, Canada and immigrant communities, so regional tiers matter more here than for a US-centric app. No survey isolates what people who already speak two languages pay for a third, which is the largest open pricing question; the ICP section names the cheapest way to answer it.

### The 3-day trial is shorter than the evidence supports

Polingual ships a 3-day trial today. RevenueCat finds trials under 4 days convert at a **25.5% median against 42.5% for trials of 17 to 32 days**, and education trials cluster at 5 to 9 days ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Education users also decide late: only 71.3% of education trials start on day 0 and **23.5% start 31 or more days after install**, the highest of any category, and trial users carry 50.4% higher LTV than direct buyers ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). The free Bucket app and the free daily games give Polingual a warm-up period before the paywall, so the trial should move to 7 days with a 14-day arm in the first experiment. Apps that run 50 or more paywall experiments post a median revenue of $914,734 against $48,848 for apps that run one ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)).

### Estimated CAC by channel

The paid funnel at medians is 6.5% download-to-trial times about 40% trial-to-paid, or **2.6% install-to-paid**. Apple Ads' 2025 US median for education is a **$2.91 cost per install**, and the global all-category median is $1.80 ([AppTweak](https://apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)). A $69.99 annual plan nets $59.49 after Apple's 15% small-business fee, and Adapty's education 12-month LTV is $45.10 ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)). Break-even CPI on year-one revenue sits near $1.50.

| Channel | Cost per paying subscriber, estimated | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Ads US, education | About $112 | $2.91 CPI over a 2.6% install-to-paid rate |
| Apple Ads, global median | About $69 | $1.80 CPI over 2.6% |
| Meta, education | Hundreds to thousands of dollars; unreliable | Superads reports a $70.3 average education CPI ranging $9.31 to $167.21 a month ([Superads](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/education)) |
| Creator on 25% of net | About $14.87 per annual payer in year one | Paid only after conversion |
| Flat creator integration | $1,500 to $4,500 per 100K-view video | Education sponsorship CPMs of $15 to $45 ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/); [Spansa](https://dev.to/tryspansa/youtube-sponsorship-rates-in-2026-what-brands-pay-1dh0)); breaks even at 25 to 76 annual payers |
| Live lobbies and share cards | Near zero media cost | Each host brings 3 to 10 players |

A mix of half organic, 30% creator-driven and 20% Apple Ads gives a blended CAC near **$27 per payer**, under first-year net. This is a planning estimate built on medians. Duolingo spends about **13.6% of revenue on sales and marketing** and credits its growth to organic brand work and retention experiments ([Duolingo Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)), which is the ceiling to plan against. Reaching $1M ARR takes about 15,000 payers at $67 net; at the median funnel that is about 575,000 installs, and at 5% install-to-paid it is under 300,000. Lifting conversion through lobbies and the free tier moves the business more than any media channel. Every figure here should be replaced with Polingual's own RevenueCat funnel within 60 days of the pricing launch.

## NSF SBIR is the grant open to Polingual today

### Federal money narrowed to SBIR

SBIR and STTR were reauthorized through September 2031 by Public Law 119-83 in April 2026 ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/learn/guides/sbir-reauthorization-2026-guide)), while the federal language programs around them were cut. The Department of Education sent all 228 Title VI grantees non-continuation notices in September 2025 and requested no IFLE funding for FY2026 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41276-026-00573-2)); STARTALK was decommissioned in April 2025 ([CAL](https://www.cal.org/projects/startalk/)); FIPSE money was redirected to AI and civil discourse and zeroed in the FY2027 request ([Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/how-higher-ed-would-fare-in-trumps-latest-budget-proposal/816653/)).

The best fit is the **NSF SBIR Learning and Cognition Technologies topic**, which funds startups turning learning research into products and states NSF "will invest up to $2 million in seed funding and take zero equity" ([NSF Seed Fund](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/topics/learning-cognition-technologies/)). It has funded game-driven learning companies such as Muzology. The process starts with a short Project Pitch and needs a US small business at least 50% owned by US citizens or permanent residents, with the PI working at least 20 hours a week at the company. Phase I amounts and deadlines conflict across third-party guides, so the Project Pitch should go in now and the current cap be confirmed on nsf.gov. ED/IES SBIR fits a classroom product: FY2026 offered **$250K Phase IA and IB awards and $1M Direct to Phase II**, released April 30 and due June 29, 2026 ([IES](https://ies.ed.gov/funding/research/programs/small-business-innovation-research-sbir/solicitation-information)). The FY2027 solicitation is unannounced, and the FY2026 timing points to spring 2027. The Teacher Pro lobby from the expansion map is the eligible use case.

### Europe funds intercomprehension through partners

Erasmus+ KA220 Cooperation Partnerships host intercomprehension work such as EVAL-IC, which built assessment descriptors for Romance intercomprehension ([Salzburg](https://www.plus.ac.at/language-centre/research/projects/completed-projects/eval-ic/?lang=en)). Coordinators must be established in the EU two years before the deadline. The 2027 call is the last of the 2021 to 2027 programme, expected in November or December 2026 with a deadline near early March, following the 2026 date of March 5 ([growthprojects.eu](https://growthprojects.eu/erasmus-2026-deadlines/)). Polingual's route is to join a consortium as the technology partner. EIC Accelerator and Innovate UK need an EU or UK entity.

### Labs that need Polingual's learners

Third-language acquisition research needs exactly the people Polingual serves. Stockholm University runs a Multilingualism and Third Language Acquisition group ([Stockholm](https://www.su.se/english/research/research-catalogue/research-groups/9/multilingualism-and-third-language-acquisition)) and UIC runs a Multilingual Phonology Lab on adult L3 ([UIC](https://hip.uic.edu/research-groups/multiphon/)); the field met at IAM L3 in Poznan in September 2026 ([IAM L3](https://easychair.org/smart-program/IAML3Conference2026/room3.html)). At CMU, Seth Wiener's 2026 study found a wordless game trained Mandarin tones as well as explicit instruction, building on Lori Holt's incidental-learning lab ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/news/news-stories/2026/wiener-game-based-learning)). UMD's Naomi Feldman builds NSF-funded models of adult speech-sound learning ([UMIACS](https://www.umiacs.umd.edu/news-events/news/clip-researchers-create-computational-models-explore-how-adults-learn-new)). Intercomprehension groups at Grenoble LIDILEM and Aix-Marseille built the Galanet and EuRom methods ([GERFLINT](https://www.gerflint.fr/Base/Europe1/Encarnacion.pdf)), and Salzburg's language centre runs a current KA220 project, CLEVER-AI ([PLUS Salzburg](https://www.plus.ac.at/language-centre/research/projects/laufende-projekte/clever-ai/?lang=en)). Polingual can feed four studies: which background language transfers when a Spanish and English speaker starts Portuguese; intercomprehension gains measured against EVAL-IC descriptors; incidental learning in games; and competitive or social effects in live lobbies. The offer to each lab is free access plus an anonymized export with a consent flow, which also produces the research partner an NSF STTR proposal needs.

### Attribution and the rules around it

RevenueCat leaves the payable event and the attribution window to the brand ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/guides/web-to-app-funnels/measuring-creator)). ## Share game rules in Rust and animate in Rive

### Every game is written three times today

Polingual Play's Snake exists as a TypeScript module, a SwiftUI Canvas view and a Jetpack Compose screen, and about 20 pure TypeScript lobby modules carry the only unit tests (local repo, `polingual/src/lib/lobby/`, `apple/Bucket/Sources/Polingual/Play/`, `android/polingual/.../play/`). The scoring, timing and board rules drift between three copies while the rendering per game stays small. Big studios solved the same drift by pulling copied code into one place: Supercell's Titan engine "started as game code" copied between games and now powers every Supercell title ([Supercell](https://supercell.com/en/news/titan-game-engine/)), and Playrix reuses modules across titles to speed features ([Habr](https://habr.com/en/companies/playrix/articles/566654/comments)). Those engines need teams of about 70. The closest genre analogues use no game engine: NYT runs Wordle and Connections as web apps in a native shell and built its two-player Crossplay in native Swift ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/we-had-to-do-a-lot-of-technological-work-that-was-sort-of-unprecedented-which-was-really-challenging-and-exciting-crossplay-producer-on-the-effort-to-bring-the-new-york-times-first-two-player-game-to-life)), and Duolingo animates its characters in Rive ([Rive](https://rive.app/blog/duolingo-s-ai-powered-video-call-brings-lily-to-life)).

### The stack in build order

**Rive first.** One file with state machines and data binding runs on iOS, Android and web through MIT-licensed runtimes, at **222 KB on web, about 1.7 MB on iOS and 2.4 MB on Android** ([Rive runtime sizes](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/runtime-sizes)). A mascot with correct, wrong, streak and level-up states closes most of the polish gap with no architecture change. **A Rust `play-core` crate second**, exposed to Swift and Kotlin through UniFFI and to the web through WASM, the pattern Crux, Ferrostar, Proton and Photoroom ship in production ([Crux](https://github.com/redbadger/crux/); [Stadia Maps](https://stadiamaps.com/blog/ferrostar-building-a-cross-platform-navigation-sdk-in-rust-part-1/)). It starts with the newest game, keeps pure functions (state and action in, state and events out) and shares golden test vectors with the existing TypeScript tests. **Supabase Realtime Broadcast stays for round-based rooms**: Supabase measures a 6 ms median and 28 ms p95 at 32,000 users, and a third party measured 58 to 60 ms end to end ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/benchmarks); [DEV](https://dev.to/kubeden/neon-realtime-vs-supabase-realtime-410g)), which is invisible in a quiz round when the server stamps round start and answer times. **An authoritative tick server only for Word Snake**, in Rust reusing `play-core` or in Colyseus. **Phaser 4 only for portal mini-games** on Poki and CrazyGames, where its 290 KB empty build fits Poki's under-8 MB target ([Poki](https://developers.poki.com/guide/web-game-engines)). **Blender offline** for flags, tokens and mascot poses rendered into Rive or sprite sheets.

This revises the games market report, which put the lobby in Next.js with Phaser 4 and WebViews on phones. The shipped code is already native on both phones, NYT moved its multiplayer game native, and SwiftUI and Compose already handle multilingual text, right-to-left scripts and VoiceOver, which a game engine would make the team rebuild. Bevy runs on all three targets but is pre-1.0 with breaking releases every few months, and its own community still points production mobile teams to Godot ([itch.io](https://itch.io/t/5033020/overcoming-the-limitations-of-winit-on-mobile-platforms)); Godot's web export on iOS Safari runs single-threaded with WebGL 2 issues ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). Defold, under 2 MB with console exports, stays the console option ([Defold](https://www.defold.com/manuals/html5/)). Rust adds a toolchain and a cross-compile setup on the Mac build box, and it pays off from the second game shipped on three platforms. The same crate also validates scores on the server, so client and server never disagree on rules. Supabase Pro caps Realtime at 500 connections, 10,000 with the spend cap off ([Supabase limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits)), which bounds how many simultaneous streamer-hosted lobbies can run before the cap comes off.

## About 100M Europeans fit the trilingual ICP

### How big the segment is

No ad platform exposes a "speaks three languages" flag, so the ICP has to be sized from surveys. The 2024 Eurobarometer found **28% of Europeans can converse in at least two foreign languages** ([European Commission](https://translation.ec.europa.eu/languages-eu-why-multilingualism-matters/europeans-language-skills_en)), about 105M people on an EU population of roughly 380M aged 15 and over (my estimate). India's 2011 census analysis found 7% know three or more languages ([Hindustan Times](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-lucknow/20181122/282196536999567)), about 85M people. Duolingo counted **over 32M learners studying more than one language** in 2023 ([CIOL](https://www.ciol.org.uk/duolingo-2023-global-trends-language-learning)) and named Finland, Germany and the UK as leaders in learners studying three or more ([Duolingo 2024 report](https://blog.duolingo.com/2024-duolingo-language-report/)). That 32M is the closest proxy for the serviceable market. The US is mostly bilingual: 22.5% of people over 5 speak a non-English language at home ([Census](https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/language-at-home-acs-5-year.html)), and US trilinguals concentrate in immigrant and international-student communities in coastal metros, which matches the NYC venue plan in the expansion map. Europeans cite jobs (51%) and culture (45%) as the top reasons to learn ([European Commission](https://education.ec.europa.eu/news/europeans-strive-toward-multilingualism-and-value-language-diversity-according-to-latest-eurobarometer)), which supports the career vocabulary feature.

### Where to find and reach them

The largest warm pools are **r/languagelearning at about 3.3M to 3.4M members** ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-languagelearning/)), **HelloTalk at 70M+ registered users** ([HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com/about?lang=de)) and **Tandem at 35M+** ([TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/compare/hellotalk-vs-tandem/)). Reddit flair such as "PL N, EN C1, RU A2/B1" declares a multi-language profile in a parseable form. SparkToro searches on profile phrases ("polyglot", "trilingual", "speaks 4 languages") return the podcasts, sites and accounts that audience follows ([SparkToro guide](https://kalynamarketing.squarespace.com/blog/sparktoro-guide-reference)), which doubles as a creator discovery tool. Prolific's prescreeners combine first language and fluent languages, with a short custom screener for a third ([Prolific](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445169-how-do-i-prescreen-for-participants-who-are-fluent-in-multiple-languages)), the cheapest route to a trilingual sample for the missing willingness-to-pay survey. LinkedIn's Profile language filter reflects UI language and misses most multilinguals ([Findymail](https://www.findymail.com/blog/sales-navigator-search-filters)); the self-listed Languages section is the better B2B signal and needs enrichment to read. Meta and TikTok audience sizes for polyglot interests need a logged-in Ads Manager pull, and the Apollo connector in this session needs authorization before it can enrich contacts.

### Prior art for the lexicon map and the credential wallet

Known-word tracking exists one language at a time. LingQ counts words marked Known plus status-4 Learned words ([LingQ support](https://lingq-support.groovehq.com/help/understanding-lingq-statistics)), Migaku tracks unknown, learning, known and ignored and lets users import known words ([Migaku](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/language-learning-progress-tracking)), WaniKani's 60 levels cover about 2,000 kanji and 6,000 words ([WaniKani community](https://community.wanikani.com/t/wanikani-users-at-higher-levels-have-you-acheived-reading-fluency/58127/3)), and Language Reactor and Readlang users ask for a known-words graph neither has ([Language Reactor forum](https://forum.languagelearningwithnetflix.com/t/see-known-words-in-a-graph/22659); [Readlang forum](https://forum.readlang.com/t/add-known-words-count-like-lingq/3025)). **No product found unifies known words across languages.** Polingual's lexicon map can adopt the LingQ and Migaku status ladder and add one node per concept with edges for cognates and false friends, and importing from Anki, LingQ and Migaku is a wedge into polyglots who already hand-merge their data. Words With Friends licenses Oxford word lists in six languages ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id321916506)), which shows multi-lexicon dictionaries are licensable for a cross-lexicon game mode.

The credential standards are ready. W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 became a Recommendation in May 2025 ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model/)), Open Badges 3.0 makes each badge an issuer-signed credential compatible with it ([1EdTech](https://www.imsglobal.org/spec/ob/v3p0/impl)), and CLR 2.0 bundles many achievements into one signed record ([Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/elearning-video/articles-elearning/certificates-badges-verifiable-credentials)). The Digital Credentials Consortium's Learner Credential Wallet accepts only 3.0 credentials ([POK](https://www.pok.tech/blog/posts/open-badge-3-0-complete-guide-digital-credentials)), and Credly has issued 100M badges to over 48M earners ([Pearson](https://plc.pearson.com/en-GB/news-and-insights/blogs/100-million-ways-pearson-has-fuelled-learning-economy); [Credly](https://info.credly.com/why-choose-credly)). Each skill-tree node becomes an OB 3.0 achievement with a CEFR level per language, the tree becomes a CLR bundle, and Bucket holds the wallet for every subject. The expansion map already placed the CEFR badge in its June to August 2027 window.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the growth budget should go. Paid installs lose money in year one at category medians, and no grant or round changes that. A creator paid a quarter of net revenue converts acquisition from a cost paid up front into a cost paid out of revenue, and the ICP is concentrated enough in creator audiences and a few communities that this channel can carry the plan. The live lobby does the same work at zero media cost, because every host recruits players. The pricing gap to close first is Polingual's own: a 3-day trial sits below every benchmark, and fixing it costs a configuration change.

The less obvious finding is that the defensible asset is data. No product unifies known words across languages, no survey measures what trilinguals pay, and L3 researchers lack a large population of people learning their third language in a game. A shared Rust rules core that logs consented learning events turns the engineering cleanup into the research partnership, the NSF proposal, the credential calibration and the investor moat at once. The largest uncertainties are conversion from live rooms to paid plans, creator acceptance of a revenue share without a flat fee, and whether Stripe's stablecoin payouts open to Polingual in time; each will show up in the first 90 days of data.
