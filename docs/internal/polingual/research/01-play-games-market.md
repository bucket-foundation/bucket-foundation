# Polingual Play should own the room

Polingual Play fits the games market as a live, language-specific team quiz for a shared screen, sold inside the Polingual subscription and seeded by free web mini-games on Poki and CrazyGames. The two proven pools it sits between are classroom live quiz tools, where Kahoot reached **$146M revenue and 24M active users** in 2022 on a subject-agnostic product, and consumer language apps, where Duolingo will book about **$1.2B in 2026 with subscriptions at 87% of revenue**. Neither pool has a language specialist built for a room full of players on one big screen, and the Flash and HTML5 portals never carried a language-learning hit. The build path that matches the team is Phaser 4 or a DOM-first renderer in TypeScript inside the existing Next.js app, with Supabase Realtime for lobbies, because web-native engines ship in under 1 MB while Unity and Godot start near 10 MB and crash on iOS Safari. Steam and consoles belong later: language games on Steam sell in the thousands of units, and console access costs months of approval and porting. Ads pay too little per player to fund the product, so they should buy reach on portals while the subscription carries revenue. The name "Polingual Play" clears a web trademark scan but sits one letter from an iOS app called PolyLingual, so a USPTO search comes before any filing.

## Flash portals ran on sponsorships and banner ads and never shelved language games

The free web game era was a portal business built on one technical fact: a Flash game was a single SWF file that anyone could copy. Flash Player 7 brought ActionScript 2 in 2003 and Flash Player 9 brought ActionScript 3 in 2006, and the plugin reached about **92% of internet users around 2000** ([Wikipedia: Adobe Flash Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Flash_Player)). Because a SWF spread across sites on its own, the money followed the branding inside the file. A sponsor paid a one-off fee to put its logo and "more games" links into the distributable build, so every copy that leaked onto another portal sent traffic home. FGL, the main marketplace, said a primary sponsorship paid about **95% of an exclusive license's value** while the developer kept the right to sell site-locked copies ([GameDev.net FGL interview](https://gamedev.net/tutorials/industry/interviews/flashgamelicensecom-interview-r2683)), and one portal paid $750 for a logo swap and site-lock code ([Stencyl guide](https://stencyl.com/help/viewPrint/134)).

The portals themselves launched between 1995 and 2007. Newgrounds opened in 1995 and built "The Portal" for Flash submissions in 1999 ([Wikipedia: Newgrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newgrounds)). Miniclip started in 2001 on a **£40,000 budget** and was valued above £275M by 2008 ([Wikipedia: Miniclip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniclip)). Kongregate went live in 2006, raised about $9M from investors including Reid Hoffman and Jeff Bezos, and was bought by GameStop in 2010 ([Wikipedia: Kongregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongregate)). Armor Games began in 2004 and sponsored hits such as Crush the Castle and The World's Hardest Game ([Wikipedia: Armor Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Games)). Smaller portals like Flonga ran the same template: a 2011 snapshot shows genre tabs, about 45 tags from Cooking to Tower defense, player approval ratings, and the line "All the games on our site are FREE to play and we launch new games every day" ([Wayback: Flonga 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110102190704/http://www.flonga.com/)). No reliable founding date or traffic figure for Flonga, Y8 or Max Games turned up.

Portals earned from IAB display units wrapped around the game frame. Miniclip's 2008 homepage carried Google ad iframes in house slots and offered "Free Website Games" for webmasters to embed ([Wayback: Miniclip 2008](https://web.archive.org/web/20080101090847/http://www.miniclip.com/games/en/)), and Kongregate's 2013 page ran 728x90 leaderboards and 300x250 rectangles next to a badge and achievement system ([Wayback: Kongregate 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130101034947/http://www.kongregate.com/)). Developers saw little of that ad money. A survey of more than 1,000 Flash developers found **48% earned through sponsorship**, a quarter through licensing and a fifth through ads, and 20% earned over $1,000 a month ([MCV UK](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/20-of-flash-devs-earn-over-1000-per-month/)). SteamBirds, a well-known hit, grossed about $45,000 by 2011 with ads under 10% of revenue, and its sequel earned about $10,000 from a brand sponsor and another $10,000 from microtransactions ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/steambirds-survival-by-the-numbers)). Kongregate paid 25% of ad revenue by default, rising to 50% with API integration and exclusivity ([Kongregate support](https://kongregatesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26759058248973-Determining-my-percentage)), and its Kreds currency produced about a third of company revenue in 2010 while only 0.5% of games used it ([SemiAccurate](https://semiaccurate.com/?p=16565)).

Education had a shelf in this world and language learning had none. Miniclip listed "Learning" and "Brain Training" categories in 2007 ([Wayback: Miniclip 2008](https://web.archive.org/web/20080101090847/http://www.miniclip.com/games/en/)), Funbrain ran vocabulary and spelling games from 1997 ([TryEngineering](https://tryengineering.org/game/funbrain-games)), and Coolmath built a whole brand on non-violent games that schools would allow, which kids used as cover for entertainment titles ([Wikipedia: Coolmath Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolmath_Games); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/flash-games-millennials-played-in-school/)). Typing and math dominated. No source named a foreign-language Flash game among the hits on Miniclip, Kongregate, Armor Games, Newgrounds or Y8.

The era ended in stages. Steve Jobs refused Flash on iPhone and iPad in April 2010, Adobe announced end of support in July 2017, and the plugin stopped on 31 December 2020 with a kill switch on 12 January 2021 ([Wikipedia: Adobe Flash Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Flash_Player)). Mochi Media, whose network held about 40,000 developers, shut on 31 March 2014 with "no meaningful position beyond Flash" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/16/with-no-meaningful-position-beyond-flash-gaming-platform-mochi-media-will-close-march-31/)). FGL's licensing totals halved in the second half of 2014 ([Game Developer, 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-online-browser-games-industry-flash-and-html5)). Kongregate stopped taking new games on 1 July 2020, and Miniclip closed its browser portal in July 2022 to focus on mobile hits like 8 Ball Pool, which had earned over $400M by 2018 ([Wikipedia: Kongregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongregate); [Wikipedia: Miniclip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniclip)). Newgrounds, Armor Games, Coolmath and the Internet Archive kept old games alive with Ruffle, a Rust and WebAssembly emulator, and Flashpoint Archive preserved over **200,000 browser games and animations** ([Wikipedia: Ruffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruffle_(software)); [Wikipedia: Flashpoint Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashpoint_Archive)).

## Poki and CrazyGames rebuilt the free portal on HTML5 and revenue share

The successors kept the Flash formula of a free, curated, ad-funded catalog and replaced one-off sponsorships with ongoing revenue share. Poki, founded in Amsterdam in 2014 without outside funding, reports **100M monthly players, 625M players in 2025 and 1B gameplays in June 2025** ([Poki blog, 2025 review](https://poki.com/blog/2025-at-poki-a-year-in-review)). CrazyGames, founded in Leuven around 2014, claims over 50M monthly users ([Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-crazygames)). Independent estimates put both nearer 30M to 35M monthly users ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/crazygames-vs-poki-vs-coolmath-games-2026/)), and Similarweb estimates Poki's revenue at $100M to $200M a year without confirmation ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/company/poki.com)). Coolmath moved to Ruffle and HTML5 in 2020, grew during COVID, and released a Steam game in December 2024 ([Wikipedia: Coolmath Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolmath_Games)).

The two portals differ in entry terms. Poki is application-based, defaults to a five-year web-exclusive deal that leaves Steam and app stores open, and reportedly splits ad revenue 50/50 on traffic it sends while the developer keeps traffic it brings; those terms come from secondary guides ([Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki); [Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/business-faqs/poki-vs-crazygames-vs-gamedistribution-revenue-share/)). CrazyGames takes open submissions, runs a QA review, then a Basic Launch to a small audience for 7 to 21 days with ads off, and promotes titles on playtime, retention and conversion to gameplay ([CrazyGames Docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/)). One 2026 CrazyGames jam paid developers 60% of ad revenue and 70% of in-game purchase revenue ([Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/business-faqs/poki-vs-crazygames-vs-gamedistribution-revenue-share/)).

The technical bar is size. Poki targets an initial download under 8 MB and requires every game to work in portrait ([Poki requirements](https://sdk.poki.com/new-requirements); [Poki blog 2026](https://poki.com/blog/building-web-browser-games-2026)). Its team says each extra megabyte costs "a couple percent of players." CrazyGames allows 50 MB, or 20 MB for its mobile homepage, measured until the SDK's gameplay-start call, and disables Unity games on iOS by default because of memory crashes ([CrazyGames tech reqs](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical/)). Language audio and extra fonts therefore have to load after the first round starts.

Language learning is still open ground on these portals. Poki's word category holds Hangman, Fast Typer and Word City Crossed with no language-learning shelf ([Poki Word Games](https://poki.com/en/words)), and CrazyGames carries Typing Rush, a listen-and-type race from April 2026 ([CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/typing-rush)). A short multilingual word game would face word puzzles as competition, with no language brand in the category.

## Classroom quiz tools prove the format while language apps prove the money

Duolingo is the category's ceiling and its warning. Its Q3 2026 letter reports **64.9M daily users, 13.1M paid subscribers and $270.0M in subscription revenue**, with advertising flat at $20.8M and in-app purchases down 38% to $6.9M ([Duolingo Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). Games at Duolingo drive retention while the subscription earns the money. It launched Chess on iOS in June 2025 and added real-time Chess PvP against other learners, reaching Android in February 2026 ([Duocon 2025](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-unveils-major-product-updates-turn-learning-real-world); [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/02/24/duolingo-chess-goes-live)). It now buys reach by placing Duo inside Brawl Stars and Eggy Party, which brought "millions of new and returning users," and it is retiring the Max tier by folding AI features into Super ([same letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). Duolingo has moved into live one-on-one play on phones; it has no product for a group around one screen.

The fastest-growing rivals sell AI speaking practice. Speak passed **$100M annual revenue in 2025, up from $15M in 2024** ([Latka](https://www.getlatka.com/companies/speak.com)), Praktika reports $55M ARR and profitability ([ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/signal/praktika-hit-55m-arr-profitable)), and Babbel, paid-only, reported €352M in 2024 ([Skillademia](https://skillademia.com/statistics/babbel-statistics/)). Drops, the closest precedent for learning as a short game, sold to Kahoot for about $50M in 2020 with 25M users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2077983)) and has an unverified 2025 revenue estimate of $4.4M ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/languagedrops.com)). Vocabulary mechanics alone have not built a large business.

Classroom live quiz is the largest proven market for game plus learning, and its winners teach no subject in particular. Kahoot went private in a **$1.7B deal** in 2024 after reaching 1.4M paid subscribers and 8M educators ([Apps Run The World](https://www.appsruntheworld.com/apps-top-500-software-vendors/kahoot/)). Blooket passed 15M users by June 2024 and sells school bundles from about $550 a year ([Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/blooket); [AhaSlides](https://ahaslides.com/blog/blooket-review/)). Gimkit runs with about seven employees and sells a $650 department license for up to 20 teachers ([Plisio](https://plisio.net/education/what-is-gimkit); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/blooket-vs-gimkit/)). A team of seven winning share against Kahoot is the most useful data point for Polingual: a language-specific room game with built-in content, native audio and pronunciation scoring would give teachers something these tools lack.

Premium language games outside apps sell small. Influent, a $19.99 Steam title from 2014, reached an estimated 200,000 to 500,000 owners over a decade ([SteamSpy](https://www.steamspy.com/dev/Three+Flip+Studios)). Koe, a 2024 Japanese-teaching JRPG, peaked at 22 concurrent players ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/672430/charts)), WonderLang Polyglot grossed an estimated $24K and Mondly VR about $20K lifetime ([DataHumble](https://datahumble.com/wonderlang-polyglot-3599480); [GameSensor](https://gamesensor.info/app/1141930)). The one language-adjacent console success, Ubisoft's DS "My Coach" line, sold about 7M across titles and rode the casual DS boom; My Word Coach alone sold 1.40M ([VGChartz](https://www.vgchartz.com/article/83097/million-seller-watch-nintendo-ds/); [Video Game Sales Wiki](https://vgsales.fandom.com/wiki/Best-selling_Ubisoft_games)). The TV party format is where demand is growing: Netflix made cloud TV games a 2026 priority, party titles reached about 10% of eligible members, and it licensed three Jackbox games in April 2026 ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/netflix-makes-cloud-based-tv-games-a-key-priority-for-2026); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/gaming/news/netflix-adds-three-jackbox-games-to-its-tv-app-17b902be-17cb-447a-8af9-59b1676a4e6c)).

| Competitor | Model | Scale | Gap Polingual Play can fill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | Subscription, games for retention | 64.9M DAU, $1.2B 2026 revenue | No shared-screen group play |
| Speak, Praktika | AI tutor subscription | $100M and $55M ARR | Solo speaking practice only |
| Kahoot, Blooket, Gimkit | Free to teachers, paid licenses | 24M, 15M users; Gimkit about 7 staff | No language content or audio |
| Drops | Vocabulary game subscription | 25M users at sale, small revenue | Solo, no live play |

## Phaser 4 in TypeScript fits the stack and Defold holds the console option

A language quiz is text, audio and UI with light graphics, so engine weight is the main cost. Poki's engine guide lists empty builds of **Phaser 290 KB, Three.js 151 KB, PlayCanvas 300 KB, Cocos Creator 709 KB and Defold 1.03 MB, against Unity about 11 MB and Godot 10 MB** ([Poki engine guide](https://developers.poki.com/guide/web-game-engines)). That table predates Unity 6 and Godot 4.3, so the figures are directional. Unity cancelled its Runtime Fee in September 2024 and raised the free tier cap to $200K ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/unity-is-canceling-the-runtime-fee)), and Unity 6 added mobile browser support ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/unity-mounts-a-comeback-with-launch-of-unity-6-game-engine-205332034.html)), yet forum reports show Unity 6 web builds crashing on iOS 18 ([Unity forum](https://discussions.unity.com/t/webgl-random-crashes-on-ios-18-works-fine-on-ios-18/1627624)). Godot 4's default web export is single-threaded and has extra WebGL 2 issues on Safari ([Godot docs 4.5](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)).

| Engine | Language | Empty web build | iOS Safari | Console path | Fit for Polingual Play |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phaser 4 | TypeScript | 290 KB | Good | None | Best for web, portals, Capacitor |
| PixiJS or DOM | TypeScript | Small | Good | None | Best for text-heavy quiz UI |
| Defold | Lua | 1.03 MB | Good | PS5, PS4, Switch, Xbox since 1.14 | Best if consoles become firm |
| Godot 4 | GDScript, C# | about 10 MB | Weak | Paid via W4 Games | Second console option |
| Unity 6 | C# | about 11 MB | Unstable | Built in after approval | Heavy for a quiz |

Phaser 4 shipped in April 2026 and targets desktop, mobile, Steam, Discord and YouTube Playables, with no consoles ([Phaser 3 vs 4](https://phaser.io/news/2026/05/phaser-3-vs-phaser-4)), and it reaches iOS and Android through an official Capacitor template ([Phaser Capacitor tutorial](https://phaser.io/tutorials/bring-your-phaser-game-to-ios-and-android-with-capacitor)). For this team the recommendation is to build Polingual Play as TypeScript routes inside the existing Next.js app, render question and answer text in the DOM, use Phaser 4 for animated rounds, and load the same web build inside the SwiftUI and native Android apps through a WebView. One codebase then serves the big screen, the phone controllers, Polingual's apps and the portals. DOM and canvas text use the browser's own shaping, which handles Arabic, Devanagari and CJK without per-script font work; Godot gets the same through HarfBuzz and ICU, but excludes ICU data by default to save about 4 MB ([Godot TextServerAdvanced](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/classes/class_textserveradvanced.html); [Godot CTL report](https://godotengine.org/article/complex-text-layouts-progress-report-3)). Pre-generated audio from Supabase Storage gives consistent voices across devices, where browser speech synthesis varies by device; that comparison is an inference without a 2026 coverage study behind it.

Consoles force a second build whatever the web choice. The Godot project ships no console exports ([Godot: console ports](https://godotengine.org/article/about-official-console-ports/)), W4 Games sells them with a Starter tier for studios under $300K revenue ([W4 Consoles](https://www.w4games.com/w4consoles)), and vendor pages quote ports at $8K to $40K for Godot and $10K to $60K for Unity without stating a method ([Coherent Labs](https://www.coherentlab.com/blog/game-porting-cost-guide)). Defold lists PS5, PS4 and Switch for approved developers and added free Xbox support in version 1.14.0 in October 2026 ([Defold Switch manual](https://defold.com/manuals/nintendo-switch); [Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/defold-1-14-0-adds-free-xbox-support-for-approved-developers/)), which makes it the console fallback for a small web-first team willing to learn Lua.

Supabase covers the live lobby. Realtime Broadcast and Presence allow **200 concurrent connections on Free, 500 on Pro and 10,000 on Pro with the spend cap off** ([Supabase Realtime limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits)). At 30 players plus a host per room, Pro with the cap on holds about 16 simultaneous classrooms, so the cap has to come off before any school rollout. A quiz sends a question, answers and scores a few times a minute, which fits Broadcast; scoring and timing should run server-side in a Postgres or Edge Function. Colyseus Cloud, from $15 a month in TypeScript, becomes worthwhile for fast reflex rounds where cheating and timing matter ([Colyseus pricing](https://www.colyseus.io/pricing)).

Netflix licenses established party games with phones as controllers, which matches Polingual Play's format, but it picks titles with a track record.

## Polingual Play clears a web scan and sits one letter from PolyLingual

No registered mark for "Polingual," "Polingual Play," "PolyPlay" or "Poly Play" appeared in web searches, though no live USPTO search was run. The risk sits in near neighbors. PolyLingual is a live iOS vocabulary app in the Education category ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1639800345)), and Polly Lingual sells lessons and tutors through a Chrome extension ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/polly-lingual/neehdmebkjkhalgkbehnklenjcaejipd?hl=en)). Both sit in the same class of goods as Polingual, and an examiner could raise likelihood of confusion if either owner has filed. "Polingual Play" is the safer games name: POLLPLAY is a registered gaming-software mark ([Justia Trademarks](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/studio-bay-and-sky-inc-2416939)), and "Poly" game names are crowded on Steam and Google Play ([Steam: Poly Party](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291110)). Keeping Play as a descriptor under the Polingual master brand lets one filing cover both. The next step is a USPTO search for POLINGUAL, POLYLINGUAL, POLLY LINGUAL, POLYPLAY and POLY PLAY in classes 9, 28, 41 and 42, plus EUIPO and WIPO checks.

## Subscription carries revenue while portal ads buy reach

The numbers favor subscription by a wide margin. Duolingo's Q3 2026 subscription revenue of $270.0M across 13.1M payers works out to about **$20.60 per payer per quarter, or about $82 a year** (calculated from the [Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). Web rewarded video pays about **$3.62 per thousand views globally and $6.98 in the US** according to a vendor with an optimistic bias ([AppLixir](https://www.applixir.com/?p=4428)). At the global rate, matching one $82 subscriber takes about 22,700 rewarded views, or about 45,000 on Poki after a 50/50 split. Ads were under 10% of a Flash hit's revenue in 2011 and remain under 7% of Duolingo's today, so ad money has never carried a learning product.

Ads still have a job. Free mini-games on Poki and CrazyGames reach tens of millions of players a month at zero acquisition cost and pay a little through the portal SDKs. The model that fits is a three-tier ladder. Free web mini-games on portals and the Polingual site run rewarded ads behind one adapter so the same build swaps between the Poki SDK, the CrazyGames SDK and Google H5 Games Ads, and every game ends with a link into Bucket or Polingual. The full Polingual Play lobby, with every language, saved progress and hosting a room, sits inside the Polingual subscription, so the game raises the value of the existing paid app. A teacher or venue license, priced against Blooket's $550 school bundle and Gimkit's $650 department license, covers classrooms and events where one paying host brings 30 non-paying players. Premium one-time sales belong only on Steam and consoles, where buyers expect them, and should be treated as marketing given the sales history of language games there.

## Web and phones first, consoles after measured traction

Target order follows cost and fit. The web build inside Polingual's apps and site comes first because the team already ships Next.js and Supabase and the big-screen lobby needs only a browser on a TV or laptop. CrazyGames comes second as a free test bed whose Basic Launch returns retention and playtime data within three weeks. Poki follows once a mini-game shows strong playtime, accepting web exclusivity for that title only. Steam comes fourth as a store page that gathers wishlists and teacher discovery. Consoles and Netflix come last, once there is a proven game and player numbers to pitch with.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Live lobby | Months 0 to 3 | Team quiz in Next.js, DOM plus Phaser 4, Supabase Broadcast, server scoring, pre-generated audio | Polingual web, iOS and Android via WebView; Bucket funnel | Weekly hosted rooms and subscription conversion from game players |
| 2. Portal funnel | Months 2 to 6 | Two or three solo or short versus mini-games under 8 MB, portrait, ad adapter | CrazyGames Basic Launch, then Poki application; Google H5 ads on own domain | Portal retention above category average; measured installs into Bucket and Polingual |
| 3. Classroom and events | Months 4 to 9 | Teacher dashboard, class rosters, curriculum packs, Supabase spend cap off | Teacher and venue licenses | Paying schools and renewals |
| 4. Steam and TV | Months 6 to 12 | Desktop wrapper of the web build, controller and couch play | Steam coming-soon page and launch; pitch Netflix party and kids lines | Wishlists, reviews, Netflix response |

## Conclusion

The Flash era and its HTML5 successors share one lesson for Polingual Play: free portals reward short, sticky games and pay developers little, so they work as distribution and fail as a business model for a learning product. Twenty-five years of portals never produced a language-learning hit, which leaves the shelf open: no language brand has tried with the content depth and audio a dedicated app can supply. The real opening is the room. Duolingo owns the solo phone, AI tutors own solo speaking, and Kahoot and its imitators own the classroom with no language expertise. A live team quiz that puts one screen in front of a family, a class or a bar, and turns every player's phone into a controller, has no direct incumbent.

The decisive engineering choice is to stay on the web stack the team already runs and defer every decision that forces a second codebase. Consoles, Steam and Netflix all pay off only after the game has proof of retention, and each of them can be reached later through a Defold rebuild or a contracted port. The open questions are the trademark search, the Poki and CrazyGames contract terms for an educational title, and whether teachers will pay for a language-specific room game at Gimkit prices; the first two phases are built to answer the last one with data.
