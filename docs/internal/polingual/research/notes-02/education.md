# Selling and Giving Polingual to Teachers, Schools and Colleges

Research date: 2026-11-15. Search snippets from third-party pricing and review sites conflict often; each conflict is flagged.

## K-12 World Language and ESL Market Size, Funding, Procurement and Price Points

### Takeaway
About 1 in 5 US K-12 students take a world language (roughly 10.6M in the last national count, 2017) and about 10% are English learners (around 5M). Federal EL money is flat at $890M and politically exposed, and ESSER money is gone, so a district sale has to fit recurring local budgets and clear a student data privacy agreement; teacher-paid plans in the $36 to $180 a year range are the realistic first price band.

### Cited Findings
- 10.6 million K-12 students studied a world language or ASL in the 2017 National K-12 Foreign Language Enrollment Survey (American Councils, ACTFL, CAL), about 20% of K-12 students: [American Councils](https://www.americancouncils.org/news/new-report-world-language-study-us-k-12-schools); [EdWeek 2017](https://edweek.org/leadership/just-20-percent-of-k-12-students-are-learning-a-foreign-language/2017/06)
- More than half of New Jersey public K-12 students take a foreign language; state rates vary widely: [American Councils FLE report](https://www.americancouncils.org/sites/default/files/FLE-report-June17.pdf)
- ACTFL's State of K-12 World Language Education Study 2026 (published April 2026) says there is no systematic national collection of world language enrollment data and relies on the NCES School Pulse Panel, January 2024, for school-offering figures: [ACTFL 2026 study](https://www.actfl.org/uploads/images/generalCardImages/State-of-K-12-WL-Education-Study-2026.pdf). The PDF is a Canva image export; the figures inside could not be extracted.
- Title III (English learners) FY2026 appropriation: $890M, flat with the prior year; the program ran $700M to $800M a year for about 15 years while the EL population grew about 30%; an expert puts ELs at about 10% of students: [EdWeek, Feb 2026](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/educators-warn-flat-english-learner-funding-falls-short-of-growing-demand/2026/02)
- The administration first proposed ending Title III, withheld 2025-26 Title III funds in summer 2025 until states sued, and the FY27 budget again proposes elimination: [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/educators-warn-flat-english-learner-funding-falls-short-of-growing-demand/2026/02); [Daily Herald](https://www.dailyherald.com/?p=1345928); [Research for Action](https://www.researchforaction.org/?p=11042)
- About 5.0M English learners in US public schools, per a vendor blog citing NCES; NCES primary source not verified: [Grantsights](https://grantsights.com/blog/title-iii-grants-explained)
- ESSER: last obligation deadline was Sept 30, 2024; ED approves no liquidation past March 28, 2026: [EdCircuit](https://edcircuit.com/how-will-school-districts-replace-aging-tech-without-esser/)
- ESSER equaled 30% to 40% of a typical district's discretionary spending over its life: [Oliver Wyman, March 2025](https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2025/mar/k-12-investment-strategies-stimulus-funds-end-2025.html)
- Districts are dropping edtech tools as federal money ends; subscriptions are recurring costs that need replacement funding: [EdSurge, Aug 2024](https://edsurge.com/news/2024-08-28-as-federal-dollars-vanish-districts-weigh-which-edtech-tools-to-drop)
- Counterpoint: EY-Parthenon called the cliff overblown for investors and expects vendor consolidation, bundling and smaller price increases: [EY-Parthenon](https://info.ey.com/rs/520-RXP-003/images/EY-Parthenon_ESSER%20expiration%20impact%20on%20K-12.pdf?version=0)
- A claimed CoSN figure that 60% of district leaders plan to cut or consolidate software subscriptions appears only in a 2026 blog and is unverified: [blog](https://theomniview.blogspot.com/2026/06/the-subscription-squeeze-edtech-audit-7.html)
- Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC, part of Access 4 Learning) publishes the National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA v2, April 2024); 28 state alliances; signed agreements go into a public registry that other districts can join without new negotiation: [A4L privacy](https://privacy.a4l.org/); [NDPA v2.2 template](https://files.a4l.org/privacy/NDPA/NDPA_v2-2_STANDARD_WEB.pdf); [ClassDojo / TEC example](https://www.k12dive.com/press-release/20250814-classdojo-fast-tracks-districtwide-adoption-through-new-privacy-agreement-w/)
- Illinois SDPC alliance membership is free to every Illinois district: [LTC Illinois](https://www.ltcillinois.org/services/sdpc/)
- More than 40 states have student data privacy laws, many requiring contract terms that flow to vendors; FERPA "school official" exception is the usual basis: [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/edtech-school-district-vendor-agreement/)
- New York districts have a new statewide privacy-agreement path as of July 2024: [NYSSBA](https://www.nyssba.org/news/2024/06/28/on-board-online-july-1-2024/schools-in-nys-have-a-new-way-to-protect-student-data-privacy/)

Price points found:
- Conjuguemos district account: $45 per school plus $5 per teacher per year: [Conjuguemos pricing](https://cdn.conjuguemos.com/pricing)
- Extempore: $8.99 per student per year for the first 30 students, volume discounts above: [Extempore how to buy](https://extemporeapp.com/how-to-buy/)
- Kahoot EDU per teacher: Bronze $48/yr, Silver $96/yr, Gold $144 to $180/yr; school/district EDU Standard $14.99 and EDU Pro $24.99 per host per month billed annually; Premium custom: [Kahoot schools plans](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/); [Kahoot school admins](https://kahoot.com/schools/hey-school-admins/)
- Gimkit Pro $59.88/yr or $14.99/mo: [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/gimkit-pricing-cost); [Eastern Herald](https://easternherald.com/2026/08/14/gimkit-review-game-modes-pricing/)
- Blooket Plus: $35.88/yr per one source, $4.99/mo billed annually per another; price hidden until login: [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/blooket-pricing); [AhaSlides](https://ahaslides.com/blog/blooket-review/)
- Transparent Language prices campus-wide licenses by FTE: [Transparent](https://knowledge.transparent.com/licensing-transparent-language-online-for-your-school/institution)

### Inferences
- The addressable US K-12 base for a language game tool is about 10M world-language students plus about 5M ELs; ESL is the larger growth segment but runs on Title III, which is flat and under threat, so it is a riskier budget line than local world-language department money.
- Post-ESSER, a new per-student district contract is a hard sell in 2026-27. A teacher-paid plan under about $60/yr, a $45-to-a-few-hundred-dollar school plan, and a free tier fit the budgets that remain.
- Signing the NDPA and listing in the SDPC registry is the cheapest way to clear privacy review in 28 states at once; without it, each district that wants a school license will send its own contract.

### Gaps
- No current national K-12 world-language enrollment number past 2017; ACTFL 2026 figures could not be read from the PDF.
- Number of US world-language teachers not found in this pass (BLS and NCES SASS would have it).
- LanguageNut and Lingco school prices not found; both quote on request.
- State-by-state privacy law list (e.g. California SOPIPA, NY Ed Law 2-d, Illinois SOPPA) not compiled with sources here.

## Teacher-Led Adoption and Freemium Competitors

### Takeaway
Every winner in classroom games (Kahoot, Blooket, Gimkit, Quizlet) went free-for-teachers first and grew through teacher word of mouth on Facebook, Reddit and TikTok, then sold paid teacher tiers and later school plans. Duolingo for Schools is free, so a language product must win on live group play and teacher control, not on content access.

### Cited Findings
- Blooket growth is credited to teacher-to-teacher sharing on Facebook, Reddit and later TikTok; early 2024: over 8M monthly site visits and about 5M registered users; another source claims 15M users by June 2024. Sources are weak aggregators and conflict: [Blooket history site](https://blooket.it.com/who-created-blooket-founder-story/); [Vocal review](https://vocal.media/education/blooket-review-2025-best-free-online-game-based-learning-tool-for-classrooms)
- Gimkit started in 2016 as a high school project by Josh Feinsilber in Seattle; widely quoted "5 million users" and "75% of US high schools" trace to one aggregator: [Plisio](https://plisio.net/education/what-is-gimkit)
- Gimkit's free Basic plan still runs live review games; Blooket's free tier is more generous, Gimkit has more game modes: [Plisio](https://plisio.net/education/what-is-gimkit); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/blooket-vs-gimkit/)
- Kahoot moved business pricing to kahoot360.com while schools pricing stays at kahoot.com/schools: [OnlineExamMaker](https://onlineexammaker.com/kb/?p=85856)
- Duolingo for Schools is free for teachers and students; about 350,000 classrooms worldwide used it at one point (undated figure): [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/introducing-duolingo-for-schools-the-ultimate-partner-for-language-teachers); [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/?p=29212); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1102612)
- Conjuguemos sells individual teacher plans plus a $45 per school plus $5 per teacher district plan: [Conjuguemos](https://cdn.conjuguemos.com/pricing)
- Extempore (speaking practice) sells per student at $8.99/yr: [Extempore](https://extemporeapp.com/how-to-buy/)
- Wayside Publishing runs its own Facebook groups for teachers using its materials: [Wayside](https://www.waysidepublishing.com/teaching-resources/join-the-conversation-engage-with-fellow-language-teachers-online)

### Inferences
- Polingual's natural wedge is the live big-screen team lobby: Kahoot and Blooket are subject-agnostic, Duolingo for Schools is individual practice. A free teacher host account with paid student app access, or free play in class and paid at-home practice, matches the pattern that grew Blooket and Gimkit.
- Pricing anchor: a teacher Pro plan near Gimkit ($60/yr) and a school plan in the low hundreds sits inside what teachers already pay out of pocket.

### Gaps
- No official user counts for Blooket or Gimkit; Quizlet teacher pricing and LanguageNut and Lingco details were not retrieved.
- Vista Higher Learning Supersite and Wayside Learning Site are bundled with textbook adoptions; their per-student digital prices were not found.

## Integrations

### Takeaway
Google Classroom share plus Clever and ClassLink single sign-on are the K-12 table stakes; Canvas LTI matters for colleges. ClassLink partner integrations are free to vendors, while Clever charges vendors for rostering.

### Cited Findings
- Clever is free to districts and paid by app developers; ClassLink is paid by districts by user count: [Bloomz](https://bloomz.com/blog/clever-vs-classlink-rostering-explained/); [Alma](https://www.getalma.com/clever-vs-classlink/)
- Clever app partners pay to roster through Secure Sync, custom quotes: [Clever rostering for partners](https://www.clever.com/rostering-for-partners)
- Edlink (a Clever competitor) says Clever Secure Sync Enterprise starts at $18 per school per month with a 50-school minimum, about $10,800/yr; unverified against Clever: [Edlink](https://ed.link/community/how-to-start-a-clever-integration/)
- ClassLink partner SSO and rostering integrations listed as free: [Teachfloor](https://www.teachfloor.com/blog/what-is-classlink)
- Clever Library and Instant Login let teachers adopt an app without district rostering: [Edlink](https://ed.link/community/understanding-clever-library-vs-instant-login-vs-rostering/)
- A Texas district spent over $40,000 configuring SSO and rosters for 40+ apps before moving to Clever: [Case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/clever/case-study/texas-district-switches-to-clever-for-streamlined-rostering)

### Inferences
- Build order: Google sign-in plus Google Classroom share first (free, teacher-level), then Clever Library/Instant Login and ClassLink SSO, then OneRoster and Canvas LTI 1.3 when a school or college contract asks for it. Microsoft Teams matters mostly outside the US.

### Gaps
- Google Classroom add-on and Canvas LTI certification costs and timelines not researched.

## Higher Education

### Takeaway
College language enrollments fell 16.6% from 2016 to 2021 (largest drop on record), with Korean the standout grower. Department licenses are small and slow; campus-side channels already exist as conversation tables, language ambassador programs and international student offices, which fit a live group quiz product.

### Cited Findings
- MLA census: US college enrollments in languages other than English fell 16.6% from fall 2016 to fall 2021, about 236,000 enrollments, the largest decline in the census's history; overall college enrollment fell about 8% in the same period: [Inside Higher Ed via Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/us-sees-record-drop-enrolment-languages-courses); [Axios](https://axios.com/2023/11/15/college-students-foreign-languages-enrollment-french-spanish-korean)
- Biggest losses: German -33.6%, Arabic -27.4%, modern Hebrew -26%, French -23.1%. Growth: Korean +38.3%, Biblical Hebrew +9.1%, ASL +0.8%. Programs reporting enrollments fell by 961 (about 8%): [Axios](https://axios.com/2023/11/15/college-students-foreign-languages-enrollment-french-spanish-korean); [Slator](https://tech.slator.com/language-course-enrollment-us-higher-education-hits-new-lows/)
- More than a third of programs reported stable or rising enrollment: [MultiLingual](https://multilingual.com/report-shows-need-for-investment-in-language-study-at-us-universities/)
- Next MLA census (fall 2025 data) results not found as of this research.
- Campus language ambassador programs exist at MIT, Stony Brook, USC and Santa Clara, where students run language and culture events: [MIT News Jan 2025](https://news.mit.edu/index%2Ephp/2025/global-languages-program-empowers-student-ambassadors-0114); [Stony Brook](https://www.stonybrook.edu/llrc/students/language-ambassador-program/how-to-participate.html); [SCU](https://www.scu.edu/modernlanguages/beyond-the-classroom/student-ambassador-program)
- University of Nebraska at Kearney runs weekly Non-English Conversation Tables where international students teach their languages: [Student Success journal](https://studentsuccessjournal.org/index.php/studentsuccess/article/download/2445/1314/10332)
- University of Iowa Campus Conversation Partners pairs English learners with English-speaking staff: [UIowa ESL](https://esl.uiowa.edu/campus-conversation-partners)
- EF Ultimate Break runs a paid campus ambassador program with travel credits, a model for commercial student reps: [EF press](https://www.ef.edu/about-us/press/articles/2023/ef-ultimate-break-launches-new-campus-ambassador-program/)
- Campus-wide language licenses are priced by FTE (Transparent Language, Mango): [Transparent](https://knowledge.transparent.com/licensing-transparent-language-online-for-your-school/institution); [Mango higher ed](https://mangolanguages.com/higher-ed)

### Inferences
- Fastest college path: language ambassador programs, conversation tables, language houses and international student offices running Polingual quiz nights, with a free host account per club and paid individual student subscriptions. Department licenses come later, priced by course section or FTE.
- Korean, Japanese and ASL are where college demand grew; German and French departments are shrinking and may welcome engagement tools but have smaller budgets.

### Gaps
- No source found for an edtech company running a language-focused campus ambassador program.
- International student counts (IIE Open Doors 2025) and study-abroad prep channels not researched in this pass.

## Teacher Communities and Conferences

### Takeaway
ACTFL's convention (Nov 20 to 22, 2026, San Antonio, about 6,000 to 7,000 attendees, 200+ exhibitors) is the one national event; state associations and Facebook groups carry most daily word of mouth.

### Cited Findings
- ACTFL 2026: Nov 20 to 22, Henry B. Gonzalez Convention Center, San Antonio: [ACTFL](https://www.actfl.org/convention-and-expo)
- 2025 New Orleans convention drew over 6,000 attendees; third-party listing gives 200+ exhibitors and 7,000+ attendees for 2026: [Bridge](https://bridge.edu/tefl/blog/event/2026-actfl-annual-convention-and-world-languages-expo/); [Tradeshowz](https://tradeshowz.com/events/2922/actfl-convention)
- State associations such as TFLA (Texas) run their own conferences: [TFLA](https://tfla.wildapricot.org/)
- Active Facebook groups: World Language Teacher Lounge (Speaking Latino), Spanish Teachers in the US, Technology for World Language Educators: [Speaking Latino](https://www.speakinglatino.com/category/spanish-teachers/page/3/); [Comprehensible Classroom](https://comprehensibleclassroom.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/64000247262)
- Research documents language teachers using virtual communities to fight isolation: [MAFLT/IALLT 2023](https://maflt.cal.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/sites/32/2023/06/Lanier2023_IALLT_TeacherIsolation-VirtualCommunityPresentation_Shared.pdf)

### Inferences
- ACTFL 2026 is days away; exhibiting is too late, but walking the expo and demoing a live lobby to teachers is possible. Plan a booth or session for ACTFL 2027 and spring state conferences.
- Free TpT listings (game packs that open in Polingual) and posts in the tech-focused Facebook groups match how Blooket spread.

### Gaps
- Member counts for Facebook groups, Reddit (r/Spanish teachers, r/Teachers) and TpT world-language seller data not found.

## Outside the US

### Takeaway
The EU has the largest captive school audience (half of upper secondary pupils take two or more foreign languages, nearly all study English); Korea's English cram schools are the largest private spend; Latin America runs national public English programs that buy at ministry level.

### Cited Findings
- EU 2023: 49% of all upper secondary pupils studied two or more foreign languages; 60% in general upper secondary; 33.8% in vocational (2022): [Eurostat Statistics Explained](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Foreign_language_learning_statistics); [Luxtoday](https://luxtoday.lu/en/education-and-science/60-of-pupils-study-two-or-more-foreign-languages)
- 2022: 96.3% of general and 76.3% of vocational upper secondary pupils in the EU learned English; nine countries had over 90% studying two or more languages: [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Foreign_language_learning_statistics)
- South Korea private education spending: 29.2 trillion won (about $20.2B) in 2024, a record; reported 27.5 trillion won in 2025, first decline in five years (secondary sources); participation 75.7%; English is the top subject at 281,000 won a month per participating student; English kindergartens cost about 1.54M won a month: [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/spending-on-private-education-jumps-60-percent-over-past-decade-data/12230869); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/3076418); [Asiae](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/visual-news/article/2026031215371432331)
- Latin America national programs: Colombia (Programa Nacional de Bilinguismo, Colombia Very Well 2015-2025), Chile (Ingles Abre Puertas, MINEDUC, uses blended learning), Mexico (PRONI, federal, every state): [Inter-American Dialogue](https://thedialogue.org/blogs/2017/10/what-is-the-state-of-english-language-proficiency-in-latin-america); [Wikipedia: English Opens Doors](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/English_Opens_Doors); [EGE resource PRONI](https://egeresource.org/profiles/programs/d5eaeced-aadb-45cf-92f9-dc40edc01284); [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1643377/full)
- Uruguay's Plan Ceibal uses blended learning including remote English teaching: [Inter-American Dialogue](https://thedialogue.org/english-language-learning)

### Inferences
- EU schools need GDPR compliance and local-language UI; their buyer is often the school, and teacher-led freemium works as in the US. Korean hagwons are paying private businesses that buy per-seat tools quickly and value competitive team games. Latin American public programs buy through ministries in large, slow tenders; private English academies and bilingual private schools there are the faster entry.

### Gaps
- Japan juku, China (after the 2021 tutoring ban), Vietnam and Gulf English markets not researched.
- No current budgets or edtech procurement details for PRONI, Colombia or Chile programs.
